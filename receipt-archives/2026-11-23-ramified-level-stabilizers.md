# Which field acts on a level frame?

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Christopher D. Pang's public audit of the arithmetic maps.** A root-of-unity scalar has height zero and preserves every angle of a Hermitian lattice. It can also disappear when an integral frame is reduced at a prime. The release's coefficient field has such a scalar at *each* of its two type primes. On the host, a rank bound prevents this. At step zero, two distinct residue characteristics give another way to detect the scalar. Keeping the acting field and the choice of level visible decides this test. ## A kernel at every selected type prime

Let $g$ be one of the type primes. The host $E$ splits at $g$. The coefficient field $K_s$ contains $E$ and a primitive $g(g-1)$-th root of unity. Thus $L=E(\mu(K_s))\subset K_s$ contains a primitive $\zeta_g$. The field $L$ is CM since it is the compositum of the CM host and a cyclotomic field. Complex conjugation on both fields commutes with all their complex embeddings and has totally real fixed field in the compositum. [The root in the actual coefficient data](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/RootOfUnityEightQMemCoeffGaloisSubfield.lean#L279-L289) · [Splitting in the host](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/NcardPrimesOverOfStabilizer.lean#L73-L108) · [Host degree and CM involution](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/CardDvdCardGL.lean#L193-L209)

At a prime $W$ of $L$ above $g$, take the additive valuation with $v_W(W)=1$. In characteristic $g$ the only $g$-th root of unity in the residue field is 1. For $1\le j<g$, $(1-\zeta_g^j)/(1-\zeta_g)$ reduces to the nonzero element $j$. The identity $\prod_{j=1}^{g-1}(1-\zeta_g^j)=g$ therefore gives
$$
v_W(1-\zeta_g)=e(W/g)/(g-1)>0 .
$$
The maps $I,\zeta_gI,\ldots,\zeta_g^{g-1}I$ all reduce to the identity on the same frame. They have exactly the same operator norm at every complex embedding. At any prime of residue characteristic different from $g$, the same product identity shows that all the factors are units, and reduction distinguishes all $g$ maps.

This extends to the release's actual column rank $k$. On the standard positive Hermitian lattice $\mathcal O_L^k$ the full integral isometry group is $\mu(L)^k\rtimes\mathfrak S_k$. In a unit column all conjugates of each entry have modulus at most one. A nonzero algebraic integer with this property is a root of unity because the characteristic polynomials of its powers have bounded integral coefficients and some powers must coincide. A column of squared norm one consequently has one nonzero entry, and orthogonality forces distinct rows. Conversely all such monomial matrices preserve the lattice. The kernel of reduction to the identity at $W$ is precisely $\mu(L)[g^\infty]^k$. Its order is $g^{ak}$ where $g^a$ is the exact $g$-part of $|\mu(L)|$. For the asserted equality, any root of unity of order prime to $g$ that reduces to 1 is 1, by factoring $T^n-1$ at $T=1$ with $n$ invertible in the residue field.

The index of the upper unitriangular group in $\mathrm{GL}_k(\mathbb F_Q)$ is $\prod_{j=1}^k(Q^j-1)$. Count independent columns and divide by $Q^{k(k-1)/2}$. When $Q$ is a power of $g$, this index is prime to $g$. Thus the index divided by the full automorphism order of the standard lattice has a denominator divisible by $g^{ak}$ for the selected $g>k$. The standard-lattice contribution alone has the full loss even if one recomputes the index with the larger residue field. Other lattice classes could affect a sum. ## A complete one-prime mass calculation

Take $E=\mathbb Q(i)$ and the rank-one form $x\bar y$. Use $\mathfrak g=(2+i)$ over 5. These are the first field and prime of the release's explicit Gaussian example. To isolate the required order 20, work in the intermediate field $S=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{20})=E(\zeta_5)$. This example tests a single level condition. The full coefficient record also requires other roots. The prime $\mathfrak g$ is totally ramified in $S/E$, with $\mathfrak g\mathcal O_S=W^4$ and residue field $\mathbb F_5$. [Gaussian host and the displayed primes 5 and 13](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Columns/BaseInst.lean#L315-L329) · [Orders imposed in the full record](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/RootOfUnityEightQMemCoeffGaloisSubfield.lean#L279-L286)

| Lattice and level ideal | Residue frames | Automorphisms | Stabilizer of a frame | Ordinary frame orbits | Sum of inverse stabilizer orders |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\mathcal O_E$, $\mathfrak g$ | 4 | 4 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| $\mathcal O_S$, $W$ | 4 | 20 | 5 | 1 | $1/5$ |
| $\mathcal O_S$, $W^4$ | 500 | 20 | 1 | 25 | 25 |

Here a frame is a basis of the rank-one module modulo the indicated ideal. Its stabilizer fixes that basis. At $W^4$ the condition is equality on the quotient, which contains nilpotents. The root $\zeta_5$ is unipotent on this quotient since $\zeta_5-1$ is nilpotent, and fails to fix its basis since $v_W(\zeta_5-1)=1<4$. Treating those two conditions as equivalent would change the last row.

The norm-one unit groups have orders 4 and 20. In these CM fields the unit has modulus one at every embedding, hence is a root of unity. Since $20$ divides the number of roots in $S$ and $\varphi(20j)>8$ for every $j>1$, there are exactly 20. At $W$ the four Gaussian units give all residue units and $\mu_5$ is the kernel. At $W^4$ the quotient is $\mathbb F_5[t]/(t^4)$ with $t=\zeta_5-1$, obtained by reducing $\Phi_5(1+t)$ at the Gaussian prime. There are $4\cdot5^3=500$ units, and $\mu_{20}$ injects. These counts give the orbit counts and weights in the table.

One can also check that the table covers the whole genus of the standard norm-one line in both fields. The Gaussian integers are Euclidean. For $S$, coprime discriminants give $|D_S|=4^4(5^3)^2=4{,}000{,}000$. The Minkowski class bound is $(4/\pi)^4\,8!\sqrt{|D_S|}/8^8<13$. Primes over $2,3,7,11$ have residue degrees $4,4,4,2$ respectively, from the orders of $2\bmod5$ and $3,7,11\bmod20$. Only the two primes over 5 can have norm at most 12. Write $z=\zeta_{20}$ and $\alpha=1-z+z^3$. Its norm is 5. In the Euclidean algorithm for $\Phi_{20}=X^8-X^6+X^4-X^2+1$ and $X^3-X+1$, the successive remainders are $X^2-2X+2$, $X-3$ and 5, giving the resultant 5. The two possible reductions of $z$ are $2,3$ in $\mathbb F_5$, and $\alpha$ vanishes only at 3. Its conjugate generates the other prime. Thus the class group is trivial. [Discriminant of composita](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/154Page/handouts/disccomposite.pdf) · [Minkowski's ideal-class norm bound](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/NumberField/ClassNumber.html)

An ideal lattice in this genus satisfies $I\bar I=\mathcal O$. Its exponents at a split conjugate pair are opposite, and its exponents at conjugation-fixed primes vanish. Choose one prime from each pair to write $I=J/\bar J$. Since $J$ is principal, $I$ has a norm-one generator and gives the same isometry class as the standard line. Thus the middle entry $1/5$ is a counterexample to the *unguarded* one-prime mass-times-index integrality assertion for the larger field.

## The second prime and the actual denominator

The Gaussian example's second type prime is 13. Include both required orders $5\cdot4$ and $13\cdot12$ by passing to $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{780})$. On its standard line the group $\mu_{780}$ has reduction kernel of order 5 at a prime above 5 and of order 13 at a prime above 13. Both residue degrees are four, by the orders of $5\bmod156$ and $13\bmod60$. The two kernels intersect in the identity. The joint action on pairs of residue frames is free, giving $(5^4-1)(13^4-1)/780=22{,}848$ ordinary orbits of the standard line. This last count concerns one lattice, with one place over each rational prime.

The release's one-prime mass integrality theorem requires $[E:\mathbb Q]k+1<g$, a prime ideal of norm $g$, and a finite group of **$E$-linear** lattice automorphisms. A nonidentity order-$g$ matrix over $E$ gives, by restriction of scalars, a rational matrix whose minimal polynomial contains $\Phi_g$, forcing $[E:\mathbb Q]k\ge g-1$. In the application the proof sets $[E:\mathbb Q]=2f$ and checks the rank inequality on precisely that host. Extending the coefficient field preserves the original host in the records. The step-zero orbit argument also supplies a two-prime check. A finite-order automorphism that reduces to a unipotent matrix in characteristic $p$ has $p$-power order if reduction is taken through a faithful local realization. Indeed the reduced matrix has $p$-power order. An element of order prime to $p$ congruent to the identity is the identity, since the geometric sum of its powers reduces to an invertible scalar matrix. Applying this to the prime-to-$p$ part leaves only $p$-power torsion. Doing it at a second, distinct residue characteristic kills the rest. The release proves finiteness from total definiteness and the lattice, applies these two local tests to the **same rational matrix**, then transfers order one to the twisted classes. Finally it drops the stabilizer divisor only at step zero under the resulting order-one premise. At the other steps the order divides `DGam` and its valuation is charged. ## Unresolved

The host group, reduced prime and extended level ideal give three different arithmetic tests. A single reduced frame upstairs really loses $g$-power information that the height cannot see. The published one-prime inequality uses the host, and the step-zero argument retains the second prime. This does not give an invalid inference on the traced branch. The remaining place to test for lost multiplicity is a transfer that counts coefficient-field choices as distinct after identifying them by a single host measurement.
