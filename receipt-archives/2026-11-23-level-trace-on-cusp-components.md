# A large-prime denominator on the cusp-form lattice itself

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Christopher D. Pang’s arithmetic-map audit.** For the actual finite level group, character and nonzero cusp lattice appearing in the low-slope comparison, the normalized character projection fails integral membership on an explicit kind of input. Cut an integral eigenform to one connected component. Averaging it back divides by the component's orbit size. On the selected trivial-character windows, that size has an odd prime factor outside the level modulus, the auxiliary level primes and the prescribed small-prime range. The release's norm estimate uses the **unnormalized sum**. A separate division takes place only in a vector space over a field. ## The two level maps

Write $F$ for the coefficient field of the deep cover, $C$ for its group changing the cofactor level $t=15^s$, and $H$ for `grpHR`. In the notation of the release $C$ is `grpR` and $H$ is the preimage of the type-prime upper unitriangular group. Its character $\chi$ factors through that type group and is trivial on $C$. On cusp forms of weight `Gk` the pullbacks are $F$-linear. Write them as $h^*$, using the right-action convention of the source, and put
$$
 T_\chi f=\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h)^{-1}h^*f,\qquad P_\chi f=T_\chi f/|H|.
$$
Then $T_\chi^2=|H|T_\chi$ and $P_\chi$ is the character projection. Each pullback is an isometry for the Petersson norm and each character value has modulus one at every complex embedding. Thus $\|T_\chi\|\le |H|$ and $\|P_\chi\|\le1$. On any nonzero character eigenform these operator norms are attained. The same statements apply to $C$ with trivial character on the invariant subspace. ## The component cut

Let $G=H$ with character $\chi$, or $G=C$ with trivial character. Pick a component supporting a nonzero eigenform and let $\mathcal O$ be its orbit. The deep coefficient field defines the individual geometric components. In the release's formalism it is algebraically closed in each component function field. Smoothness in characteristic zero gives geometrically integral components, and the construction supplies their individual idempotents. Multiplication by the component idempotent $e_Z$ preserves the cusp lattice and keeps exactly the restriction on $Z$. In particular a sum of such idempotents cuts an eigenform to its whole orbit while preserving the eigencondition. [Geometric hypothesis for the deep field](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/NormNegCmConstantEqAtAllEmbeddings.lean#L187-L203) · [The condition on a component function field](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/CompHt.lean#L95-L101) · [Integral component cuts and their sum](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/CuspFormsSupLatticeSplitOverComponents.lean#L20-L59)

Write $t_{\mathcal O}=|\mathcal O|$, and choose an integral eigenform $v\ne0$ supported on $\mathcal O$. For one $Z\in\mathcal O$ take the genuine integral input $f=e_Zv$. Counting the translates of that idempotent gives
$$
 T_\chi f=|\mathrm{Stab}_G(Z)|\,v,\qquad P_\chi f=\frac{v}{t_{\mathcal O}},
$$
where for $G=C$ the sum and projection refer to $C$. Every term is a section of the same weight.

One can enforce the exact denominator simultaneously at every prime over $t_{\mathcal O}$. Intersect the finite cusp lattice with the $F$-subspace of eigenforms supported on $\mathcal O$. The resulting nonzero module $J$ is finitely generated and torsion-free over $\mathcal O_F$. At each of the finitely many primes $\lambda\mid t_{\mathcal O}$ choose a nonzero class in $J/\lambda J$ and lift all the classes to a single $v\in J$ by the Chinese remainder theorem. Over each localized discrete valuation ring $v$ is primitive. Since $J$ is the intersection with a vector subspace, dividing this vector cannot restore ambient integrality without restoring membership in $J$. The ideal of integral scalars clearing $P_\chi f$ in the cusp lattice is therefore exactly $t_{\mathcal O}\mathcal O_F$. This constructs the witness from the actual lattice without needing Fourier coefficients for a named cusp form. ## A determinant visible before any norm is taken

Here is a lower bound for **every** such orbit. Put $\mathfrak b_t=\mathfrak b+(t)$. ## A prime forced beyond the level

On the trivial-character host put $b=2^e$, where $2^{e+2}$ is the dyadic conductor, and write $f=[E:\mathbb Q]/2=2b$. For sufficiently large windows $e\ge2$. At either $p=3$ or $5$, the residue degree in the real dyadic cyclotomic field is $b$. The class of $p$ has order $b$ in $(\mathbb Z/2^{e+2})^\times/\{\pm1\}$. For $k\ge1$, $v_2(p^{2^k}-1)=k+2$ for both primes, by repeatedly factoring a difference of squares. Neither a smaller odd power nor a smaller power of two gives $-1$ modulo $2^{e+2}$ (for 3, odd powers are $3\bmod8$). The two additional quadratic factors of $E$ are unramified at $p$ and have residue degrees at most 2, which divides $b$. Thus $p$ has four primes of degree $b$ in $E$. It splits already in $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$, so complex conjugation pairs them and the half level selects two above each $p$. Consequently
$$
 |D_t|=15^{2b(s-1)}(3^b-1)^2(5^b-1)^2.
$$
These factors count cofactor determinant markings on a geometric orbit. [The host subgroup and the small split primes](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L19-L37) · [Identification with the field supplied on the window](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/IsChiField.lean#L5-L25)

For a prime outside the level, put $B=(3^{b/2}+1)/2$. It is an odd integer greater than one, and $v_2(B-1)=e$. Every prime $\ell\mid B$ has $\operatorname{ord}_\ell(3)=b$, so $\ell\equiv1\bmod b$. If all prime divisors had $\ell\equiv1\bmod 2b$, their product with multiplicities would also be $1\bmod 2b$. Thus at least one has
$$
 \ell\ge b+1,\qquad v_2(\ell-1)=e,\qquad \ell^2\mid |D_t|.
$$
All the released level primes are $1\bmod 2^{e+2}$, so this $\ell$ is outside that list. For large $e$ it is different from 2, 3 and 5, and congruences also exclude the host primes $q,d$. The scale has $b$ of order $x^{2+8\varepsilon}$ up to fixed factors, far beyond the prescribed small-prime splitting range $Y^{\varepsilon/5}$. Therefore the true geometric projector can incur a denominator at a growing prime where the coefficient-field trace from the same deep cover is integral. The latter has degree supported on $2,3,5$. ## Which operation enters the proof?

The released maps send $f$ to the **raw** trace of $f$ times a translated cusp section. Membership follows from summing integral translates with root-of-unity coefficients. The Petersson estimate puts $|H|$ in front of the norm of each product, then sums the squares over $|H|$ outputs, giving $|H|^3$ times the squared multiplication bound. A nonzero determinant of the translates supplies joint injectivity over the field. The slope consumer passes the same maps, their lattice membership and that upper estimate. ## Unresolved
