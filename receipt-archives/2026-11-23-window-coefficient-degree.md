# The coefficient field must outgrow every polynomial on the zeta windows

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Christopher D. Pang's public arithmetic-map audit.** On the released construction for the trivial character, the relative degree of the auxiliary coefficient field $K_s$ over the selected host field $E$ exceeds $x^A$ for every fixed $A$, eventually along its windows. This is a necessary cost on the actual window hypotheses. One can derive it on paper from the required splitting of small primes and the root of unity in the coefficient record. It refutes any reading of the construction that fixes the coefficient field or bounds its relative degree polynomially in the window scale. The measured lattice height uses a normalized average; the checked step still makes sense with this growing degree. ## The fields and quantifiers

Fix the small positive $\varepsilon$ in the supply theorem. Its constants $J\ge1$ and $c\ge1$ are fixed before taking arbitrarily large scales. For a selected $x$ there is a $Y$ with $x^{1/J}\le Y\le x$. Write $B=Y^{\varepsilon/5}$. The pair $q,d$ consists of primes and satisfies

$$
q\equiv3\pmod8,\qquad d\equiv7\pmod{16},\qquad
\log q\le B,\qquad \log d>\tfrac12 B(\log Y)^2.
$$

Both $q$ and every odd prime $p\le B$ divide $d+1$. The release sets $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. In particular every one of these primes splits: $-d\equiv1\bmod p$. The dyadic exponent $e$ satisfies $c\,2^e\le2x^{2+8\varepsilon}$. ## How many distinct classes the window forces

If an algebraic integer of $K$ has odd norm, write it as $a+b(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$. Its norm is $a^2+ab+((d+1)/4)b^2$. Because $(d+1)/4$ is even, odd norm forces $b$ even. Hence **every nonrational algebraic integer of odd norm has norm at least $d$**.

For each odd prime $p\le B$, choose one of the two primes $\mathfrak p$ above it. For each subset $U$ form $I_U=\prod_{p\in U}\mathfrak p$. If two subsets represent the same ideal class, $I_U\overline{I_V}$ is an integral principal ideal, since $I_V\overline{I_V}$ is generated by a rational integer. Its odd norm is at most $(\prod_{p\le B}p)^2\le e^{2B\log B}<d$ for all sufficiently large $Y$. A generator must therefore be a rational integer. Such an integer has equal valuations at $\mathfrak p$ and $\bar{\mathfrak p}$; here these valuations are precisely the two subset indicators. Thus $U=V$. Writing $\pi(B)$ for the number of rational primes up to $B$ gives

$$
h(K)\ \ge\ 2^{\,\pi(B)-1}.
$$

There is also a bound for a **particular** prime. For a split odd $p$ above, let $r_p$ be the ideal-class order of $\mathfrak p$. A generator of $\mathfrak p^{r_p}$ has norm $p^{r_p}$. It cannot be rational because its valuation at the conjugate prime is zero. Therefore $p^{r_p}\ge d$. In particular, eventually
$$
r_3>\frac{B(\log Y)^2}{2\log3},\qquad r_q>\tfrac12(\log Y)^2 .
$$
The second bound uses $q\mid d+1$ separately, even when $q>B$.

A small congruence control uses the prime $d=5368439$. For $p=3,5,7,11$, the sixteen oriented products correspond to distinct reduced primitive forms $(n,1,(d+1)/(4n))$, one for each divisor $n$ of $1155$. This verifies the class separation directly in a finite example. The size-window assertion above follows from the inequalities for arbitrary qualifying $d$. ## Why the class growth reaches the coefficient field

The class group of $K$ has odd order. Here is a direct proof. If a class has square one, a fractional ideal $\mathfrak a$ representing it satisfies $\bar{\mathfrak a}=(\gamma)\mathfrak a$ with $\gamma\bar\gamma=1$. Write $\gamma=t/\bar t$: take $t=1+\gamma$ unless $\gamma=-1$, in which case take $t=\sqrt{-d}$. Then $(t)\mathfrak a$ is invariant under conjugation. Inert primes are rational ideals, split primes in such an ideal occur with equal exponents, and the sole ramified prime is generated by $\sqrt{-d}$. Every conjugation-invariant fractional ideal is therefore principal. This excludes an element of order two.

Extension of ideals from $K$ to $E$ is injective on the class group. Its composition with ideal norm is raising to the power $[E:K]$, which is coprime to the odd class number. Consequently $h(E)\ge h(K)$. The release requires $K_s$ both to principalize the ideals of $E$ and to contain a primitive $h(E)$-th root of unity. The latter is actually read when placing unramified character values in the coefficient field. [Both requirements](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/RootOfUnityEightQMemCoeffGaloisSubfield.lean#L279-L294) · [Construction of principalizing generators](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/AuxiliaryFrameNormalizedSpaces.lean#L147-L161) · [Use of the root of unity by a downstream consumer](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ValPowClassNumber.lean#L48-L62)

* From principalization alone, the order $r_3$ divides $[K_s:E]$. Indeed the ideal above 3 retains order $r_3$ in $E$, and taking a principal generator's ideal norm from $K_s$ to $E$ makes its $[K_s:E]$-th power principal.
* The primitive root forces $[K_s:\mathbb Q]\ge\varphi(h(E))$. For all $m\ge1$, $\varphi(m)\ge\sqrt{m/2}$: the squared totient of each odd prime power is at least that prime power; only $2^1$ loses a factor of two. Thus
  $$
  [K_s:E]\ \ge\ \frac{2^{(\pi(B)-2)/2}}{8x^{2+8\varepsilon}} .
  $$
  [Cyclotomic degree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Cyclotomic/PrimitiveRoots.lean#L180-L185) · [Irreducibility over the rationals](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/Cyclotomic/Roots.lean#L184-L194)

One elementary estimate finishes the growth calculation. Set $m=\lfloor B/2\rfloor$. The central binomial coefficient is at least $4^m/(2m+1)$. Each prime contributes at most $2m$ to it, since each power of a prime contributes at most one to its valuation in the factorial ratio. Hence $\pi(B)\gg B/\log B$. As $B\ge x^{\varepsilon/(5J)}$ with fixed $\varepsilon,J$, the relative degree eventually exceeds every fixed power of $x$.

## What survives division by the degree?

Let $S$ be any such coefficient field. For a prime $\mathfrak p$ of $K$ of norm 3, the extended ideal has absolute norm $3^{[S:K]}$. In the release's normalized finite-place height it contributes exactly

$$
\frac{1}{[S:\mathbb Q]}\sum_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}
 e(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)\log N\mathfrak P
 =\frac{\log 3}{2}.
$$

The growing multiplicity cancels because $\sum e f=[S:K]$. At complex places the source also counts every lift with its correct weight. The Gaussian lattice bound uses the **host** root discriminant, and the class-number budget explicitly includes the factor $h(E)$ with an upper bound in terms of a power of $b=\exp(x^\varepsilon)$. Neither checked bound places a polynomial cap on $[K_s:E]$. The independent November 22 reconstruction likewise enlarges a field of definition and averages over its embeddings. ## Finite cross-check on the rank dependence

In the [preceding discriminant $-439$ example](2026-11-23-vacuum-scalar-field.md), the degree-six obstruction can be made exact for the trace lattice as well. With the notation there, let $E=K(\sqrt{13})$, $w=(1+\sqrt{13})/2$, $Y=\mathfrak P^{-1}\oplus\mathfrak P^{-1}$. In the integral basis $(1_1,1_2,w_1,w_2)$ the trace Gram matrix has blocks $(2I,I;I,7I)$.

For a 2-local reference, start with $(w_1,w_2,7-w_1,7-w_2)$, of squared lengths $7,7,91,91$. On the last two columns apply the matrix with rows $(2,-3),(3,2)$ divided by 13. All four resulting squared lengths are 7. Multiply the four columns by the quaternion multiplication matrix for $(2,1,1,1)$ divided by 7; this makes them orthonormal. The determinant of this reference in the integral basis is $1/13$. Every denominator is odd. At odd primes other than 13, start from $(1_1,1_2,\sqrt{13}_1,\sqrt{13}_2)$, mix the first two columns by the matrix with rows $(1,1),(1,-1)$ divided by 2, and the last two by the matrix with rows $(1,-5),(5,1)$ divided by 26. This orthonormal basis has determinant $-1/13$.

At 13 use the same first two columns and mix the last two by the same two-by-two matrix as the first pair, multiplied by $c=(13/\alpha)(4+s)/(4-s)$. Here $c\bar c=1/13$ and $c$ has valuations $-1,0$ above 13. These span precisely the required two local lattices: the $\sqrt{13}$ directions are divided by 13 above $\mathfrak p$ and unchanged above $\bar{\mathfrak p}$. The determinant in integral coordinates is $c^2$, so the transition determinant has valuations $-1,+1$. Its fractional ideal across places is $I^{-1}$. This proves the same minimum degree six for this reference.

Repeat that four-dimensional block $t$ times. The determinant ideal becomes $I^{-t}$. Its class has order $3/\gcd(3,t)$ and an odd $t$ forces even ramification at 13. Raising the previous correcting scalar to the appropriate signed $t$-th power attains both requirements, giving exact minimum degree **$6/\gcd(6,t)$**. The application uses even source and target ranks, so $t=NN'/2$ is even for this repeated model. The degrees remaining in this model are 1 or 3. This test concerns a chosen trace lattice; the superpolynomial window result above uses the actual host requirements and does not depend on this example. ## Unresolved

We have a human-checkable lower bound on an auxiliary field required by the construction. A refutation still needs a downstream inequality that bounds an **unnormalized** coefficient-field quantity too tightly, or a use that replaces this field with a smaller one. The height average, class-number upper estimate and character-value use checked here survive. The coefficient-field degree and the cost of the arithmetic maps remain distinct quantities.
