# Matching the four lifts to the unitary and Galois local conditions

**24 November 2026.** The [four-dimensional summand](2026-11-24-two-ramified-hecke-fields.md) occurs in an actual space of algebraic modular forms. Here we check how the definite group, its local level, the ordinary condition and its cuspidal base change relate to the published Case I construction. We use the standard stable base change for the odd unitary group, with auxiliary twisting character one. The argument below uses established representation-theoretic results independent of the RH release.

## Center, infinity and the two split primes at 571

The definite group is $U(3)$ of the standard Hermitian lattice over $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, whose class number is one. At infinity use the trivial coefficient representation. At split 571 take the inverse image of a line stabilizer, with quadratic character of the two-dimensional quotient determinant. All remaining finite stabilizers are the full integral ones. The global unitary central character is trivial. Scalar units act trivially even at 571 since their action on the quotient determinant is a square. A norm-one idele modulo these central units gives a fractional ideal of norm one. It is principal, and its generator has complex absolute value one since the ideal norm is one. Thus it is already rational on the norm-one torus. In particular the product of the three normalized Satake eigenvalues at a good split prime is one.

On the source model the inertia at 571 has one trivial and two quadratic characters and no monodromy. A regular algebraic conjugate-self-dual cuspidal candidate transfers at the split prime to the usual $\mathrm{GL}_3$ representation. Its unramified character and the two quadratic twists of unramified characters give the displayed parahoric character. In a principal series, order its inducing characters with the trivial inertia character first. A vector supported on the Borel times the line stabilizer transforms by the required character. At other split primes the unramified representation has its spherical vector. In the reverse direction a vector of our type has principal-series inertial support $1,\psi,\psi$. One can restrict to the Iwahori with its diagonal character and use the principal-series type theorem. The full $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_{571})$ on the equal-character block selects its spherical component after untwisting. To see the last assertion, induce first from the $\mathrm{GL}_1\times\mathrm{GL}_2$ Levi. The two inertial character types are distinct, so only the matching line coset contributes to the indicated parahoric eigenspace. Its vectors require invariants under the maximal compact in the two-dimensional block up to the determinant character. This also rules out monodromy in that block. ## The ramified prime 7 and the coefficient prime 5

For the original arithmetic normalization write $\rho$ for a three-dimensional representation of $G_K$ and $\varepsilon$ for cyclotomic. The source attaches $\rho\varepsilon^{-1}$ using geometric Frobenius and multiplies the unitary Satake parameters by $Nw$. These factors cancel for $\rho$. Its ordinary inertia characters are $\varepsilon,1,\varepsilon^{-1}$. The corresponding infinitesimal character at each complex embedding is $(1,0,-1)$ in the unitary normalization. Subtracting the half-sum of positive roots gives highest weight $(0,0,0)$ on compact $U(3)$. Local-global compatibility for cohomological conjugate-self-dual cuspidal $\mathrm{GL}_3/K$ identifies the Weil–Deligne parameters away from 5 and gives the Hodge–Tate and crystalline comparisons at 5. Rank three satisfies Shin’s odd-rank hypothesis. At ramified 7, a candidate from the source has unramified restriction to $G_{K_7}$, determinant one and residual eigenvalues $3,1,2$. Conjugate self-duality makes its normalized Satake multiset $\{a,1,a^{-1}\}$, with $a\ne\pm1$. This is a regular tempered local parameter. On $U(3)(\mathbb Q_7)$ it is the stable base change of principal series from an unramified character of $K_7^\times$ with value $a$ on a uniformizer and the trivial character on $U(1)$. The pair $a,a^{-1}$ is distinct. Its centralizer has one connected torus for the pair and $\{\pm1\}$ for the middle line, with the latter component already accounted for by the fixed dual center. The tempered packet on the quasi-split group has a single member. The full self-dual lattice stabilizer has a fixed vector in that principal series. To prove transitivity on rational isotropic lines, start with a primitive integral isotropic vector, choose an integral partner of pairing one, and subtract half its norm times the first vector to make the partner isotropic. The orthogonal rank-one complement has the same norm class for any such pair by the determinant of a unimodular basis. This gives the Borel–compact decomposition. The inducing character is trivial on their compact intersection. The global central character is trivial on $-I$ as well. [Haines, special-parahoric Satake parameters](https://math.umd.edu/~tjh/Satake_parameters_v4.pdf) · [Local and global packet conventions](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~kaletha/unitary.pdf)

The [two-step matrices](2026-11-24-one-hecke-line-at-571.md) check this place numerically too. At 7 the two neighboring vertex degrees are $7+1,7+1$. At inert $p=3,5$ they are $p^3+1,p+1$. On the hyperspecial or specified special vertices, the two-step operator omits immediate returns. Relative to an end of the tree the remaining paths give one inward move, $p-1$ moves of unchanged height and $p^4$ outward moves at an inert prime. At ramified 7 the last number is $7^2$. Normalized induction therefore gives
$$T_p=p^2(a+a^{-1})+(p-1)\quad(p\text{ inert}),\qquad
T_7=7(a+a^{-1})+6.$$
Here $a$ is the unramified inducing character evaluated at a uniformizer of the quadratic local field, so the stable base change has parameters $a,1,a^{-1}$. The trivial representation gives row counts $p^4+p$ and 56 respectively. The measured $T_7=1\bmod5$ gives $a+a^{-1}=0\bmod5$, the required distinct roots. At 3, the reading $4\bmod5$ gives $a=-1\bmod5$ at the place of norm 9. [Ballantine–Ciubotaru, the biregular tree and its Hecke algebra](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.3504)

Every one of the four lifts gives $T_5=0\bmod\mathfrak p$ at the chosen 5-adic prime. Thus $25(a+a^{-1})=T_5-4$ is a unit congruent to 1. The two valuations of $a,a^{-1}$ are $2,-2$ and the unit root after scaling the negative-slope parameter by 25 reduces to 1. For a crystalline Galois representation with the parallel weights above $K_5$ this gives ordinarity. The Newton slopes meet the Hodge bounds at each break. The smallest-slope Frobenius line must itself be weakly admissible by the Hodge inequality, then so must the next line in the quotient. This gives the flag, starting with the character of highest cyclotomic exponent. Its graded characters are unramified twists of $\varepsilon,1,\varepsilon^{-1}$. The middle character is 1 and the outside unramified factors are inverse, by conjugate duality at this inert place and determinant one. The slope-zero Weil–Deligne parameter is 1. In either Frobenius convention the outside unit factors reduce to 1. They cannot be roots of unity in a tempered representation, since the corresponding complex normalized parameter would have magnitude 25 or $1/25$. This gives the required unlinked flag, even after positive powers.

Conversely, for a putative point the local comparison at 5 makes its three crystalline flag characters the Weil–Deligne semisimplification. Its finite inertia is trivial. A nonzero monodromy map between adjacent slopes would require equal unramified multipliers in ratio cyclotomic between the actual characters, which the source’s additional `Unl` condition excludes. Thus its local base change is unramified and the hyperspecial test at 5 applies. ## Why the four computed systems are stable cuspidal

Use the kernel of the quadratic parahoric character when regarding our space as a subspace of fixed-level automorphic forms. At 7 the full integral stabilizer contains an Iwahori subgroup. Theorem 7.3(1) of Evra, Feigon, Maurischat and Parzanchevski, applied to this rank-three definite group, rules out the non-tempered A-type alternative for a non-one-dimensional form at this level. The inert-prime table checks the same exclusion. An Iwahori-spherical A-type at 5 has parameter $-5$ up to inversion, giving $T_5=-126\equiv4$, in conflict with the reading 0. A one-dimensional representation at split 571 factors through the determinant. A determinant-one diagonal matrix in the parahoric can have nonsquare quotient determinant, which excludes our character. The remaining forms are tempered. The same authors spell out that Rogawski’s base change in this case agrees locally and is either stable cuspidal or a proper isobaric sum. [*Ramanujan bigraphs*, Theorem 7.3 and proof of Theorem 7.2](https://math.huji.ac.il/~parzan/Ramanujan_bigraphs.pdf#page=90) · [Inert A-type criterion, Corollary 7.15](https://math.huji.ac.il/~parzan/Ramanujan_bigraphs.pdf#page=95)

A proper tempered sum of total rank three has a conjugate-self-dual Hecke character constituent. In type $2+1$ the rank-one constituent must be fixed by conjugate duality. In type $1+1+1$ that involution fixes at least one constituent, counted with multiplicity. Its infinity type is $(x/\bar x)^s$ with $s=-1,0,1$ from the compact weight. The finite part is unramified away from 571 by the spherical local parameters, including 7. At the two primes over 571 it is either trivial at both or quadratic at both by conjugate duality and the inertial support just identified. Class number one then makes its value on an ideal $(b)$ away from the conductors $(b/\bar b)^s\psi(Nb)^\delta$, $\delta=0,1$, up to inverting the reciprocity convention. The unit $-1$ introduces no ambiguity.

At the prime generated by $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-7})/2$ over 2, $\omega/\bar\omega$ has trace $-3/2\equiv1\bmod5$ and norm 1. It has order six in $\mathbb F_{25}^\times$. The two nonzero exponents $s$ therefore retain a factor three in their residual order even after a quadratic twist. But both Hecke readings at 2 and the trivial center give the normalized residual polynomial $(X-1)(X^2+1)$, with roots $1,2,3$ all in $\mathbb F_5^\times$. For $s=0$, the nontrivial finite character gives $\psi(2)=-1$, also absent from that polynomial. Only the trivial character remains. If 1 is an eigenvalue everywhere, determinant one forces the other two eigenvalues to be inverses. At a good split prime the two directed traces would then be identical in characteristic zero. Both conjugate pairs have a nonzero difference at 2 of valuation $1/2$. This excludes the last character and proves that the four systems have stable cuspidal base change.

Attach the characteristic-zero Galois representations to these cuspidal forms by the cited local-global theorems. If such a Galois representation were reducible, it would again have a conjugate-self-dual one-dimensional constituent. Its de Rham characters correspond by class field theory to the same algebraic Hecke character possibilities, with the finite part unramified at 5 by crystallinity. The same calculation excludes them. At every finite place the local constituents have toral support and the normalized inducing characters have complex absolute value one. Strong multiplicity one identifies any other cuspidal system with the same cofinite attachment. Together with the unlinked calculation at 5 this verifies the classical meaning of the extra witness conditions in `Adm3U_s`.

## Frobenius on the unramified line at 571

The character on inertia at 571 alone does not tell us what Frobenius does on its fixed line. This can be measured with just one carefully chosen row. Let $\pi=-7+18\omega$, $s=\bar\pi/\pi$, $v=(1,1+\omega,2)$ and $h(v,v)=9$. The matrix $\gamma=I+(s-1)vv^\dagger/9$ is unitary, has determinant $s$, and is integral away from the two primes over 571 since $\bar\pi-\pi=-18\sqrt{-7}$. In the chosen split embedding it multiplies the line $v$ by $s$ and fixes $v^\perp$. Choose the local frame $k=(v,e_2,e_3)$ of determinant one.

For the parahoric $P$ stabilizing the line, the double coset of $g=\operatorname{diag}(s,1,1)$ has representatives $p_b g$ with $p_b$ lower unipotent, lower-left column $571b$, $b\in\mathbb F_{571}^2$. They all have character $+1$. The columns of $\gamma^{-1} k p_b g$ give integral frames of determinant one. Their first vectors run through the affine plane $v+v^\perp$ modulo 571 because $571s$ is a unit and $\gamma^{-1}$ reduces to projection along $v$ onto $v^\perp$. The action on the residual function $F$ is exactly
$$ (U F)(v)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_{571})}
          \left(\frac{9\,v^\dagger y}{571}\right)F(y),$$
with the Legendre symbol of zero equal to zero and normalized line representatives as in the code. This row has $F(v)\ne0$ and gives $(UF)(v)/F(v)=1\bmod5$. The operator commutes with the good-prime system, so this single nonzero coordinate determines its eigenvalue on the unique line.

In the normalized local principal series write $\nu$ for the unramified character on the one-dimensional block. The parahoric eigenspace for this type is one-dimensional. On its vector supported on the Borel times $P$, only $b=0$ contributes at the identity because the nonzero $b$ move the line out of that cell. The modulus factor gives $U=571\,\nu(\pi)^{-1}$. Hence the Frobenius on the unramified line reduces to $+1$ in either convention. This sign is used in the [global residual check](2026-11-24-global-residual-character-at-571.md).
