# Does a phase stay a phase at the other embedding?

**23 November 2026.** We followed the normalization in the November 20 proof from a nonzero local theta vector to a rational class sum at another complex embedding. The danger is real. Even an algebraic unit of absolute value one can acquire arbitrarily large absolute value under an embedding that fixes the host imaginary quadratic field. The actual comparison controls several different factors.

## An exact failure of transporting absolute value

Let $a$ be a root on the unit circle of $X^4-X^3-X^2-X+1$. The polynomial reduces modulo two to the fifth cyclotomic polynomial, irreducible there since $2$ has order four modulo five. Dividing by $a^2$ shows that $t=a+a^{-1}$ satisfies $t^2-t-3=0$. One value of $t$ lies strictly between $-2$ and $2$, giving our choice of $a$. The other exceeds $2$, giving a real conjugate $b>1$. Both $a$ and $a^{-1}$ are algebraic integers.

Take the coefficient field $\mathbb Q(a,i)$. The field $\mathbb Q(a)$ admits a real embedding, so its intersection with $\mathbb Q(i)$ is $\mathbb Q$. There is an embedding fixing $i$ and sending $a$ to $b$. The factor $a^N$ preserves every norm at the starting embedding. At the other it multiplies lengths by $b^N$. A receiving-place isometry cannot undo this. The same construction works with any imaginary quadratic field in place of $\mathbb Q(i)$.

## Three factors in the actual comparison

* The normalized finite character value $y_P$ includes the factor $N(P)^{-1/2}$. At a principal ideal $(x)$, the product of the $y_P^{v_P(x)}$ is the inverse of the relative norm used by the chosen CM type. An embedding over the host fixes this norm. Hence the quotient of the new and old values defines a character of the ideal class group. Its values have finite order. This proves their absolute values at the receiving place without applying a field embedding to an absolute-value identity. ## A zero hidden in section evaluation

The proof of constancy on the cover uses rules tested on reduced finite-type algebras. The generic evaluator returns zero if a point cannot be obtained from such a test. This distinction can affect even the constant rule $1$. For the functor $P(S)=\{z\in S:z^2=0\}$, take $S=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ and $z=\varepsilon$. Every point in every reduced test ring is zero, so none maps to $z$. For the affine line the point $z=\varepsilon$ **does** come from a reduced test, by $k[T]\to S$, $T\mapsto\varepsilon$. The distinction is factorization through a test algebra. [Test-ring rules](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/AlgebraMapCAt.lean#L24-L36) · [Evaluation with a zero fallback](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/AlgebraMapCAt.lean#L51-L57)

At the cover comparison the values being compared live in $\mathbb C$, and the definition of equality of the rules quantifies over reduced finite-type test rings. On each such ring the intersection of the maximal ideals is zero. Residue fields embed in $\mathbb C$ over the chosen embedding, so testing all the resulting complex points detects equality. The moduli record also requires smoothness of its ambient space and the framed points retain their vertical tangent maps from dual numbers. This closes the nilpotent attack on **this use** of evaluation. The source states its moduli representation property on reduced bases and adds a separate test on first-order deformations.
