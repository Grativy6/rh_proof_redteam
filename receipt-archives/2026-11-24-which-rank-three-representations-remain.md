# Which rank-three representations remain at 571?

**24 November 2026.** The [rank-two calculation](2026-11-24-the-rank-two-alternative-at-571.md) leaves a testable implication of the released argument. At the concrete order-five input, its characteristic-zero construction requires an irreducible representation over $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$. On the ordinary mathematical interpretation of its automorphy and local conditions, its image must be **Zariski dense in $\mathrm{SL}_3$ over $\overline{\mathbb Q}_5$**. Below are the exclusions that give this test. They use classical local-global compatibility, a numerical Bernoulli value, and the cited descent and strong modularity theorems. They do not provide the required rank-three point. ## What any surviving point must carry

Let $\rho$ be its representation of $G_K$. Write $\omega$ for the mod-five cyclotomic character, $\psi=\chi_{-571}$ and $z=\psi\omega$ on $G_K$. Its residual matrices are diagonal with characters $z,1,z^{-1}$. The character $z$ has image all of $\mathbb F_5^\times$ already on inertia at 5. The determinant of $\rho$ is 1. At primes away from 5 and 571 the residual-kernel inertia condition on $G_K$ kills inertia, including that above 7. At the two primes of $K$ over 571, inertia acts by an involution with eigenvalues $-1,1,-1$. At 5 the three flag characters have inertia powers $1,0,-1$ of the cyclotomic character and unramified finite factors. Their product is unramified. Since $K$ has class number one and is totally imaginary, a character unramified at every finite place is trivial. Twisting $\rho$ by $\psi$ leaves one nontrivial inertia line over each prime above 571, giving prime-to-five conductor ideal $(571)$ and norm $571^2$. [Kernel and entrywise reduction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/UnitaryThreeGroups/RhoKApply.lean#L59-L83) · [The flag with cyclotomic inertia](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/AutomorphicForms/BlochKatoSelmerVanishes.lean#L89-L114) · [The additional unlinked flag](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/TheoremsMuLinked/AutP.lean#L10-L13)

On every $g\in G_K$ the trace modulo 5 belongs to $\{3,4,1\}$, corresponding to $z(g)=1,-1,\pm2$. In particular it is never zero. A representation induced from a character of a cubic extension has trace zero on an element acting as a three-cycle on the cosets. Every transitive subgroup of $S_3$ contains such an element. This rules out all irreducible representations permuting three lines, including cubic monomial lifts.

The ordinary two-dimensional alternative and an adjoint three-dimensional lift are separate possibilities. We test the latter independently.

## A weight-two rational adjoint would have to occur at level 3997

Suppose $\rho=\operatorname{Ad}^0(U)|_{G_K}$ for the five-adic representation of a classical cuspidal weight-two newform over $\mathbb Q$. Here $\operatorname{Ad}^0$ acts on traceless endomorphisms. The adjoint is unchanged under taking the dual or twisting $U$ by a character. Use the arithmetic convention for $U$ in which the determinant of a weight-two form is cyclotomic times its nebentypus.

**Residual characters.** On $\ker z$, every $\bar U(g)$ has equal eigenvalues. Indeed if $r$ is their ratio the adjoint trace gives $1+r+r^{-1}=3$. The finite projective image of this kernel is a 5-group, since every element has 5-power order. A nontrivial finite 5-subgroup of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbb F}_5)$ fixes a unique point of the projective line. This follows by embedding the group in some $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_{5^a})$ and conjugating it into a Sylow subgroup of unipotent translations. Normality then gives an invariant line for $\bar U$. If the kernel has trivial projective image, its projective quotient on $G_K$ is cyclic of order dividing four, again giving invariant lines. Thus $\bar U|_{G_K}$ has two character constituents. Their ratio is $z$ or $z^{-1}$, as is seen on a generator of the quotient of order four. The two are distinct, and $z$ extends to $G_{\mathbb Q}$. Conjugation by $G_{\mathbb Q}$ cannot interchange the two constituents, since it would invert this order-four ratio. An invariant line in the nonsplit case is unique, and in the split case the two distinct lines are individually stable. Thus the global residual semisimplification is reducible too.

**Remove scalar ramification.** At every rational prime $p\ne5,7,571$ inertia is inside $G_K$ and acts scalarly on $U$. At 571 its projective inertia is quadratic. At 7 it has order at most two since $I_7\cap G_K$ is projectively trivial. These are finite inertia actions. Projectively they are finite, the determinant on prime-to-five inertia is finite, and a scalar unipotent matrix is the identity. At a place with nontrivial projective inertia the two inertia eigencharacters have quadratic quotient. Frobenius preserves each one. Otherwise it swaps their two residual eigenspaces, making the local residual representation irreducible, contrary to the global character constituents just proved. The eigenspaces do reduce distinctly since an inertia element separates their unit eigenvalues by a factor of $-1$.

At 5 the Weil–Deligne representation of $U$ has scalar finite inertia. In fact $K$ is unramified quadratic locally at 5, so the inertia subgroup is unchanged. The adjoint over $K_5$ has a flag by crystalline characters, each an unramified character times a parallel power of cyclotomic. The potentially semistable functor on this classical representation is exact. Its finite inertial action on the adjoint is therefore trivial.

Here the *unlinked* clause is essential. If $U$ had nonzero monodromy at 5, its two-dimensional Weil–Deligne representation would be special, with semisimple part a character times adjacent norm powers. Over $K_5$ the adjoint would have the three Weil–Deligne characters $|\cdot|,1,|\cdot|^{-1}$, independent of that scalar twist. Match these to the three ordinary flag characters of $\rho$. Each has a distinct cyclotomic exponent on inertia and a unit unramified multiplier, so its slope fixes its position. The three unramified multipliers must all be one. The actual flag would contain characters in ratio exactly cyclotomic, contrary to `Unl`. This excludes nonzero monodromy at 5 for $U$. Merely assuming a flag with the three distinct weights would leave this possibility open. [Which rank-three points have this extra condition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/NumberU3US.lean#L25-L29) · [Equality the flag forbids](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/TheoremsMuLinked/AutP.lean#L10-L13) · [Saito, Sections 2b–2c](https://www.ms.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~t-saito/pp/suams.pdf#page=8)

At every ramified prime, choose the finite scalar inertia character or one of the two Frobenius-invariant inertia characters just identified. At 5 use the scalar character of Weil–Deligne inertia. Each extends to a finite character of the local Weil group by assigning value 1 to a Frobenius lift. Local class field theory gives a character of the local unit group. The Chinese remainder theorem puts the finitely many inverse unit characters into one Dirichlet twist of $U$. It removes scalar inertia everywhere and leaves a single ramified tame quadratic character at each place of nontrivial projective inertia away from 5. A dual reciprocity convention selects the inverse Dirichlet twist and has the same effect. Local-global compatibility takes these conductors to the primitive classical level. At 571 the remaining inertia character is $\psi$ locally. The weight-two nebentypus must be even, while $\psi$ is odd. This forces the nontrivial tame quadratic character at 7 as well. The resulting newform has level precisely $D=7\cdot571=3997$ and primitive nebentypus $\chi_D=\eta\psi$, with $\eta=\chi_{-7}$.

For clarity about the residual condition at 5 after this twist, the representation of $U$ is crystalline there. Its restriction to $G_{K_5}$ is triangular. One way to see this directly is to take the first invariant line in the adjoint flag in $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. A nilpotent line gives an invariant kernel in dimension two. A semisimple line gives a pair of eigenlines preserved as a set. If they are exchanged, the other two root lines span an irreducible two-dimensional subquotient whenever their diagonal ratio has infinite image. The distinct cyclotomic weights force that infinite image and the complete adjoint flag excludes the exchange. The two characters of $U$ thus have ratio $\varepsilon_5$ or its inverse on inertia. Their determinant on inertia is $\varepsilon_5$. The weight-zero constituent is crystalline and has square trivial on inertia, hence is unramified. Indeed a finite inertial twist of an unramified weight-zero character is crystalline only if it is unramified. It follows that the two residual inertia characters are $1,\omega$.

## A finite sum rules out that level

Order the global residual characters with their quotient restricting to $z$ on $G_K$. That quotient globally is $\omega\psi$ or $\omega\psi\eta$. On complex conjugation the ratio must be $-1$ since a classical weight-two Galois representation is odd. Both $\omega$ and $\psi$ are odd, so it is $\omega\chi_D$. The normalized determinant just computed is also $\omega\chi_D$. Thus the two characters can be written
$$\alpha,\quad\alpha\chi_D\omega,\qquad \alpha^2=1,$$
where $\alpha$ is unramified at 5. The orientation here is fixed by the distinct inertia characters at 5. At each of $7,571$ exactly one of $\alpha,\alpha\chi_D$ ramifies, and its conductor exponent is one. Hence their Serre conductor is $D$, their weight is 2, and the ratio of the two characters after removing cyclotomic is $\chi_D$.

Billerey and Menares, Theorem 1, characterize when this odd reducible semisimple representation is modular in its Serre level and weight for $\ell>k+1$. Here $5>3$. One alternative requires $\chi_D(r)r^2=1\bmod5$ at a prime $r\mid D$. The character is ramified at both and its value by their convention is zero. The other requires $B_{2,\chi_D}=0\bmod5$. Since $\chi_D$ is nontrivial and even,
$$ B_{2,\chi_D}=\frac{1}{3997}\sum_{a=1}^{3997}
          \left(\frac{a}{7}\right)\left(\frac{a}{571}\right)a^2
        =22416\equiv1\bmod5.$$
The primitive character, tame level and Serre weight agree with those in their theorem. **Neither alternative occurs.** Consequently no classical weight-two rational adjoint can supply this point. The calculation concerns the minimal level forced by its inertia. Extra primes in a looser numerical level bound could change the answer to a modularity test. ## Self-duality would imply precisely such an adjoint

This step uses the automorphy required of the point. Let $\Pi$ be its regular algebraic cuspidal representation on $\mathrm{GL}_3/K$. In the release the attachment is for $\rho\varepsilon_5^{-1}$ and multiplies the unitary Satake parameters at a prime $w$ by $Nw$. On geometric Frobenius these two factors cancel, so $\rho$ itself reads the unitary Satake multiset. Its determinant one implies that $\Pi$ has trivial central character by the degree-one multiplicity-one theorem. The defining self-duality of the source is **conjugate** self-duality. [Required automorphic realization](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/AfStmt.lean#L27-L33) · [Content of the automorphy predicate](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/AutomorphyLifting/IsAutCsdA.lean#L9-L12) · [Existence, regularity and conjugate duality](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/AdmissibleResidues/IsAutomorphicOverImagQuad.lean#L32-L36) · [Normalization of the attachment](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/AutomorphicForms/BlochKatoSelmerVanishes.lean#L69-L85)

If $\rho$ were self-dual, equality of the good local polynomials and strong multiplicity one would give $\Pi^\vee=\Pi$. Conjugate self-duality would then give $\Pi^c=\Pi$. Quadratic cyclic base change descends this invariant cusp form to a cusp form on $\mathrm{GL}_3/\mathbb Q$. Its central character pulls back trivially, so it is $1$ or $\eta$. Twisting the descent by $\eta$ if necessary makes its central character trivial because the rank is odd. Its dual has the same base change and hence differs by $1$ or $\eta$ by the fiber theorem. The latter twist changes its determinant, so the normalized descent is self-dual. Its infinitesimal character is regular algebraic, as can be checked after restriction of the real Weil parameter to $\mathbb C^\times$, which is the archimedean base change. [Arthur–Clozel, cyclic base change](https://press.princeton.edu/books/ebook/9781400882403/simple-algebras-base-change-and-the-advanced-theory-of-the-trace-0) · [Clozel–Rajan, prime-degree case and description of fibers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.02513#page=3)

Ramakrishnan's Theorem A and Corollary B identify this self-dual rational cusp form of determinant one with the adjoint of a non-dihedral, regular algebraic cusp form on $\mathrm{GL}_2/\mathbb Q$. Its regular algebraic archimedean factor gives a classical holomorphic newform up to twist. At good primes the Galois adjoint of that form and $\rho$ have the same characteristic polynomials over $K$. Chebotarev and semisimplicity identify them. For a form of weight $k$ the adjoint Hodge–Tate weights are $1-k,0,k-1$ up to the harmless sign convention. The source flag has weights $-1,0,1$, so $k=2$. This gives exactly the adjoint excluded above. [Ramakrishnan, Theorem A and Corollary B](https://math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/Ramakrishnan.pdf#page=1)

Even an isomorphism $\rho^\vee\simeq\rho\otimes\xi$ forces ordinary self-duality here. Determinants give $\xi^3=1$. The residual traces of $\rho$ and its dual are equal and nonzero on every element, so $\bar\xi=1$. Reduction at 5 is injective on third roots of unity. In particular both self-dual and essentially self-dual rank-three points have been excluded.

## Observable consequence at split primes

For the algebraic closure of the image, absolute irreducibility makes its connected unipotent radical trivial. Its connected reductive component acts either irreducibly, by scalars, or with three distinct lines permuted transitively by the full group. This follows by decomposing into isotypic summands and using that three is prime. Scalar action would give finite projective image, contradicted by the distinct infinite cyclotomic ratios at 5. The three-line possibility was excluded by the trace on a three-cycle. In dimension three the irreducible connected semisimple possibilities inside $\mathrm{SL}_3$ are $\mathrm{SL}_3$ and the three-dimensional irreducible image of $\mathrm{SL}_2$. This is the low-dimensional classification of simple Lie algebras and their representations in characteristic zero. The latter image preserves a unique line of nondegenerate symmetric forms. Its normalizer preserves that line and makes the full representation essentially self-dual. The surviving algebraic group is therefore $\mathrm{SL}_3$.

Write the normalized Hecke polynomial as $X^3-a_wX^2+b_wX-1$. Residually $a_w=b_w$ at every good prime. On a genuine point $a_w$ and $b_w$ cannot agree at every good prime, since otherwise $\rho$ and its dual have equal characters and are isomorphic. Conjugate self-duality gives $a_{w^c}=b_w$. A good prime fixed by conjugation is blind to this difference. There must be **split** rational primes where $a_w-a_{w^c}$ is nonzero and reduces to zero modulo the chosen prime above 5. In fact the polynomial $g\mapsto\operatorname{tr}g-\operatorname{tr}g^{-1}$ is nonzero on $\mathrm{SL}_3$. A finite-index open subgroup has the same connected algebraic closure. Thus a Frobenius with unequal traces can be found in each of the four residual scalar classes, avoiding any fixed finite exclusion. One can use Chebotarev in a finite normal closure over $\mathbb Q$ for an open neighborhood inside $G_K$, which gives a split rational prime.

## Unresolved

This is a necessary condition for a characteristic-zero point in the released construction on the stated input. We have not identified such a point independently or established its nonexistence. Testing a self-adjoint Hecke sum, a rational adjoint, or a single inert comparison prime cannot answer it. The [first directed Hecke calculation](2026-11-24-directed-hecke-test-at-level-571.md) keeps both prime ideals distinct in a concrete definite-unitary comparison space. Tracking the same system across primes and matching the ramified local type remain open.
