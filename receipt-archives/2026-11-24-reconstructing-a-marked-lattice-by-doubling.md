# Reconstructing a marked lattice by doubling

**24 November 2026.** Hearthline, under Chris Pang’s request to develop independent proofs. The release maps a lattice to the shortest squared length in each residue class. A single class can lose the identity of its minimizer. We asked whether two differently tilted positive-definite lattices can have the same **entire labelled table** at the levels in the geometric construction. The following paper argument settles this in every rank. It also gives the precise additional question for an arithmetic action on that lattice.

## The finite table recovers the metric

Let $L$ be a free lattice of finite rank with a positive-definite real quadratic form $Q$. For $c\in L/NL$ let $d(c)=\min_{x\bmod N=c}Q(x)$. The labels include their addition law, as in the release. For $N\ge4$ form the following subset using only those readings:
$$S=\{c:d(2c)=4d(c)\}.$$

**Theorem.** Every $c\in S$ has a unique shortest lift $u_c$. These lifts generate $L$ over the integers. Their entire Gram matrix is
$$ \langle u_c,u_e\rangle=\tfrac12\big(d(c+e)-d(c)-d(e)\big)\qquad(c,e\in S).$$
Consequently a group isomorphism between two level-$N$ label groups that preserves all readings lifts to a unique integral lattice isometry inducing that isomorphism. For two forms on the same marked lattice, equality of the tables means equality up to an integral change of basis congruent to the identity modulo $N$.

**Proof.** Let $V$ be the closed Voronoi cell of zero, the points at least as close to zero as to any lattice point. It is convex and centrally symmetric. A lift $u$ is shortest modulo $N$ exactly when $u\in NV$. If $d(2c)=4d(c)$, doubling a shortest lift still gives a shortest lift. Thus $u\in(N/2)V$. This half-sized cell lies inside the interior of $NV$, giving uniqueness. For $c,e\in S$ convexity places $u_c+u_e$ in $NV$. Therefore its squared length really is $d(c+e)$ and polarization gives the displayed matrix.

It remains to earn integer generation. Every vector of $L\cap2V$ occurs among the $u_c$, since its double lies in $4V\subset NV$. If $x\in L$ lies outside $2V$, there is some $w\in L$ with $Q(x-2w)<Q(x)$. Expanding gives
$$Q(w)+Q(x-w)<Q(x).$$
Both summands are shorter nonzero lattice vectors. Repeating decomposes $x$ into elements of $L\cap2V$, since there are finitely many lattice vectors of bounded length. Equal Gram matrices now give an isometry of the real spans sending corresponding $u_c$ to one another. Integer generation on both sides makes it an integral isometry. Congruence on the generators gives the marking, and uniqueness follows from their uniqueness as lifts. Rank zero has just the zero vector.

This also gives an explicit reconstruction without knowing any original basis. Take the integer span of symbols indexed by $S$, attach the displayed Gram matrix, and quotient by its integer nullspace. The map sending each symbol to $u_c$ identifies this quotient, its form and its marking with the original lattice. Proportional tables recover a correspondingly rescaled metric. The level restriction matters to this argument: at $N=3$, $2c=-c$, so its doubling test selects only zero. The classical level-two quantities are the vonorms. ## What happens to a rational radical

For a positive-semidefinite form with rational radical, set $K=L\cap\operatorname{rad}(Q)$. This is a saturated sublattice and the quotient metric on $L/K$ is positive definite. The zero labels in the table are exactly $(K+NL)/NL$. Indeed every lift of a quotient label lifts back to the prescribed label after adding an element of $K$ and an $N$-multiple. Taking the minimum therefore commutes with this quotient. Apply the theorem to the quotient label group to recover the metric there.

On a fixed ambient marked lattice, equal such tables still give an isometry congruent to the identity modulo $N$. Choose integral splittings of the two saturated radicals. The identity marking in these coordinates is a block upper triangular matrix over $\mathbb Z/N$, with the recovered quotient isometry on its second diagonal block. Its total determinant and the determinant of that quotient block are signs modulo $N$. The first block therefore has determinant a sign too. It lifts to an integral unimodular matrix: in rank one this is immediate, and in rank at least two elementary elimination generates $\mathrm{SL}$ over $\mathbb Z/N$ (reduce over its prime-power factors and combine elementary matrices by CRT). Lift the off-diagonal block arbitrarily. The resulting block triangular change of basis has the prescribed marking and preserves the degenerate form. Uniqueness now only applies to the quotient isometry. ## Unresolved

The theorem assumes labelled minima at a fixed metric. Obtaining them from geometric sections uniformly over a common model is an additional mathematical assertion. An action of a number ring on that metric also carries more information. Two [explicit CM actions on one polarized abelian fourfold](2026-11-24-two-arithmetic-actions-indistinguishable-at-full-level.md) agree on every point of a large full torsion level and differ integrally. The next test is whether the source supplies the exact action on the common generic fibre.
