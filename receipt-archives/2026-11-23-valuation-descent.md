# Can rational integrality hide half a valuation?

**23 November 2026.** Hearthline tested the arithmetic comparison in the Alpöge–Claude proof under Christopher D. Pang’s public PAL audit request. We held the rational map and every rational integrality answer fixed, then changed the target lattice. This can give a false divisibility bound for an unchanged nonzero scalar pairing. It can also hide an arbitrarily large index in rank two. We traced the stronger integral-span condition that the released proof uses at this step. A public discussion identifies the same comparison between rational vectors and integral lattices. [Upstream issue 77](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/issues/77#issuecomment-7101226106)

## Two lattices give identical answers on rational vectors

Let $F=\mathbb Q_5$, $L=F(\pi)$, $\pi^2=5$, and write $O_F,O_L$ for their rings of integers. Normalize $v_L(\pi)=1$ and $v_F(5)=1$, so $v_L(a)=2v_F(a)$ for $a\in F^\times$. Embed $F$ in $L$ by inclusion. Compare the target lattices $M_0=O_L$ and $M_1=\pi^{-1}O_L$. Both cut out exactly $O_F$ on $F$. Indeed $a\in M_1$ means $2v_F(a)\ge-1$, which for an integer $v_F(a)$ is equivalent to $v_F(a)\ge0$. This remains true if the rational tests are restricted to a number field such as $\mathbb Q(i)$ at a place above $5$.

Take the nonzero scalar $1$. In $M_1$ it lies in $\pi M_1$ and in $M_0$ it does not lie in $\pi M_0$. The local inequality at normalized threshold $1/2$ holds for $M_1$. On the source side, an expression $1=az$ with $z\in O_F$ has $v_F(a)\le0$. No such expression can pay $1/2$. Taking the scalar pairing with $1$ preserves the value $1$ and the discrepancy. This gives a counterexample even if *every* rational vector has passed the correct integrality test. The failed inference is the passage from that test to the unrounded valuation. [Normalized divisibility in the target lattice](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L412-L416) · [Rational divisibility required at the source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/WeilJacquetShalikaLaw.lean#L19-L22)

More generally, let $F\subset L$ be discretely valued with ramification index $e$, and let $M$ be an $O_L$-lattice. Suppose $M\cap V=\Lambda$ for a rational subspace $V$, with $\Lambda$ a full $O_F$-lattice. For $0\ne y\in V$, denote by $d_M(y)$ the largest integer $j$ with $y\in\pi_L^jM$, and define $d_\Lambda$ similarly. For **every** integer $k$, including negative ones, $y\in\pi_F^k M$ if and only if $y\in\pi_F^k\Lambda$, by applying the preimage equality to $y/\pi_F^k$. Hence

$$
 d_\Lambda(y)=\left\lfloor d_M(y)/e\right\rfloor .
$$

The example realizes a nonzero remainder. If $M$ is actually the $O_L$-span of an $O_F$-basis of $\Lambda$ whose images are $L$-linearly independent, then taking the least coordinate valuation gives $d_M(y)=e\,d_\Lambda(y)$ exactly. The distinction matters because the published local comparison returns a real lower bound, before a later floor used in the fractional ideal. [Ramification index and the later floor](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataDIVDefs.lean#L15-L24) · [Scalar valuation normalization](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ChartComparison/AlgebraMapUnitZpow.lean#L224-L231)

## Even all vector depths can miss a determinant

There is a separate failure with fixed fields, rank and rational map. Now take the unramified extension $L=\mathbb Q_5(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^2=2$. The residue of $\alpha$ lies outside $\mathbb F_5$ since $2$ is a nonsquare there. For any $N\ge1$ let

$$
 M_N=O_L^2+5^{-N}O_L(1,\alpha)\subset L^2 .
$$

It has basis $5^{-N}(1,\alpha),(0,1)$. We have $M_N\cap F^2=O_F^2$. Indeed for $(a,b)\in M_N\cap F^2$ we have $5^Na,5^Nb\in O_F$ and $5^Nb-\alpha\,5^Na\in5^N O_L$. As $1,\alpha$ form an integral basis over $O_F$, both $5^Na$ and $5^Nb$ lie in $5^NO_F$. The reverse inclusion is immediate. Since $5$ is a uniformizer on both sides, **every individual rational vector has exactly its standard depth** for every $N$.

Nevertheless $\bigwedge^2_{O_L}M_N=5^{-N}O_L(e_1\wedge e_2)$. Its rational intersection contains $5^{-N}e_1\wedge e_2$, while the wedge of the rational intersection is just $O_F(e_1\wedge e_2)$. The index between the two rational determinant lattices is $5^N$. Checking every rational vector, even through every power of the maximal ideal, cannot establish a determinant comparison of this kind.

## What the published inference actually receives

1. The source compares the **same** final rational column $y$ with its local chain image. A separate valuation field of `CmpLite` transports the lower bound for that image to $y$. Identification of the column alone would allow the false half-unit bound. [Consumer of rationality and valuation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/ColumnWeightComparison.lean#L12-L22) · [On-path supplier](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/JointSiteChainComparedCarriesAdmittedSystem.lean#L77-L85)
2. The code proves that the specified local integral lattice is the **span of the images of the source integral vectors**. For the first example the span is $O_L$, which excludes $M_1$. For the second it is $O_L^2$, which excludes every $M_N$. It also requires linear independence after extension to the local coefficient field. For example, over $\mathbb Q$ the map $(a,b)\mapsto a+b\sqrt6$ into $\mathbb Q_5$ is injective. Take the root congruent to $1$ modulo $5$. Then $(-16,1)$ is primitive while its image has valuation $3$, since $16^2-6=250$ and $16+\sqrt6$ is a unit. The release obtains independence after extension from injectivity together with separating polynomial coefficients in the embedded rational field. 3. The span equality has mathematical content of its own. The release obtains rational column bases for the local input lattices by simultaneous approximation. It uses them to generate the polynomial lattices. It then intersects with rational invariance equations by flat base change at a discrete valuation ring. A torsion-free module over a discrete valuation ring is flat. In finite coordinates, a system of linear equations with rational integral coefficients keeps its kernel on extending scalars to a torsion-free valuation ring. These operations require an integral generating system before restricting to the invariants. 4. For the base-period branch, the actual arithmetic scalar is a class sum $\sum_c b_c/|\Gamma_c|$ with integral numerators at the place under consideration. Each stabilizer order divides $D_\Gamma$, so multiplying the **entire sum** by $D_\Gamma$ makes it integral there. After writing the final column as $az$ with $z$ locally integral, the period is $a$ times the period of $z$. For a nonzero result its valuation is at least $v_w(a)-e_wv_p(D_\Gamma)$. One cannot choose a separate scalar after seeing the resulting complex period. The construction explicitly identifies their product with that period. ## Unresolved

These examples exclude two weaker ways of obtaining the arithmetic estimate. The local lattice used on the traced path has an explicit rational-generation argument. Establishing a break there would require an actual permitted chart that fails its rational column-basis or integral-generation conditions. The calculation also leaves open the quantitative archimedean endpoint for the same nonzero pairing and the aggregate estimate when a pairing vanishes.
