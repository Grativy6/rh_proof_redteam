# The theta height at a thin lattice and in the actual average

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Chris Pang’s public audit request.** A genuine residual term survives at a single thin CM lattice. After subtracting the leading multiple of the usual eta height, it grows as the logarithm of the shape parameter. The mean across embeddings used for the standard vacuum pays for this term. We derive both statements and identify the average used in the proof. ## A single embedding can be expensive

Write a reduced period basis as $\Lambda'=\omega_1(\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z)$ with $y=\Im\tau\ge\sqrt3/2$. The normalized half section is determined by its period signs and its value one at zero. In $w=z/\omega_1$ it is
$$
 e^{\pi w^2/(2y)}
 \frac{\vartheta_{\alpha,\beta}(w,\tau)}{\vartheta_{\alpha,\beta}(0,\tau)},\qquad
 \vartheta_{\alpha,\beta}(w,\tau)=
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{\pi i(k+\alpha)^2\tau+2\pi i(k+\alpha)(w+\beta)} .
$$
Indeed its signs on $1,\tau$ follow by shifting $k$, and the elliptic ratio of two such sections is constant as in the [fourfold calculation](2026-11-23-fourfold-theta-lattice.md). To find the characteristic for each actual CM class, take its reduced form $[a,b,c]$ with $b^2-4ac=-d$. The ideal basis $(a,(-b+\sqrt{-d})/2)$ has norm form divided by its ideal norm equal to $a u^2-buv+c v^2$. Its period signs are $(-1)^a,(-1)^c$. This works also for an ideal representative of even norm, because $Q$ is integral and its polar form is the alternating pairing modulo two. More explicitly, $b$ is odd and $ac$ even. The two idempotents on the ideal modulo $2$ each have rank one (the ideal is locally free). The normalized norm on either axis is even, as the split local norm is the product of the two components times a $2$-adic unit in a local ideal basis. Its polar form is nondegenerate. Thus the same unkilled class gives the norm sign on every representative. Scaling the whole ideal leaves $Q$ and the signs invariant. Hence $2\alpha=a\bmod 2$, $2\beta=c\bmod 2$. The characteristic is always one of the three even ones. [Half section and doubled-point test](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GL3Theta.lean#L198-L204) · [Theta series and periodicity](https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.2)

In $w=x+\tau v$, with $0\le x,v<1$, the Gaussian density becomes $|\vartheta_{\alpha,\beta}(w,\tau)|^2 e^{-2\pi y v^2}/|\vartheta_{\alpha,\beta}(0,\tau)|^2$. Integrating in $x$ removes unequal Fourier indices. The diagonal terms in $v$ tile the real line and integrate to $\int_\mathbb R e^{-2\pi y t^2}dt=(2y)^{-1/2}$. The release uses a normalized mean, hence
$$
 N_\theta(\tau)=\|\mathrm{th0}\|^2
   =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2y}\,|\vartheta_{\alpha,\beta}(0,\tau)|^2}.
$$
This independently recovers the single-place normalization. On the principal class $\tau=(\pm1+i\sqrt d)/2$ and $t_0=1/2$ in period coordinates. The characteristic gives $\vartheta_2$, with absolute value $2e^{-\pi y/4}(1+O(e^{-2\pi y}))$ at zero. Set $B(\tau)=-\log(\sqrt y\,|\eta(\tau)|^2)$. From the eta product $B=\pi y/6-\frac12\log y+O(e^{-2\pi y})$. It follows that
$$
 \log N_\theta=\frac{\pi y}{2}-\tfrac12\log(2y)-\log4+O(e^{-2\pi y}),\qquad
 \log N_\theta-3B=\log y-\log(4\sqrt2)+O(e^{-2\pi y}).
$$
In particular an additive absolute constant cannot complete the pointwise estimate $\log N_\theta\le3B+C$ along these discriminants. Primes $d\equiv7\bmod16$ occur without bound by Dirichlet's theorem. This tests the elliptic inputs. It does not assert that a given small discriminant instantiates all the independent rank and host parameters of the RH proof. The release has a principal-ideal choice in its construction. ## Does the release discard that size?

For any reduced representative the two theta constants with $\alpha=0$ stay bounded away from zero, by the series bound $2\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-\pi y n^2}<1$. For $\alpha=1/2$ the even characteristic has $\beta=0$. Factoring out its two equal leading terms leaves a remainder of relative magnitude at most $\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-\pi y k(k+1)}<1$. These bounds are uniform for $y\ge\sqrt3/2$. Hence $\log^+N_\theta\le C_0(1+y)$ with an absolute $C_0$. The eta product has uniformly bounded remainder on the same domain. Since $B=\pi y/6-\tfrac12\log y+O(1)$, there are absolute constants $C_1,C_2>0$ with $1+y\le C_1+C_2B$. One can choose them on the compact part and use the linear leading term for large $y$. Also $\Delta(\Lambda')=1$ implies $|\omega_1|=2\pi|\eta(\tau)|^2$ by the modular discriminant formula. Thus $\log(1/\operatorname{area}\Lambda')=2B-\log(4\pi^2)$. Both positive log readouts are controlled by the same average of $B$. [Positive logarithms in the release](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/EmbLLMem.lean#L29-L38) · [Modular discriminant and eta](https://dlmf.nist.gov/23.15)

The mean of $B$ has a classical evaluation. Let $h$ be the class number, $w_K$ the number of units and $\chi$ the quadratic character of $K$. For $s>1$ set $Z(\tau,s)=\sum' y^s/|m+n\tau|^{2s}$. A nonzero element of an ideal $\mathfrak a$ gives the integral ideal $(x)\mathfrak a^{-1}$ in its inverse class, with exactly $w_K$ choices per ideal. Since $\operatorname{area}\mathfrak a=N\mathfrak a\,\sqrt d/2$, summing over one representative per class gives
$$
 \sum_{[\mathfrak a]} Z(\tau_{\mathfrak a},s)
     = w_K(\sqrt d/2)^s\,\zeta(s)L(s,\chi).
$$
At $s=1$ the first Kronecker limit formula gives $\pi/(s-1)+2\pi(\gamma-\log2+B(\tau))+O(s-1)$ for each summand. Comparing first the residues and then the constant terms gives
$$
 \frac1h\sum_{[\mathfrak a]} B(\tau_{\mathfrak a})
     =\tfrac14\log d+\tfrac12\,\frac{L'(1,\chi)}{L(1,\chi)}
         +\tfrac12(\log2-\gamma).
$$
This uses a classical theorem proved independently of the audited release. Proposition 1 in the linked paper uses $Q(m,n)$ and $4ac-b^2$ and gives the same normalization on setting $Q=|m+n\tau|^2/y$. At prime fundamental discriminant $-d$ the character agrees with the quadratic character modulo $d$ by quadratic reciprocity. [Independent proof of the Kronecker formula](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11139-022-00586-0) · [Actual logarithmic derivative](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L12-L20)

Why is the arithmetic average the right one? Each ideal lattice has endomorphism ring $\mathcal O_K$ because the ideal is invertible. The first main theorem of complex multiplication identifies the conjugates of its $j$ with the $h$ distinct class values. The Hilbert class polynomial is irreducible over $K$ and hence over $\mathbb Q$. The fields used here contain $g_2,g_3$, hence $j$. Every embedding of $\mathbb Q(j)$ has exactly the same number of extensions to any fixed finite such field. Since $B$ depends only on lattice shape, its arithmetic mean is exactly the displayed class mean. The inverse-area and theta bounds also apply to every allowed lift of the half point. For the version averaging first over extensions of a chosen embedding at each infinite place, each chosen embedding has the same number of extensions and each place accounts for at most two embeddings. Nonnegativity gives at most twice the unrestricted embedding mean for the source's place normalization. As $|\operatorname{disc} E|=d^{[E:K]}N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak D_{E/K}$, we have $\operatorname{rd}_E\ge\sqrt d$. The source sets $v=\operatorname{rd}_E/2$. Here $d\ge7$, so $1+\log d\ll\log v$ with an absolute constant. This proves the standard-vacuum bound for the actual scale $1+\log d+|L'/L(1,\chi)|$, which is covered by the chain's allowance. For ranks $a,b$ the common multiplier $abf$ was derived for each embedding in the [transport calculation](2026-11-23-theta-transport-at-a-place.md). At the coupled last two steps the weighted argument uses the **sum of the two bounds at the same embedding**. Given nonnegative weights adding to one, there is a supported embedding whose sum is at most that weighted mean of the sums. Every supported embedding lies over the specified place. Thus this particular averaging step preserves the joint bound, provided the arithmetic entries already obey their two-factor estimate there. The source writes that joint premise explicitly.
