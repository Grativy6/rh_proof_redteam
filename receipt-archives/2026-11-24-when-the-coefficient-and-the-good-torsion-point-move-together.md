# When the coefficient and the good torsion point move together

**24 November 2026.** In the [nonsplit threefold](2026-11-24-the-order-nine-carry-in-a-nonsplit-degeneration.md) the two lattice periods move a point on the good elliptic curve by two independent torsion points. Passing to the released eighth-power polarization gives a direct test of which order of choices is possible. An individual coefficient can attain its required size, yet a single torsion probe can miss a whole class even when the class has a unique shortest lift. This happens at arbitrarily large allowed base levels. The release lets the probe depend on the coefficient.

## A moving zero on precisely the same theta section

Use the elliptic curve of $j$-invariant zero over a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_5$, the symplectic basis $P,Q$ of its $k$-torsion, and the period data constructed in the companion receipt. For the eighth power write $B_8=W_8+W_8^t$, and index periods by $n=(x,y)\in(\mathbb Z^8)^2$. The torus character is $(B_8x,B_8y)$ and the period on the good part is
$$\Phi(n)=(x_i P+y_i Q)_{i=1}^8\in B[k]^8.$$
Every good $k$-torsion point is an image. In a normalized integral frame the ordinary Fourier coefficient indexed by this period is a scalar of the prescribed quadratic order times the translated good theta section $t_{\Phi(n)}^*\Theta_{B_8}$. This is the period recurrence at index zero: for a principal polarization the period-to-character map is unimodular, so translation from zero supplies every coefficient. The remaining fibre identifications are isomorphisms of lines. The good line is the Poincaré construction on $B^8$. The quadratic order for equal diagonal period parameters $q^2$ is $v(q)(|x|_{B_8}^2+|y|_{B_8}^2)$; each Poincaré multiplier is bilinear and the off-diagonal gluing units have order zero. The [ordinary good theta section](2026-11-24-a-theta-zero-at-every-toric-depth.md) vanishes at the origin. On every root elliptic line its characteristic-zero divisor is exactly twice the origin. Thus changing the index can move a literal zero, even when all line trivializations are units. At a good point $h$, coefficient $n$ vanishes whenever $\Phi(n)=-h$. Multiplying the lift by a toric unit does not change this. For $m$ divisible by $k$, the same translation holds at **every** $n'\equiv n\bmod m$. Consequently the true class sum vanishes at this good point and remains zero under every toric probe and subdivision of that class. For example, along a root $r$ the choices of good point $0,-rP$ and coefficient index $0,(r,0)$ give zero in two opposite positions and nonzero in the other two in characteristic zero.

This failure occurs in classes with a unique shortest lift. For each $h\in B[k]^8$ choose $x_i,y_i$ giving $-h_i$ with integer entries of absolute value at most $(k-1)/2$ for odd $k$. The seven edges and eight diagonal entries of the pinned $E_8$ matrix give $|x|^2+|y|^2\le15(k-1)^2$. The shortest nonzero length of the doubled lattice is $\sqrt2$. If $m\ge9k$, any distinct congruent pair is at least $m\sqrt2$ apart, greater than twice the length of the chosen vector. It is the unique minimum in its class. Thus for every possible **single** base probe above a good $k$-torsion point, at least one such uniquely minimized class is invisible there.

One may take $k$ to be the actual entire base torsion order. For example in theta dimension 24 use $N=3^{12}, r=3^{400}, k=rN=3^{412}$ and $m=9k$. At depth take $N'=m^2$, $r'=3^{20100}$. Both setups satisfy the three numerical size bounds. Finite extensions suffice to realize the torsion and the period data. The construction proves the failure for one fixed local family at these levels. It does not specify the global generic moduli witness of the released argument. ## The probe available for each coefficient

Fix an index. Reduction of the translated primitive good coefficient is a nonzero section of a translate of a principal line. For the elliptic eighth power, the line degree on each coordinate elliptic curve is two. The same slicing proof as in the previous receipt gives at least $(k^2-2)^8$ nonzero good readings on the full $k$-torsion grid, including after translation by $\Phi(n)$. On the good quotient every such point lifts to $G[k]$: lift any point, then correct its $k$th multiple inside the divisible torus. This argument works in the nonsplit extension. On a trait in an integral frame the nonzero reduced value is a unit, giving exactly the prescribed scalar valuation. If $m$-division is then required, a further lift is killed by $km$ and maps to the same chosen $k$-torsion point. At a unique toric minimum, larger terms cannot cancel this reading.

There is a proof of individual attainment even at a small prime-to-characteristic order $n\ge2$ for a general good principally polarized abelian part $B$. For a principal line $L$, the pullback of its theta section by $[n]$ is an eigensection for the deck group $B[n]$. The finite étale pushforward $[n]_*\mathcal O$ splits as the sum of the character lines in $\widehat B[n]$, so the sections of $[n]^*L$ decompose as the one-dimensional spaces $H^0(B,L\otimes P_\chi)$. For each character line, the summand has dimension one because it is a translate of $L$. The translates of the pullback section by $B[n^2]$ reach every summand: if $[n]t=x\in B[n]$, the line downstairs is $t_x^*L$, and principal polarization identifies these $x$ with all the character lines. Rigidifying the trivial pullback of $t_x^*L/L$ changes only its scalar. Thus the translated sections span.

The bundle $[n]^*L$ is globally generated. For a symmetric line it is $L^{n^2}$, and any translate gives the same conclusion. A power $L^d$ with $d\ge2$ has a section nonzero at any prescribed point: choose $d$ translates of theta avoiding it whose translation parameters sum to zero, and multiply their sections using the theorem of the square. These choices exist over the algebraically closed field because only finitely many proper closed conditions on the $d-1$ free parameters are excluded. If all points of some coset of $B[n]$ lay on theta, then all the spanning translates of the pullback section would vanish at an $[n]$-preimage of that coset representative. This contradicts global generation. The argument uses the prime-to-characteristic hypothesis for the character decomposition. [Classical torsion and theta-group argument](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.09296#page=1)

In the release the primitive norm is imposed on a family satisfying the period recurrence. At ordinary power one all coefficient indices belong to the same period orbit. Translation carries a surplus in coefficient valuation at any one index to a uniform surplus. Hence global primitivity rules out that surplus. It does **not** force an arbitrary evaluation of the resulting coefficient section to be nonzero. The source separately chooses good torsion after fixing both the starting point and character, lifts it in the torus extension and evaluates in a unit fibre frame. At the approximation step it chooses one evaluation label per product factor. A single simultaneous probe for all lattice characters is absent from these requirements. ## A uniformly bounded set of good probes

For a principally polarized good abelian variety of dimension $h\ge1$, an ample theta divisor $\Theta$, and $b$ invertible with
$$b^2>3^{h-1}(h!)^2,$$
there are $h+1$ points of $B[b]$ whose translates of $\Theta$ have empty common intersection. Therefore this fixed set of probes detects every translated ordinary coefficient section at every good point. In the eight-dimensional good part above it gives nine choices once the displayed bound holds. This is stronger than selecting a new probe by trial for each index.

Indeed, the [intersection bound](2026-11-24-a-theta-zero-at-every-toric-depth.md#recovering-a-unit-reading-on-the-good-factor) gives at most $3^{h-1}h!b^{2h-2}$ prohibited torsion translations at any one point. Suppose $j$ translated divisors already meet properly. Their intersection has at most $h!$ irreducible components, since its intersection degree against $L^{h-j}$, with positive multiplicities, is $L^h=h!$. Choose one geometric point on each component. The union of prohibited subsets of $B[b]$ is smaller than $b^{2h}$ under our bound. The next divisor can miss all the chosen points, so it contains no component. Induction gives a zero-dimensional intersection after $h$ steps and an empty one after the next. No $h$ translates can have empty common intersection on the whole abelian variety, since their intersection product has positive degree. Thus $h+1$ is optimal for covering **all geometric translations**. Even on a finite grid this lower bound can occur: for a product of $h$ elliptic curves with its product theta divisor, at most $h$ proposed probes can all be killed by choosing a different vanishing coordinate for each. This last example is a sharpness control for the general bound.

## Unresolved

This settles the torsion-dependent shift of zeros for a genuine nonsplit torsion extension. The exhibits also distinguish ordinary torsion from the kernel of its restricted polarization. The source’s individual-probe quantifier survives these tests. Whether an actual global degeneration loses the required correspondence of primitive line, valuation and marked good point at a later handoff remains a separate question.
