## One complete golden cycle

Let $\alpha=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ and let $u=(\alpha,1)$ and $v=(-1,\alpha)$ be normalized to Euclidean unit length. Put $B_\epsilon=uu^t+\epsilon vv^t$. At $g=2$ the release’s initial Voronoi function is $V(B)=\sum_{c\bmod2}\min_{x\equiv c}x^tBx$ on positive forms. The zero class contributes zero. For any modulus on an irrational rank-one boundary the corresponding infimum can exist without a lattice point attaining it. For $\alpha^{-4}\le\epsilon\le1$ the entries of $B_\epsilon$ have nonnegative cross term at most both diagonal entries. The three minimizing representatives can therefore be taken to be $e_1,e_2,e_1-e_2$. Their bounds follow from the three nonnegative squares in the [binary cone calculation](2026-11-24-an-explicit-support-for-the-zero-orbit.md#a-full-rank-two-instance). Since $\alpha/(\alpha^2+1)=1/\sqrt5$, adding their squared lengths gives
$$V(B_\epsilon)=2\big((1-1/\sqrt5)+(1+1/\sqrt5)\epsilon\big).$$
The unimodular Fibonacci matrix $T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has eigenvalues $\alpha$ and $-\alpha^{-1}$ in these two directions, so $T^t B_\epsilon T=\alpha^2 B_{\epsilon\alpha^{-4}}$. Thus $V(B_\epsilon)/\sqrt\epsilon$ repeats whenever $\epsilon$ is divided by $\alpha^4$. On each cycle its exact range is
$$[8/\sqrt5,\ 4].$$
The minimum occurs at $\epsilon=\alpha^{-2}$ in the displayed cycle and the maximum at both endpoints. The leading order is a square root. Its coefficient keeps oscillating, even though the limiting rank-one matrix is fixed. The same scaling identity holds for every admissible invariant homogeneous $\phi$. The explicit extrema were calculated for the stated seed $V$.

At the limiting matrix $uu^t$ every nonzero parity class has infimum zero and no minimizing vector. Indeed the values of $\alpha x+y$ in each such class are dense, and $\alpha x+y=0$ at an integral point only for $x=y=0$. The source defines positivity against **real** coefficients on independent lattice vectors and defines its semidefinite cone by pullback from a positive-definite form on an integral quotient. An irrational real kernel containing no nonzero lattice vector is excluded by these definitions. ## All possible limits at a rational rank-one ray

Let $\phi$ be **any** rank-two release-admissible polarizing function and $R_0=\mathrm{diag}(0,1)$. Then the cluster set of $\phi(B)$ for positive-definite $B\to R_0$ in the ordinary matrix topology is exactly
$$[0,\phi(R_0)].$$
For the upper bound take a cone containing $R_0$ and its strict integral supporting functional, divided by its scale. It equals $\phi$ at $R_0$ and dominates $\phi$ on the domain. The extension of the integral inequality to real forms follows by expressing each form as a nonnegative sum of integral cone rays where $\phi$ is linear. Nonnegativity follows in the same way. A fixed linear upper bound agreeing at a primitive ray also gives continuity toward it *inside the diagonal cone*: superadditivity bounds the diagonal reading below by the sum of the two scaled ray readings and the upper bound differs from that estimate by at most a constant times the shrinking entry.

For $0<t<1$ write $U_n=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The forms
$$U_n^t\,\mathrm{diag}(t/n^2,1-t)\,U_n
 =\begin{pmatrix}t/n^2&t/n\\t/n&1\end{pmatrix}$$
all tend to $R_0$. By integral invariance their readings tend to $(1-t)\phi(R_0)$. Diagonal forms give the upper endpoint. The forms $U_n^t U_n/n^2$ give the lower endpoint. The $n$ can all be multiples of any prescribed level, preserving that torsion marking. One can take rational $t_n\to t$, clear denominators, and realize the diagonal positive integral periods by two Tate curves. Choosing the ramification index to clear the normalized valuations recovers these rational normalized metrics. These examples make no assumption that all the arcs have the same centre or the same integral coordinates in one degeneration model.

There is a complementary distinction at every irrational rank-one ray $ww^t$. Positive-definite approaches all have $\phi(B)\to0$. Choose successive continued-fraction approximants giving an integral basis on both of whose vectors the linear form $w^t$ is arbitrarily small. A fixed supporting functional at the identity bounds $\phi$ above. Apply it after changing to that basis, then take the limit in the four entries. The resulting upper bound can be made arbitrarily small, while the readings are nonnegative. The result concerns the limit from the domain. The axioms for $\phi$ on this cone do not assign a boundary value at an excluded point.

## Why this does not defeat the finite-chart assertion

For a finite set of positive-semidefinite generators $P_i$, every $B$ in their positive cone satisfies
$$|B_{12}|\le C B_{11},\qquad C=\max_{i:(P_i)_{11}>0}|(P_i)_{12}|/(P_i)_{11}.$$
A zero first diagonal entry forces a zero cross term. If all first diagonals vanish the conclusion holds with $C=0$. The sheared sequence above has $|B_{12}|/B_{11}=n$. It leaves every such fixed cone and every finite union of them. For a fixed labelling modulo $N$ the class of $e_2$ also shows the frame cost directly. At $U_n^tU_n$ with $N\mid n$, $N\ge3$, its unique shortest lift is $(-n,1)$. It cannot belong to one fixed list of minimizing lifts while $n$ increases unboundedly.

The source gives a finite cover on the cone spanned by a **fixed finite list** of integral positive forms, or on the forms sharing a whole fixed minimizing system. There is a direct arithmetic proof of the former once the integral strict supports have a common denominator. Evaluate each supporting functional on the finite list of generators. These nonnegative integer tuples have finitely many coordinatewise minimal elements by Dickson’s lemma. At a point of the positive cone a minimizing functional can be replaced by one of those minimal tuples. Its value can only decrease, and the common lower bound forces equality. Strictness then locates the selected cone. ## A frame supplied by a normal local ring

Here is a local algebra criterion for the same phenomenon. Let $A$ be a normal noetherian local domain and $X$ a free lattice. Suppose $p(x)$ are nonzero elements of $A$ whose principal divisors $D(x)$ depend quadratically on $x$ in the divisor group. Define this to mean a homogeneous quadratic map with biadditive polarization. Let $A\to V$ be a map to a discrete valuation ring keeping the relevant periods nonzero. Then all the forms $x\mapsto v(p(x))$ from such maps lie in **one fixed finitely generated rational cone of positive-semidefinite forms**.

To prove it, choose a basis and take the finitely many periods at basis vectors and sums of distinct pairs. Their divisors involve finitely many height-one primes $P_1,\ldots,P_s$. Quadratic expansion shows that all $D(x)$ are supported there. Each $q_i(x)=\operatorname{ord}_{P_i}(p(x))$ is integral, quadratic and nonnegative. On the subgroup generated by the chosen principal divisors, the trait orders give an additive functional: two Laurent products with the same divisor differ by a unit. A vector of nonnegative divisor coefficients corresponds to a regular Laurent product by normality. Its value therefore has nonnegative valuation. This last assertion also holds with a kernel in the map to $V$, since the displayed product is expressed using periods with nonzero images and can be evaluated after clearing their product. The cone cut out by nonnegativity in this rational span is rational polyhedral. By finite-dimensional linear duality (Farkas’ lemma), the nonnegative functional is the restriction of a nonnegative linear combination of the coordinate functionals. Hence $v(p(x))=\sum_i t_i q_i(x)$ with $t_i\ge0$. No height-one prime had to be principal.

In the released degeneration data a period coordinate is defined up to local units. Evenness and bimultiplicativity, through the integral rigidification and cubical isomorphisms, give exactly the even quadratic divisor law after taking height-one orders. Concretely, the divisor of $\tau(x,y)$ is $D(x+y)-D(x)-D(y)$, the cubical generator changes coordinates by a unit and gives additivity in either argument, and the symmetry generator gives $D(-x)=D(x)$. Bimultiplicativity gives $D(0)=0$ using the rigidification. Thus $2D(x)$ is the diagonal of its bilinear polarization. All these statements can equally be read on the doubled divisor lattice to avoid halves. The release extracts its periods from one fixed first-coordinate sublattice. The fixed-frame criterion applies to those periods provided one carries the supplied integral degeneration structure into the completed normal local ring. A single statement merely listing pointwise values of $p$ would be weaker. ## Unresolved

The actual passage from generic to special traits copies the valuations of a finite list of **nonzero** periods and a divisor generator up to a common multiplier. Polarizing the values on basis sums yields exactly proportional complete forms. Homogeneity and integral isomorphism invariance then give the same scaled reading. There is no ambient continuity requirement at this step. The finite-ratio device can also be seen algebraically: for a list with positive orders $k_i$, fix one $a_j$ of order $k_j>0$ and adjoin the unit ratios $a_i^{k_j}/a_j^{k_i}$ and their inverses to the local ring inside its fraction field, then localize at the centre of the test trait. A dominating injective discrete valuation on that ring preserves all the displayed ratios exactly. Completion preserves their orders. If every $k_i$ is zero, the elements are already units for a local test. The remaining geometric question is the common integral period structure at the particular global centre, especially for a nontrivial abelian part.
