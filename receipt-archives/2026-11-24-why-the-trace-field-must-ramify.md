# Why the surviving trace field must ramify at 577

**24 November 2026.** Let $\rho$ be an absolutely irreducible rank-three representation of $G_K$ obtained from a characteristic-zero marked ordinary point for the [actual conductor-256 input](2026-11-24-a-class-inside-the-zeta-windows.md). Normalize $v_{577}(577)=1$. There is an element $g\in G_K$ with
$$0<v_{577}\big(\operatorname{tr}\rho(g)-\operatorname{tr}\rho(g^{-1})\big)\le\tfrac12.$$
In particular the local field generated by the traces must ramify over $\mathbb Q_{577}$. This excludes all unramified trace-field candidates, even those whose residual traces agree at every prime. A finite family of short trace words detects the bound. The sharper test below gives two particular words built from inertia at 2 and Frobenius at 12407809. This is a necessary test on any surviving point. Its existence remains open. ## Normalize the mark compatibly with duality

Set $O=\mathbb Z_{577}$, $\Psi=\psi\varepsilon^{296}$, $z=\Psi\bmod577$, $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, and $\Theta(M)=JM^{-t}J$. Write $M^\dagger=JM^tJ$. At the source mark $g_1\in G_K$ the residual values are $z_1,1,z_1^{-1}$ with $z_1^2\ne1$. Complex conjugation $c$ has matrix part one. For $M=r(g_1)_{\rm mat}$ the source diagonalizes $Z(M)=M-M^\dagger$ by imposing two off-diagonal conditions. Applying $\Theta$ generally changes that coordinate normalization. For instance an upper triangular matrix with diagonal $s,1,s^{-1}$ and equal entries $t$ in positions 12 and 23 has diagonal $Z(M)$, whereas $Z(\Theta M)_{12}=-t(s-s^{-1})$. For the calculation impose diagonal form on
$$W(M)=Z(M)-Z(M^{-1}).$$
This satisfies $W(\Theta M)=W(M)$ exactly. It is skew-adjoint for $J$, and its residual eigenvalues are $2(z_1-z_1^{-1}),0,-2(z_1-z_1^{-1})$. Their differences are units. Hensel projectors give three integral rank-one eigenspaces. The opposite lines pair perfectly and the middle line has unit norm congruent to one. Normalize that norm using its square root congruent to one, and normalize the opposite pairing to one. This gives a $J$-isometry congruent to the identity that diagonalizes $W$. The same procedure works for $Z$ when returning to the original mark. It preserves ordinarity, the inertia conditions and every trace on $G_K$. An actual point can be reframed this way at any mark with $z_1^2\ne1$.

## A complete local ring with three coordinates

Use the framed representation functor with determinant one, the prescribed multiplier, the fixed residual matrices, the two normalizations above, the killed inertia away from 577, and the ordered ordinary flag at 577 with the prescribed inertia characters. This is an independent description using necessary matrix conditions on the released ordinary points. Here are details of the power-series argument.

The finite residual kernel has a topologically finitely generated maximal pro-577 quotient unramified outside the fixed finite set of places. Indeed its mod-577 character space is finite by class field theory, and Frattini generation applies. Consequently a finite set of matrix generators suffices after adjoining lifts of the finite residual quotient. Write variables for their entries around the chosen residual lift and impose the group relations in the complete local ring. Impose the inertia and mark equations there as well. The local flag is a closed condition in this example. Choose an inertia element with three distinct residual eigenvalues, impose its specified characteristic polynomial, and use its three polynomial spectral projectors. The denominators are unit eigenvalue differences. Vanishing of the lower blocks for the local subgroup inside $G_K$ and the equations for the diagonal blocks on inertia express the flag condition. Near-identity eigenvectors put the flag in the required integral basis. This construction gives a complete Noetherian ring $R$, with the diagonal base point $r_0$ over $O$, and commutes with finite unramified extension of $O$.

The torus $T(t)=\operatorname{diag}(t,1,t^{-1})$ acts by conjugation. Duality acts by applying $\Theta$ to all matrix parts. These actions commute, preserve the conditions, and fix $r_0$. Let $\gamma$ be the absolute commutator at 2 and 12407809 from the [three-class calculation](2026-11-24-three-deformation-directions-at-577.md#read-all-three-classes-on-one-word). In the universal representation put
$$M_\gamma=r(\gamma)_{\rm mat},\qquad A=(M_\gamma+\Theta M_\gamma)/2,\qquad B=(M_\gamma-\Theta M_\gamma)/2,$$
$$X=A_{21},\qquad U=B_{12},\qquad V=B_{13}.$$
These are actual matrix-entry functions. Their tangent readings on the three indicated cocycles are $511,468,95$ respectively, all units. Their other first-order readings are zero. The marked tangent space has dimension three, so topological Nakayama gives a surjection $O[[X,U,V]]\twoheadrightarrow R$. The torus weights of these coordinates are $-1,+1,+2$, and their duality signs are $+,-,-$. Lowercase letters will denote their values at an individual point.

Weights here are integer weights. To see that a homogeneous function admits a homogeneous power-series lift, work first to any finite degree. Adjoin enough prime-to-577 roots of unity by a finite unramified extension, then average the torus action against the specified character. Choose the group larger than the range of possible weights at that degree. The resulting selection of monomials has coefficients in $O$ and descends by faithful flatness. Passing through the truncations is legitimate because the relation ideal is closed. This argument applies to the relation ideal itself. Averaging with the duality involution also preserves integrality.

## What the lower coordinate does on its own

On $U=V=0$ positive-weight entries vanish and weight-zero entries equal their diagonal base values. Duality acts trivially there. A lower triangular $J$-orthogonal matrix with this diagonal has the form
$$\exp(t(g)L)\operatorname{diag}(\Psi(g),1,\Psi(g)^{-1}),\qquad L=E_{21}-E_{32},$$
where $\exp(tL)=1+tL+t^2L^2/2$ exactly. The group law is the crossed-homomorphism law for $t$ with coefficient $\Psi^{-1}$. The symmetric mark forces $t(g_1)=0$, since its 21 entry there is $2t(g_1)(\Psi(g_1)+1)$. Each cohomology class has exactly one such normalization. Away from 577 the killed inertia condition is precisely the unramified class condition for this character. Locally at 577, subtract a coboundary making $t$ zero at one inertia element with distinct residual eigenvalues. Its unique ordered flag is then the standard upper flag. The lower extension must vanish on local $G_K$ and hence is cohomologically zero on the full decomposition group, of relative index two. Conversely every such local coboundary satisfies the flag condition. The pure lower representations are therefore exactly the normalized strict classes.

For any finite $O$-module $M$ the strict cohomology in $M(\Psi^{-1})$ is naturally $M[577]$ after fixing the residual generator. For cyclic $M=O/577^s$, this follows from the exact group $C_{577}$ for the divisible coefficients, the zero local invariants at 577, and the divisible or zero inertia invariants elsewhere. The generator is the residual cocycle included by multiplication by $577^{s-1}$. The natural map obtained by multiplying that cocycle by $M[577]$ is an isomorphism on every finite direct sum of cyclic modules. Apply this to the maximal ideal of a finite coefficient algebra. At $\gamma$ a normalized generator reads 511 and coboundaries read zero. Thus $X$ is the lower parameter up to a unit. Conversely the pure lower representations have $U=V=0$. We have proved an equality of functors and rings
$$R/(U,V)\simeq O[[X]]/(577X).$$
In particular there are pure lower families to every parameter degree in characteristic 577, and no nontrivial characteristic-zero pure lower point. This is a closed slice. A vertical slice alone does not imply that the entire component is vertical.

## A forced integral relation

Write $I$ for the kernel of the power-series presentation. The image of $I$ at $U=V=0$ is exactly $(577X)$. Choose a relation restricting to $577X$, project it to weight $-1$ and to the positive duality sign. The restriction stays $577X$. Every other surviving monomial is
$$X^{1+b+2d}U^b V^d=X(XU)^b(X^2V)^d,\qquad b+d>0\text{ even}.$$
Consequently at any point with $x\ne0$ in characteristic zero there is an equality
$$577+\sum_{b+d\ge2,\ b+d\ {\rm even}} c_{bd}(xu)^b(x^2v)^d=0,\qquad c_{bd}\in O.$$
The infinite series converges because all coordinates are in the maximal ideal. If $x=0$, every matrix entry of negative weight vanishes, since all remaining variables have positive weights. Such a point is upper triangular on $G_K$. It follows that at an irreducible point $x\ne0$ and
$$\min\{v_{577}(xu),v_{577}(x^2v)\}\le\tfrac12.$$
Both products reduce to zero. This estimate uses the order of the **same** strict class and the computed tangent directions. No modularity or finiteness of the generic trace catalogue enters.

There is also a direct irreducibility test. If $x\ne0$, the 21 and 32 entries of $M_\gamma$ are $x$ times units, with opposite nonzero leading readings. This follows by projecting to weight $-1$ and using the pure lower slice. Any invariant subspace on $G_K$ is stable under $W$, which is a combination of four group elements of $G_K$ and has three distinct eigenvalues. Hence it is a span of coordinate axes. The two lower entries leave only the bottom line or the bottom coordinate plane as proper possibilities. Polarization exchanges these two subspaces by $J$-orthogonal complement, so their existence forces lower triangularity. Then $u=v=0$, since applying $\Theta$ preserves lower triangularity. Thus a point with $x\ne0$ and a nonzero upper coordinate is irreducible. In particular the nontrivial corner alternative is already excluded on this marked problem by this Galois calculation.

## Read the inequality in traces

Let $P_i$ be the projector onto the $i$th eigenline of $W$. In the chosen frame these are the coordinate projectors. Their formulas in $W$ have degree two and integral coefficients at the point, since the eigenvalue differences are units. Direct matrix multiplication gives
$$xu=\operatorname{tr}(P_2 A P_1 B),\qquad
x^2v=\operatorname{tr}(P_2 A P_1 B P_3 A^\dagger).$$
Duality holds $W,P_i,A$ fixed and negates $B$. In these expressions $\Theta M_\gamma=r(c\gamma c)_{\rm mat}$, and the adjoint transpose of a group matrix is the matrix at $cg^{-1}c$. Expanding the products therefore expresses **twice** each displayed number as an integral linear combination of $\operatorname{tr}\rho(g)-\operatorname{tr}\rho(g^{-1})$. The same coefficients work at the dual point because $W$ is exactly fixed. The words $g$ have at most nine letters drawn from $g_1,\gamma$, their complex conjugates and their inverses. Every letter lies in $G_K$. One can take $g_1$ to be the specified inertia element at 2 when testing a point over $K$, by reframing as above.

If all these directed differences had valuation greater than $1/2$, so would both products, contradicting the integral relation. Every difference has positive valuation because the residual trace is invariant under inversion. This proves the claim. A finite local trace field with integral value group cannot contain this reading. A coefficient DVR on which the full marked residue is split must even have ramification index at least four, since $xu$ has at least two powers of its uniformizer and $x^2v$ has at least three. The latter statement concerns an integral marked realization, which can require a larger field than the traces. The release permits a finite coefficient DVR above the initial unramified coefficient ring for an ordinary point. Its unramified base-ring hypothesis alone does not assert that the witness traces are unramified. ## Two particular words suffice

Use the specified inertia element $h$ at 2, and put $\gamma_i=h^i\gamma h^{-i}$. Define
$$g^{(2)}=h[\gamma_0,\gamma_1],\qquad g^{(3)}=h[\gamma_0,[\gamma_1,\gamma_2]],\qquad
d_i=\operatorname{tr}\rho(g^{(i)})-\operatorname{tr}\rho((g^{(i)})^{-1}).$$
**At least one of $d_2,d_3$ has valuation in $(0,1/2]$.** These words use the original arithmetic cocycles' common Frobenius at 12407809 and inertia at 2. The statement holds for each compatible choice of lifts specified in the cocycle calculation.

Here is a direct check. The inertia matrix at $h$ has order dividing 64 and has the fixed three distinct eigenvalues $\psi(5),1,\psi(5)^{-1}$ because reduction distinguishes the 64th roots. Its own directed difference is exactly zero. At first order the matrix at $\gamma$ is $1+XL+UP+VQ$ with $L=E_{21}-E_{32}$, $P=E_{12}+E_{23}$, $Q=E_{13}$. For $\gamma_i$ the three coefficients are multiplied by $s^{-i},s^i,s^{2i}$ respectively, $s=510\bmod577$. A commutator of two matrices congruent to one begins with the Lie bracket of their leading terms. Since $[L,Q]=P$, $[P,Q]=0$ and $[L,P]=\operatorname{diag}(-1,2,-1)$, the degree-two coefficient of $a=XU$ in $d_2$ is
$$(s-s^{-1})\big(4-2(s+s^{-1})\big)=102\bmod577.$$
In $d_3$ there is no degree-two coefficient. For the degree-three coefficient of $b=X^2V$ first bracket the degree-one parts at $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$: their $XV$ term is $(s^3-1)XV P$. The next bracket gives $(s^3-1)X^2V[L,P]$. Forming the directed trace after multiplication by $h$ weights the diagonal by $T(s)+T(s)^{-1}$. This gives
$$(s^3-1)\big(4-2(s+s^{-1})\big)=473\bmod577.$$

Both directed differences are weight-zero functions and vanish on the base, so they admit integral power-series expressions in $a,b$. They are odd under duality. In the two invariant variables their linear coefficient matrix is upper triangular modulo 577, with diagonal 102 and 473 and determinant **355**, a unit. The off-diagonal coefficient of $b$ in $d_2$ is unnecessary. Lifting the expressions to the power-series ring and applying the formal inverse function theorem gives $(d_2,d_3)=(a,b)$ at each point. Equivalently one can apply Nakayama to the two generating pairs. Thus $577\in(d_2,d_3)^2$ at an irreducible characteristic-zero point. A direct truncated matrix multiplication, using the actual cocycle readings 511, 468 and 95, gives mixed leading coefficients 421 and 573 independently of the bracket computation.
