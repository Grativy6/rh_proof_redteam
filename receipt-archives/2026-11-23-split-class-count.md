# A split prime can supply two incompatible witnesses

**23 November 2026.** In the global class count we tested two identifications, each of which would change the answer. Testing a Hermitian lattice at the two halves of a split prime independently would give **infinitely many** inequivalent lattices in the genus of the standard line. The release's mass fallback would then return **zero**. Its actual genus test gives mass **$1/12$**, and a direct count at two auxiliary levels gives **15,552 classes**. A second test produces **288** classes where using the wrong residue field would give **192**. Both distinctions are present at the places where the proof uses them. ## What must one isometry preserve?

Work over $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3,i)$, with real subfield $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$, integer ring $O$ and CM conjugation. Put $L=O^k$ with its standard positive Hermitian form and $M_n=13^nL$ for $n>0$. The prime 13 splits completely, and $13=\pi\bar\pi$ for $\pi=3+2i$. At a prime of $E$ above $\pi$, multiplication by $(\pi/\bar\pi)^n$ is a rational unitary isometry that maps $L$ to $M_n$ after ordinary one-prime localization. At a prime above $\bar\pi$, its inverse does the same. At all other primes the identity works.

These are infinitely many different global isometry classes. For example the smallest squared norm at the two complex embeddings combined scales by $13^{2n}$. If one's definition accepted the separate just-given witnesses, a finite set of class representatives could not exist, and the release's numeric default would give zero. This effect also works over any CM field at a completely split rational prime. At a chosen prime and its conjugate, choose $a$ with valuations one and zero by the Chinese remainder theorem, then use $a/\bar a$ for the one-prime test. The original release even includes an example host with two explicitly specified completely split primes. [Concrete splitting check](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/SpanPowerMembership.lean#L13-L29) · [Finite representatives required](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/ThetaLiftIndependence/Statement.lean#L139-L142)

The genus in the release uses denominators that are units at **both** $P$ and $\bar P$. Indeed $s$ and $\bar s$ must both lie outside $P$ in `hcompl`. Its `localization₂`, used for module lengths, permits all $s\notin P$. A unitary transformation acting at both places has $v_P(\det g)+v_{\bar P}(\det g)=0$. Sending the standard rank-$k$ lattice to $M_n$ would require both valuations to equal $nk$. This rules it out. The one-prime index calculation also detects the defect once its second lattice is the Hermitian dual. Here $M_n^\#=13^{-n}L$, giving `jIdx` equal to $-2nk$ at each conjugate prime. [Denominators and the genus condition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/HermitianLattices/StatementLat.lean#L57-L81) · [Index against the dual](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/HermitianLattices/StatementLat.lean#L92-L99)

In rank one, the true genus consists of fractional ideals $I$ with $I\bar I=O$. To see sufficiency, at a split pair realize the two opposite exponents by one scalar $a/\bar a$ as above. At fixed primes the exponent is zero. Globally write $I=A/\bar A$ by assigning the positive and negative exponents on conjugate pairs. The absolute discriminant of $E$ is 144, so the Minkowski bound on the least ideal norm in a class is $(4!/4^4)(4/\pi)^2\sqrt{144}<2$. Thus $A$ is principal and a single norm-one scalar generates $I$. The standard line has one class and its automorphisms are its twelve roots of unity. Its actual mass is $1/12$. ## A count in finite sets

At rational primes 13 and 37 the extension $F/\mathbb Q$ splits and $E/F$ splits at each resulting place. Put the upper unitriangular reduction condition of the release at all four places and leave the other stabilizers unchanged. In rank one the local subgroup consists of units congruent to one. There are $(13-1)^2(37-1)^2=186624$ possible residue labels. The twelve global units act freely, giving $186624/12=15552$ classes. The [checker](check_split_class_count.py) enumerates those orbits. This tests the two auxiliary levels in the class formula. The full release can include further level data. Change 37 to 5. The prime 5 is inert in $F$ because $3$ is a nonsquare modulo 5. Its one place of $F$ splits in $E=F(i)$ since $i\in\mathbb Q_5$. The unitary group is still split at that place. Its residue field now has 25 elements, so the actual count is $(25-1)(13-1)^2/12=288$. Using two factors of $5-1$ gives just 192. The released predicate `SplitsF` checks both the number of places over the rational prime and their residue cardinalities. The host construction passes a separate congruence which yields complete splitting. In rank four at 13 and 37, the index in a single local $\mathrm{GL}_4$ is $\prod_{j=1}^4(q^j-1)$, by counting ordered independent columns and dividing by the size of the upper unitriangular group. Thus the four-place index is

 A=\left(\prod_{q\in\{13,37\}}\prod_{j=1}^4(q^j-1)\right)^2 .

## Can two places still miss a finite stabilizer?

Yes, if the two places lie over the same rational prime. At both places of $F$ over 13 let $B=(\mathbb Z/13)^a$ act by translations on the standard positive lattice with coordinates indexed by $B$. Its permutation matrices preserve the lattice everywhere. At either place their reductions can all be made upper unitriangular in one basis. In characteristic 13 the regular module is the truncated polynomial ring in $a$ variables $t_j$ with $t_j^{13}=0$. Translation is multiplication by $1+t_j$, which is unitriangular in the monomial basis ordered by degree. That residue basis lifts to a local integral basis. The fixed subspace of this subgroup has just one direction among $13^a$. The primitive all-ones vector maps to $13^a$ times the generator in the coinvariants. Even the two rational lines of invariants and coinvariants thus differ integrally by the full factor $13^a$.

For the actual fine count the two rational primes are distinct. Every rational stabilizer element has finite order because it preserves a full lattice up to bounded denominators and belongs to a compact group at every infinite place. At a place of residue characteristic $q$ with unitriangular reduction, a finite-order element has $q$-power order: its reduction has $q$-power order and the successive principal congruence quotients are additive groups of that characteristic. The observation applies to conjugated levels as well. Two distinct rational residue characteristics force order one. The construction uses this at the stage where it puts independent vectors on each class representative. ## Unresolved

The independent split-prime witnesses would destroy the class count even in a positive definite example. The native paired localization excludes them, and the level index uses the correct residue fields. The calculation identifies what a further attack has to change in the arithmetic setting. In an indefinite space the rational stabilizer of a finite lattice can be infinite while the adelic quotient has finite positive volume. The rank argument for step zero uses the definite group.
