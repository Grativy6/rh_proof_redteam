# How far can an integral retraction tilt?

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Christopher D. Pang’s continuing audit of the arithmetic maps.** For the indefinite complex components at the actual low-slope call, the determinant marking group gives the exact size $t$ of each relevant orbit. Let $\mathfrak a$ be the ideal of scalars clearing the denominators of a coefficient-field-linear retraction $P$ onto the character eigenspace. The same map obeys
$$
  \frac{1}{[F:\mathbb Q]}\sum_{\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}\log\|P_\sigma\|_{\rm op}^2
   +\frac{\log\mathrm N\mathfrak a}{[F:\mathbb Q]}\ \ge\ \log t .
$$
In particular an integral retraction pays the entire $\log t$ in the **mean logarithmic squared norm**. Concentrating distortion at a few archimedean places still costs this much in the release's averaging convention. The input and output have the Petersson metrics already used at the call. No equivariance, positivity or prescribed averaging formula is required of $P$. This quantifies what happens if the reverse map is changed after its symmetric average fails integrality. ## Exact orbit size in the indefinite case

Use $G$ for either the full level group `grpHR` in the component-cut calculation or the subgroup `grpR` acting on the cofactor alone. Write $M=g_0g_1\,15^s$, $\mathfrak b$ for the half-level ideal and $\mathfrak c=\mathfrak b+(15^s)$. Set $\Delta=(\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak c)^\times$. The preceding calculation shows that the cofactor determinant $G\to \Delta$ is surjective and that any stabilizer of a component lies in its kernel. We check the reverse inclusion using strong approximation. This step uses the indefinite signatures **at this call**. Fix a marked polarized complex point of a component, with its homology lattice $\Lambda$ and nondegenerate Hermitian space $V$ over the CM field $E$. Let $E^+$ denote the real subfield. The special unitary group $\mathrm{SU}(V)$ over $E^+$ is simply connected and absolutely almost simple. The Hermitian space has dimension $r\ge2$, and at an infinite place of signature $(p,q)$ with $p,q\ge1$ its real points are noncompact. Consequently its rational points are dense in its finite adeles. Equivalently one can apply strong approximation over $\mathbb Q$ to its restriction of scalars. [Milne, Theorem 4.16](https://jmilne.org/math/xnotes/svi.pdf) · [Platonov, strong approximation](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/im2220) · [Addendum for type A](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/im2446)

Take a level element with trivial cofactor determinant. It has determinant one at the type primes as well. At each relevant finite place of $E^+$ the CM algebra is split: the type primes split completely, and 3 and 5 already split in the quadratic field $K$ with $E=E^+K$. The local special unitary group acts as $\mathrm{SL}_r$ on the selected half, with inverse-adjoint action on the other half. Any prescribed determinant-one matrix over a residue quotient lifts to an integral determinant-one matrix. Lift the entries and correct the determinant by an invertible diagonal factor congruent to 1. Impose those local congruences, preservation of $\Lambda$ everywhere else, and congruence to the identity modulo 2 at the dyadic places. These are nonempty open adelic conditions. Strong approximation gives one rational special unitary element satisfying all of them. Preservation of the lattice at every finite place makes it a global $\mathcal O_E$-lattice automorphism.

This lift stays in the same geometric component. On the marked period domain it changes the complex structure by a real special unitary transformation. The real groups $\mathrm{SU}(p,q)$ and their compact signature analogues are connected. A path to that transformation gives a path of polarized complex structures with the marking held fixed. At its endpoint the global lattice automorphism identifies the object with the requested level translate of the original point. The auxiliary level $A$ in the moduli problem is 1. The dyadic condition preserves the semicharacter of the symmetric polarization bundle as well. Indeed its values are signs, and the semicharacter law $\alpha(x+y)=(-1)^{\langle x,y\rangle}\alpha(x)\alpha(y)$ makes it a function of $x\bmod 2\Lambda$. Rigidification fixes the scalar in the line isomorphism, and the canonical cubical structure is compatible with that isomorphism. This ensures the lift preserves the extra line-bundle data in the actual moduli problem. ## The norm and denominator inequality

Let $V_{\chi}$ be the desired eigenspace in the cusp space over the number field $F$, and let $\Lambda_F$ be its ambient integral cusp lattice in this paragraph and below. For the group acting only on the cofactor take $\chi=1$. The preceding receipt gives an orbit $\mathcal O$ on which there are nonzero eigenforms. Write $U=e_{\mathcal O}V_\chi$, $m=\dim_F U>0$, and $J=\Lambda_F\cap U$. Here $J$ is a full finite lattice in $U$ because the ambient lattice is full. At all embeddings, Petersson norms square-add on disjoint component supports. Within one orbit the pieces $e_Zu$ of an eigenform $u\in U$ have equal norm since level translations are isometries and character values have modulus one. Therefore
$$
   \|e_Zu\|_\sigma=\|u\|_\sigma/\sqrt t \qquad(Z\in\mathcal O).
$$
Restriction to any component is injective on $U$. The pieces on different components together form an orthogonal direct sum of $t$ scaled copies of $U$. [Disjoint components in the Petersson integral](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ComponentsPeterssonOrthogonalAndMasses.lean#L78-L85) · [Orthogonality for the component metric](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ComponentsPeterssonOrthogonalAndMasses.lean#L149-L166) · [Finiteness and fullness](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/IsProbabilityMeasureMuPart.lean#L804-L812) · [Character norms and isometries](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/SumSqNormWCuspPhLe.lean#L49-L76)

Let $P$ be any $F$-linear retraction onto $V_\chi$. Define its nonzero denominator ideal by $\mathfrak a=\{a\in\mathcal O_F:aP(\Lambda_F)\subseteq\Lambda_F\}$, and put $D=(\mathrm N\mathfrak a)^{1/[F:\mathbb Q]}$. For every $Z\in\mathcal O$ the endomorphism $A_Zu=e_{\mathcal O}P(e_Zu)$ maps $J$ into $\mathfrak a^{-1}J$ and $\sum_Z A_Z=\mathrm{id}_U$. Choose a finite normal field $F'$ over $\mathbb Q$ containing $F$ and all the eigenvalues of the $A_Z$. If $\alpha\ne0$ is one of these eigenvalues, then $(\alpha)\mathfrak a\mathcal O_{F'}$ is integral. Indeed for each prime of $F$, a local generator of $\mathfrak a$ times $A_Z$ has an integral matrix on the localized lattice. Its eigenvalues are locally integral. The product formula for ideals therefore gives
$$
 \left(\prod_{\rho:F'\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}|\rho\alpha|\right)^{1/[F':\mathbb Q]}\ge D^{-1}.
$$

Let $j$ be the number of nonzero eigenvalues of all the $A_Z$ combined, with algebraic multiplicities, and $S_\rho$ the sum of their squared absolute values at $\rho$. Their sum is exactly $m$ because $\sum_Z A_Z=\mathrm{id}$. Thus $j>0$ and Cauchy–Schwarz gives $S_\rho\ge m^2/j$ at each embedding. Applying arithmetic-geometric mean to the same $j$ terms at each $\rho$ and multiplying over embeddings gives $\operatorname{GM}_\rho S_\rho\ge j/D^2$. Combining these two bounds yields
$$
        \operatorname{GM}_\rho S_\rho
           \ \ge\ \max\{m^2/j,\ j/D^2\}\ \ge\ m/D .
$$
The order of operations here matters. Each algebraic eigenvalue is carried through all conjugate places before the logarithmic average is taken.

Write $\sigma=\rho|_F$. On the orthogonal direct sum of the component copies, the block row $[A_Z]_{Z\in\mathcal O}$, with each copy of $U$ given its ordinary norm, has operator norm at most $\|P_\sigma\|/\sqrt t$. Its target has dimension $m$, so the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the row is at most $m\|P_\sigma\|^2/t$. The same squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm is the sum of those of its blocks. By unitary Schur triangularization that sum is at least $S_\rho$, including for nondiagonalizable blocks. Hence $\|P_\sigma\|^2\ge tS_\rho/m$. Taking geometric means proves the displayed height and denominator inequality. All embeddings of $F$ occur with the same multiplicity in $F'$. For a rational integer $k>0$ clearing denominators one has $D\le k$, and
$$
 k\,\operatorname{GM}_\sigma\|P_\sigma\|^2\ \ge\ t .
$$

* An integral retraction has geometric-mean complex operator norm at least $\sqrt t$. An integral splitting exists because the intersection with the field subspace is saturated and its torsion-free quotient is projective over the ring of integers.
* A retraction contractive at every embedding needs an integer denominator at least $t$. At a single embedding a contractive projection fixing a Hilbert subspace is its orthogonal projection.
* The tradeoff is sharp already on the displayed sum of copies of $U$. For $1\le k\le t$, recover an eigenform from each of $k$ chosen component samples and average those reconstructions. The squared operator norm on this subspace is $t/k$. The integral lattice on it is exactly the sum of the $e_ZJ$: one integral sample reconstructs integrally using one level translate for each component, with a root-of-unity coefficient. No assertion of an equally small retraction on the full space is needed.

There is a two-sample check in the actual coefficient fields, which contain $\sqrt2$ through the real dyadic conductor. Take integral weights $\sqrt2-1$ and $2-\sqrt2$ on two components in the displayed subspace and zero on the rest. They sum to 1. The squared operator norm at the two restrictions of an embedding is $t(9-6\sqrt2)$ and $t(9+6\sqrt2)$, respectively. The first lies below $t$, and their geometric mean is exactly $3t$. By comparison, equal weights $1/2,1/2$ have denominator 2 and squared norm $t/2$ at every embedding. This is an exact test on the component sample space described above.

## Unresolved

The raw sum in the published low-slope family has a cardinality allowance and satisfies a one-sided norm bound. The upper bound is compatible with the obstruction above. A failure on this route still needs a downstream operation that fixes the eigenspace, preserves integral membership and uses constants on the forbidden side of the inequality. Computing the denominator of the normalized projector on other inputs would also require controlling the action inside an individual component.
