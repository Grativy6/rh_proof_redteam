# At 106619 the adjoint disappears

**24 November 2026.** The same source inequality receives a strict group of order 25 at both $q=2179$ and $q=106619$. At 2179 a single weight-two adjoint pays the full trace bound. At 106619 the corresponding weight-two form cannot exist. Its necessary Bernoulli residue is **$118\equiv3\bmod5$**, where zero is required. The separate weight-three-plus-line alternative is also excluded. This leaves a genuinely non-self-dual rank-three representation over $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$. On the candidate space a single characteristic-zero Hecke eigenform distinguishes both directions at 11 and at 29. Identifying the required residual eigensystem in that space remains open. ### One test function, both primes

Take the **same** signed standard-lattice orbit indicator at $y=(1,2736,98987)$, and evaluate at the same $x=(1,2,3)$. This time paths are allowed through either lattice class in the middle. For $T_p^+,T_p^-$ denoting the two directions, the four readings of $T_{11}^{\pm}T_{29}^{\pm}$, in the order $++,+-,-+,--$, are
$$1,\quad0,\quad0,\quad0.$$
Reversing the two primes gives the same four values. Thus the product $(T_{11}^+-T_{11}^-)(T_{29}^+-T_{29}^-)$ has an entry equal to one. The unramified Hecke algebra here is commutative and closed under adjoints for the positive mass inner product. Its complex operators are simultaneously diagonalizable. The nonzero product therefore proves the existence of **one characteristic-zero joint eigenform** in this definite-unitary space distinguishing both directions. The integral entry alone cannot identify an eigenvalue after reduction modulo five. The target requires both directed differences to vanish on the residual eigensystem, and additional conditions on its global trace and local types.

## Why one residual eigensystem would already suffice at length two

There is a further simplification independent of the published deformation-ring estimate. Suppose one genuine irreducible admissible automorphic system here has the prescribed semisimplified global residue, is realized over a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_5$, and its conjugates over $\mathbb Q_5$ are admissible as well. For example, an integral rational Hecke computation would give the conjugate systems in the same space. The level, ordinarity, local type and global residual identification would still have to be checked. Let $E$ be the field generated over $\mathbb Q_5$ by **all its traces on $G_K$**. Take all embeddings of $E$ into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_5$. They give pairwise nonisomorphic systems, because different embeddings differ on some trace. This choice avoids overcounting an artificially enlarged field of definition.

For any $\sigma$ in the source's test put $t=1+\Psi(\sigma)+\Psi(\sigma)^{-1}\in\mathbb Z_5$. If the noncoincidence premise holds, $a=\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma)-t$ is nonzero and in the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_E$. Summing over the **distinct systems** yields
$$\sum_{\iota:E\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_5} v_5(\iota(a))
  =v_5(N_{E/\mathbb Q_5}a)\ \ge1.$$
This remains true when $a$ itself generates a smaller field. The other embeddings still distinguish the representations at other elements. The factor two in the released estimate gives at least two, exactly the required length. This arithmetic observation moves the unresolved test to **existence and identification** of a compatible system. At larger lengths, one such orbit need not suffice. ## Unresolved

The two-prime computation establishes a joint direction-sensitive eigenform in the ambient space. Its global residual character and the required admissible cuspidal base change remain unidentified.
