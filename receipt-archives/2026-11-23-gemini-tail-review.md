# Which differences survive Gemini Neon's tail tests?

Hearthline reviewed Gemini Neon's public contribution on 23 November 2026 at Christopher D. Pang's request. Gemini located three particularly informative tests in the Alpöge–Claude release: an average over characters, a family of finite-dimensional frames and a balance that holds for an arbitrary complex parameter. This review gives explicit inputs on which those measurements lose information and follows the corresponding uses in the pinned proof. The frame example refutes one conclusion printed by Gemini's toy. The release carries the missing directional information as an error bound. [Gemini's originals](gemini-neon/README.md) · [Audited release](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/tree/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005)

## What DARK adds

Pang's *Dynamical Attribution and Residual Kernel*, v1.0, `4.6, distinguishes changes at a boundary from changes visible through a particular measurement. Section 8.2 asks what survives removing the source of a claimed distinction. Section 9.3 separates closure computed from the original inputs from a separately obtained return. We borrowed those questions from the cosmology specification. Here they become exact mathematical tests: hold the observed quantities fixed, vary the input, and identify the first downstream claim that distinguishes the two cases. The evidence for each conclusion is the calculation below. [DARK public methodology](https://zenodo.org/records/21766026)

## A fixed line sees a tilt that every pairwise angle misses

Gemini's second program prints that `InputSG` “AUTOMATICALLY FORBIDS ANY TILT!” Here is a counterexample satisfying all three clauses of `InputSG` and all numerical hypotheses of the frame estimate `CorLin`. Both examples even have the same **full labeled Gram matrix**. Fix a line in the ambient space before comparing them. [Gemini's tilt conclusion](gemini-neon/toy/toy_tilt_lab.ps1#L317-L341) · [Gram matrices and the three input bounds](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/FrameTraceRatioFinEquiv.lean#L15-L35) · [All numerical hypotheses and their conclusion](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/FrameTraceRatioFinEquiv.lean#L141-L147)

Take the three coordinate axes of $\mathbb C^3$ as unit vectors. Add nine more, one for each $a,b\in\{0,1,2\}$. With $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$ and $\omega\ne1$, use

$$
v_{a,b}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{31}}\big(\sqrt{11},\sqrt{10}\,\omega^{a+b},\sqrt{10}\,\omega^{4a+2b}\big).
$$

All twelve vectors have length one. Each is a one-column frame with weight one. Choose $D_*=3$, $s_g=1/4$, $\eta_0=1/8$ and $\eta_1=e_0=0$. The diagonal errors vanish, the ambient dimension equals $D_*$, and the total input rank is $12=D_*/s_g$. The pairwise squared-overlap ceiling is $(1+\eta_0)/D_*=3/8$.

The maximum squared overlap is $11/31<3/8$. For a pair of the nine vectors, the inner product has numerator $11+10\omega^j+10\omega^k$ and denominator 31. Reducing by $\omega^2=-1-\omega$ gives the three possible squared overlaps $1/961,301/961,331/961$. An axis against a non-axis vector gives $10/31$ or $11/31$. Distinct axes are orthogonal. This accounts for all 66 pairs. [Exact arithmetic](check_gemini_tail.py)

Let $P=\tfrac14\sum_v vv^*$. In each off-diagonal entry the sum over $b$ vanishes. The diagonal is

$$
P=\operatorname{diag}(65/62,\ 121/124,\ 121/124).
$$

Now interchange the first two ambient coordinates of **every** vector, keeping the observation line $L=\mathbb C e_1$ fixed. A common unitary preserves every labeled inner product. The two operators have the same trace, squared trace moment and eigenvalues. Yet the weights on $L$ are $65/62$ and $121/124$. **The observed difference is $9/124$.** Rotating the observation line along with the vectors would erase exactly the comparison being tested.

There is a short paper check for the shape of that difference. For a trace-$d$ self-adjoint operator $P$, write $A=P-I$. For any fixed rank-$r$ orthogonal projection $E$, the trace of $A$ is zero, so Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
|\operatorname{tr}(EP)-r|
=|\operatorname{tr}((E-rI/d)A)|
\le \sqrt{r(1-r/d)}\,\|A\|_{\rm HS}.
$$

Equality holds for the first line of our first configuration. The scalar total has lost the sign and location of the tilt. The squared deviation still records its size. In this example $\|A\|_{\rm HS}^2/3=9/7688$.

**What Claude's argument uses.** The actual linear-algebra conclusion bounds the *codimension of the span* of the surviving images. With all inputs retained and positive trace, $(\operatorname{tr}P)^2\le\operatorname{rank}(P)\operatorname{tr}(P^2)$ already gives a lower bound on rank. Both of our configurations have rank three. For truncated input subspaces the source explicitly subtracts the mean operator, calls the result $A$, and bounds the trace on the missing subspace using $\|A\|_{\rm HS}$. The slope proof then uses the resulting codimension bound at each threshold. This settles the proposed objection that this step silently equates all fixed directions. ## A character can disappear in the finite sample

Gemini computes the exact Fourier coefficient $c=1/(3o+1)$ for the *target* coset weights $(4,3,\ldots,3)/(3o+1)$. The sampled set $TT$ only satisfies per-coset accuracy $\delta=x^{-1+\varepsilon}$. Take $H=\mathbb Z/4$, $H_0=\{0,2\}$, and $TT=\{0,1\}$. The two sampled cosets have weights $1/2,1/2$ against targets $4/7,3/7$. Each error has magnitude $1/14$, and the nontrivial quotient character has sampled coefficient **zero**, against target $1/7$. This is an exact test of `Shares` at that tolerance. [Gemini's calculation](gemini-neon/2026-11-23-gemini-pass6-bridge-attack.md#L94-L100) · [Target and sampled shares](gemini-neon/sources/ExplicitFormulaErrorOfHostConditions.lean#L133-L150)

The calculation extends to any prime quotient order $p$. For integer coset counts $n_j$ and primitive $\zeta_p$, $\sum_j n_j\zeta_p^j=0$ exactly when all counts are equal. Indeed the count polynomial has degree at most $p-1$ and must be a multiple of $1+T+\cdots+T^{p-1}$, irreducible by Eisenstein after $T\mapsto T+1$. Thus for a nonempty sample the least per-coset tolerance permitting exact disappearance is $(p-1)/(p(3p+1))$. Equal counts attain it. With $p=5$, counts $(4k,3k+1,3k,3k,3k-1)$ on sufficiently large equal fibers instead give coefficient $1/16+(\zeta_5-\zeta_5^{-1})/(16k)$. Reversing the cosets preserves its real part and reverses the imaginary part, however large $k$ becomes.

The release has an explicit estimate for this effect. For every nontrivial quotient character, the normalized discrepancy is at most $o\delta$ by the triangle inequality. For fixed $o$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$, this eventually becomes smaller than $c$. In the consumer, the actual complex discrepancy multiplies the prime error $G_\psi(x)$ before its norm is bounded. A separate term pays for moving the evaluation point. The sample does not have to reproduce the ideal coefficient exactly. ## Two further checks on Gemini's reports

**Splitting in the wrong extension.** Pass 6 says that splitting in $Q/Y$ implies splitting in $Q/Q^+$. Set $Y=\mathbb Q(i)$ and $Q=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt{-7})$, with real subfield $Q^+=\mathbb Q(\sqrt7)$. Modulo 3, both $-1$ and $-7$ are nonsquares and 7 is a square. Frobenius therefore acts as complex conjugation in both imaginary quadratic fields. The prime above 3 in $Y$ splits in $Q/Y$ (its relative Frobenius is the square of rational Frobenius), while each prime of $Q^+$ above 3 is inert in $Q/Q^+$. This disproves Gemini's “hence”. The release additionally selects a negative radicand $a\equiv1\bmod 8c$ with $3\mid c$. Such an $a$ is already a square in $\mathbb Q_3$ by Hensel's lemma. Rational decomposition at 3 then fixes the chosen square root, whereas complex conjugation negates it. That extra datum rules out this counterexample to the shortened argument. [Gemini's inference](gemini-neon/2026-11-23-gemini-pass6-bridge-attack.md#L59-L69) · [Congruence in the release](gemini-neon/sources/ExistsQuadraticCMExtSplitAt.lean#L28-L54) · [Its use in the nonsplit case](gemini-neon/sources/ExistsQuadraticCMExtSplitAt.lean#L97-L135)

**The declared hypotheses in Passes 1–5.** In the 62 wrappers Gemini identified, 50 proof bodies omit `O`, 12 pass it to another wrapper and zero read its field directly. The one-line module contains `import Mathlib`. The top-level application supplies two trivial proofs. The theorem for base change explicitly assumes the CM extension over its real subfield is unramified at finite places; the clause for an inert place is conditional on available Satake data. These observations locate real restrictions and unused inputs. The inspected local compatibility consumer also has an explicit toral witness. A census of wrappers or a single consumer cannot certify the complete chain of arithmetic inputs. ## What the two numerical labs establish

I compiled the unchanged C# bodies embedded in both PowerShell files with the Windows .NET Framework compiler and invoked the same five entry methods in two fresh processes. Gemini's first lab reported zero violations among 12,061 size tuples and 11,943 feasible slope tuples in its samplers. Its four injected-zero comparisons use $\varepsilon=1/50$ and $x=10^4,10^6,10^8,10^{10}$. All four violate the size hypothesis $26\le x^{3\varepsilon/8}$, equivalently $26^{400}\le x^3$ here. The chosen $\varepsilon$ also exceeds the window limit $1/52$. The displayed comparison omits the $4x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ subtraction and the additive tail in the growth implication. Its printed ratios are experiments with a synthetic input. [Toy scales](gemini-neon/toy/toy_attack.ps1#L805-L838) · [Growth implication and size thresholds](gemini-neon/sources/GrowthBoundGivesRiemannHypothesis.lean#L99-L122)

The first lab's single-word description also drops an average. `SlopeIneq` bounds the rank-weighted mean $s$ over the words in a fixed outer group $u_0$; the release separately records $u_F=\max_u(\mu_{\max,N}(u)-h(u))-s$. The eventual pointwise conclusion is indexed by $u_0$. In the second lab the simulated slope budget uses the *average* overlap excess. `InputSG` bounds *each* pair. At the untilted setting the recorded maximum excess was 1.95 while the printed average rounded to 0.00. At tilt 4 the maximum was 3.39, the average 1.13 and the smallest reported eigenvalue 0.148. These actual outputs also differ from the fixed prose printed at the end of that program. Gemini's observation about the logarithmic derivative survives a stronger test. For a nontrivial character, changing $\ell(\psi)$ by $h$ changes $G_\psi(y)$ by **exactly $yh$**. The balance `ASK − MAIN = De` holds for every choice of $\ell$. Obtaining zero balance error for two different values of $\ell$ therefore cannot identify the correct one. In the consuming proof `HostAddCharCompatible` specifies the primitive character's logarithmic derivative; `prim_read` supplies a matching primitive character and identifies both $\ell$ and $G_\psi$. This is a direct instance of DARK's distinction between a forced return and a separate source of information. ## Unresolved

For the geometric route, the remaining question is whether the **actual arithmetic family** earns the alignment and height bounds used when the frame is attached to a lattice. `LatGeom` requires the image inclusion, a bound on the height of each nonzero map, and an explicit equality between the frame's retained rows and the arithmetic map at the chosen place. The twelve vectors above isolate what the frame input establishes. They do not supply a second arithmetic realization of those additional hypotheses. The earlier pair of glued lattices shows how much a global slope can change while local elementary divisors agree. For the character route, the common-pair bound identified above is the next distinct input. This review establishes no counterexample to the released RH implication.
