# The raw theta lift and its integral image at the host primes

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Chris Pang's continuing public audit.** The release claims that its raw theta map from the fixed larger space $V$ reaches the full complex row space $W$. This gives the [congruent good and bad row eigenforms](2026-11-23-good-bad-hecke-eigenvalues.md) preimages at the same fixed larger level. Passing to the integral source raises a separate question. At either host prime $p=q,d$, the actual class-sum formula lets us identify its image with the span of the individual traced theta series. It also gives a lower bound on the loss that must occur between that span, the good projection and the full row lattice.

## Which preimages exist?

Fix the base Whittaker type $U,\psi$ and a tuple at a large trivial-character indefinite stage. Use the release's size condition $8fr+4\le n_V$, rational class-sum identity with nonzero phase, arithmetic models and the upstream hypotheses of the [spectral denominator calculation](2026-11-23-good-type-spectral-denominator.md). Write $\theta$ for the rational class-sum map before the good projector $P$. Its defining equation compares the same arithmetic map, after the specified embedding, to the raw theta integral on the corresponding source forms. The complex comparison explicitly gives $\operatorname{im}(\mathrm{thF})=W_{\mathrm{all}}$. The next map is $\mathrm{thG}=P\,\mathrm{thF}$ in row coordinates. Rational class forms span this larger complex space by the class presentation. Write the preimage as a complex combination of those forms and apply the $\psi$-character average on the row side. ## Two candidate denominators are units

Let $O$ be the localization of the outer coefficient ring at $\lambda\mid p$. Let $D_O$ be the release's source lattice `lvlNab` localized there, and $Y=\theta(D_O)$. Write $S_O$ for its `thetaTuple` after localization. The generators of $S_O$ are individual theta series with a coefficient from the specified coefficient lattice and the raw Whittaker character trace, with the same phase as $\theta$. The preceding rank argument makes $Y$ a full lattice in the rational type space. Membership of its elements in the ambient row lattice $\Lambda$ is a separate comparison. The release supplies $S_O\subseteq Y$ even before localization. In the converse calculation, for each integral source vector $y$ the release constructs $z_c$, indexed by the finite definite-unitary class set, such that $\theta(z_c)\in S_O$ and

$$
 |U|\,\theta(y)=\sum_c |\Gamma_c|^{-1}\theta(z_c).

Here $\Gamma_c$ is the actual stabilizer of the indicated class. The source uses this formula over the coefficient field. Inspecting the denominators gives more at the host primes. Each finite $\Gamma_c$ embeds faithfully in $\mathrm{GL}_{n_V}(E)$ and hence in $\mathrm{GL}_{[E:\mathbb Q]n_V}(\mathbb Q)$. If $p$ divided $|\Gamma_c|$, an element of order $p$ would have a primitive $p$th root as an eigenvalue and its rational characteristic polynomial would contain $\Phi_p$, of degree $p-1$. The large-stage hypotheses give $p-1>[E:\mathbb Q]n_V$ because $[E:\mathbb Q]=2f$, $f n_V$ is polynomially bounded in the window scale, and $\min(q,d)$ eventually exceeds every fixed power. This rules out $p$-torsion for every class at once. The displayed formula therefore holds with unit denominators in $O$ and gives **$Y=S_O$**. The argument works at any stage satisfying the explicit prime and dimension inequalities. ## Where the known loss must occur

In the fixed Whittaker space write $G=\Lambda\cap\operatorname{im}P$ and define the three finite $O$-modules

$$
 T=P\Lambda/G,\qquad
 A=\Lambda/(\Lambda\cap Y),\qquad
 J=(G+PY)/G.
$$

$A$ measures which integral rows lack an integral preimage for this raw class sum. $J$ measures how far projecting its integral source outputs goes outside the good row lattice. The previous calculation supplies a lower bound for $T$. These definitions allow $Y$ to be a fractional lattice, so the argument does not require a choice of normalization making $\theta$ integral.

View $T$ and $J$ in the same ambient good space modulo $G$. The map $x\mapsto Px$ induces a surjection $A\to T/(T\cap J)$ because $x\in\Lambda\cap Y$ gives $Px\in PY$. Also $T\cap J$ is a submodule of $J$. Thus

$$
 \operatorname{length}_O T\le \operatorname{length}_O A+\operatorname{length}_O J,\qquad
 d_\lambda(T)\le d_\lambda(A)+d_\lambda(J),
$$

where $d_\lambda(M)$ is the least $k\ge0$ for which $\lambda^kM=0$ locally. At both host primes the earlier Fourier witness gives $d_\lambda(T)\ge e_\lambda\sum_v a_v$, with $e_\lambda$ the ramification index and $p^{a_v}$ the local twist order at each selected place $v$. It follows that the actual raw preimage defect and the projected-image defect cannot both vanish. At least half this valuation depth must be present in one of them. This uses the same $\theta$ and $P$ throughout. The group-order allowance for $P$ at the slope bound is compatible with the inequality. [Integral price at the slope bound](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/ThirdPlaceSeries/CutAtOfRows.lean#L18-L26) · [Fourier witness on the actual row lattice](2026-11-23-good-type-spectral-denominator.md)

For a hand check, take $\Lambda=O^2$ over $O=\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, $P(x,y)=(x+y/p^n,0)$ and $\theta_s(x,y)=(x,p^sy)$, for $0\le s\le n$. Then $Y=O\oplus p^sO$, $T\simeq O/p^nO$, $A\simeq O/p^sO$, and $J\simeq O/p^{n-s}O$. The same rational range allows every distribution of the $n$ units of length between the two terms. At $s=n$ even the projected map on integral inputs is integral. The abstract projector on $\Lambda$ still has denominator $p^n$.

## Unresolved

Determining either defect separately for the actual series span requires its saturation in the full row lattice or integral valuations of its projected generators. The class-stabilizer weights and the base character trace contribute zero to that comparison at $q,d$. The proven equality `X₃` = `Im` concerns a different pair of lattices from the two used to define the later local Hecke length.
