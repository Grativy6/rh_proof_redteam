# Which dyadic class survives in the cyclotomic tower?

**24 November 2026.** The [two-place example](2026-11-24-dyadic-class-enters-late.md) supplies an order-two Selmer class on the zeta fields. We followed that same character into the tower the release uses to bound cohomology. It remains nonzero there. There is also a genuine finite-coefficient group whose order stabilizes and whose classes all disappear in its direct limit. The transition maps distinguish the two phenomena. ## The quadratic step

Write $C_s=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^s})$, $E=C_s^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$, $L=E(i)$ and $L_\infty=\mathbb Q(\mu_{2^\infty},\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$ with the prime conditions of the example. For $s\ge4$ the 2-power cyclotomic part is disjoint from the odd-conductor biquadratic part. Globally $L/E$ is quadratic. Locally, at either dyadic place, it is the totally ramified quadratic step missing from $C_s^+$. Both local and global Galois groups over $L$ have the same cyclotomic image $1+2^s\mathbb Z_2$. Indeed the completion at either place is $\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_{2^s})$ times the unramified quadratic extension. Adjoining further 2-power roots has the full global degree and is totally ramified there. [Base fields](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/QuadraticExtensions/ModulusAdjoinISqrtNegPrimes.lean#L23-L32) · [Released subgroup index](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/QuadraticExtensions/CompIndex.lean#L143-L161) · [Use of the quadratic extension](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/QuadraticExtensions/IsAlgClosureRat.lean#L112-L128)

For the discrete coefficient $A=(\mathbb Q_2/\mathbb Z_2)(-k)$, $k\ge1$, the unrestricted kernel of restriction from $H^1(E,A)$ to $H^1(L,A)$ is $H^1(C_2,A^{G_L})$. One can see the inflation-restriction description by subtracting a global coboundary to make a kernel cocycle vanish on $G_L$. Its values then lie in $A^{G_L}$ and it factors through $C_2$. The invariant subgroup is cyclic of order $2^{s+v_2(k)}$, since $v_2((1+2^s)^k-1)=s+v_2(k)$. The nonidentity involution acts on it by $(-1)^k$. For even $k$ its $H^1$ is the two-torsion subgroup. For odd $k$ it is the quotient by twice the group. Thus the unrestricted loss has length exactly one.

On strict Selmer classes restriction to $L$ is injective in these fields. A normalized kernel cocycle, already zero on $G_L$, is locally a coboundary at a dyadic place. Its local witness must be fixed by $G_{L_w}$. The equal cyclotomic images above give $A^{G_{L_w}}=A^{G_L}$, and the local decomposition group maps onto all of $C_2$. The same witness therefore trivializes the global cocycle. This explains why the source’s allowance of one for the unrestricted kernel is sufficient when applied to the strict group. [Index-two kernel bound](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/QuadraticExtensions/KerTwo.lean#L18-L49) · [Use on Selmer lengths](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/QuadraticExtensions/ShaTwo.lean#L11-L27)

In particular, the explicit quadratic character $\chi_\alpha$ remains nonzero on $G_L$. Its extension is split at both dyadic places of $L$ and is unramified everywhere else. It stays nontrivial after passage to $L_\infty$, since every nontrivial finite subextension of $L_\infty/L$ has full local degree at each dyadic place. The cyclotomic action on $A$ is trivial on $G_{L_\infty}$. There the cocycle is a nonzero homomorphism and every coboundary is zero. This is precisely the type of locally constant character admitted by the source’s infinite-level module, with zero on inertia and on the dyadic decomposition groups. ## A stable count whose classes do disappear

On the fixed base $\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$, the cyclotomic extension has group $\mathbb Z_2$ with generator of cyclotomic value 5. On its subgroup of index $2^m$ the twist acts by $w=5^{-k2^m}$. Put $h=2+v_2(k)+m$. Every continuous cocycle on this procyclic group with finite coefficients $A_n=A[2^n]$ is determined by its value at a generator, where any value is possible. Indeed the geometric sum of $2^j$ successive powers of $w$ has 2-adic valuation $j$, so the cocycle factors at some finite cyclic level. Coboundary values are $(w-1)A_n$. It follows that
$$H^1(\mathbb Z_2,A_n)=A_n/(w-1)A_n,\qquad |H^1|=2^{\min(n,h)}.$$
For $n\ge h$ these groups have identical orders. Under $A_n\hookrightarrow A_{n+1}$, the coordinate $a/2^n$ becomes $2a/2^{n+1}$. On the stable cyclic quotients the map is multiplication by 2, so every class dies within $h$ steps. The divisible-coefficient quotient $A/(w-1)A$ is zero, as multiplication by a nonzero 2-adic scalar is surjective there. The root hypothesis $k\ne0$ is essential. The earlier $\chi_\alpha$ lives on the absolute group of the infinite field and is visible under restriction to it. The vanishing classes are inflated from the quotient $\mathbb Z_2$. ## How many generators can the two primes introduce?

For the trivial starting character one can check the uniform rank input across varying $q,d$ without assigning a separate unknown constant to every field. In $K_\infty=\mathbb Q(\mu_{2^\infty})$, an odd prime $p$ has $[\mathbb Z_2^\times:\overline{\langle p\rangle}]$ places. The even powers of $q\equiv3\bmod8$ generate $1+8\mathbb Z_2$ topologically, since $v_2(q^2-1)=3$. For $d\equiv7\bmod16$ the corresponding valuation is 4, giving $1+16\mathbb Z_2$. The odd powers form the other coset of the even powers. Thus the exact numbers of places are **two over $q$ and four over $d$**. The finite-level computation in the script checks stabilization starting at modulus 16. [Orbit bound in the release](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/QuadraticExtensions/OrbTwo.lean#L11-L27)

There is no nontrivial quadratic extension of $K_\infty$ unramified at every finite place. Suppose $K_\infty(\sqrt a)$ were one. Adjoining all conjugate square roots gives a Galois pro-2 extension $N/\mathbb Q$, finite over $K_\infty$ because $a$ is algebraic over $\mathbb Q$. It is unramified away from 2, and $N/K_\infty$ remains unramified at every place. The closed inertia subgroup $I$ at 2 in $\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbb Q)$ has trivial intersection with $\operatorname{Gal}(N/K_\infty)$. If the latter group were nontrivial, $I$ would be proper. A proper closed subgroup of a pro-2 group has proper image in some finite 2-group quotient, hence lies in an open normal subgroup of index 2. The corresponding quadratic field over $\mathbb Q$ would be unramified at every finite place, impossible by the quadratic discriminant formula.

Now allow ramification only over $q,d$. A quadratic character is determined by its restrictions to inertia at those six places, since a difference vanishing on them would be unramified everywhere. Each inertia group has at most one independent mod-2 character because wild inertia at these odd primes is pro-$p$ and the 2-primary tame quotient is procyclic. The space of characters has dimension at most six. The maximal pro-2 Galois group with this ramification therefore has at most six topological generators by the Frattini quotient. Adjoining $\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d}$ gives an index-four subgroup. These two extensions are unramified above 2. Every unramified quadratic extension of $L_\infty$ belongs to this same pro-2 tower by taking its conjugates over $K_\infty$. The Schreier bound on the subgroup is $1+4(6-1)=21$. Thus its unramified quadratic character space has dimension at most 21, including the subspace split at 2. The class above shows that the latter has dimension at least one. This counting argument concerns the zeta branch specifically.
