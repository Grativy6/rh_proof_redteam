# Two tame inertia entries detect the marked-point obstruction

**24 November 2026.** At the actual nonsplit twist-296 point, Frobenius at 2309 and 3463 reads both independent dual obstructions on a diagonal matrix. This gives a second way to measure the two unknown ordinary quadratic forms. It also puts a sharp limit on an easier search. If tame ramification is allowed at these two primes, every odd tangent lifts through $k[t]/(t^{1154})$. Reaching that length in the relaxed search gives no evidence about which slopes survive at the released level. The very next coefficient tests the original quadratic condition.

## How long the auxiliary condition can imitate a lift

Here only the away condition at $q_1,q_2$ is relaxed. Work in the normalized nonsplit marked deformation problem, over complete local $k$-algebras in this paragraph. Wild inertia at either prime has trivial image under a lift of the unramified residual point because the congruence kernel is pro-$p$. Let $T_i$ be the image of a generator of tame $p$-inertia and $G_i$ the image of arithmetic Frobenius, so $G_iT_iG_i^{-1}=T_i^{q_i}$.

The ordinary matrix $G_i^2$ has three distinct residual eigenvalues $z(q_i)^2,1,z(q_i)^{-2}$. Lift its eigenspaces. In that basis $T_i$ is diagonal. If its off-diagonal entries have order at least $n$, the corresponding entries of $T_i^{q_i^2}$ equal $q_i^2$ times them modulo order $n+1$. Since $q_i^2=1$ in $k$ and the Frobenius eigenvalue ratios differ from one, comparison with conjugation by $G_i^2$ raises the order again. Completeness finishes the argument. Thus $T_i^{q_i^2-1}=1$. Both $q_i+1$ and $(q_i-1)/p$ are prime to $p$, so $T_i^p=1$ and $G_i$ fixes $T_i$ under conjugation. The outer Frobenius exchanges the extreme eigenspaces and dualizes. The middle entry, equal to its inverse and congruent to one, is one. Locally the tame diagonal therefore has the form
$$ T_i=\operatorname{diag}(1+d_i,1,(1+d_i)^{-1}),\qquad d_i^p=0 . $$
The eigenprojector separates its first entry functorially. Conversely any lift of the Frobenius matrix admits this tame inertia when $d_i^p=0$, since the unit-separated eigenspaces of its square lift. Thus this equation gives the only extra local failure of lifting. Across a square-zero last layer its scalar entry is $t_i/2$.

Allowing this one tangent line at each prime pairs against the old dual Selmer group by $M$. Poitou–Tate and its nonzero determinant leave zero new dual Selmer and preserve the original two-dimensional global tangent space. All old tangents were unramified at both primes. For a small extension, once the two local tame equations permit lifts, there is a global lift. Indeed the kernel of localization of the global second cohomology is dual to everywhere locally trivial dual classes, which vanish here. After lifting globally, the obstruction to fitting the prescribed local tangent cosets pairs with the modified dual Selmer group, which also vanishes. The original conditions at the other places are liftable in this normalized problem. This argument uses the small-extension obstruction for the indicated finite Galois modules. Write $S=k[[a,b]]$ for a minimal presentation in the two odd directions and lift the two tame entries to $D_1,D_2\in S$. They have order at least two since global first-order inertia vanishes. If $I$ is the ideal of the relaxed marked problem, then
$$ R_{\rm tame}=S/(D_1^p,D_2^p),\qquad R_{\rm original}=S/(D_1,D_2). $$
For the first equality, $J=(D_1^p,D_2^p)\subset I$. Over $S/(\mathfrak m I+J)$ the universal representation over $S/I$ lifts. Locally the chosen lifts of $d_i$ have vanishing $p$th powers independently of their square-zero ambiguity, and the remaining global obstruction just vanished. This argument can be made on Artinian truncations, then passed to the limit by completeness and the finiteness of the residue field. The resulting section of the square-zero extension changes coordinate lifts only by $I/(\mathfrak m I+J)$. Since $I\subset\mathfrak m^2$, substitution into any relation makes this change zero. Hence $I=\mathfrak m I+J$, and Nakayama gives $I=J$. The second equality sets both inertia generators to the identity, precisely the released away condition at these unramified primes. For any slope over a finite residue extension and any $N\le2p$, substitution $a=\alpha t,b=\beta t$ automatically kills the relaxed equations modulo $t^N$. At $N=2p+1$ their first possible coefficients are $D_{i,2}(\alpha,\beta)^p$, where $D_{i,2}$ is the quadratic part. Higher terms in the chosen path cannot change either coefficient. Thus a nonzero slope extends to $k'[t]/(t^{1155})$ with these tame allowances exactly when that slope passes the original two quadratic tests. This is the earlier [ordinary trace-cube criterion](2026-11-24-two-raw-traces-on-the-released-subgroup.md#a-numerical-milestone-for-the-next-obstruction) in different coordinates.

The two rings have the same reduced ring. If the original characteristic-$p$ fiber has finite length, the relaxed one has exactly $p^2=332929$ times that length, by the monomial basis over $k[[a^p,b^p]]$. In a characteristic-$p$ domain the two tame parameters already vanish. This identifies both the finite artifacts that can be pruned and the domain test that still matters.

## Unresolved

The two tame coordinates have been identified, and their change-of-basis matrix has been measured. Their actual quadratic coefficients on the cubic tangent, the quartic tangent and their sum remain unknown. Neither a long relaxed jet below the calculated threshold nor an auxiliary Selmer lift establishes a failure at the original domain endpoint.
