# One arithmetic map, two infinite places

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, at Christopher D. Pang's request.** The arithmetic map used for the lattice-height estimate is the same bilinear pairing that supplies the retained rows of the analytic frame. At that step a change of coordinates preserves rank; a separate equality of Hermitian metrics preserves the measured height. We found a family of integral maps whose frames become arbitrarily close to ideal at the chosen embedding while their arithmetic heights grow without bound. This refutes recovery of the height from the one-place frame, even with increasingly strict tolerances. Our attempted counterexample fails the release's separate all-place estimate. [Previous frame test with Gemini Neon and DARK](2026-11-23-gemini-tail-review.md) · [Source revision](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/tree/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005)

## The hidden height on paper

Put $u=(3-\sqrt5)/2$. Its other real conjugate is $u^{-1}=(3+\sqrt5)/2$. These are positive algebraic units with product one. Over $K=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt5)$ give the standard lattices and their duals the ordinary Hermitian metrics at both complex places. For $n\ge1$, take the integral map

$$
B_n(z)(y_1,y_2)=z(u^n y_1+y_2).
$$

At every finite place the column $(u^n,1)^t$ is primitive. It is the second column of an integral change of basis with determinant one. At the embedding with $u<1$ it approaches the coordinate axis. Its squared operator norm there is $1+u^{2n}$. At the other place that squared norm is $1+u^{-2n}$. Averaging the two logarithms, exactly as the release defines `htMap`, gives

$$
h(B_n)=\tfrac12\log\!\left[(1+u^{2n})(1+u^{-2n})\right]
      =\log(u^n+u^{-n}).
$$

The integers $L_n=u^n+u^{-n}$ satisfy $L_0=2$, $L_1=3$, $L_{n+2}=3L_{n+1}-L_n$. They tend to infinity. For $n=10$ the arithmetic height is **$\log 15127$**, while the squared norm at the chosen place is less than $1+1/15126^2$. The input lattices, metrics and finite elementary divisors stay fixed. The maps differ. ### The effect survives the full set of angle checks

To test whether oversampling or an increasingly strict frame would exclude the example, fix an odd prime $p\ge23$, and work over $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,\zeta_p)$. Exactly half of its complex places restrict to each embedding of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$. For each $a,b\in\mathbb F_p$ let $c_{ab}$ be the integral column with $j$-th coordinate $\zeta_p^{a j^2+bj}$. Add the $p$ coordinate columns. Use weight $1/p$ for each of the $p^2$ non-coordinate columns and weight one for each axis. The resulting normalized columns $v_i$ form $p+1$ orthonormal bases. Vectors from distinct bases have squared overlap $1/p$. To check this, square the absolute value of the quadratic sum and write the two indices as $k+h,k$. For distinct quadratic coefficients the sum over $k$ vanishes whenever $h\ne0$, leaving $p$. The linear sums within one basis vanish by the finite geometric series.

Replace every integral column $c_i$ by $A_nc_i$, where $A_n=I+u^n E_{12}$. This common integral matrix and its inverse preserve the whole lattice. All the maps still take the rank-one source lattice into the dual target lattice. The second axis has exactly the growing height above. Its norms depend only on $\sqrt5$, so extension to $K$ does not change their mean.

At the preferred embedding, even with the identity coordinate charts, put $t=u^n$, $d=2t+t^2$. Then $\|A_n^*A_n-I\|\le d$. Every diagonal squared defect is at most $e_0=d^2$. Every distinct squared overlap is at most

$$
(p^{-1/2}+d)^2=(1+\eta_0)/p,\qquad \eta_0=2\sqrt p\,d+p d^2.
$$

Take $D_*=p$, all input ranks one, the restriction matrices equal to the identity and zero discarded rows or columns. At $p=23$, $s_g=1/24$ gives $D_*/s_g=552$, exactly the total input rank. At $n=10$, both $\eta_0$ and $\sqrt{e_0}$ are below $1/48$. This meets the numerical frame hypotheses including $s_g\le1/20$. A common invertible chart $Y'=A_n$ at that place would even make the displayed columns exactly the original columns. The small-error test already works with $Y'=I$. This example tests a tail as well. For any sufficiently large $x$, fixed $C_H,\varepsilon>0$, and any prescribed finite height ceiling $H(x)$, choose $p+1\ge x^{C_H}$ and then increase $n$. With $s_g=x^{-C_H}$ the rank supply holds. The two local errors can be made smaller than $\exp(-x^{\varepsilon/8})$ and than any prescribed positive multiple of $s_g$, while $h(B_n)>H(x)$. ## Where the actual map comes from

Write $P_u(\phi,F)$ for the complex bilinear pairing on a word's coefficient space and the row space. The constructor `bkOf` asks whether **every** value on the prescribed rational coordinates, for **every** word, lies in the image of the coefficient field. If so, it takes the unique preimage under the embedding and proves bilinearity there. If this test fails even once, the constructor returns zero for every word. For instance, over $\mathbb Q(i)$ the two one-dimensional rules $xy$ and $\sqrt2\,xy$ trigger the fallback together, including for the word with value one at $(1,1)$. In the released construction `Bk` is assigned `bkOf`, and the row metric is explicitly assembled at the distinguished and remaining places. [The exact fallback and its quantifiers](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ExistsDivStageOfPk.lean#L17-L48) · [Construction of the lattice and map](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ExistsDivStageOfPk.lean#L76-L96) · [Metric and rational-value clauses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ExistsDivStageOfPk.lean#L110-L127)

The live rationality argument has other inputs. The row data identify the period on rational ambient vectors with the embedding of a specified field-valued column. The row Gram products take values in the field. The quotient matrix is surjective. Given a rational lift of a row, one can subtract its orthogonal projection onto the rational kernel: Gram–Schmidt here divides by nonzero field elements, so the lift stays rational. Evaluating the specified column on that lift supplies the required field element. Injectivity of the embedding fixes the value of `Bk`. The proof also identifies the coordinate period before quotienting by checking vanishing on the actual quotient kernel. The fallback example fails the period premise on its second word. The unnormalized Fock squared norms are respectively $6+2=8$ and $2+2=4$; each monomial is weighted by the product of its exponent factorials. The target extremal $x^2$ has squared norm 2. To check the maximum over all inputs, write a source polynomial as $A_1s+A_2v+A_3w$ with cubic $A_i(x,y,z)$. The defining column invariance is exactly $xA_1+yA_2+zA_3=0$. In row multidegree $m=(a,b,2)$, $a+b=2$, let $c_i$ be the coefficient belonging to the $i$th second-column variable, omitting $i$ when $m_i=0$. The condition is $\sum c_i=0$. The normalized source squared norm on this block is

$$
\frac{a!b!\,2!}{8}\sum_{i:m_i>0}\frac{|c_i|^2}{m_i}
\ \ge\ \frac{a!b!}{4}|c_3|^2 .
$$

Indeed the first two coefficients sum to $-c_3$, their available multiplicities sum to 2, and $m_3=2$. The normalized target contribution is $2a!b!|c_3|^2/T^2$. Distinct row multidegrees are orthogonal; all other blocks are killed. Equality is attained on $\xi$, whose image is $2t^{-2}x^2$. Hence the **exact squared operator norm is $8/T^2$**. The exact block calculation gives that same nonzero squared singular value three times in a 15-dimensional source. No finite truncation enters. ## Does an average sneak back in upstream?

The kernel estimates extend the coefficient field to $M$ and apply a bound for **every** embedding $\rho:M\to\mathbb C$. Over a fixed infinite place $v$ of the original totally complex field $K$, the weights are constant on the embeddings above $v$ and zero elsewhere. Each fiber has $2[M:K]$ embeddings. Every one of its bounds applies to the same operator at $v$. The proof can therefore average the log bounds *within that fiber*. For a coupled last pair of factors it averages their sum at the same embedding. It then accounts for the full mean over all places. In the shear example the other place necessarily receives its own weight. [Which embeddings receive weight](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/FinrankEqTwoMul.lean#L28-L50) · [The same operator and the coupled factors](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/PrimeWordLocalConstants.lean#L107-L134) · [The common coefficient field](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/KernelHalfChainNumbersAnyLevelPrime.lean#L274-L285) · [Use by the kernel-height calculation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/KernelHalfChainNumbersAnyLevelPrime.lean#L315-L330)

## Unresolved

The same-map comparison passes this audit, conditional on the stated arithmetic input for the selected family. The hidden-height example disproves the frame-only inference for unbounded scales. The zero fallback, the allowed coordinate changes and the tested terminal map yield no counterexample to the released implication. The next distinct test is whether the conjugated **normalized theta coefficients of the actual matrix entries**, including the field-of-definition condition, earn the bounds used for each lift of each place. The coefficient field explicitly contains the standard vacuum coefficients; the release separately specifies which further jets must lie in the smaller field.
