# The corrected test can leave the selected component

**23 November 2026.** A trace projection can select a nonzero primitive component while its uncorrected test pairs to **exactly zero** after the release's power substitution. The coefficient correction recovers the pairing. It can also give the corrected test a primitive component *outside* the selected rational type. This is an explicit instance of a change in mathematical property between the projected vector and the final test. We followed both objects through the relevant calls. ## Two factorials give different answers

Work over a $p$-adic coefficient ring with $w^2=p$, $p\ge5$. The Fock pairing in the release makes distinct monomials orthogonal and gives the monomial of exponent $\alpha$ squared pairing $\prod_i\alpha_i!$. On two lower letters, one vector and one covector in dimension $n$, put $m_i=x_i y_i$ and $C=\sum_i m_i$. Under the diagonal $GL_n$ action these are the matrix units $E_{ii}$ and the identity. Write $e$ for the projection onto the invariant line. Then $e(m_i)=C/n$. [Pairing and valuation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ObjPol.lean#L45-L64) · [Projection formula](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/RootWeights/TraceProjectionIsInvProj.lean#L150-L165)

Take $P=p^s$ with $s\ge1$, $n=P$, and a second power $Q$ of $p$. Set the low digit of the first vector coordinate to one and all the others to zero. On this block the power substitution and label multiplication in the source send
$$
 m_i\longmapsto L(m_i)=X_1 X_i^P Y_i^Q.
$$
The common factorial is $P!Q!$. After dividing it out, the squared pairings are $P+1$ for $i=1$ and $1$ for the other indices. Both are $p$-adic units. The correction in the release uses their **values**. Its single-coordinate factor is $\gamma(P,\rho,b)=(\rho+Pb)!/(\rho!\,b!\,(P!)^b)$. [Assembling the high-degree monomials](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/DiagonalSupport/TwiceValuationFactorialProduct.lean#L23-L38) · [Factorial unit and common factor](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/DiagonalSupport/PickDefs.lean#L11-L29)

Choose $y=m_1-(P+1)m_2$ and take the high-degree input $L(y)$. Both lower monomials have unit coefficients and $e y=-C$ is primitive too. The choice $e$ therefore satisfies the valuation requirement $v_{\rm BB}(ey)\le v_{\rm BB}(y)$ with equality. A coefficient of $m_1$ on the projected vector is a unit. Scaling the projected monomial test $e(m_1)$ by $P$ gives the integral test $C$. Any further common nonzero scalar multiplies the pairings below by the same scalar. [Component choice](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/TwistedSubstitution/JointEIntList.lean#L263-L281) · [Selecting a coefficient of the projected vector](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/DiagonalSupport/PickCore.lean#L47-L77)

| Test | Lower pairing with $y$ | High pairing with $L(y)$, divided by $P!Q!$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Pure trace $C$ | $-P$ | $0$ |
| Corrected trace $C'=(P+1)^{-1}m_1+\sum_{i>1}m_i$ | $(P+1)^{-1}-(P+1)$ | $-P$ |

In particular, **every multiple of the pure trace test** gives zero after assembly on this input. No scalar adjustment of that test can recover the nonzero value. On the displayed block the exact inverse correction is the diagonal operator $1-\frac{P}{P+1}x_1\partial_{x_1}$.

Now project the corrected test. One gets
$$
  eC'=\frac{P}{P+1}C,\qquad
  (1-e)C'=-\frac{P}{P+1}m_1+\frac{1}{P+1}C.
$$
Although $C'-C$ is divisible by $P$, the traceless part has a unit coefficient. The trace projection has divided by $n=P$. Any normalized coefficient approximation to $C'$ modulo $p^c$ with $c>s$ still has this primitive traceless part and gives a high pairing of valuation exactly $s$ after the common factorial is removed. At $c=s$, the uncorrected multiplier already agrees with the exact multiplier to that precision on this block, and the displayed pairing can vanish.

The cancellation can also occur with dimension smaller than the prime. Use two coordinates, degree $b=p-1$ in each of the two letters, and the invariant polynomial $C_b=(x_1y_1+x_2y_2)^b$. Each pure diagonal monomial $m_i=(x_i y_i)^b$ projects to $C_b/p$, as follows either from the trace on $\operatorname{Sym}^b$ or from $\langle C_b,C_b\rangle=p(b!)^2$. With $P=Q=p$ and the same low digit, the ratio of the two high factorials is $R=1+pb$. The input $m_1-Rm_2$ has primitive projection $-bC_b$. Every pure invariant test still has high pairing zero.

## A linear allowance in the precision can be needed

Consider $h$ separate vector–covector pairs with the same diagonal $GL_n$ action. For the vector letter in pair $j$, allow any exponent $P_j=p^{g_j}$, $g_j\ge1$, and use the low digit $\rho_j=P_j/p$ on coordinate one. Each lower letter has degree one. On the diagonal entries the relative factor on coordinate one is
$$
 u_j=\binom{P_j+\rho_j}{\rho_j}
     =\prod_{i=1}^{\rho_j}(1+P_j/i)\equiv 1+p\pmod{p^2}.
$$
Only $i=\rho_j$ contributes at order one. Take $v_p(n)=1$. For the input $\bigotimes_j(m_{j,1}-u_jm_{j,2})$, projecting successively onto the invariants after each completed pair gives a unit times $\bigotimes_j C_j$. The exact normalized correction of this latter test is
$$
  \bigotimes_{j=1}^h\bigl(C_j+(u_j^{-1}-1)m_{j,1}\bigr).
$$

Conjugate all matrices by the integral shear $I+E_{1n}$. The coefficient on $\bigotimes_j E_{1n}$ has valuation exactly $h$, since, up to sign, it is $\prod_j(u_j^{-1}-1)$. This coefficient has first torus weight $h$. If $c>h$, any coefficientwise approximation modulo $p^c$ retains it after the same integral change of basis. Thus the first-weight bound on the corrected test must allow at least $h$ above the previously selected zero weight. Taking $h=c-1$ proves a linear lower bound for this signed-word and factorial test. The argument concerns the local polynomial operations. Realization by the weight and source conditions of the entire global family is a separate question. ## Where the corrected object goes

The released normal test requires integral coefficients, the specified degrees, and a first-weight upper bound of $\kappa_1+4c$ at each prefix. The source proves the bound $\kappa_1$ for the *uncorrected* projected monomial. It applies a binomial polynomial of total degree at most $4c$ to obtain the test that is actually used. Both strict and inclusive prefix bounds downstream retain the $4c$ term. The plane estimate covers this change with an explicit margin. Write $s$ for the available separation at the relevant letter and $m_*$ for the basic plane cost. The threshold includes a term $\kappa_1+m_*+s$, and the plane lower bound subtracts $s/2+60c$. Thus it leaves at least $\kappa_1+m_*+4c$ once $s\ge128c$, since $128/2-60=4$. For an even letter the proof uses the current position and the normal bound after that letter. For an odd letter it uses the next position and the normal bound before that step. It proves that the next position exists and checks the margin there too. The parameter estimate actually supplies $s\ge100r^2c$ with $r\ge2$, before weakening it to $128c$. ## Unresolved

This correction needs room to move between types. The local membership estimate carries the full $4c$ allowance. Proposition 5 exports a bound for the pairing, and the exact normal label is local to the construction. Global realization of our input remains open. A further independent burden is the plane test. For the actual space `XX` and the restricted test space `YY`, the proof must supply a test with a nonzero pairing and a bounded valuation. Ambient nondegeneracy alone does not imply that restricted assertion.
