# Read the arithmetic window at three points

**24 November 2026.** For the zeta branch, the release chooses two primes subject to congruences and then requires simultaneous analytic bounds for three nonprincipal quadratic twists. A finite difference of the actual prime ramp eliminates its unknown $L'/L(1)$ term exactly. Three consecutive readings recover the sign at a single prime power. Three more widely spaced readings measure the imbalance in the four possible sign pairs on an interval. We certified two legal inputs to `WinPair` with the same scale and first prime and with opposite signs at a specified prime in the running window. ## The sign survives the sum

For a nonprincipal character write
$$G_\chi(y)=\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)(y/n-1)+b_\chi y,\qquad b_\chi=L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi).$$
The endpoint term $n=y$ is zero. For $h>0$ let $\Delta_h^2G_\chi(y)=G_\chi(y+2h)-2G_\chi(y+h)+G_\chi(y)$. Every term with $n\le y$ is affine across the three evaluations, as is the full $b_\chi y$. Terms with $n\ge y+2h$ also contribute zero. The exact result is
$$\Delta_h^2G_\chi(y)=
 \sum_{y<n<y+2h}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\frac{\min(n-y,y+2h-n)}{n}.$$
In particular, for an integer $m\ge2$,
$$m\,[G_\chi(m+1)-2G_\chi(m)+G_\chi(m-1)]=\Lambda(m)\chi(m).$$
For the principal ramp the known $-y\log y$ correction must first be added back. Its other correction is affine. This identity shows why changing the prime label at an isolated integer changes a whole segment of the ramp. [Definitions of the principal and nonprincipal ramps](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/CharFamily.lean#L10-L22)

There is an exact interpolation check as well. Between consecutive integers all raw ramps are affine. In a positive-weight family let $a$ be the sum of the principal weights. For $y=m+t$, $m\ge1$, $0\le t\le1$, the combined real ramp exceeds its linear interpolation between $m$ and $m+1$ by
$$a[(1-t)m\log m+t(m+1)\log(m+1)-y\log y].$$
This lies between zero and $a\,t(1-t)/(2m)$ since $(y\log y)''=1/y$. Thus observing all consecutive integers in the original variable has an explicit error bound. Sampling at a fixed spacing in $\log y$ is a different observation, for which the [oscillatory model](2026-11-24-log-window-tail.md) gives a failure.

## Two actual congruence windows

Take $\epsilon=1/55$, $Y=2^{40}$, $q=3$, and set
$$r=2^{521}-1,\quad c_j=2^{197}+21+24j,\quad d_j=2c_jr+1.$$
Both $j=138$ and $j=1078$ give primes. In the release’s `WinPair`, $z=Y^{\epsilon/5}=2^{8/55}$ satisfies $1.10<z<1.11$. The first prime has $z/2<\log3<z$ and is $3\bmod8$. Each $d_j$ is $7\bmod16$, and $3\mid d_j+1$. The small odd-prime clause is empty at this particular $z$. Both $d_j$ are between $2^{719}$ and $2^{720}$, which puts $\log d_j$ strictly between $z(\log Y)^2/2$ and $z(\log Y)^2$. For example $0.69<\log2<0.70$ gives a lower limit under 436, both actual logs between 496 and 505, and an upper limit over 837. These are legal examples of the released arithmetic predicate at a common scale. [Exact size and divisibility conditions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L27-L38)

The [certificate](check_prime_window_readouts.py) first iterates $x\mapsto x^2-2$ from 4 and checks zero after 519 steps modulo $r$. Here is why that certifies primality. For any prime $p\mid r$, one has $p\ne2,3$. In $\mathbb F_{p^2}$ take $v=2+\sqrt3$, of inverse $2-\sqrt3$. The recurrence computes $v^{2^k}+v^{-2^k}$. The final zero forces $v^{2^{520}}=-1$, so $v$ has order $2^{521}$. Frobenius sends $v$ to $v$ or $v^{-1}$, hence this order divides $p-1$ or $p+1$. Thus $p\ge r$. For each $d=d_j$ the certificate verifies $2^{d-1}=1\bmod d$ and $\gcd(2^{(d-1)/r}-1,d)=1$. Every prime divisor of $d$ must then be $1\bmod r$ by the multiplicative order of 2. Since $r>\sqrt d$, each $d$ is prime. The logarithmic bounds in the script use rational upper and lower series bounds.

Now take $m=2^{40}+15=1099511627791$. Trial division through its square root certifies it is prime. The three consecutive arguments $m-1,m,m+1$ all lie in $[Y,Y^2]$. Euler’s criterion gives $(m/d_{138})=-1$ and $(m/d_{1078})=+1$. The observed curvatures for the corresponding primitive quadratic characters are exactly $-\log m/m$ and $+\log m/m$. The analytic correction and every other prime power cancel. This instantiates the `i=0,j=1,M=1` twist with each prime. The two choices have the same $Y,\epsilon,q$, residue conditions and admissible size intervals. Their complete prime $d$ distinguishes them.

The conclusion `PairEx` asks for a good pair after an unspecified threshold. `PairGood` includes the analytic bounds in addition to `WinPair`. A sign difference of this size is allowed by either bound. These examples verify that congruence and size data alone leave genuine arithmetic variation at the actual observation scale. ## A finite test of a proposed good pair

Put $\alpha=1/2+\epsilon/2$. On the $M=1$ branch consider $\chi_q,\chi_d,\chi_q\chi_d$. For odd distinct primes these are the primitive Legendre characters and their primitive product. Suppose $Y\le y<3y\le X$ in a candidate good window and assume $q,d>3y$ so every sampled integer is prime to both. For $a,b\in\{-1,1\}$, let $M_{ab}$ be the sum of $\Lambda(n)\min(n-y,3y-n)/n$ over $y<n<3y$ with signs $(a,b)$. Let $M$ be the total. For each nontrivial twist, its signed sum is $\Delta_y^2 G_\chi(y)$, hence its absolute value is bounded by
$$B_y=(1+2\,2^\alpha+3^\alpha)y^\alpha$$
under `PairGood.bounds`. Expanding $(1+a\chi_q(n))(1+b\chi_d(n))/4$ proves
$$|M_{ab}-M/4|\le3B_y/4.$$
This criterion needs only primes, prime powers and their signs in a finite interval. A violation excludes that particular claimed good pair. To disprove `PairEx` one would have to rule out all eligible pairs for some fixed parameters at arbitrarily late scales. The local sign probes above need only $m$ prime to $qd$. The four-bin bound has the stronger $q,d>3y$ hypothesis, which the eventual construction has room to impose. ## Do exceptional primes get different witnesses?

The mean-square input for the selection stage bounds, at every $t\in[Y,X]$ and for **every** bounded coefficient sequence $\kappa$,
$$\sum_{p\in P}|S_p(t)|^2\le C t\log(t)|P|,\quad
 S_p(t)=\sum_{n\le Z}\Lambda(n)\min(1,t/n)\kappa(n)(n/p).$$
An exceptional prime may choose its own worst $t$. Here is an independent derivation of the simultaneous bound for exactly these sums. Away from the finitely many integer breakpoints,
$$tS'_p(t)=S_p(t;\kappa\,1_{n>t}).$$
The new sequence is common to all primes and is still bounded by one. Thus $\sum_p|S'_p(t)|^2\le C|P|\log(t)/t$. Each $S_p$ is continuous and piecewise affine, so its variation is the integral of that derivative. On $T\le t\le\min(2T,X)$ the fundamental theorem and Cauchy–Schwarz give
$$\sum_p\sup_t |S_p(t)|^2
 \le C T|P|\,[\,2(1+\log2)\log T+(\log2)^2\,].$$
This follows by bounding each supremum by $2|S_p(T)|^2+2T\int |S'_p(t)|^2dt$ and integrating $\log t/t$. No prime has to choose the same maximizer as another. [The sum and its quantifiers](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/MeanSquareBounds/PrimeSetTwoBounds.lean#L13-L27) · [Derivative as a masked sum](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/MeanSquareBounds/BadShare.lean#L76-L98) · [The source’s use of that mask](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/MeanSquareBounds/BadShare.lean#L146-L177)

Take $T=2^jY$, $Y\ge2$. If a prime exceeds $t^{1/2+\tau}/2$ anywhere in this block, its squared supremum exceeds $T^{1+2\tau}/4$. For $\tau>0$ put $r_0=2^{-2\tau}$. Summing the proportions given by Markov’s inequality bounds the proportion bad anywhere in $[Y,X]$ by
$$4C Y^{-2\tau}\left[
 \frac{2(1+\log2)\log Y+(\log2)^2}{1-r_0}
 +\frac{2(1+\log2)\log2\,r_0}{(1-r_0)^2}\right].$$
Empty intervals or prime sets give the corresponding cardinal inequality immediately. For fixed $C,\tau$ the displayed bound tends to zero independently of $X,Z$ and of the prime-specific bad ordinates. This verifies the quantifier passage conditional on the exact `MeanSq` input. ## Where the forced small-prime bias goes

In the late windows $d\equiv-1\bmod p$ for every odd $p\le z$ and for $p=q$, while $d\equiv7\bmod16$. Quadratic reciprocity gives $(p/d)=1$ for every such odd prime, and $(2/d)=1$. Thus $\chi_d$ agrees with the principal character on every integer up to $z$. An averaging argument that gave cancellation on these forced columns would fail. The released mean-square estimate includes even prime powers and powers of primes dividing the fixed progression modulus in an explicit sum `RR`. [Forced congruences](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L27-L38) · [Columns set aside](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/JacobiCharacterSums/MeanSquareHypWIff.lean#L48-L55) · [Mean-square split](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/MeanSquareBounds/MeanSqAbs.lean#L9-L40) · [Use in the uniform estimate](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/MeanSquareBounds/meanSqK.lean#L51-L73)

Their size can be checked directly. At a fixed $p$ the sum of their weights over all powers is at most $\log y+2\log p$. The powers up to $y$ contribute at most $\log y$, and the remaining geometric tail contributes at most $2\log p$. For the forced small primes this costs at most $z(\log y+2\log z)$. For all even powers together the sum is at most $\sum_{m\le\sqrt y}\Lambda(m)+y\sum_{m>\sqrt y}\Lambda(m)/m^2=O(\sqrt y)$ by Chebyshev’s elementary upper bound and partial summation. At the actual $z=Y^{\epsilon/5}$, $y\ge Y$ and $\epsilon<1/52$, the forced small-prime contribution is $o(\sqrt y)$. Large primes dividing the modulus lie above the chosen truncation in the application. This accounts for the fully correlated columns within the square-root scale. The other correlations still require estimates. [Coprimality, exceptional label and cutoff](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/MeanSquareBounds/meanSqK.lean#L105-L129)

For odd squarefree $s$ prime to the fixed modulus $m_0$, the CRT classes $b\equiv a_0\bmod m_0$ that are units modulo $m_0s$ run freely over $(\mathbb Z/s)^\times$. Their Jacobi weights sum to zero when $s>1$. If a real exceptional character modulo $m_0s$ occurs, its fixed-modulus factor is constant on this fiber. The remaining weighted sum is zero unless its restriction to $(\mathbb Z/s)^\times$ equals the Jacobi character there, by character orthogonality. In that exceptional case its absolute value is the size of the fiber. The script checks a conductor-21 example at $m_0=240$ where the resonance survives at $s=7$ and cancels at $s=77$.
