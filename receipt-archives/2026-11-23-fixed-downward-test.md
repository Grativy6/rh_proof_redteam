# Can two forms with the same label cancel in the fixed downward test?

**23 November 2026.** This follows the two questions in the [downward residue calculation](2026-11-23-downward-residue.md). For the nonzero level-place factor we checked the real character and the finite support. For generation from the fixed test, we found a full-rank matrix that every translate of that test misses. We then checked the additional hypothesis that makes this missing matrix invisible to the pairing at a split type prime.

## The real character and the same vector

If the two factors in the compact integral both transform by the character $z\mapsto z$ of $U(1)$, their product integrates to zero. Pairing $z$ with $\bar z$ gives $1$ with probability Haar measure. The two products agree at the identity.

In the release let $y$ be the proposed nonzero vector in the kernel, including a difference of two forms with the same Hecke label. The construction chooses a nonzero coefficient vector $w$ in an irreducible unitary representation $\rho$ of the compact real group $K$. The oscillator coefficient is a positive scalar times $c(b)=\langle w,\rho(b)w\rangle$. The scalar component of $y$ contributes a positive scalar times $\overline{c(b)}$. Each assertion follows by polarizing a squared norm and applying Schur's lemma to an invariant Hermitian form. On the automorphic side the vector-valued form transforms under right translation by $\rho(b)^{-1}$. Its positive norm comes from the injective realization of this very $y$. On the oscillator side the intertwiner has nonzero value at the chosen $w$. With probability Haar measure the integral of $|c(b)|^2$ is $\|w\|^4/\dim\rho$. To see this, average the rank-one operator $v\mapsto w\langle w,v\rangle$ over conjugation by $K$. The result commutes with $\rho$ and has trace $\|w\|^2$. Evaluate the resulting scalar operator against $w$. Any other positive Haar normalization rescales the integral. This argument applies to $y=y_1-y_2$ whenever that difference is nonzero.

## A finite-place test that isolates a subgroup

At an active split level place the auxiliary hyperbolic space supplies $m=2t$ rows for $n=r+t$ columns. Since $t\ge r+5$, the integral frame $E_0=\binom{I_n}{0}$ exists. The finite datum requires the identity on its top block and zero below, with trivial character. The split idempotent chooses one of the two local factors. Over a local field with integers $O$, uniformizer $\pi$ and residue cardinality $q$, put $f=1_{E_0+\pi M_{m,n}(O)}$. If both $f(X)$ and $f(Xg)$ are nonzero, the top square blocks of $X$ and $Xg$ lie in $I_n+\pi M_n(O)$. Taking their ratio shows $g\in I_n+\pi M_n(O)$. Conversely such a $g$ preserves the ball and its determinant has absolute value one. In the normalized right matrix action the coefficient is exactly $q^{-mn}1_{I_n+\pi M_n(O)}(g)$ when the unit matrix lattice has volume one. A character trivial on this subgroup preserves the answer.

Losing a row can make this false. In residue characteristic $5$, take $m=n-1$ and retain the first $n-1$ standard rows. Right multiplication by $\mathrm{diag}(1,\ldots,1,2)$ preserves the ball, although its reduction is nonidentity. The release uses a full frame. Its global argument proves that the small vector is fixed on the subgroup and has zero matrix coefficient off it within the integration domain. On the subgroup the height factor is one. The surviving constant is the small vector's norm squared times the real integral above, with the chosen Haar normalizations. ## A full-rank point that all translates miss

The generation argument uses a different rectangular matrix, now with $r+t$ rows and $r$ columns in adjoint coordinates. Set $U_0=k^r\oplus0$ over $k=O/\pi O$, and $E=\binom{I_r}{0}$. Right translates of the selected support are the sets

$$
 \mathcal U_g=\{X:Xg\in E+\pi M_{r+t,r}(O)\},\qquad g\in\mathrm{GL}_r(K_v),
$$

where $K_v$ is the local field. For each $X$ let $S=(\mathrm{im}_{K_v}X)\cap O^{r+t}$, its saturated column lattice. Then $X$ lies in some $\mathcal U_g$ exactly when $X$ has full column rank and the reduction $\bar S$ equals $U_0$. Indeed a frame congruent to $E$ has this property. Conversely, lift the standard basis of $U_0$ to a basis of $S$, then use a right change of basis to make those vectors the columns of $Xg$. [Adjoint coordinates and the fixed test](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/DatumVec.lean#L109-L120) · [Right translates in the release](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/InertiaEigenvalues/LocalGenerationThetaFunAtTypePrime.lean#L45-L57)

For example, let $\pi=3$, $r=2$ and $t\ge1$. Take $X_1=3[e_1,e_2]$ and $X_2=3[e_1,e_3]$. Both have rank two over $\mathbb Q_3$, and both reduce to zero. The first belongs to $\mathcal U_{(1/3)I}$. The second belongs to no translate. The indicator of $3M_{2+t,2}(\mathbb Z_3)$ is compactly supported and invariant under every invertible integral change of rows. It takes value $1$ at $X_2$, so translates of the fixed frame cannot span even all the level-invariant tests.

The missing point matters if it can contribute to the pairing on a good type. The release first removes the images of subgroup averages whose pairings vanish on these types. The remaining test $\Phi$ has zero average over each subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_r(k)$ fixing a hyperplane pointwise, acting on its top rows. It is invariant under row operations that reduce to the identity on $U_0$ pointwise. The character in the original average cancels under the chosen lifts. Take $X$ outside the translates. Then $\bar S\cap U_0$ is proper in $U_0$. Choose a hyperplane $H$ containing this intersection. For $a$ fixing $H$ pointwise, its action on $U_0$ extends to an invertible map acting identically on $\bar S$. Extend this map to the rest of the residue space. Since $S$ is a saturated direct summand, lift the result to an invertible integral matrix $A$ fixing $S$ pointwise, using a basis adapted to $S$. For any lift $\tilde a$ of $a$, the matrix $\mathrm{diag}(\tilde a,I)A^{-1}$ reduces to the identity on $U_0$ pointwise. Its action on $X$ equals $\mathrm{diag}(\tilde a,I)X$. Invariance makes every term of the hyperplane average equal $\Phi(X)$. The average can vanish only if $\Phi(X)=0$. At $X_2$ in the example, the subgroup has six elements, so this is $6\Phi(X_2)=0$. ## Can the finite set of translates depend on the observer?

For one test function the preceding result leaves it supported on disjoint cells $\mathcal U_g$ up to repetitions. The permitted row operations are transitive on each cell. Complete $Xg\equiv E\bmod\pi$ by the final standard columns to get one such operation. A locally constant compactly supported scalar test is therefore a finite combination of the cell indicators.

The proof uses a whole family $\Phi_\ell$, obtained by applying different linear functionals to one theta vector. Even in a fixed compact matrix lattice, the indicators of $\mathcal U_{\pi^{-j}I}$ for $j=1,2,\ldots$ need infinitely many distinct cells between them. A common support bound alone would fail. The source obtains one integer $N$ giving both a support bound $\pi^{-N}M(O)$ and a period lattice $\pi^NM(O)$ for **every** functional. [The common level before varying the functional](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/InertiaEigenvalues/LocalGenerationThetaFunAtTypePrime.lean#L550-L575)

There is a direct finite bound. If any functional is nonzero on $X$ in this family, its test is nonzero on the whole ball $X+\pi^NM(O)$. Every matrix in that ball must therefore lie in some cell. Let $B$ be the top square block of $X$. All its elementary-divisor valuations must be strictly less than $N$. Otherwise an integral perturbation scaled by $\pi^N$ can cancel a diagonal entry in Smith coordinates and make the top block singular. Thus $\pi^NB^{-1}\in\pi M_r(O)$. If $X\in\mathcal U_g$ then $Bg\equiv I\bmod\pi$, so $\pi^N g\in\pi M_r(O)$ too. The whole ball is in that **same** cell. The common support lies in only $q^{2N(r+t)r}$ residue classes modulo this period lattice. This gives one finite set of cells for all the functionals.
