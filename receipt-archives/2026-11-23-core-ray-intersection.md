# Which part of the ray field still has to be added?

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Christopher D. Pang’s continuing audit of the arithmetic maps.** The earlier calculation found an unramified extension of degree $h(K)\varphi(M)/2$ inside the ray field used for the deep cover. The incoming core already contains much of it. On sufficiently large selected trivial-character windows, for the actual core with the release's `KsExact` identification and its specified cover construction, the *whole extension from the core to the cover coefficient field* is unramified at every finite prime. Its degree is one of
$$
 4\cdot 15^{s-1},\qquad 8\cdot 15^{s-1},
$$
where $s=\lfloor3x^\varepsilon/\log3\rfloor$. In particular the odd part is exactly $15^{s-1}$. The root discriminant is unchanged. The symmetric projection back has that full extension degree as its exact lattice denominator. One factor of two remains unresolved. ## An upper bound from the ray-group kernel

The primes 3 and 5 split in $K$ on these windows. The four primes in $m_*$ are distinct and exceed 5. The field $E$ on the trivial-character branch is the compositum of a real dyadic cyclotomic field with $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-q})$ and $K$, so it is unramified at 3 and 5 once $q,d>5$. For $K$ the units are $\{\pm1\}$, and only $1$ is congruent to 1 modulo $m_0$. The ray class exact sequence and the Chinese remainder theorem identify
$$
 \mathrm{Gal}(R_M/R_{m_0}) \simeq
 (\mathcal O_K/t\mathcal O_K)^\times \simeq U_t\times U_t,\qquad
 U_j=(\mathbb Z/j\mathbb Z)^\times .
$$
Choose one prime from each conjugate pair over 3 and 5 for the first factor. The action on $\zeta_t$ is given by $(a,b)\mapsto ab$, up to inverting the Artin convention. This follows from the norm of an ideal on cyclotomic roots, or from the local units at the two split places. The subgroup fixing $R_{m_0}(\zeta_t)$ is exactly $\{(a,a^{-1}):a\in U_t\}$. It follows that $v=[N:A]$ divides $|U_t|=8\cdot15^{s-1}$. ## Why the core cannot absorb the odd part

Fix complex conjugation $c$ in the absolute Galois group of $\mathbb Q$. We place the factors of the *exact* list inside a compositum $BC$ of finite normal fields with the following properties.

* The inertia at 5 fixes $B$. The wild inertia at 3 fixes $B$.
* On $C$, complex conjugation commutes with every Galois automorphism.

For $B$ take the normal closure of the compositum of $E$, its $m_*$-ray field and the discriminant-root and torsion field in the list. The first two are unramified at 3 and 5. The source identifies the discriminant-root field with the 12-torsion field of an elliptic curve over $K(j)$ having complex multiplication by $\mathcal O_K$. Here $K(j)$ is the Hilbert class field of $K$, unramified at both primes. The curve has good reduction off $d$, and the release also proves that prime divisors of its torsion-field discriminant divide $6d$. Thus all inertia at 5 fixes its normal closure. At 3 we need the action on the *full* 12-torsion. Over $K(j)$ every CM endomorphism is defined. Indeed its scalar on the tangent line lies in $K$, and in characteristic zero the differential distinguishes endomorphisms. The field of 12-torsion over $K(j)$ consequently has group inside $(\mathcal O_K/12\mathcal O_K)^\times$, since the complex period description makes this torsion a free module of rank one over the residue ring. Both 2 and 3 split in $K$ ($-d\equiv1\bmod8$ for 2). The unit group has order $\varphi(12)^2=16$. The Hilbert field is normal over $\mathbb Q$. Taking the compositum of its conjugate torsion fields therefore still gives a 2-extension over the Hilbert field. Its ramification at 3 is tame. Normal closures and composita preserve the asserted inertia bounds. [Complex multiplication on torsion and its field of definition](https://noter.math.ku.dk/phd21fc.pdf) · [Complex multiplication and the period lattice](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CM.pdf)

For $C$, a useful criterion is that every conjugate $w'$ of a generator $w\ne0$ satisfy $w'\overline{w'}=b$ with the **same rational** $b>0$. Then $c(w')=b/w'$, so $c$ commutes with every automorphism on the normal closure. Apply the criterion to each generator separately. Roots of unity satisfy it. For the type-norm roots, a class representative ideal $J$ has $J^h=(k)$, and
$$
 z^h=\prod_i\phi_i(k)^P\overline{\phi_i(k)}^{\,Q}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z'\overline{z'}=\mathrm N(J)^{P+Q}.
$$
The embeddings $\phi_i$ run once over the conjugate pairs. Each Galois conjugation permutes those pairs, and $|N_{E/\mathbb Q}k|=\mathrm N(J)^h$. This calculation works for fractional ideals as well. For the infinite lists of Hecke values the same rational-modulus property must be checked on each value, rather than inferred from inclusion in the larger Kummer field. Write $\mathfrak p$ for the unramified input prime for the character, $Q_{\mathfrak p}$ for its absolute norm, and $r_0$ for the character's twist integer. The release chooses $h>0$ and $(k)=\mathfrak p^h$. For $z=\chi^{\rm tw}(r_0/2,\mathfrak p)$ its power identity expresses $z^h$ as a product with $i$-th factor
$$
  \phi_i(k)^{-n_i}\big(\phi_i(k)\overline{\phi_i(k)}\big)^{(n_i-r_0)/2}.
$$
The parity hypothesis makes the exponents integral. For any conjugate, multiplying this product by its complex conjugate gives $|N_{E/\mathbb Q}k|^{-r_0}=Q_{\mathfrak p}^{-hr_0}$. Thus $z'\overline{z'}=Q_{\mathfrak p}^{-r_0}\in\mathbb Q$ at every conjugate. The source applies this with $r_0=0$ or $1$ to the character-field factors, and the remaining finite character values are roots of unity. The field generated by all the specified values is finite, so finitely many suffice for the normal closure $C$. This proves $A\subseteq BC$. ## Two local units and their commutator

At a chosen prime of $K$ over 5, lift the local unit $a\in U_{5^s}$ to an absolute inertia element $\sigma$ having its ray action on $R_M$. At a chosen prime over 3 do this with $a$ in $1+3\mathbb Z/3^s\mathbb Z$ and take $\sigma$ in wild inertia. Local reciprocity maps inertia onto the units' image, and wild inertia onto the principal units' image for these odd split primes. By construction $\sigma$ fixes $B$. Normality of $B$ gives the same for $c\sigma c^{-1}$. The commutator $c\sigma c^{-1}\sigma^{-1}$ also fixes $C$ by centrality there. Hence it fixes $A$.

On $R_M/K$ the two split places are exchanged by $c$. The commutator acts by $(a^{-1},a)$ in the corresponding two local unit factors and by 1 at the other modulus primes. These elements are distinct in the ray group since the only global unit trivial modulo $m_0$ is 1. Restriction to $R_M$ therefore embeds a subgroup of order
$$
 |U_{5^s}|\cdot|1+3\mathbb Z/3^s\mathbb Z|
   =4\cdot5^{s-1}3^{s-1}
$$
in $\mathrm{Gal}(N/A)$. Together with the upper bound, this leaves one bit. More precisely $v=4\cdot15^{s-1}$ if $R_{3m_0}\subseteq A$ and $v=8\cdot15^{s-1}$ otherwise. Restriction of the anti-diagonal subgroup to $R_{3m_0}$ tests the residue $a\bmod3$, because the frame at $m_0$ is fixed. Its kernel is exactly the subgroup just exhibited. This gives a concrete test for the remaining factor.

## Consequence for the reverse map

Since $N/A$ is unramified at finite places, the trace image of $\mathcal O_N$ is all of $\mathcal O_A$, including at primes over 3 and 5. Locally this follows by lifting the surjective trace of the finite residue-field extension. The averaged trace therefore has image $v^{-1}\mathcal O_A$ and is contractive on each complex fibre with its maximum norm. Conversely every nonzero integral $A$-linear map $N\to A$ has maximum complex operator norm at least $v$, by the trace-dual bound with unit relative different. Now $v$ is forced to grow on the actual selected cores. The numerical field-size estimate still counts $\log v$ through the degree. ## Unresolved

The remaining factor of two is the containment of $R_{3m_0}$ in the exact core. The ramification and conjugation arguments above settle the entire odd part and the full factor from the units at 5. They yield no contradiction to the field-size allowance. A counterexample to the released proof still requires a call that treats the averaged trace as integral on all inputs or transports a sharper norm to an integral reverse map. The [finite checks](check_deep_level_traces.py) enumerate the relevant local-unit kernels through depth four. The field and inertia arguments above are Hearthline's paper derivation and self-review.
