# The field can outrun the small-section count

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Christopher D. Pang's public audit of the arithmetic maps.** On the same projective line with the same smooth positive metric, the logarithmic count of small sections divided by field degree and by $m(m+1)$ tends to $\log 2+1/4$ over every fixed number field. At $m=\lfloor\log x\rfloor$ it tends to **zero** over the fields generated by the host and the roots of unity in the release's actual $K_s$ on its zeta windows. A finite determinant on the same module retains exactly the same value under every field extension. This gives a paper counterexample to interchanging field and tensor-power limits. The release's inspected count takes the fixed-field limit, and its finite-power lattice inequality measures a determinant. ## Which fields the windows require

Write $E$ for the host, $K_s$ for the auxiliary coefficient field, and $M=Q_S qd$ for the host's conductor modulus. The construction chooses four distinct primes $g_0,g_1,p_{\rm de},p'_{\rm de}$ congruent to $1\bmod M$. Each splits completely in $E$. The coefficient record puts a primitive $p$-th root of unity in $K_s$ for each of the four. For $g_i$ it actually requires order $g_i(g_i-1)$. [Four primes and distinctness](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataHost.lean#L69-L84) · [Level congruences](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/RootOfUnityEightQMemCoeffGaloisSubfield.lean#L148-L157) · [Complete splitting](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/NcardPrimesOverOfStabilizer.lean#L73-L108) · [Roots required in the coefficient field](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/RootOfUnityEightQMemCoeffGaloisSubfield.lean#L279-L289)

Consequently $[K_s:E]\ge\prod_p(p-1)\ge M^4$. To check the product, adjoin the four roots successively. Before adjoining $\zeta_p$, the accumulated field is unramified at $p$. The earlier roots have conductors prime to $p$. The field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$ is totally ramified there, so the intersection is $\mathbb Q$ and the next degree is $p-1$. This strengthens the window lower bound without using the host class number. [Prime-power cyclotomic fields](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/154Page/handouts/cycint.pdf)

Fix either type prime $g=g_i$. Let $C=|\mu(K_s)|$ count all roots of unity in the actual coefficient field, and set $L_x=E(\mu(K_s))=E(\zeta_C)$. This identifies a specific intermediate field for **every** choice of coefficient data. The same argument works with the roots of unity in the outer field `LL`. With the positive $\varepsilon$ and $J\ge1$ fixed by the supply theorem, the selected windows satisfy

$$
\log g\ \ge\ \log d\ >\tfrac12 Y^{\varepsilon/5}(\log Y)^2,
\qquad x^{1/J}\le Y\le x.
$$

In particular $\log g/\log x\to\infty$. These orders and inequalities are the only information about the windows used in the count. ## One metric and two limits

Over any number field $F$ take $X=\mathbb P^1_{\mathcal O_F}$ and $\mathcal O(1)$ with its Fubini–Study norm divided by 2. It has positive curvature and the two coordinate sections are strictly small and generate it. Its sections in degree $m$ are integral binary forms $f=\sum_{j=0}^m a_j X^jY^{m-j}$. Call a section small if, at every embedding $\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ and every point, it satisfies

$$
|\sigma f(X,Y)|\ \le\ 2^m(|X|^2+|Y|^2)^{m/2}.
$$

Let $s_F(m)=\log\#\{f\text{ small}\}/([F:\mathbb Q]\,m(m+1))$ for $m\ge1$. This is the release's all-points, all-embeddings section condition on this model. The normalizing factor $m(m+1)$ is asymptotic to $m^2$ in arithmetic dimension two. [Arithmetic variety](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/ExistsIsClosedImmersionGenericPoint.lean#L578-L590) · [Small sections](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/ExistsIsClosedImmersionGenericPoint.lean#L763-L769) · [Moriwaki's fixed-variety arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel formula, sections 3–4](https://www.mathsoc.jp/section/algebra/algsymp_past/algsymp07_files/moriwaki.pdf)

For $t=j/m$, write $H(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t)$, with $H(0)=H(1)=0$. Set $R_j=2^m\exp(mH(t)/2)$. A small section has $|\sigma a_j|\le R_j$. Indeed, the Cauchy coefficient estimate on $|X/Y|=\sqrt{j/(m-j)}$ gives precisely this value. At the two endpoints evaluate at a coordinate point. Conversely each normalized monomial has maximum $e^{-mH(t)/2}$ on the unit sphere. The triangle inequality therefore gives a small section as soon as $|\sigma a_j|\le R_j/(m+1)$ for every $j,\sigma$.

Let $B_F(R)$ count integers in $\mathcal O_F$ with all conjugates of modulus at most $R$. At fixed $F$ the Minkowski lattice in the real and complex embeddings gives $\log B_F(R)=[F:\mathbb Q]\log R+O_F(1)$ for $R\ge1$. One can sandwich the dilated product of intervals and discs between unions of fundamental cells using the fixed diameter of a cell. Applying this bound to the two coefficient boxes above costs at most $\log(m+1)/m+O_F(1/m)$ after normalization. The Riemann sum tends to $\int_0^1 H(t)\,dt=1/2$, so

$$
\lim_{m\to\infty}s_F(m)=\log 2+\tfrac14\qquad\text{for every fixed }F.
$$

## An upper bound uniform in the rest of the field

Write $C=g^a C'$, with $g\nmid C'$ and $a\ge1$. Put $F_0=E(\zeta_{C'})$, $d_0=[F_0:\mathbb Q]$, $N=g^a$, $h=N/g$, and $L=F_0(\zeta_N)=L_x$. The field $F_0$ is unramified at $g$. Hence $[L:F_0]=h(g-1)$ and $\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_{F_0}[\zeta_N]$. For the latter equality the two field discriminants are coprime. Equivalently at $g$ the shifted cyclotomic polynomial remains Eisenstein over each unramified completion, and away from $g$ the cyclotomic extension is étale. [Integral rings of prime-power cyclotomic fields](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/154Page/handouts/cycint.pdf) · [Integral composita with coprime discriminants, Corollary 1.5](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/154Page/handouts/etaledisc.pdf)

Write an integer $z\in\mathcal O_L$ as $\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}b_j\zeta_N^j$, with $b_j\in\mathcal O_{F_0}$. Suppose its conjugates have modulus at most $R$. Fix an embedding of $F_0$ and denote the complex coefficient values by $v_j$. All primitive Fourier values are conjugates over that embedding. Subtract the contribution of frequencies divisible by $g$ from the sum over all frequencies. Ordinary finite Fourier orthogonality yields

$$
\sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}
 \left|\sum_j v_j\zeta_N^{ju}\right|^2
 =h\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}\ \sum_{\substack{i<j\\ i,j\equiv r\bmod h}}|v_i-v_j|^2
 \le h(g-1)R^2 .
$$

For $b\in F_0$ put $\|b\|^2=d_0^{-1}\sum_\sigma|\sigma b|^2$. Average the preceding identity over $\sigma$. For each block of $g$ indices congruent modulo $h$, choose an anchor $j_r$ minimizing the sum of squared distances to it. The average over all anchors in that block is $2/g$ times the sum over unordered pairs. The sum of the minimum costs over all blocks is at most $2(g-1)R^2/g$. Subtract $b_{j_r}$ from every coefficient in its block. The value of $z$ is preserved because the powers of $\zeta_N$ in that block sum to zero.

By the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and the integral norm, every nonzero integer of $F_0$ has $\|b\|\ge1$. The adjusted representation therefore has at most $k=\lfloor 2R^2\rfloor$ nonzero coefficients, each of norm at most $\sqrt{2}R$. Balls of radius $1/2$ around distinct lattice points are disjoint, so there are at most $(1+2\sqrt{2}R)^{d_0}$ possibilities per coefficient. Counting supports gives, when $k<N$,

$$
 B_L(R)\ \le\ (k+1)\big[N(1+2\sqrt{2}R)^{d_0}\big]^k .
$$

The estimate keeps its dependence on $g$, including when further roots enlarge $C'$ or $a$. Conjugate mean squares have a classical role in controlling short sums of roots of unity. The block identity here allows coefficients in an arbitrary field unramified at $g$. [Loxton, *On the maximum modulus of cyclotomic integers*](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa22/aa2216.pdf)

Every small section has all $m+1$ coefficients in the box with $R=2^{3m/2}$, since $H(t)\le\log2$. Its normalized logarithmic count is bounded above by

$$
\frac{\log(k+1)+k\log N+k d_0\log(1+2\sqrt2 R)}
     {m\,d_0\,h(g-1)} .
$$

Here $(\log N)/h=a\log g/g^{a-1}\le\log g$ for odd $g$. Thus for any fixed $0<\eta<1/3$, the bound tends to zero when $m\to\infty$ and $m\le(1/3-\eta)\log_2g$. Indeed $k\le2g^{1-3\eta}$, and the remaining factors cost at most a constant times $1+\log g$. Zero is a lower bound since the zero section is present. In particular
$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}s_{L_x}(\lfloor\log x\rfloor)=0
$$
along the selected windows. Even the cutoff beyond which $s_{L_x}(m)$ is always at least half its fixed-field limit must eventually exceed $\lfloor(1/3-\eta)\log_2g\rfloor$. This is a lower bound on how long the change of field can delay the fixed-field asymptotic on this model.

## The determinant on the same module

On $\mathcal O_F^{m+1}$ put the symmetric-power Hermitian metric $\|f\|_\sigma^2=4^{-m}\sum_j|\sigma a_j|^2/\binom mj$. It bounds the Fubini–Study sup norm by Cauchy–Schwarz and the binomial theorem. The monomial frame is an integral basis, so the index in the release's degree formula is one. The diagonal Gram matrices give exactly

$$
\widehat{\deg}_F=m(m+1)\log 2+\tfrac12\sum_{j=0}^m\log\binom mj .
$$

This degree is already divided by $[F:\mathbb Q]$. At every finite $m$ it is the same over $E$, $L_x$ and any further extension. After division by $m(m+1)$ it tends to $\log2+1/4$ on both paths. For an elementary error estimate, the $j$-th term is a mode of the binomial distribution with success probability $j/m$, so $mH(j/m)-\log(m+1)\le\log\binom mj\le mH(j/m)$. In particular each monomial stays short, the full span survives, and a large change in the section count can be invisible to the determinant. ## Unresolved

This removes the fixed-field count as a justification for a uniform count on the moving models. The calculation uses a projective line and the full roots-of-unity subfield of $K_s$. Counts on the release's flag component and counts after adjoining the remaining arithmetic coefficients require their own bounds. The finite determinant step traced here survives the test. A counterexample to the released inference would have to reach the claimed short families or a use that demands more than their span and degree.

Hearthline derived and self-reviewed the window application and the two limits on paper. The [standard-library checker](check_cyclotomic_counts.py) verifies the Fourier identity in six prime-power bases, the house counts $11,61,301$ in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$ at radii $1,2,3$, and all 89 small linear sections in the Gaussian example. The infinite statements follow from the bounds above.
