# Which of the 27 possible directions survive the local test?

**24 November 2026.** This supplies the finite end of the [real dyadic tower calculation](2026-11-24-one-strict-class-in-the-entire-real-dyadic-tower.md). At conductor at most 256 there are 27 nontrivial character and twist residues with a zero 577-divisor test. All 27 yield genuine odd ideal-class directions in the indicated fields. Exactly one yields a strict Galois class. We check the principal character separately. ## Count what the relative class number allows

Write $p=577$, $C=2^s$, $f=Cp$ and $m=C/4$. At these conductors $m\mid576$, so the primitive even characters have values in $\mathbb Z_{577}$. Label them by odd $b\bmod m$ using $\bar\psi_b(5)=5^{(576/m)b}$. The program sums the actual upper half of the units modulo $f$, using $\tilde a=a^{577}\bmod577^2$ to lift nonzero mod-577 values to Teichmüller units. For every even $j$ modulo 576 the multiplier is computed modulo $577^2$.

For each candidate let $B$ be the field cut out by the Teichmüller lift of $\lambda=\bar\psi_b\omega^{j+1}$. It is cyclic CM of degree $D=\operatorname{lcm}(m,576/\gcd(576,j+1))$. Its odd characters are the odd powers $\lambda^i$ with $1\le i<D$. Each remains primitive at $C$, and its exponent at $p$ is odd, so all have conductor $Cp$. The upper-half sum with lifted inverse weights equals $2B_{1,\lambda^{-i}}$. Indeed in $\lfloor2a/f\rfloor=(2a-(2a\bmod f))/f$, pairing $a$ with $a+f/2\bmod f$ cancels the remainder term because the primitive dyadic character changes sign.

By the relative class number formula, $v_p(h(B)/h(B^+))$ is the sum of those Bernoulli valuations. The powers of two and Hasse unit index are units at 577. The field does not contain $\mu_{577}$, since every odd character of $B$ is nontrivial on dyadic inertia while $\omega$ is trivial there. Each vanishing residue in this cyclic subgroup contributes exactly one. As a second check the program forms $2\sum_{a=1}^f a\tilde\lambda^{-i}(a)$ on the full interval modulo $577^3$, divides out $577$, and divides by $C$ modulo $577^2$. This computes $2B_1$ independently for every entry in the table of 27 possible lines. The code counts the distinct odd powers using $(b i\bmod m,\ (j+1)i-1\bmod576)$. ## Construct one class for each allowed direction

Take either splitting prime $r_A=1920257$ or $r_B=2363393$, both $1\bmod(256p)$. Choose the least primitive root modulo $r$ and the character $\chi$ taking it to $\zeta_f$. Put $J=\sum_{x\ne0,1}\chi^{-1}(x(1-x))$. At a compatible place over $r$, $v(\sigma_aJ)=\lfloor2a/f\rfloor$ for unit $0<a<f$. Reduction of the finite binomial sum gives zero or a unit, and $J\bar J=r$ limits a positive valuation to one. For each $\lambda$ form $\beta=\prod_a(\sigma_a J)^{w(a)}$ with $1\le w(a)\le576$ representing $\lambda(a)^{-1}$. The weights are constant on $\ker\lambda$, so $\beta\in B$. Its mod-$p$ power class transforms by $\lambda$. Every divisor exponent is divisible by $p$ because the carry vanishes, independently of which of the two splitting primes we used. [Jacobi divisor proof](2026-11-24-why-the-classes-stop-at-twenty-five.md#a-divisor-identity-at-a-splitting-prime)

At $p$, use $Y$ of order 256 with residue field $\mathbb F_{577}[Y]/(Y^4-20)$ and $X=\log(1+(\zeta_p-1))$ in the truncated expansion. For conductor $C$ use $Y^{256/C}$. For units $v\bmod C$ let $J_v(X)$ denote the Jacobi sum with dyadic root raised to $v$ and ramified root replaced by $\exp X$. For $k=j+1$ reduced to the odd residue from 1 to 575, compute
$$D_\lambda=\sum_v \bar\psi_b(v)^{-1}\,[X^k]\log (J_v(X)/J_v(0)).$$
The sum over ramified conjugates kills the other degrees. Its surviving scalar, combined with raising $\beta$ to $577^4-1$ to remove the residue-field unit, is one modulo $577$. A nonzero result obstructs a local $577$th power. In fact the $p$th power of any principal unit in this field is 1 modulo $(\zeta_p-1)^p$, by the binomial theorem. All digits used for the 27 candidates have $k<576$. The code obtains the logarithm coefficients from $X J'_v/J_v$. For the first prime it also compares two ways of counting the Jacobi summands, using the logarithm of $x(1-x)$ and the sum of the logarithms of its two factors. At **each** splitting prime, 26 of the 27 candidate digits are nonzero. The only zero is $(s,b,j)=(8,39,296)$. On that line the global power-residue test for $\beta_{r_A}$ at $r_B$ is $1497311$, of exact order 577. Thus all 27 lines have a radicand which is a global non-power. In their odd components the unit kernel is zero, as proved in the [tower calculation](2026-11-24-one-strict-class-in-the-entire-real-dyadic-tower.md#which-finite-calculation-can-rule-out-a-character). Their divided divisors give nonzero order-577 ideal classes.

For a given cyclic field $B$, take the constructed classes for all relevant odd powers of its defining character. The corresponding subfields embed in $B$ with degree prime to $p$, so extension of ideals preserves each class. Distinct characters give linearly independent vectors. Their number equals the computed valuation of $h(B)/h(B^+)$. Since 2 is invertible on the $p$-part, this exhausts the minus class group at $p$ and gives dimension one for each of these characters. An invariant class after extension from $B$ to the full cyclotomic field descends by the norm divided by the prime-to-$p$ index. Therefore the indicated radicand spans the possible residual line. Every candidate twist here has $j\not\equiv0\bmod576$, so its coefficient has no residual invariants on $p$-inertia. A residual local obstruction cannot disappear on passage to the divisible coefficients. This proves strict vanishing for all 26 obstructed lines.

For a concrete example take $C=64,b=3,j=96$. Its cyclic field has degree 576. The four contributing powers of $\lambda$ have $i=1,121,385,551$. Their labels are $(3,96),(11,216),(3,480),(5,454)$. The four upper-half multipliers divided by 577 are $5,194,42,536\bmod577$. The local digits at $r_A$ are $93,425,522,311$, all nonzero in $\mathbb F_{577}$. Thus this field has an actual minus class group with 577-primary part $(\mathbb Z/577)^4$, and all four reflected strict tests give zero. All four characters inflate to the actual zeta hosts. Their twists are even, $576$ divides none of the four doubled twists, and 577 is outside the trivial outer character’s exceptional set. They therefore occur in Case I. ## The principal character

For the remaining trivial dyadic label a strict order-$p$ class has a residual preimage locally zero at $p$. Even when local invariants occur, the global and local $p$-adic cyclotomic images over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb Q_p$ are both $\mathbb Z_p^\times$. Their invariants on $A_j$ agree, so a local connecting class from $H^0/p$ can be subtracted globally. At other primes inertia acts trivially on the divisible module, leaving the residual preimage unramified. Over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$ it gives an unramified character of the ideal class group with coefficient action $\omega^{-j}$. For even $j$ this factors through the plus part. The prime-to-$p$ degree $p-1$ preserves a nonzero residual class under restriction.

We verified the vanishing of this plus part at $p=577$ by the criterion of Buhler and Harvey. The Bernoulli recurrence modulo 577 gives just the irregular index $k=52$ among $2,4,\ldots,574$. At the prime $r=2309$ choose $z=16$ of order 577. The product of $(z^c-z^{-c})^{c^{576-52}}$ over $1\le c\le288$ is $631\bmod2309$, whose fourth power is $973\ne1$. This certifies $577\nmid h(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{577})^+)$ and settles the trivial label for all positive even twists.
