# What the zero chain buys

**23 November 2026.** The Alpöge–Claude release actually sets every map in an auxiliary chain to zero and uses the result twice to certify divisibility. We followed the resulting statement to a specific quotient of lattices. We also found a short proof that spending this certificate on any nonzero arithmetic period would give a contradiction. The period calculation supplies a separate comparison for the chain it uses. [Construction and both uses of zero](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GroupLattices/AdmissibleFamilyNoKernel.lean#L166-L210) · [Call that exports the result](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GroupLattices/AdmissibleFamilyNoKernel.lean#L224-L239)

## Hold the source fixed and change the output

Fix a word, a place over a prime $p$, the level lattices, and the operators $Y_t$ attached to the selected Frobenius orbits. Write $C$ for the chain of $m$ maps out of the starting space. The source's test `vKge` asks whether $C(F)$ belongs to the indicated power of the endpoint lattice. With at least one step, replacing every map by zero sends every $F$ to zero. Zero belongs to every such power, including arbitrarily high positive powers. The release invokes this observation for both the generator estimate and the estimate on the selected sublattice. [Definition of the output test](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L414-L430) · [The two certificates](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GroupLattices/AdmissibleFamilyNoKernel.lean#L193-L210)

Now expand the source sublattice `NP`. Write $M$ for the original lattice, $V$ for the selected orbit set, $s_t$ for the nonnegative integer price of omitting orbit $t$, and $T$ for the nonnegative integer cut coming from $\tau$. The displayed product has the order specified by the source. Its terms are the $m$-fold iterates of the local orbit operators.

$$
 N=\left(\sum_{A\subseteq V}p^{\sum_{t\in V\setminus A}s_t}
           \left(\prod_{t\in A}Y_t^m\right)M\right)
      \cap p^T M\cap V_{\rm ram}.
$$

Every symbol on the right is preserved by `zeroCh`. In particular $Y_t$ acts on the starting space and comes from the place assigned to $t$. The replaced maps belong to $C$. This proves equality of the **submodules themselves** for the two chains. The quotient $(M\cap V_{\rm ram})/N$ therefore has exactly the same length. [Orbit products, submodule and quotient](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L453-L479) · [Fields retained by the replacement](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GroupLattices/AdmissibleFamilyNoKernel.lean#L166-L171)

For a hand calculation take $M=\mathbb Z_5^2$, one orbit, $Y=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$, $s=3$, $T=1$ and $V_{\rm ram}=M$. Then $N=5\mathbb Z_5e_1\oplus125\mathbb Z_5e_2$ has quotient length four. Take the input $5e_1$. A chain of identities followed by its first-coordinate functional gives $5$, of valuation one. A chain of zero maps gives zero at the same input and passes the divisibility test at every threshold. The example isolates precisely the information that the unchanged index cannot supply.

## What would break on a nonzero period

Fix the release's arithmetic data at an eligible place $w$, with $m\ge1$, and take the tuple $\mu=\mathrm{muAt}(w)$. Its use and its exemption from the excluded primes for that tuple are supplied at the actual call. Fix a source vector $\varphi$ and a target $F$ in the prescribed integral lattice and target subspace. Suppose their complex period $P$ is nonzero. Keep $\varphi,F,w,\mu$ fixed as the real threshold $s$ varies. [Word, tuple, admissible vectors and local call](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GroupLattices/AdmissibleAtGeneratorPlaces.lean#L171-L195)

If the zero chain could supply the comparison `CmpV` at this input, it would give the arithmetic column estimate for **every** $s$. With the release's period integrality and stabilizer hypotheses, `period_geTU` would give $y_s$ in its number field, with

$$
 \iota(y_s)=P,\qquad y_s=0\quad\text{or}\quad
 v_w(y_s)\ge e_w\bigl(s-v_p(D_\Gamma)\bigr).
$$

Injectivity of the fixed embedding $\iota$ forces every $y_s$ to be the same nonzero element $y$. Its valuation is a finite integer. Since the ramification index $e_w$ is positive, take $s=v_p(D_\Gamma)+(v_w(y)+1)/e_w$. The alleged comparison gives $v_w(y)\ge v_w(y)+1$. This rules out the substitution even though the arithmetic witness is allowed to depend on $s$. ## A prime whose selected orbit set is empty

For a candidate $p$, the release empties the selected set when $\Sigma_p>x^{-\epsilon}$. Equality passes the gate. At a rejected candidate the source lattice becomes $M\cap V_{\rm ram}$, its length cost is zero, and the baseline gain terms from unserved orbits still enter the formula. The target slots omitted at a low inert prime appear in a separate loss estimate. [Candidates and the strict rejection test](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L279-L303) · [Costs, baseline gain and target slots](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L341-L364) · [Exact empty-set lattice](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/MultisetValuation/StabilizerCount.lean#L392-L421) · [Charge for omitted slots](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/LogarithmicSums/DfcHigh.lean#L175-L200)

Put $\theta=x^{-\epsilon}$ and $R=\{p\text{ candidate}:\Sigma_p>\theta\}$. For the fixed selected weights, the inequality `WReq` bounds the sum of $(\log p/p)(2\,{\bf1}_{p\in R}+4\Sigma_p)$ by $x^{-9}$. As all $\Sigma_p$ are nonnegative, dropping the terms outside $R$ gives

$$
  \sum_{p\in R}\frac{\log p}{p}\ \le\ \frac{x^{-9}}{2+4\theta}.
$$

At each such prime the omitted amount requested per slot and lift is at most $3a/p$, where $a$ is the scale parameter. Thus the threshold rejection alone can remove at most $3m|TT|a\,x^{-9}/(2+4\theta)$ of logarithmically weighted requests. Other exclusions, including small Frobenius order and forbidden prime ranges, carry their own estimates. This argument makes no independence assumption across primes.
