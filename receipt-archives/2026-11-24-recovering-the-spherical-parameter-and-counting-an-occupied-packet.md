# Recovering the inert spherical parameter and counting an occupied packet

**24 November 2026.** Christopher Daniel Pang's PAL and APCI asked what an observation of an unlabelled family can certify. Hearthline applied that question to the Alpöge–Claude release's exact list of reciprocal Satake parameters. The paper derives an integral inverse on the invariant coordinates, then matches the separate global count against the work of Labesse, Mœglin–Waldspurger and Ramakrishnan. Those authors supply the global representation theorems. ## The inverse on the unlabelled parameters

Let $n=2m+\delta$ with $\delta\in\{0,1\}$. At a spherical inert place the release's map sends $m$ nonzero parameters $z_i$ to the multiset of $z_i,z_i^{-1}$ and $\delta$ copies of $1$. Its own equivalence permutes the $z_i$ and independently inverts them. Write $t_i=z_i+z_i^{-1}$ and $e_j=e_j(t_1,\dots,t_m)$ with $e_0=1$. The observed characteristic polynomial is
$$P(X)=(X-1)^\delta \prod_{i=1}^m(X^2-t_iX+1).$$
[Map and equivalence](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/LineOppositeParabolicUnitaryLeviSectionEq.lean#L867-L878) · [Inert place of the base change](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/GaloisCohomology₂/BaseChangeDefiniteUnitary.lean#L145-L163) · [Comparison on complex spherical parameters](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/BcParamEqOfSatUEq.lean#L46-L62)

**Theorem.** Over every commutative coefficient ring $R$, the coefficients of $P$ generate the full ring of invariant Laurent polynomials in the invertible variables $z_i$ under these permutations and inversions. The first $m$ coefficients following the leading term of $P/(X-1)^\delta$ recover the $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ by a triangular formula with diagonal entries $\pm1$.

**Proof.** In one Laurent variable, invariance under inversion pairs the coefficients of $z^k$ and $z^{-k}$. The sums $z^k+z^{-k}$ are integral polynomials in $t=z+z^{-1}$ by the recurrence $s_0=2,s_1=t,s_{k+1}=t s_k-s_{k-1}$. Distinct powers of $t$ have distinct highest Laurent terms with leading coefficient one. Iterating in the variables gives $R[t_1,\ldots,t_m]$ as the ring fixed by the independent inversions. The elementary symmetric polynomials generate its permutation invariants over $R$ by the usual leading-monomial subtraction. These arguments use no averaging or division by the group order.

Divide by the known monic factor $(X-1)^\delta$, and let $c_j$ be the coefficient of $X^{2m-j}$ in the quotient. A term of defect $j$ selects $j-2a$ linear coefficients and $a$ constant coefficients. After choosing the linear factors there are $\binom{m-j+2a}{a}$ choices for the constants. Thus for $1\le j\le m$,
$$(-1)^j c_j=e_j+\sum_{a=1}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}\binom{m-j+2a}{a}e_{j-2a}.$$
This recovers each $e_j$ with earlier readings alone. In rank three, $P=X^3-(t+1)X^2+(t+1)X-1$. In rank four, $P=X^4-e_1X^3+(e_2+2)X^2-e_1X+1$. These identities include repeated roots, $z_i=\pm1$ and coefficient characteristic two.

**A distinction the same data lose.** In $R=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ the labelled point $z=1+\epsilon$ has inverse $1-\epsilon$. Their sum is $2$, the same reading as at $z=1$. The Weyl orbit of the point $1$ consists only of $1$, so these two marked-ring points really are distinguishable before taking invariant observations. Over $\mathbf C$, the observed multiset does determine the Weyl orbit: non-self-inverse roots pair with their inverse, and the multiplicities of the self-inverse roots are halved after removing the known spare $1$. The release uses complex spherical representations at this stage. Its fixed-weight deformation question later has additional global data. ## Which global summands the count includes

Let $E/F$ be the CM quadratic extension, unramified at every finite place, with $F$ its totally real subfield. The group in the release preserves the identity Hermitian form. Fix the highest weight at infinity. At every inert finite place the level is the one fixed integral subgroup. At split places the chosen local representation must have fixed vectors at the specified level. For a fixed regular algebraic, conjugate-self-dual cuspidal $\Pi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf A_E)$, the predicate `At` matches Satake multisets at almost all **split** places. The integer counts copies of irreducible automorphic representations of the definite unitary group in a direct decomposition of the whole level-and-weight space. An individual summand may have more than one level-fixed vector. [Exact count](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/MultiplicityOne/CompOfBis.lean#L13-L34) · [Where a nonzero summand is selected](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/TCountE.lean#L161-L185)

Several of the matching hypotheses admit short independent checks.

1. The quadratic idele-class character of $E/F$ is unramified at all finite places and is the sign at each real place. Its value on the rational element $-1\in F^\times$ is both $1$ and $(-1)^{[F:\mathbf Q]}$. Hence $[F:\mathbf Q]$ is even, meeting Labesse's lower bound of two.
2. At a finite inert place, the extension is unramified quadratic. The norm on units is surjective and the trace of the integer ring is the whole base integer ring, including at dyadic places. For the norm, first lift a solution in the residue field, then correct each successive residue digit by the surjective residue trace, and use completeness. For the trace, lift an element with nonzero residue trace and scale by its trace unit. In each pair of coordinate lines choose $x=(1,t)$ with $N(t)=-1$, and $y=(1,0)$ with $\langle x,y\rangle=1$. Replace $y$ by $y-bx$ with $b+\bar b=1$. These integral vectors give a hyperbolic plane (their determinant is a unit). Repeating and retaining the last unit line in odd dimension gives an integral quasi-split model of the very same lattice. Its stabilizer is the fixed hyperspecial subgroup. This fixes which subgroup “spherical” refers to, even where there are multiple conjugacy classes of hyperspecial subgroups. [Integral change of basis in the definition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/GaloisCohomology₂/BaseChangeDefiniteUnitary.lean#L130-L140)
3. At infinity, the identity form gives a compact unitary group. One highest weight selects one complex irreducible representation there. Labesse explicitly checks property $(*)$ for this compact case. [Labesse, §5.1](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michael.harris/CBCM.pdf) · [Guerberoff, §2.4](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X10005154)

**Identifying an occupied packet.** Start with an actual summand satisfying `At` and the prescribed local levels and weight. Corollary 5.3 of Labesse supplies a weak transfer with discrete $\mathrm{GL}$ factors. At almost all split primes its normalized Satake multiset therefore agrees with that of $\Pi$. A discrete Speh factor of length $b\ge2$ would already be visible at a single ordinary unramified split prime. Its multiset includes the constituents' parameters multiplied by $q^{(b-1)/2}$. At least one parameter of the underlying unitary cuspidal representation has modulus $\ge1$ because the product has modulus one. The multiset would have an entry of modulus at least $\sqrt q$. Every normalized parameter of the unitary cusp $\Pi$ has modulus strictly smaller than $\sqrt q$ at such a place (the unconditional Luo–Rudnick–Sarnak bound suffices). The same argument works with a unitary twisting character. Thus the transfer in this comparison is an isobaric sum of unitary cusp forms. [Labesse, Corollary 5.3](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michael.harris/CBCM.pdf) · [Mœglin–Waldspurger, residual spectrum of GL](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/asens.1595/) · [Sarnak on the unconditional exponent bound](https://web.math.princeton.edu/sarnak/FieldNotesCurrent.pdf)

In this quadratic extension the primes of $E$ of relative degree one are precisely the split primes. Ramakrishnan's Theorem A for isobaric representations now identifies the transfer with $\Pi$ over $E$. Its 2015 cuspidal corollary alone would require both candidates already to be cuspidal. This use of the later theorem, together with the Speh test, accounts for the quantifier in `At` which leaves out all inert primes. [Ramakrishnan, Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.08429) · [Earlier quadratic cuspidal corollary](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~dinakar/papers/mildTcheb-JNT.pdf)

For this cuspidal transfer, at split places the local component is determined by $\Pi_w$ (local base change on the split group). At an inert place with the fixed hyperspecial level, the transfer parameter determines its unique spherical representation for that subgroup. The polynomial proof above gives the inversion on its normalized Satake coordinates. The fixed archimedean highest weight determines the remaining components. Labesse's proof of Theorem 5.4 gives precisely the uniqueness of the finite part under the spherical requirement and multiplicity one for an occurring $\sigma$. Thus at an **occupied** packet satisfying these comparisons there is one isomorphism class and it occurs once. In a direct decomposition of the fixed-level space such a multiplicity-one component contributes one index whenever its fixed part is nonzero: projection of the whole decomposition to that irreducible component must recover that part. This addresses the two possible sources of a double count. [Labesse, Theorems 5.4 and 5.9](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michael.harris/CBCM.pdf) · [Guerberoff, Theorem 2.2](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X10005154) · [Release's fixed-level decomposition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/MultiplicityOne/CompOfBis.lean#L16-L34)

The argument uses the published global transfer and multiplicity theorems. It gives an independent comparison at the nonzero summands used in the dimension step. Establishing existence with every prescribed local condition for an arbitrary input to the release's abstract `Mult` is an additional assertion. The release derives a positive uniform factor via a global trace identity. ## A rank-one count on paper

In rank one write $\pi$ for the Hecke character of $E$ and $T=U(1)$. Conjugate self-duality says $\pi(z)\pi(\bar z)=1$. The restriction to the ideles of $F$ is trivial on norms and on $F^\times$. By the quadratic norm-index theorem, it is either trivial or the character $\eta_{E/F}$. At a complex place, conjugate self-duality gives a unitary angular character. The integral infinitesimal weight $a$ in rank one makes it $(z/\bar z)^a$. It is trivial on $\mathbf R^\times$, whereas the local $\eta$ is the sign. Thus the restriction is trivial. [Weight normalization](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/GaloisCohomology₂/BaseChangeDefiniteUnitary.lean#L139-L143) · [Labesse on the two types of characters, §4.10](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michael.harris/CBCM.pdf)

The map $z\mapsto z/\bar z$ onto $T(\mathbf A_F)$ has kernel $\mathbf A_F^\times$. Local Hilbert 90 gives surjectivity, with unit lifts at unramified inert places: after choosing a lift, divide by a uniformizer of $F$ to the appropriate power. At split places a norm-one pair $(u,u^{-1})$ has lift $(u,1)$. Global Hilbert 90 gives rational lifts of $T(F)$. Hence $\sigma(z/\bar z)=\pi(z)$ defines an automorphic character. At inert places an unramified $\pi$ is trivial on units and on the common uniformizer from $F$, and the resulting character of the compact norm-one group is trivial. At split places the stipulated fixed-vector condition selects exactly a level on which this character is trivial. The formula at infinity gives highest weight $a$.

Any other contributing $\sigma'$ at that level pulls back to $\pi$ at almost all split places and at every inert place. The quotient of the two pulled-back characters is supported at a finite set of places including infinity. It is trivial on $E^\times$, which is dense in the product of those local multiplicative groups by weak approximation. Thus the characters agree everywhere. A translation eigenfunction on the compact adelic torus quotient is determined by its value at the identity, so its multiplicity is exactly one. This gives the full rank-one count at the specified level. The proof uses classical class field theory and Hilbert 90.

## Reproduction and scope

The [integer check](check_distinguished_prime.py) expands the actual polynomials and independently reconstructs the symmetric generators in 25,110 coefficient-ring cases, including rings with nilpotents. It also verifies four first-order marked collisions in $\mathbf Z/p^2$. The infinite statements follow from the displayed proofs and the cited theorems.
