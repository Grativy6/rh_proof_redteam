# A nonzero arithmetic reading that the factor −80 erases

**24 November 2026.** Chris asked where an actual map loses information. On the zeta fields, the release’s class-counting construction has a geometric reading equal to a specified integer times a finite-field character value. We constructed an admissible input whose raw value is nonzero and whose geometric reading is zero. For the first twist the integer is **−80**. In this family at least **128 distinct classes** have the trivial reading at every finite tower layer. The subsequent counting theorem has a separate allowance for the kernel. Its displayed injectivity statement applies to inputs already separated after powering. ## The same class in the actual twisted norm

Use the same field as the [dyadic window example](2026-11-24-dyadic-class-enters-late.md), with parameters
$$q=3,\quad u=1+4\cdot3^{205},\quad d=3u^2-4,\quad \alpha=2+\sqrt{-d}.$$
Put $L=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$ and $F_\nu=L(\zeta_{2^\nu})$ for $\nu\ge c=2$. The group of the extension is cyclic of order $H=2^{\nu-c}$. Take its generator $\gamma$ acting on $\zeta_{2^\nu}$ by $\kappa=1+2^c=5$. The two odd quadratic fields are disjoint from the 2-power cyclotomic field. Thus $L$ has degree eight and four complex places. The case $e=0$ of `SClass` and `SFew` has twist $k=e+1=1$ and $M=2e+3=3$. Both required strict inequalities, $3<2^c$ and $3<r_2(L)$, hold. [Cycle construction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ClassCounting/LemmaKOfSteps.lean#L84-L96) · [Counting hypotheses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ClassCounting/LemmaKOfSteps.lean#L131-L138) · [Cyclotomic layer, divisor condition and twisted norm](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ClassCounting/HasVal.lean#L118-L141)

Every valuation of $\alpha$ in $L$ is even. The extension $L(\sqrt\alpha)/L$ is nontrivial, unramified and split at both dyadic places. It stays nontrivial in every $F_\nu$ and even in their union, because every nontrivial subextension of the remaining cyclotomic tower has full local degree at the dyadic places. These facts also hold for an appropriate $\alpha$ for every eligible $q,d$ with $q\mid d+1$. [Construction and valuations](2026-11-24-dyadic-class-enters-late.md) · [Survival in the tower](2026-11-24-dyadic-class-in-the-tower.md)

More generally put $a=\kappa^k$ for positive odd $k$, and take a base containing exactly the $2^c$-th roots of unity in its 2-primary roots. Use
$$x=\alpha^{(a-1)/2},\qquad J=-\tfrac12\operatorname{div}_{F_\nu}(\alpha).$$
Since $\alpha$ comes from the base and has even valuations, $J$ is an integral divisor fixed by $\gamma$. This gives the **exact** `DivDel` hypothesis $\operatorname{div}x=(1-a)J$ in these coordinates. When invoking the released geometric tests we impose $2k+1<2^c$ and $2k+1<r_2(L)$. The norm on every indicated layer is
$$N_a(x)=\prod_{j=0}^{H-1}(\gamma^jx)^{a^j}
       =\alpha^{(a^H-1)/2}.$$
The exponent has 2-adic valuation $\nu-1$ by lifting the exponent from $v_2(\kappa-1)=c\ge2$ and odd $k$. Hence $N_a(x)$ has exact order two modulo $F_\nu^{\times 2^\nu}$. A $2^\nu$-th root generates precisely $F_\nu(\sqrt\alpha)$ because one such root is an odd power of $\sqrt\alpha$, and $F_\nu$ contains the $2^\nu$-th roots of unity. This remains a nonzero class after quotienting by all roots of unity. A $2^\nu$-th root of any root of unity in $F_\nu$ can be obtained inside its infinite cyclotomic 2-tower, including the odd-order part where the power map is invertible. The displayed quadratic extension is absent from that tower. ## The measured zero

For $k=1,c=2$ the source requests the product over the other two modes,
$$\operatorname{pe}(5,3,1)=(5-1)(5-25)=-80.$$
At any good residue prime and any character $v$ with values in $\mathbb Z/2^\nu$, the raw $v(N_a(x))$ is killed by 2. At a detecting prime it equals $2^{\nu-1}$. Multiplication by $-80$ makes it zero in both cases. Thus every evaluation specified for the constructed cycle agrees with the evaluation for $x=1$, even though the raw twisted norms are distinct. This assertion concerns the specified evaluations. The homology classes of the cycles themselves could carry more information. [Evaluation formula](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ClassCounting/LemmaKOfSteps.lean#L84-L94) · [Two explicit detecting primes and their readouts](2026-11-24-every-place-splits-in-the-limit.md)

The loss has an elementary explanation. An integral polynomial annihilating the two modes $1,\kappa^2$ is divisible by $(T-1)(T-\kappa^2)$. Its value at $\kappa$ is therefore divisible by $\kappa(\kappa-1)^2$. Even if those two modes are annihilated only modulo $2^\nu$, the same lower bound $2c$ on its valuation holds for $\nu\ge2c+1$. Write $P(T)=(T-1)Q(T)+P(1)$. Then $Q(\kappa^2)$ is divisible by $2^{\nu-c-1}$ and $Q(\kappa)\equiv Q(\kappa^2)\bmod 2^c$. This accounts for the price of separating those modes with such a polynomial.

For the general central mode $k$ among $0,\ldots,2k$, the exact valuation is $2kc+2v_2(k!)$. The $2k$ factors contribute $c+v_2(|k-j|)$ each. In passing to `Few` the release further multiplies its evaluation by $2^{2k}$ and uses the conservative exponent $b=2k+(2k+1)(c+2k+1)$. For $k=1,c=2$ this gives $b=17$. The scalar $-80$ has valuation four, and the extra factor four contributes two more bits before that conservative bound. [Coefficient definition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ClassCounting/HasVal.lean#L138-L142) · [Divisibility used by the proof](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/CyclotomicDiscriminants/SFewOfCut.lean#L55-L83) · [Additional power and exact separation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/CyclotomicDiscriminants/SFewOfCut.lean#L185-L204)

## How many genuine inputs collide?

Use additive notation for $Y=F_\nu^\times/\mu(F_\nu)$, let $g=\gamma^{-1}$ and $\Delta=g-a$. The release forms
$$W=\{x\in Y:\operatorname{div}x\in\Delta I\}/\Delta Y,$$
where $I$ is the group of divisors with the induced action. The unweighted twisted norm defines $f_1:W\longrightarrow Y/2^\nu Y$. The identity $\Delta N_a=N_a\Delta=(1-a^H)g$ proves well-definedness. The map actually counted is $f_{2^b}=2^b f_1$. Our representative $x$ gives an element of order exactly two in $W$. Its double is $\Delta(-\alpha)$ in additive notation and its image under $f_1$ is nonzero even modulo roots of unity. It belongs to the kernel of $f_{2^b}$. [Arithmetic quotient](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ClassCounting/SelmerOrderCounts/TwoLe.lean#L27-L49) · [Telescoping identity and counted map](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ClassCounting/SelmerOrderCounts/KerFDelNm.lean#L20-L52)

Finiteness here also has an arithmetic check. Send a representative $x$ to the ideal class of the unique $J$ with $\Delta J=\operatorname{div}x$. This maps $W$ onto the kernel of $\Delta$ on the finite ideal class group. The remaining kernel is the free unit group modulo $\Delta$ of that group. It is finite because $g$ has finite order and $a>1$, hence $g-a$ is invertible over the rationals. Thus ordinary finite cardinalities apply in this comparison.

There is a large subgroup of the same kernel for $k=1$. Write $L=C_{2^c}(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$ here with $C_{2^c}=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^c})$. At $\nu=c$ the layer group is trivial. The base quotient $W_0$ consists of elements with divisor divisible by $2^c$, taken modulo $2^c$-th powers and roots of unity. Mapping a representative to the ideal class of its divisor divided by $2^c$ gives an exact sequence with kernel the free units modulo $2^c$ and image $\mathrm{Cl}(L)[2^c]$. In particular
$$|W_0|=2^{\,c(r_2(L)-1)}|\mathrm{Cl}(L)[2^c]|.$$
The class group has even order, since the explicit nontrivial unramified quadratic extension over this totally complex field gives a class-group character.

This whole $W_0$ injects into $W$ at **every** $\nu\ge c$. Indeed the norm of a base element $y$ is $y^{1+a+\cdots+a^{H-1}}$, and $v_2(1+a+\cdots+a^{H-1})=\nu-c$. If the norm vanishes in $Y/2^\nu Y$, torsion-freeness of $Y$ implies $y\in\mu(F_\nu)F_\nu^{\times 2^c}$. Thus a $2^c$-th root of $y$ lies in $L_\infty=L(\mu_{2^\infty})$. For $y\in L^\times$, such a root defines a character $\operatorname{Gal}(L_\infty/L)\to\mu_{2^c}$. A $2^c$-th root of $\zeta_{2^c}$ defines a generator of this character group, as the generator of the Galois group acts on $\zeta_{2^{2c}}$ by $1+2^c$. Dividing the root of $y$ by a suitable power of $\zeta_{2^{2c}}$ gives an element fixed over $L$. It follows that $y\in\mu(L)L^{\times 2^c}$ already. Divisors divisible by $2^c$ satisfy `DivDel` upstairs, and base $2^c$-th powers are $\Delta$-images. This proves injectivity. Each of these inputs has raw norm killed by $2^c$, so all their specified geometric readings vanish. For a finite list of representatives, multiply their nonzero exclusion parameters. At every prime allowed by that common parameter the readings agree for the whole list.

For $c=2$, $r_2(L)=4$ and the kernel has at least $4^3\cdot2=128$ members, uniformly in the layer. For $c=9$, $r_2=512$ and the same calculation gives at least $2^{4600}$. The latter is an allowed base with a larger fixed 2-power conductor. No numerical value of the release’s classically chosen generator bound is assumed here.

**Small exact control.** At $q=3,d=23,c=\nu=2$, PARI/GP 2.15.4 gave $\mathrm{Cl}(L)=C_6$, $\mu(L)=C_{12}$ and unit rank three, with `bnfcertify(B) = 1`. Thus the **entire** $W$ has 128 elements and the weighted map is zero. Its exponent divides four and it contains $C_4^3$ from the free units, so it is $C_4^3\times C_2$. Here the half divisor of $2+\sqrt{-23}$ is principal. Dividing by the square of its generator leaves a nonsquare in the free-unit component. This explicitly distinguishes the unit representing our kernel element from the nontrivial class-group *character* attached to the quadratic extension. This small pair meets the arithmetic hypotheses of the class construction. The 656-bit prime is the separate certified `WinPair`. ## Does the downstream count assume injectivity on this subgroup?

The function labelled injective in `sFewOf` is restricted to a finite set already assumed separated **after raising the norm to $2^b$ modulo $2^\nu$-th powers**. The pair $1,x$ fails that incoming hypothesis. In the class-counting application, `fewLe` chooses representatives only for the *range* of the powered map. The subsequent bound multiplies by an allowance for its kernel. One can trace the exact missing bit into that allowance. For $w\in W[2]$, lift it to $x\in Y$ and write $2x=\Delta y$. The operator $\Delta$ is injective on $Y$ because $gy=ay$ implies $(a^H-1)y=0$ in a torsion-free group. Thus the class of $y$ modulo $2Y$ is independent of the lift. Its divisor is even, since $\operatorname{div}x=\Delta J$ and $\Delta$ is also injective on divisors. It is fixed modulo $2Y$ and gives an injection from $W[2]$ into the squareclasses the release calls `SelInv`. If $y=2z$, torsion-freeness gives $x=\Delta z$. In our example $y$ is the class of $\alpha^{-1}$, an actual base squareclass with even valuations. This supplies the missing distinction by a named arithmetic datum. For an independent size check, take the squareclasses in $F_\nu$ of elements with even valuations away from 2, then quotient by the root-of-unity classes. In the cyclic extension $F_\nu/L$, passing to invariants before this quotient can lose at most a factor 2, from $H^1(G,\mu(F_\nu)\bmod F_\nu^{\times2})$. Inflation-restriction and Kummer theory cost at most another factor 2, since $|H^2(G,\mu_2)|\le2$ for cyclic $G$. An element of $L^\times$ whose restriction has even valuations away from 2 already has them over $L$, by absence of odd ramification. The base group of such classes has size at most $2^{2[L:\mathbb Q]+\lfloor\log_2 h_L\rfloor}$. The $S$-units have at most $[L:\mathbb Q]+|S|\le2[L:\mathbb Q]$ generators modulo squares including the root-of-unity contribution, and the remaining factor is at most the class number (or its 2-part). The latter bound on its 2-part is $2^{\lfloor\log_2 h_L\rfloor}$. This gives the source’s $|W[2]|\le2^{g_L}$ with $g_L=2[L:\mathbb Q]+\lfloor\log_2 h_L\rfloor+2$. Finally, if $2^bN_a x=2^\nu y$ in $Y$, apply $\Delta$ and cancel $2^\nu$. Since $v_2(a^H-1)=\nu+v_2(k)$ and $g$ preserves $\Delta Y$, the 2-primary part of the kernel in $W$ is killed by $2^{b+v_2(k)}$. A group with at most $g_L$ generators and that exponent has cardinal at most $2^{(b+v_2(k))g_L}$. This is the factor carried in the source. It is independent of $\nu$ with the base and twist fixed. There is a concrete scale for that next test. In the $q=3,d=23$ example $\mathcal O_L^\times\simeq C_{12}\times\mathbb Z^3$. The determinant $GL_{10}(\mathcal O_L)\to\mathcal O_L^\times$ has a section $u\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(u,1,\ldots,1)$, hence integral homology of the units is a direct summand. The homology of a cyclic group and the Künneth formula give torsion $C_{12}^3$ in degree two and $C_{12}^4$ in degree three just from that summand. Those are exactly the two degrees entering `SFew` for $k=1$. On the geometric side a scalar root of unity fixes every Hermitian form, so the stabilizer information deserves a direct test. The release states a bound for the full torsion groups, including the small primes. Soulé’s published bound for torsion in algebraic $K$-groups explicitly sets aside small primes, so citing that endpoint alone would not verify these integral terms. ## Unresolved

The geometric observations genuinely erase the raw class. The reconstructed kernel estimate accounts for it and for other collisions of this powered norm. A further obligation is the bound on the integral homology used for the range. In particular `SUpper` includes torsion in two adjacent degrees. A rational-rank calculation cannot measure those torsion factors.
