# The price of projecting onto the trace

**23 November 2026.** The release applies $T\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(T)I/\dim V$ to a particular integral endomorphism of trace one. We calculated the minimum power of $p$ that makes the result integral in the **lattice used at that call**. For binary forms the release's exponent is exact in every degree. For suitable highest weights the trace denominator alone consumes the full exponent in every rank. We also found exactly where interpreting a path label as a pure component would give a false result. ## Two costs on the same input

Use characteristic zero and a coefficient discrete valuation ring $O$ in the release's local setting. For example, one can use an unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ with $\sqrt p$ adjoined and take its integers, for any $p\ge5$. All exponents below count **powers of $p$**. For a highest vector $v$, the release uses two lattices. $\Delta$ is generated from $v$ by integral divided powers. In $\nabla$, the highest-weight coordinate of every such word applied to a vector must be integral. The endomorphism lattice consists of maps sending $\nabla$ into $\Delta$. The chosen rank-one map sends $x$ to its highest coordinate times $v$. Its trace is one. [Integral operators](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ObjPol.lean#L229-L249) · [The two lattices](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ObjPol.lean#L252-L265) · [Endomorphism lattice](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/RootWeights/DeltaIntegralEnvelopingStable.lean#L16-L24) · [Value on the rank-one map](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/RootWeights/LinfAdjointLeEllWtHighest.lean#L56-L69)

Take the degree-$d$ symmetric power of the standard $n$-dimensional representation, $n\ge2$, with $v=x_1^d$. For $|\alpha|=d$ write $m_\alpha=d!/\prod_i\alpha_i!$. Here
$$
 \nabla=\bigoplus_\alpha O x^\alpha,\qquad
 \Delta=\bigoplus_\alpha O\,m_\alpha x^\alpha.
$$
Moving $\alpha_i$ copies of $x_1$ to each $x_i$ by divided powers produces $m_\alpha x^\alpha$. Conversely, moving $j$ units from coordinate $b$ to coordinate $a$ preserves the displayed lattice. If $\alpha'=\alpha+j e_a-j e_b$, then $m_\alpha\binom{\alpha_b}{j}=m_{\alpha'}\binom{\alpha_a+j}{j}$. The diagonal binomial operators act by integers. Finally, moving every variable back to the first coordinate reads the coefficient of any chosen $x^\alpha$ with multiplier one. This proves both lattice formulas.

The dimension is $\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}$. On the projected map $I/\dim V$, the exact cost is therefore
$$
 B_p(n,d)=v_p\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}+\max_{|\alpha|=d}v_p(m_\alpha).
$$

There is a closed formula requiring no enumeration of monomials. Set $D=d+n-1$. Then
$$
 B_p(n,d)=
 \sum_{j\ge1}\left(\min\left(n-1,\left\lfloor\frac{D}{p^j}\right\rfloor\right)
               -\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p^j}\right\rfloor\right).
$$
To verify it, write the integer being maximized as $D!/((n-1)!\prod_i\alpha_i!)$. Place $n-1$ bars among $1,\ldots,D$ and let the $n$ gap lengths be the $\alpha_i$. For $m=p^j$, the sum $\sum_i\lfloor\alpha_i/m\rfloor$ is at least $\max(0,\lfloor D/m\rfloor-(n-1))$. This follows either by counting remainders or by using $\sum_i\alpha_i=D-(n-1)$. Choose the bars at the $n-1$ numbers with largest $p$-valuations. When $\lfloor D/m\rfloor\le n-1$, every multiple of $m$ is a bar and every gap is shorter than $m$. Otherwise every bar is a multiple of $m$, and the gaps contain exactly $\lfloor D/m\rfloor-(n-1)$ multiples, each gap starting after zero or a multiple of $m$. This single choice attains all the bounds at once. Summing the factorial valuations gives the formula.

For $n=2$ the result is $\lfloor\log_p(d+1)\rfloor$. The native budget is $N_0(n)\lfloor\log_p(L+n-1)\rfloor$, where $N_0(n)=n(n-1)/2$ and $L$ is the largest absolute coordinate of a trace-zero weight of the adjoint action. Here $L=d$ because there is a map from $x_2^d$ to $x_1^d$. Thus the two costs together exhaust the native bound in every binary degree. At $p=5$ the distribution moves even between adjacent degrees. ## An example exhausting the rank-dependent coefficient

For $p>n-1$ and $h\ge1$ take the $GL_n$ highest weight $\lambda_i=(p^h-1)(n-i)$, in one-based indices. Every ratio in the Weyl dimension product is $p^h$, so $\dim V=p^{hN_0(n)}$. The largest coordinate difference is $(p^h-1)(n-1)$, and a map between the highest and reversed extreme weights attains this width in the adjoint action. The native exponent is exactly $hN_0(n)$. For $n=4,p=5,h=2$, the image of the trace-one input is $I/5^{12}$.

The equality also holds in the actual endomorphism lattice for these weights. To identify the lattice, one can use the classical Steinberg representation theorem. Restricted to $SL_n$, this highest weight is $(p^h-1)\rho$, and the Weyl module of that weight is simple after reduction over an algebraic closure of the residue field. The normalized contravariant form pairs the highest vector with itself to one. Its integral dual to the divided-power lattice $\Delta$ is precisely $\nabla$. Transposition interchanges the raising and lowering divided powers and computes the highest-coordinate tests. The form is integral on $\Delta$. Its reduced radical is a submodule and the highest vector has unit norm, so simplicity forces that radical to vanish. Hence $\nabla=\Delta$ here. The full $p$-power loss comes from the scalar $1/\dim V$. The symmetric-power calculation above is independent of this external theorem. [Steinberg, *Representations of Algebraic Groups*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0027763000011016) · [The Weyl module and dimension at this weight, proof of Lemma 7.10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-021-00749-9)

## Testing the interpretation of a path label

Take a degree-one vector $x$ and a degree-one covector $y$. Identify $x_i y_j$ with the matrix unit $E_{ij}$. The released wedge and contraction recursion gives $\sum_i x_i y_i=I$ on the path ending in weight zero. On the path ending in $(1,0,\ldots,0,-1)$ it gives $x_i y_j$ for every pair except $(1,1)$. The excluded pair is the one that fails the standard tableau condition between the positive and negative columns. These polynomials together are an integral basis, since $E_{11}=I-\sum_{i>1}E_{ii}$. But $E_{22}$ on the latter path has trace **one**. Its component on the invariant line is $I/n$. It cannot belong to the pure trace-zero component in characteristic zero. [Recursion and standard indices](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ObjPol.lean#L419-L463) · [What the path label and the exact component dimension certify](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/StatementOfDegreeAndBound.lean#L277-L295)

This difference can survive reduction. For $O=\mathbb Z_p$, write $A=OI$, $B=\ker(\operatorname{tr})$ in $M=M_n(O)$. Both are saturated and $A\cap B=0$. Their images modulo $p^e$ meet in the scalar matrices $aI$ with $na=0$ modulo $p^e$, a module of length $\min(e,v_p(n))$. Thus reducing separate exact components can produce an intersection that was absent before reduction. The release's integral intersection assertion takes dominance-lower sets at the ordered prefixes. A lower set containing the adjoint weight here must also contain weight zero. Its proof of saturation uses a subset of a common integral basis. [Dominance-lower sets](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/StatementOfDegreeAndBound.lean#L98-L103) · [Integral intersection assertion](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/StatementOfDegreeAndBound.lean#L298-L313) · [The common-basis argument](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ValuationLowestDegreeMapFractionField.lean#L1607-L1638)

## Nested projections can spend a fresh denominator at every step

Let $W=V\otimes V^*=\operatorname{End}(V)$ with $\dim V=n$. In $W^{\otimes m}$, take the sets of the first $2,4,\ldots,2m$ letters. These are strictly nested sets of alternating vector and covector letters of degree one. On each prefix project onto the trivial representation. Starting with $E_{11}^{\otimes m}$, the first projection gives $I/n$ on the first pair. That factor is invariant. The next prefix projection therefore acts on the next pair by the same trace formula. Inductively the output is $I^{\otimes m}/n^m$.

This computation determines the product operator on every simple tensor. Reversing the product in the formula has no effect, since each earlier projection intertwines the later diagonal action and hence preserves the later isotypic components. The required exponent on the ordinary tensor lattice is exactly $m v_p(n)$. It is attained on the displayed input, whose output has a coefficient $1/n^m$. In the multilinear degree all monomial factorials are one, so the same example tests the factorial-weighted coefficient valuation. [Coefficient and factorial-weighted lattices](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ObjPol.lean#L45-L64) · [Published statement for nested sets](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/CovariantDegrees.lean#L98-L120)

For $n=p=5$ and $m=100$, replacing the sum of prefix costs by their largest value would supply only $10\lfloor\log_5(404)\rfloor=30$ powers of 5. The example requires 100. The published sum is 2,650. We checked the subsequent call as well. It charges each projection individually and sums the costs when composing them. The choice of an output component with suitably low valuation depends on the input vector. The same effect occurs at primes larger than the dimension of $V$. Group $b$ pairs into a block and impose the invariant constraint only at its end. The input $E_{11}^{\otimes b}$ is a trace-one endomorphism supported on $\operatorname{Sym}^b V\subset V^{\otimes b}$. Over characteristic zero the symmetric power is irreducible. Hence the invariant projection of that input is the symmetrizer on $V^{\otimes b}$ divided by $\dim\operatorname{Sym}^b V$. Indeed the endomorphisms supported on the symmetric summand form a subrepresentation, and the trace there determines the scalar projection. Between two words with the same multiplicities $\alpha$, the symmetrizer has entry $\prod_i\alpha_i!/b!$. All other entries vanish. Its exact cost on this input is therefore the same $B_p(n,b)$ computed above. Imposing the constraint after successive blocks of sizes $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ gives a tensor product of the block outputs and cost $\sum_j B_p(n,b_j)$, since every completed block is invariant. For $n=2,p=17$ and 100 blocks of 16 pairs, the exact cost is 100, although 17 does not divide $n$. The largest individual bound in the release's prefix formula is only $\lfloor\log_{17}(6401)\rfloor=3$. The sum in that formula covers the example.

There is a different failure for crossing sets. On $V\otimes V^*\otimes V$, project onto the invariants of the first two letters by $A$ and of the last two letters by $B$. If $L(z)=\sum_i e_i\otimes e^i\otimes z$ and $R(z)=\sum_i z\otimes e^i\otimes e_i$, then $B L(z)=R(z)/n$ and $A R(z)=L(z)/n$. Thus $ABA=A/n^2$, which excludes commutation when $n>1$. In the application, prefixes belonging to one segment are nested and the index sets for different segments are disjoint. ## Unresolved

A labelled integral path vector can have a nonzero component in a lower rational type, and that component can have a genuine denominator. The traced path bound explicitly allows the lower type. The trace projection pays the exact cost in the examples above. Nested projections can consume the denominator repeatedly, and the traced composition sums the costs. The next issue is how the chosen low-valuation component is turned into an integral dual test for the same arithmetic input.
