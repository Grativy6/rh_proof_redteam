# A theta zero at every toric depth

**24 November 2026.** Hearthline, following Chris Pang’s request to make independent branches where the geometry warrants it. The [split-torus experiment](2026-11-24-the-torsion-reading-that-sees-through-the-zero.md) exposes a vanished leading term at the next torsion order. There is a different mechanism in an actual product with good elliptic reduction: its distinguished section can vanish at **every** toric order. Here the additional abelian torsion or the choice of a different section is essential.

## An exact zero with the released polarization

Let $E/\mathbb Q$ be $y^2=x^3+1$, of $j$-invariant zero. On $E^8$ take the polarization from the release’s $W_8$ and the elliptic Poincaré bundle. Over $\mathbb C$ choose $\tau=(-1+i\sqrt3)/2$ and put $B=W_8+W_8^t$. Its section is, up to a scalar,
$$\Theta_B(z,\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^8} \exp(\pi i\tau n^t B n+2\pi i n^t Bz).$$
The Poincaré bundle is obtained by pulling the elliptic theta line under addition and dividing by the two individual pullbacks, with the rigidifying factor at zero. The elliptic signs cancel in this ratio. Tensoring these mixed factors with exponents $W_{ij}$ gives the factor $\exp(-\pi i\tau v^tBv-2\pi i v^tBz)$ under $z\mapsto z+u+\tau v$. This agrees with the displayed series by reindexing. Since $B$ is unimodular, the period recurrence determines all Fourier coefficients from one, so this is the ordinary principal section. [Released construction of the line bundle](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/LabRange.lean#L33-L38) · [Pinned matrix](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/CharacterTheory/W8AddTransposeEqE8.lean#L8-L18)

Put $F(\tau)=\Theta_B(0,\tau)$. Evenness gives $F(\tau+1)=F(\tau)$ and Poisson summation on the self-dual rank-eight lattice gives $F(-1/\tau)=\tau^4 F(\tau)$. The transformation $\tau\mapsto-1/(\tau+1)$ fixes our chosen $\tau$, while $(\tau+1)^4\ne1$. Hence $\Theta_B(0,\tau)=0$ **exactly**. Vanishing of the algebraic section at the identity is preserved by extension of fields. It therefore holds for the same elliptic curve over an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q_5$. [Rank-eight transformation law](https://www.ce.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cgn/lnt/events/MCM2015/mcm2015_belfiore.pdf)

There is a sharper characteristic-zero reading. On the elliptic line $z=r z_1$ for any root $r$, the theta section has divisor precisely $2[0]$. Indeed it has degree $r^tBr=2$ and is even. To see it is not identically zero on the line, differentiate twice at $z_1=0$. On each norm shell the sum of $(n,r)^2$ is $|r|^2/8$ times the sum of $|n|^2$. In the standard $E_8$ realization coordinate permutations equate the diagonal sums, and changing signs of two coordinates kills each off-diagonal sum. The second derivative is therefore a nonzero constant times $F'(\tau)$. The weight-four valence formula gives a simple zero there: its cusp value is 1, and the compulsory order-one zero at the elliptic point accounts for the full valence $4/12$. Thus every nonidentity point on that root elliptic line has a nonzero ordinary theta reading in characteristic zero. [Valence formula, Theorem 2.11](https://www.williamstein.org/books/modform/stein-modform.pdf)

There is a second proof of the nonzero derivative using only four lattice shells. Put $a=\exp(-\pi\sqrt3)<1/200$, so the Fourier parameter is $-a$. The numbers $R_n$ of vectors of squared length $2n$, for $n=1,2,3,4$, are $240,2160,6720,17520$. They can be counted using the eight doubled coordinates of equal parity with sum zero modulo four. Disjoint balls of radius $\sqrt2/2$ give $R_n\le(2\sqrt n+1)^8\le3^8 n^4$. In the logarithmic derivative $\sum n R_n(-a)^n$, all terms after the first have combined absolute value below $23a$: bound the first three remainder terms directly and bound the tail starting at $n=5$ by $3^8 n^5 a^n$, whose successive ratio is at most $(6/5)^5/200$. The first term is $-240a$. The [exact shell and rational bound](check_marked_lattice_and_torsion.py) verify the sign independently of the valence formula.

Now take the product of a split Tate elliptic curve and this good elliptic curve, and apply the same $E_8$ construction to its eighth power. The Poincaré bundle of a product is the external product of the factors, hence so is the ordinary section. At level $N=m^2$ its distinguished pullback restricted to the unit torus, with good elliptic coordinate zero, contains the factor $\Theta_B([m]0)=0$. Every toric character projection of this section vanishes on all such toric points, for every $m$. The zero includes classes with a unique toric minimum. A nonzero normalizing theta constant can be chosen away from the identity. This example directly separates the toric filter from the other choices in the published test. ## Recovering a unit reading on the good factor

At residue characteristic 5 choose a primitive integral ordinary section on the good $E^8$. Its reduction is nonzero. The restriction of its line bundle to any translate of a coordinate elliptic factor has degree two, given by the corresponding diagonal of $B$. In fact any nonzero section of this bundle is nonzero at at least
$$(m^2-2)^8$$
points of the grid $E[m]^8$ for $m\ge2$ prime to 5. To prove it, at most two coordinate slices can have identically zero restriction: fixing the other seven coordinates where the one-variable section is nonzero bounds such slices by the degree two. On every remaining slice the same argument applies to the next variable. In the last variable at most two of the $m^2$ distinct points can be zeros. This reasoning is valid over the algebraic closure of the residue field and permits a finite residue extension.

Lift a nonzero grid point and then lift it under $[m]$ to a point $P$ of order dividing $m^2$. These lifts exist over finite unramified extensions by finite étaleness. Translation by $P$ preserves the isomorphism class of the $m^2$th power of the principal line. The translated pullback section now has a **unit** value at the origin in an integral frame. It can be included in a choice of integral section basis there, or expanded in one: if an integral linear combination has unit value then one of its terms has unit value. Tensor with the original toric pullback. The recovered good-factor value multiplies every toric reading by that same unit as the toric periods vary. In this product a single good-factor choice therefore works across the entire toric wall. The conclusion applies to an integral basis with the specified lattice; arbitrary rescaling of a generic basis can change unit readings.

The other class-sum construction can vary the base torsion input in its subgroup generated by labels with maximal theta value. For this product the bound also checks that choice. At a base level $b\ge5$ prime to the residue characteristic, $(b^2-2)^8>b^{16}/2$. In product torsion frames take a toric label attaining the toric maximum. Pairing it with the more than half of good labels of unit value attains the product maximum. The differences of a subset occupying more than half of a finite group fill the group. Thus the generated subgroup contains the entire good $b$-torsion factor, even though the identity theta value is zero. A good label with unit reading is available there. [Definition of the subgroup and the geometric test](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Degenerations/ArithBase.lean#L84-L106)

There is also a general bound when a positive-dimensional good abelian factor $B$ and a translated torsion grid are available. If $\dim B=h$, $L$ is a principal polarization, $D$ is an effective divisor numerically equivalent to $dL$, and $p\nmid M$, then
$$\#((z+B[M])\cap D)\le d\,3^{h-1}h!\,M^{2h-2}.$$
Translate the divisor to take $z=0$. Under the finite étale map $[M]$, the multiplicity of $[M]_*D$ at zero is the sum of local multiplicities on the fibre, hence bounds the number of points. Intersect with $h-1$ general hyperplanes through zero in the very ample system $|3L|$. They give the same upper bound by the total intersection number. Projection formula, $[M]^*L\equiv M^2L$ and $L^h=h!$ give exactly the displayed quantity. For multiple leading coefficients the union bound gives simultaneous nonvanishing whenever their total divisor degree satisfies the resulting inequality. Applying this to a nonsplit degeneration requires identifying the actual evaluation grid and its line bundles. [Lefschetz’s very-ampleness theorem](https://www.mathematik.uni-marburg.de/~tbauer/1996-On-tensor-products.pdf)

This count also has a precise nonsplit use. In an extension of an abelian variety by a torus over an algebraically closed field, the map on $b$-torsion onto the abelian $b$-torsion is surjective. Lift the abelian point to any $g$. Its multiple $bg$ is toric, and one can subtract a torus point with this same $b$-multiple. Thus a translated torsion grid on the abelian quotient requires no splitting. If the zero set is the inverse image of a divisor downstairs, each fibre of the finite torsion map has the same size, so its fraction of zeros is exactly the downstairs fraction. The release explicitly supplies the inverse-image condition using an **integral ordinary degree-one expansion**: the nonzero Fourier modes are divisible by the maximal ideal, and the surviving weight-zero section lies on the abelian quotient. An arbitrary Laurent polynomial on a torus, such as $t^b-1$, would fail the conclusion and does not meet that expansion condition. For a nonzero ordinary principal coefficient the bound above gives fewer than one sixth of zeros whenever the source imposes $6N^g g!<b^2$, $N\ge4$, since $3^{h-1}h!\le N^g g!$ for $1\le h\le g$. The case $h=0$ gives a nonzero constant. ## Unresolved

This gives an exact mixed-reduction zero in the specified $E_8$ geometry and an independent good-fibre calculation of the additional information needed to see through it. The release asks for existence of a good phase **and** section index, and in its other class-sum construction it can also vary the integral torsion input. Our product example separates these choices. For a genuinely nonsplit Raynaud extension, the coefficient lines and abelian torsion evaluation points can move together. Their common identification over the arithmetic model remains an open test.
