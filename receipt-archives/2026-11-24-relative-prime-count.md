# Is the prime-counting error small compared with the difference?

**24 November 2026.** The [preceding audit](2026-11-24-one-exception-across-moduli.md) isolated the positive exceptional-character class, where two large terms subtract. There is an unconditional external theorem that pays for this cancellation. We matched its exception, range, weights and error to the released prime count and independently checked where the release pays for smoothing and prime powers. This addresses a prime-selection input. The [explicit Selmer-window failure](2026-11-24-dyadic-class-enters-late.md) tests a separate assertion about the fields built from the selected primes.

## Independent check against the published estimate

Let $8\mid M$, $M>0$, $b$ a unit residue, and $L=\log M$. The release chooses an optional exceptional pair $(\chi,\beta)$. Write $a=\chi(b)\in\{-1,1\}$ when it exists, and $a=0$ otherwise. For $a=0$ take $\beta=1$ in the formulas with zero coefficient. The main term for the prime count times $\varphi(M)$ is
$$B(x)=\operatorname{li}(x)-a\operatorname{li}(x^\beta),\qquad
  \operatorname{li}(x)=\int_2^x du/\log u.$$
The requested error is bounded by a constant times **$B(x)$ itself** times $\exp(-c\log x/L)+\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x})$, for $\log x\ge C_0 L\log(L+2)$. The constants are existential and independent of $M,b,x$. [Exact released statement](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/JacobiCharacterSums/MeanSquareHypWIff.lean#L24-L46)

Thorner and Zaman, Corollary 1.4, bound the sum of $\log p$ over primes $p\le x$ in the class for $x\ge M^{12}$. Their error is relative to $D(x)=x-a x^\beta/\beta$, after the same $\varphi(M)$ normalization. Their second exponential has $(\log x)^{3/5}/(\log\log x)^{1/5}$ in place of $\sqrt{\log x}$, which gives at least the required decay after a change of absolute constants for large $x$. Their theorem counts primes directly and does not assume RH. [Thorner and Zaman, Corollary 1.4 and Remark 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10878#page=3)

**Which exceptional zero?** At height zero the paper singles out a possible zero with $1-\beta\le1/[13\log(3M)]$. The release’s outer condition is $1-\beta<1/(8L)$. Its selector first looks in a narrower region of width $c_Z/\log(2M)$ with $0<c_Z\le1/20$. For $M\ge8$ that inner region lies inside the paper’s uniqueness region, and the paper’s region lies strictly inside the release’s outer one. A zero in the inner region is thus exactly the zero both must use. If the choices differ, any selected zero in either convention has $1-\beta\ge c_Z/\log(2M)\gg1/L$. In this case $x^{\beta-1}\ll e^{-c'\log x/L}$, so adding, removing or interchanging the secondary terms costs at most the allowed error after weakening $c'$. Indeed $B(x)\gg x/\log x$ here once the range constant is large enough. This also covers equality at either boundary. **Removing the weights.** In the difficult $a=1$ class put $h=1-\beta$ and $\mu=\min(1,h\log X)$. The lower endpoint of the integral defining $\operatorname{li}$ cancels in the difference. For $X^\beta\ge2$ there is the exact identity
$$B(X)=\int_\beta^1 X^v/v\,dv\ \ge\ (1-e^{-1})\,\mu X/\log X.$$
For $a=-1$ or $0$ take $\mu=1$ and use $B(X)\ge\operatorname{li}(X)\gg X/\log X$. Work on $[\sqrt X,X]$ and increase $C_0$ so $\sqrt X\ge M^{12}$. There $D(y)\ll y\mu$ because in the positive class $1-y^{-h}/\beta\le1-y^{-h}\le\mu$, and $1-y^{-h}/\beta\ge0$ at these scales since $\beta\ge1/2$ and $\log y$ is large. Thus $e(y)=\varphi(M)\vartheta(y;M,b)-D(y)$ is $O(y\mu\,E_X)$, where $E_X=\exp(-c'\log X/L)+\exp(-c'\sqrt{\log X})$. On this interval both exponential errors have lost at most a constant in their exponents.

Abel summation propagates the error as
$$\frac{e(X)}{\log X}-\frac{e(\sqrt X)}{\log\sqrt X}
 +\int_{\sqrt X}^X\frac{e(y)}{y\log^2 y}\,dy.$$
This is $O(\mu E_X X/\log X)$, hence $O(E_X B(X))$. Differentiation gives $B'(y)=D'(y)/\log y$ with the same lower-limit convention. The remaining boundary contribution $\varphi(M)\pi(\sqrt X;M,b)-B(\sqrt X)$ is $O(M\sqrt X)$. The lower bound on $\mu$ in the next paragraph makes its ratio to $B(X)$ at most $O(M^2\log X/\sqrt X)$, exponentially smaller on $\log X\ge24L$. This translates the independent weighted theorem to the release’s unweighted relative assertion, including the nearly cancelling class. **How small may $h$ be?** Only $hL\gg1/M$ is needed here. A nonprincipal real character inducing a zero $\beta$ in the outer strip has a primitive quadratic character of conductor $f\mid M$ with the same zero, since the missing Euler factors are nonzero at positive real $\beta$. The quadratic class number formula gives $L(1,\chi_f)\gg f^{-1/2}$ since the class number is at least 1, there are at most six roots of unity, and a real quadratic fundamental unit is at least $(1+\sqrt5)/2$. On $[\beta,1]$, $|L'(\sigma,\chi_f)|\ll(1+\log f)^2$. Sum through $f$ directly and use partial summation thereafter with the character partial sums bounded by $f$. The factor $f^{1-\sigma}$ is at most $e^{1/8}$. The mean value theorem gives $h\gg f^{-1/2}(1+\log f)^{-2}$. Thus $\eta=\min(1,hL)\gg M^{-1}$ and $\mu\ge\eta$ at the required scales. For no exceptional zero set $\eta=1$. The cited paper also states a sufficient lower bound for its exceptional zero. ## Where the released proof pays the same cost

The independent theorem establishes the AP endpoint even with different proof machinery. The release proves a stronger intermediate bound on the **sum over characters of absolute errors**. A per-class prime count by itself does not prove that stronger sum. The following checks reconstruct its relevant algebra and keep the density theorem, zero-free region, repulsion and explicit formula as named inputs. [Character-sum statements](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/JacobiCharacterSums/DeuringHeilbronnM.lean#L24-L51) · [Assembly](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/JacobiCharacterSums/PNTFinal.lean#L16-L25)

**The counted zeros.** In the large-modulus range take $T=M^{12}$, $\ell=\log(M(T+3))\asymp L$, $u=\log y/\ell$, $v_\rho=(1-\Re\rho)\ell$. The finite set includes multiplicities with a stated $O(\ell)$ bound per unit-height interval for each character. Partition occurrences greedily in order of ordinate into $O(\ell)$ lists per character with separation at least 1 in each list. The local multiplicity hypothesis supplies the number of colors, including repeated occurrences at the same point. Combining equal colors across characters and applying the stated density theorem gives weighted count $O(\ell^b e^{aV})$ for $v_\rho\le V$ and some fixed $a,b$. The zeros under consideration are in $\Re\rho>1-\kappa\ge0.95$, so the asserted density range suffices. Larger bins can keep the last admissible cutoff. Remove one copy of the selected exceptional zero if it is in the list. Any zero inside the narrow zero-free region is that selected zero and is simple. The remaining zeros have $v_\rho\ge c_*>0$. If an exceptional zero is outside the narrow region, its $hL$ is bounded below and the unremoved copies require no extra small factor. In the close case the release’s weaker repulsion input gives
$$v_\rho\ge c_2\log\!\left(\frac{c_1}{h\ell^N}\right)$$
when $h\ell<c_1$. This permits a fixed logarithmic loss. To see how it is paid, split $e^{-u v_\rho}$ into three equal exponential factors. The sum of the first is $O(\ell^b)$ by the preceding density bound and unit-width bins in $v$, once $u/3>a+1$. The second is at most $e^{-c_*u/3}$. The third is at most $h\ell^N/c_1$ if this is below 1 and $u/3\ge1/c_2$. In the remaining cases the trivial bound 1 is at most $C\eta\,\ell^{N+1}$, using $\ell\asymp L$, or the lower bound on $\eta$ outside the narrow region. This gives $O(\eta\,\ell^{b+N+1} e^{-c_*u/3})$. The range $u\ge C\log(\ell+2)$ absorbs the polynomial and leaves $O(\eta e^{-cu})$. A bound on the zero count alone would lose the factor $\eta$. **Undoing the smoothing.** The smoothed sum divided by $y$ is a finite von Mangoldt ramp. Difference it at $y$ and $(1+\delta)y$ and multiply by $y(1+\delta)/\delta$. The constant $K$ disappears exactly. Every $n\le y$ now has coefficient 1. The extra terms for $y<n\le(1+\delta)y$ have coefficients between 0 and 1 and cost at most $(\delta y+1)\log(2y)$. A zero term becomes $y^\rho/\rho$ times
$$(1+\delta)\delta^{-1}\int_1^{1+\delta}w^{\rho-2}\,dw.$$
Its error against $y^\rho/\rho$ is at most $6\delta y^{\Re\rho}$ for $0<\delta\le1/2$ and $1/2\le\Re\rho<1$. Indeed $|w^{\rho-2}-1|\le|\rho-2|(w-1)$, $|\rho-2|/|\rho|\le5$ and $1/|\rho|\le2$. The bound is independent of the ordinate. The principal logarithm term contributes an additional $O(\delta y)$ since $(1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)/\delta=1+O(\delta)$. The release allows constants 20 and 4 for these errors. Take the actual $\delta=M^{-5}$. The smoothed explicit-formula error is
$$E\ll M^{13C}y^{-\kappa/2}+(\log(2y))\ell^2/M^{12}.$$
After summing up to $M$ characters, its normalized contribution is $O(M^6 E)$. In the large-modulus branch $c_A\sqrt{\log y}<L$, so $\log y\ll L^2$. The term $M^{-6}O((1+\log y)\ell^2)$ is $O(M^{-2})$. The remaining power of $M$ is absorbed by $y^{-\kappa/2}$ since $\log y\ge C_0 L\log(L+2)$. The normalized short-interval cost $M(\delta+y^{-1})\log(2y)$ is likewise $O(M^{-2})$. A missing exceptional zero in the finite explicit formula must have $\beta<1-\kappa/2$ by its inclusion condition, and its normalized contribution is $O(y^{-\kappa/2})$. An included exceptional zero adds only one $O(\delta)$ smoothing term. Finally $e^{-c\log y/L}\ge M^{-1}$ for $c\le c_A^2$, and $\eta\gg M^{-1}$. The raw $O(M^{-2})$ errors all fit. In the other branch, if $L\le c_A\sqrt{\log y}$, the release starts with a one-character absolute error $O(y(e^{-c\sqrt{\log y}}+e^{-\sqrt{\log y}/2}))$. ## Consequence for the selected prime progression

For $P$ counting eligible primes between $\sqrt U$ and $U$, the lower bound from the AP main term is $\gg \eta U/(\varphi(M)\log U)$, before removing at most $\sqrt U$ entries. The progression moduli used in the Jacobi tests have $M=m_0s$, $s\le Y^A$, with $\log M\le5\log U/(\log Y)^2$ eventually for fixed $\epsilon,A$. Also $Y^{\epsilon/6}\le\log U\le Y^{\epsilon/5}(\log Y)^2$. It follows that subtracting the lower prefix costs $o(1)$ of the main term. For the original $m_0$ progression this in particular gives $|P|\ge U^{9/10}$ eventually. The two relative-error exponentials at the larger moduli are $o(Y^{-B})$ for every fixed $B$. Thus the proposed loss from normalizing by a nearly cancelled class count does not invalidate this step. ## Unresolved
