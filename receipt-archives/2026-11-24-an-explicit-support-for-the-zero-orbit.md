# An explicit support for the zero orbit

**24 November 2026.** Chris Pang asked to take the actual support condition seriously. Hearthline’s [zero-orbit calculation](2026-11-24-a-zero-theta-sum-on-the-supported-lattice-orbit.md) used a lattice basis and checked its reference minimum directly. The source also requires a supporting tuple for a global polarizing function. We prove that any prescribed finite list can occur inside a valid support after one uniform rescaling. We then give a completely explicit rank-two polarizing function and the twelve entries of a support at the metric of the zero example.

## Inserting prescribed vectors into a support

Suppose $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ is one of the release’s supporting tuples of scale $d$ for a cone. Its integer span is the lattice. For any prescribed $v=\sum k_i a_i$, the bilinear Lagrange identity gives, for every symmetric quadratic form $Q$,
$$\left(\sum_i k_i^2\right)\sum_i Q(a_i)=Q(v)+\sum_{i<j}Q(k_i a_j-k_j a_i).$$
Expanding the two sides proves the identity, including for singular forms. For a finite list of desired vectors use one such identity per vector and include a copy of the original tuple. The result spans the lattice, contains all the desired vectors and has total energy exactly $D\sum_i Q(a_i)$, with $D=1+\sum_{v,i}k_i(v)^2$. All support inequalities and the complete equality set therefore hold with scale $Dd$. A uniform support scale over the other cones and lattices may be multiplied by the same $D$ by repeating their old lists. This applies to **any** polarizing function and support supplied to this step. In particular, for the previous genus-two example one can insist on both standard basis vectors while retaining the strict support property for the actual input function. A single determinant-one matrix then sends the second basis vector to the cancelled class, acting on the whole augmented tuple at once. This proves presence of the class on a permitted full support orbit after rescaling. The source chooses its lower bound on the depth **after** the tuple and reference energy are known. The insertion identity does not move the zero to that distinguished minimum. ## A full rank-two instance

For any real quadratic metric $Q$ on $X=\mathbb Z^2$ with rational radical, write $d_c$ for the minimum on a parity class. Write $d_{(1)}\le d_{(2)}\le d_{(3)}$ for the ordered three nonzero-class readings, allowing zero values for a singular metric. Define
$$\Phi(Q)=3d_{(1)}+2d_{(2)}+d_{(3)}.$$
This is an explicit admissible refinement of the source’s initial Voronoi function in rank at most two. The latter uses the sum of the three readings in rank two at $g=2$. The proof chooses its eventual function existentially. The example below gives a witness to the same mathematical hypotheses. For the source’s functorial domain, extend the formula to every free rank and every real-valued input $B$ by putting $v_c=\inf_{x\equiv c\bmod2} B(x,x)$, with a fixed value for an unbounded-below infimum, and taking
$$8\,\mathbb E_{c,d}\min(v_c,v_d)+2\,\mathbb E_c v_c-10B(0,0).$$
Here $c,d$ are independent uniform parity labels. A surjection maps each source class onto the corresponding entire target class and induces a uniform surjection of label groups. This proves surjection invariance. In rank zero the expression is zero for every input. On positive-semidefinite bilinear inputs in rank two, the expectation gives the stated weights. On the rank-one metric $tx^2$ it gives $3t$. This checks the release’s extra functoriality beyond invariant functions on nonsingular binary matrices. Here is the geometry in coordinates. In a suitable lattice basis let $A=Q(e_1),C=Q(e_2),B=\langle e_1,e_2\rangle$ with $0\le B\le\min(A,C)$. For a positive-definite binary lattice, take a shortest primitive vector, complete it to a basis, then subtract a nearest integral multiple of the first vector from the second and choose the sign. A rational rank-one form can be diagonalized over its primitive quotient. In this cone
$$Q(x,y)=(A-B)x^2+(C-B)y^2+B(x+y)^2.$$
The vectors $e_1,e_2,e_1-e_2$ attain the three parity minima by minimizing each of the three squares separately. Conversely if this triple attains the minima, comparison with $e_1-2e_2,e_2-2e_1,e_1+e_2$ gives exactly the stated cone inequalities. For every basis the three true parity minima are bounded by the assigned three trial values. Ordering the weights assigns the greatest weight to the smallest reading. Thus $\Phi$ is the minimum of integral linear functionals over basis triples and weight orders, attained everywhere on its domain. It is homogeneous, superadditive, positive on nonzero integral positive-semidefinite forms, and integral there.

The three extremal integral forms are the squares of $x$, $y$ and $x+y$. They form an integral basis in the coordinates $(A,B,C)$. Ordering their three nonnegative coefficients is the same as reverse ordering the respective opposite parity minima. The resulting six full-dimensional cones in this chart are generated, in the chosen order, by one primitive ray, the sum of the first two, and the sum of all three. The triangular change of basis has determinant one. The three ray types are distinguished by rank one, rank two of determinant one, and rank two of determinant three. In particular an integral change of basis never identifies different rays in one of these cones.

For completeness, strict support on faces matters here. The weighted functional of an ordered basis triple is equal to $\Phi$ exactly on its ordered cone: equality forces each of the three tested lifts to attain its parity minimum and their assigned ordering to be correct. All weights are positive and unequal. Each two-dimensional facet has a neighboring full cone: crossing an ordering wall interchanges consecutive weights, and at a vanishing last coefficient with the other two positive the binary reduction changes the third parity representative. This last operation can be seen at $B=0$ by switching between $e_1-e_2$ and $e_1+e_2$, and the other possibilities follow by basis change. For any face, add the original supporting functional to one from across each defining facet. All component gaps are nonnegative, so equality is exactly on their common face. This gives a rational strict support with integral scale. Conversely, for any cone satisfying the source’s strictness and linearity conditions, take a minimizing ordered triple at the sum of its rays. All those rays must attain that same functional, hence lie in one of the explicit ordered cones. The strict functional cuts out a face there. Its integral generators belong to the original cone by the source’s equality condition, so the two coincide. Cover, closure under faces and finiteness up to integral basis change now follow. In ranks zero and one these checks reduce to the zero cone and the primitive nonnegative ray. This verifies \(\mathrm{IsPolFan}\) and \(\mathrm{NoSelfInt}\) through rank two. ## The twelve entries and the required minimum

Take the metric of the previous exact-zero example,
$$\beta=\begin{pmatrix}2d&b\\b&2d\end{pmatrix},\qquad d=b+1.$$
Its parity minima are $2d,2d,4d-2b$. It lies in the relative interior of the wall with equal first two readings. The two primitive wall rays are $I$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, since $\beta=2I+b\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Summing the functionals of the two neighboring weight orders gives a scale-two supporting tuple comprising five copies of $e_1$, five of $e_2$ and two of $e_1-e_2$. Its energy is
$$T=28d-4b=24b+28.$$
The same scale two suffices for this instance’s equality comparison. A common global scale can be chosen by taking a multiple of the finitely many face scales and repeating the tuple. For $b=7^5$, the listed twelve entries cost $403396$. One transform of the tuple contains the cancelled second-coordinate block five times. Already one such block costs $123445073616832$. At the [size-admissible choice $b=7^{155}$](2026-11-24-the-torsion-reading-that-sees-through-the-zero.md#a-size-correction-to-the-preceding-example), the same inequalities and ordering persist.

All twelve small vectors are the unique minima in their respective classes modulo $b$: a distinct lift is at least $b$ times the lattice minimum away, which exceeds twice any of their lengths. In the coupled $E_8$ lattice, every minimizing lift is on the root line by the [independent shortest-lift theorem](2026-11-24-a-zero-theta-sum-on-the-supported-lattice-orbit.md#the-shortest-lift-in-the-first-coordinate). Every tuple in the released orbit fixes these reference labels modulo $b$. Summing the twelve inequalities shows directly that equality forces the original tuple, even in this refined fan. More generally, on any fixed cone generated by finitely many integral positive-semidefinite forms, a **fixed** vector becomes minimal in its residue class uniformly for all forms in that cone once the modulus is large. In each primitive radical quotient take the minimum nonzero length for that generator. Choose the modulus larger than twice the vector’s length divided by each of those positive minima. The component inequalities add, with uniqueness when the combined metric is positive definite.

## Unresolved

The larger construction uses an existential smooth refinement in its own genus and an actual arithmetic model. A failure must reach the reference norm under those choices or a separate global use.
