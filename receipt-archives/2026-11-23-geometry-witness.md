# A mixed intersection that changes when the divisors are reversed

**Work key 01 · 23 November 2026 · Local counterexample established.** In a geometry model selected on the dependency path of the released RH theorem, the supplied intersection operation gives **2** for $(L,2L)$ and **8** for $(2L,L)$. On the corresponding flag surface, the classical intersection is **4** in both orders. The two explicit calls we located on the RH path use repeated copies of one divisor. On those inputs the model gives the correct diagonal degree. We have not established a failure of the RH deduction.

## 4. Work

Take two copies of the rank-two flag, so `Tau` has two elements and $N=2$. The constructor’s dimension formula gives $d=2$. Write the two weights as $(a_0,a_1)$ and $(b_0,b_1)$. Specializing the integer definition gives

$$
Q=2(a_0-a_1)(b_0-b_1).
$$

There are two permutations in each copy of the flag. In the squared linear form the coefficient of the term selecting coordinate $i$ from the first copy and $j$ from the second is $2a_i b_j$. Taking the four coefficients with their permutation signs gives the displayed formula. The executable check expands the square over the integers and performs that signed sum. [Dimension and permutation exponents](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/DegreeFormula/FlagDegFormula.lean#L9-L16) · [Integer coefficient definition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/LatticeGeometry/Inhabited.lean#L210-L221)

Put $(1,0)$ in both copies for $L$ and $(2,0)$ in both for $2L$. These are positive multiples of the same weight. On the classical flag surface $\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1$ they correspond to bidegrees $(1,1)$ and $(2,2)$. Each ruling has self-intersection zero and the two rulings meet once. Thus the genuine pairing of bidegrees $(a,b),(c,d)$ is $ad+bc$. Equivalently the common diagonal degree gives the mixed value by polarization: $(Q(3L)-Q(L)-Q(2L))/2=(18-2-8)/2=4$.

| Ordered divisors | Supplied `inter` | Classical intersection |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $L,L$ | 2 | 2 |
| $2L,2L$ | 8 | 8 |
| $L,2L$ | **2** | **4** |
| $2L,L$ | **8** | **4** |

The same distinction holds in dimension $d\ge2$ whenever $Q(L)\ne0$. With one $2L$ and all other entries $L$, moving $2L$ into the first slot changes the model’s value from $Q(L)$ to $2^d Q(L)$ by homogeneity. A multilinear intersection gives $2Q(L)$ in either order. The concrete example uses legal inputs to the generic constructor. Its occurrence at a particular analytic scale would be an additional claim.

## 5. Result

The mixed-intersection interpretation is refuted on the constructor’s own declared domain. The weaker diagonal contract survives. Diagonal values actually determine a symmetric bilinear form by polarization on this surface. The supplied operation does something else on mixed inputs. The second test separately shows that matching the stored row logs cannot certify a surface realization.

The empty case is motivated by the release itself. Its divisor producer starts from `x0`, applies `reQ` and constructs a geometry witness using `divXFl`. The chosen `divXFl` inherits surface fields from its input. The graft keeps the geometric part of its first argument and takes the row/log part of its second argument. At the weak interface the construction supplies `trivial` and a separate log comparison. The singleton perturbation above establishes a result for this interface. We did not assume it satisfies every later arithmetic bundle.
