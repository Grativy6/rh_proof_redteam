# A division by 34 where the denominator budget at 17 is zero

**23 November 2026.** In an actual arithmetic specialization of the toric denominator theorem, there are **34 ring classes**. The source recovers each class function from the character functions by dividing by 34. Its later bound permits **no denominator at 17**. The character matrix has rank 34 over characteristic zero and rank **two** in residue characteristic 17. There are explicit congruences, through order 16, which are necessary and sufficient to recover all 34 normalized values integrally. We checked where the inverse is used. It supplies complex analytic continuation. For integrality, the proof invokes a separate estimate on each individual lattice and then forms a sum. The [CM denominator calculation](2026-11-23-toric-cm-denominators.md) tests those actual values independently and proves integrality for this example. ## An allowed field, conductor and character

Let $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt7)$, $E=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt{-7})=F(i)$ and $K_d=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$. Use $d=7$, $s=5$, both CM embeddings extending $\sqrt{-7}\mapsto i\sqrt7$, and both derivative weights $\kappa_i=0$. The relative discriminant of $E/F$ is one. Indeed $d_E=(-4)^2(-7)^2=784$ and $d_F=28$. The roots of unity in $E$ comprise $\mu_4$, and the product over the two chosen embeddings is one on $\mu_4$. Take the prescribed period lattice $O_{K_d}=\mathbb Z[(1+i\sqrt7)/2]$. Take $\mathfrak f=(12+\sqrt7)$, of norm 137. This rational prime splits completely in $E$, as $12^2\equiv7$ and $37^2\equiv-1\bmod137$. The conductor exponent at the chosen place of $F$ is one. The auxiliary extra set $Q$ is empty. These data satisfy the field and base-conductor restrictions of the stated denominator theorem. They are not identified with a specific member of the final proof's asymptotic family. Both $F$ and $E$ have class number one. The real quadratic bound is $\sqrt7<3$ and $3+\sqrt7$ has norm two. The quartic Minkowski bound is $42/\pi^2<5$. At two the two primes have norm two and are principal. The integer $i+(1+\sqrt{-7})/2$ has relative norm $1+i$ down to $\mathbb Q(i)$, hence absolute norm two. Conjugation over $\mathbb Q(i)$ exchanges the two primes. At three both quadratic characters of $i$ and $\sqrt{-7}$ are inert, giving no prime of norm three. This accounts for every possible ideal norm below five.

The ring-class order is $O_F+\mathfrak f O_E$. Since $O_E$ has class number one, its ring-class group is the quotient of the residue-unit group at the conductor by diagonal units and global units. At the split pair, taking the ratio of the two coordinates identifies the first quotient with $\mathbb F_{137}^{\times}$. For a global unit $u$, that ratio is the reduction of $u/\bar u$, a root of unity. The full $\mu_4$ occurs. The element $u=(1+i)/(3+\sqrt7)$ has $u^2=i(8-3\sqrt7)$ a unit, and $u/\bar u=i$. Thus
$$
 \operatorname{Pic}(O_F+\mathfrak f O_E)\simeq\mathbb F_{137}^\times/\mu_4\simeq C_{34}.
$$

For an explicit permitted infinity type, on $K_d$ take the unramified unitary Hecke character with finite ideal value $(\bar a/a)^s$ on $(a)$ and infinity component $(z/\bar z)^s$. The imaginary quadratic class bound is $2\sqrt7/\pi<2$ and its only units are $\pm1$, so this is well defined. Pull it back by $N_{E/K_d}$. The result is trivial on the ideles of $F$, where the norm comes from the rational ideles, and has phase exponent $2s$ at the two CM embeddings. Multiply by any ring-class character of exact order 17. Its local components at the split conductor pair are inverse nontrivial tame characters. The finite conductor is exactly as stipulated, and the character remains trivial on $F$ because $F$ has class number one and its local units have diagonal residue. Twisting this base character by all 34 ring-class characters covers the full Fourier matrix. ## Where no power of 17 is available

There is one real ideal class. Its partial zeta value at $1-2s=-9$ is $\zeta_F(-9)=105911461682/33$. Using the even quadratic character $\chi_{28}$, this follows from $B_{10}=5/66$, $B_{10,\chi_{28}}=4236458467280$ and $\zeta_F(-9)=B_{10}B_{10,\chi_{28}}/100$. The reduced fraction after dividing by $2^{[F:\mathbb Q]}$ is $52955730841/66$. The other inputs to the bound are $v_2(d_E)/4=1$, $v_7(d_E)/4=1/2$ and the conductor exponent one at 137. With zero derivative weights the term `nl` reduces to $20c$ and the weight of the period is 20. The resulting nonzero exponents allowed in the denominator of the **twelfth power** are

| Rational prime | 2 | 3 | 7 | 11 | 137 |

| Exponent | 252 | 12 | 120 | 12 | 480 |

In particular the bound requires integrality at 17 for each class. The common normalization is $C_0=28^{9/2}(9!)^2$. Fix one $\Omega$ with $\Omega^{12}=\Delta(O_{K_d})$. Normalize each class term by the common factor $C_0/\Omega^{20}$ before taking a Fourier transform. The release's $\beta$ is the twelfth power after this normalization. Choosing a different common twelfth root changes the normalized terms by a unit. The genuine unnormalized character sums are nonzero in this example. Each lies within **0.202** of 1. To check this, choose one residue ratio per coset of $\mu_4$, with 1 representing the identity. Each representative idele is a finite unit, and its lattice consists of integers $x\in O_E$ whose two residues satisfy the corresponding ratio condition. At $\sigma=0$ the summand is $N_{E/K_d}(x)^{-10}$, of absolute value $|N_{E/\mathbb Q}(x)|^{-5}$, and the series converges absolutely. A unit ideal contributes precisely 1 in the identity class. For a nonzero integral ideal prime to the conductor there are four generators modulo $O_F^\times$, and exactly one of their ratios was selected across all 34 classes. If both residues vanish, all four occur in every class, but the ideal is then divisible by $\mathfrak f O_E$, of norm $137^2$. With only one residue zero it occurs nowhere. Since all character weights have absolute value one,
$$
 |T_\chi-1|\le \zeta_E(5)-1 +136\,137^{-10}\zeta_E(5)<0.202 .
$$
Here $\zeta_E(5)\le\zeta(5)^4\le(67/64)^4$ by summing the positive tail with an integral bound. The principal **class** term is real by conjugation and the same tail estimate puts it strictly above zero. This real estimate leaves the 17-adic question open. ## What inverse Fourier transformation would cost

Let $\zeta$ be a primitive 17th root and $\varpi=\zeta-1$, so $(\varpi^{16})=(17)$ in $\mathbb Z_{17}[\zeta]$. The $C_2$ transform is a unit at 17. For $C_{17}$ write $H_j=\sum_t z_t\zeta^{jt}$ and take ordinary forward differences. Then
$$
 \Delta^k H_0=\sum_t z_t(\zeta^t-1)^k,\qquad 0\le k\le16.
$$
The difference operation is an integral invertible triangular change of coordinates. After dividing row $k$ by $\varpi^k$, the matrix reduces to $(t^k)_{k,t}$ over $\mathbb F_{17}$. Its Vandermonde determinant is a unit. Thus the elementary-divisor exponents of the full matrix are $0,1,\ldots,16$, each **twice**. Equivalently, after undoing the $C_2$ transform, the 17 conditions $\Delta^k H_0\in\varpi^k O$ on each block are necessary and sufficient for integral normalized class values. The largest possible inverse loss is exactly one power of 17.

For an explicit counterexample to the inverse inference put $z_t={\bf1}_{t=0}+1/(17\cdot128)$ on $C_{34}$. All 34 character sums are 17-adic units. The trivial one is $1+2/128$ and the rest are one. All lie inside the same complex disk used above. Yet every $z_t^{12}$ has denominator exponent 12 at 17. Even the last finite-difference test is indispensable. Replace the common correction by $1/(256\cdot17^{1/16})$ using the positive real root. In the affected $C_{17}$ block each nonconstant forward difference at zero has valuation $15/16$. The first fifteen divisibilities hold and the sixteenth fails. Both arrays are counterexamples to the inverse inference. The CM evaluations are checked separately.

## Where the proof uses the inverse

The factor $1/34$ occurs in the construction of an entire **complex** lattice sum from the Hecke functions. The proof checks that it agrees with the raw series in its convergence region. In the arithmetic direction, `toricDenominator` chooses an equivalent idele which is one above the prime being tested. At 17 it applies `LatIntUnr` to the individual lattice and only then takes the character sum. It invokes the arithmetic bound for every embedding into the 17-adic closure. There is no inverse character transform in that deduction.
