# A degree the deep cover's root discriminant cannot see

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Christopher D. Pang's continuing public audit.** The deep-cover constructor takes a full ray class field $R$ of the quadratic field $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. On the selected zeta windows, its subextension over $K(\zeta_M)$ has degree $h(K)\varphi(M)/2$ and is unramified at every finite prime. Its relative different is the unit ideal. The symmetric projection back across that very extension has exact denominator $h(K)\varphi(M)/2$. This is an arithmetic place where the normalized discriminant loses the whole degree. The release's field-size budget also counts the logarithm of the degree. [Trace and integral descent on the window lattice](2026-11-23-window-trace-and-integrality.md)

## The field the construction actually chooses

Let $A$ be the coefficient field `T.Q.c.F` of the incoming core. The cover constructor uses the integer modulus $M=g_0g_1\,15^s$, where $s$ is the exponent in its cover level. The proof chooses the full ray class field $R$ of $K$ for the finite modulus $M\mathcal O_K$ and embeds it in $\mathbb C$. Its final component coefficient field is the compositum $N=AR$. Both coefficient records in the construction use this same compositum. The constructor shows $\zeta_M\in R$. The incoming field $A$ contains the host embeddings and hence $K$. This identifies the fields on this construction without equating the component field with the earlier `Ks`. Why the full ray class field? The release requires an abelian Galois extension, unramified away from the modulus, whose Frobenius elements generate the Galois group. An ideal prime to the modulus has trivial value under every character of its Artin symbol exactly when it is ray-principal. Characters separate a finite abelian group, giving the full ray group as the quotient. This is the class-field characterization with trivial subgroup of the ray class group. ## Exact size on the split windows

The primes $g_0,g_1$ split in the host, hence also in $K$. They are distinct and prime to 15 in this call. For sufficiently large selected windows the splitting conditions include 3 and 5, because each divides $d+1$. Thus every prime dividing $M$ splits in $K$ and $d$ is coprime to $M$. The only units of $\mathcal O_K$ are $\pm1$, with distinct residues modulo $M$. [Window conditions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L27-L37) · [Splitting of the type primes](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/NcardPrimesOverOfStabilizer.lean#L73-L93) · [Level coprimality in this call](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsGoodCoverOverSizedField.lean#L111-L122)

For $p^a\parallel M$, splitting and the Chinese remainder theorem give $\mathcal O_K/p^a\mathcal O_K\simeq(\mathbb Z/p^a)^2$ as rings, with componentwise multiplication. The exact sequence from residue units to the ray class group to the ordinary class group therefore gives
$$
[R:K]=h(K)\,\varphi(M)^2/2 .
$$
Write $L=K(\zeta_M)$. The fields $K$ and $\mathbb Q(\zeta_M)$ are linearly disjoint, since the first ramifies at $d$ and the second is unramified there. Consequently
$$
u=[R:L]=h(K)\varphi(M)/2 .
$$
The release uses the upper estimate $[R:K]\le h(K)M^2$. When $s\ge1$ and the type primes grow, the ratio of the exact degree to this upper bound tends to $\tfrac12(8/15)^2=32/225$. [The degree used in construction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsGoodCoverOverSizedField.lean#L74-L90) · [The general ray-field estimate](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/RayClassFieldDegreeLeAndRootDiscrBound.lean#L19-L37) · [Ray class group exact sequence](https://jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf)

Now fix a place of $K$ above $p^a\parallel M$. Its completion is $\mathbb Q_p$. Under local reciprocity its inertia image in $R/K$ is a quotient of $\mathbb Z_p^\times/(1+p^a\mathbb Z_p)$, of size $\varphi(p^a)$, because of the ray modulus. Already $L/K$ contains the totally ramified local cyclotomic extension $\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^a})$ of degree $\varphi(p^a)$. Thus the upper and lower inertia bounds agree at *each* prime over $M$, including 3 and 5 when $s>1$. The ray field is unramified outside $M$ over $K$. It follows that $R/L$ is unramified at every finite prime. [Local and global reciprocity, Milne](https://jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf) · [Prime-power cyclotomic ramification](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/154Page/handouts/cycint.pdf)

Write $\mathrm{rd}(F)=|D_F|^{1/[F:\mathbb Q]}$. From the unit relative different, coprime discriminants and the cyclotomic discriminant formula,
$$
 \mathrm{rd}(R)=\mathrm{rd}(L)
 =\sqrt d\, M\prod_{p\mid M} p^{-1/(p-1)}.
$$
For the cyclotomic factor, the prime-power discriminant has absolute value $p^{a\varphi(p^a)-p^{a-1}}$ by differentiating $\Phi_{p^a}$ at a primitive root. The factors for distinct primes multiply in the root discriminant by coprimality. Hence the release's size quantity on the selected ray field is exactly
$$
\operatorname{fieldSz}(R)=
  \log\!\big(h(K)\varphi(M)^2\big)+\tfrac12\log d+\log M
  -\sum_{p\mid M}\frac{\log p}{p-1}.
$$
With $h(K)\le d$, as used by the source, this is at most $\tfrac32\log d+3\log M$. The constructor allows $20(\log M+\log d+1)$ in addition to the core's field size. ## What averaging across this extension costs

On $R/L$, with $u=[R:L]$, the trace dual of $\mathcal O_R$ is $\mathcal O_R$. The trace image is all of $\mathcal O_L$. Locally at any finite prime this follows from the trace of the separable residue-field extension being onto and unramified reduction of the trace. In a product of local factors one may take an element supported in a single factor. A trace image that is the unit ideal at every prime is the unit ideal globally. This works even in residue characteristic dividing $u$. [Trace dual and relative different](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/252Page/handouts/galoisrep.pdf)

The unique Galois-invariant $L$-linear retraction is $P=\operatorname{Tr}_{R/L}/u$. On the complex fibre with the maximum norm its operator norm is 1, and
$$
P(\mathcal O_R)=u^{-1}\mathcal O_L .
$$
Thus exactly $u$ clears the denominator on the whole lattice. Every nonzero $L$-linear map taking $\mathcal O_R$ into $\mathcal O_L$ has maximum complex operator norm **at least $u$**. Write it as $\operatorname{Tr}(c\,\cdot)$, where $c$ is integral by the unit different. At an embedding $\sigma$ the norm is $\sum_{\rho\mid\sigma}|\rho c|$. Its geometric mean over $\sigma$ is at least $u|N_{R/\mathbb Q}(c)|^{1/[R:\mathbb Q]}\ge u$. The ordinary trace attains this lower bound among nonzero integral maps. A trace-one integral weight exists, giving an integral retraction, whose complex norm is also subject to the bound. [Detailed operator argument and the ramified comparison](2026-11-23-window-trace-and-integrality.md)

The same calculation follows the compositum all the way to $N$. Put $A'=AL=A(\zeta_M)$. Unramified extensions remain unramified after finite base change, since a finite étale algebra stays finite étale and one can select the field factor. Therefore $\mathrm{rd}(N)=\mathrm{rd}(A')$ exactly. If $v=[N:A']$, the symmetric retraction $N\to A'$ has denominator $v$, and any nonzero integral map in this direction has maximum complex operator norm at least $v$. Intersections with the core can reduce $v$. For a **fixed** admissible core, $v\ge u/[A:K]$ by the tower law, and $u$ grows without bound as the constructor's exponent $s$ increases. For changing cores on the selected windows the exact intersection with the ray field remains to be determined.

## Finite arithmetic check

Take $d=359$, $q=3$, $g_0=25{,}849$, $g_1=47{,}389$ and $s=1$. Both type primes are $1\bmod(4\cdot3\cdot359)$, and $3,5$ split in $K$. Reduced primitive positive forms of discriminant $-359$, grouped by leading coefficient $a=1,\ldots,10$, have counts $1,2,2,2,2,4,0,2,2,2$. These can be checked by taking $-a\le b\le a$, requiring $c=(b^2+359)/(4a)$ integral with $a\le c$, and keeping $b\ge0$ on a reduction boundary. Thus $h(K)=19$. Here $M=18{,}374{,}373{,}915$, $\varphi(M)=9{,}799{,}080{,}192$ and
$$
 [R:L]=93{,}091{,}261{,}824,\qquad \mathrm{rd}(R)/\mathrm{rd}(L)=1 .
$$
This finite example checks the split-modulus arithmetic. The field-degree and unramified assertions use the paper argument above. It does not require constructing a field of that degree or instantiating a whole zeta window.

## Unresolved

The normalized discriminant is insensitive to the entire unramified layer. The actual size proof includes its logarithmic degree, and the cusp-form slope theorem measures the forward pullback and a different-weighted lattice inclusion. An invalid inference would require a use that carries these estimates to the reverse projection without paying its degree. The extent to which the incoming core already contains the unramified layer is a further concrete question before making a claim about $v$ on moving windows.
