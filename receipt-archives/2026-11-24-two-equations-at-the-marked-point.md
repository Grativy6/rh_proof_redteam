# Two equations for the remaining marked point

**24 November 2026.** At the [fixed order-577 input](2026-11-24-a-class-inside-the-zeta-windows.md) the remaining Galois existence question can be tested in two invariant coordinates. The coefficients of the two equations below have **not** been computed. The reduction explains what would constitute a counterexample to the published finite-image step and what would independently establish an irreducible Galois lift for this datum. Automorphy is a further obligation.

Use the ring $R$ and its [three explicitly detected coordinates](2026-11-24-why-the-trace-field-must-ramify.md#a-complete-local-ring-with-three-coordinates) $X,U,V$ over $O=\mathbb Z_{577}$. Write $a=XU$, $b=X^2V$ and $k=\mathbb F_{577}$.

## Count the available obstructions

For the fixed determinant-one problem the residual Lie algebra splits over $G_\mathbb Q$ into the eight one-dimensional lines in the [tangent table](2026-11-24-three-deformation-directions-at-577.md#the-ordinary-tangent-space). The allowed local tangent condition $\mathcal L$ at 577 is full in an upper root, zero in a lower root, and unramified on the diagonal. It is unramified at other finite places. Apply global Poitou–Tate duality to each line $C$. In the dimension formula
$$h^1_{\mathcal L}(C)-h^1_{\mathcal L^\perp}(C^\vee(1))
=h^0(\mathbb Q,C)-h^0(\mathbb Q,C^\vee(1))
+\sum_v\big(\dim\mathcal L_v-h^0(\mathbb Q_v,C)\big),$$
the prime-to-577 local differences vanish. At the real place subtract one for an even character and zero for an odd one. Each off-diagonal character has no local $H^0$ or $H^2$ at 577 and its full local $H^1$ has dimension one. For the two diagonal lines the 577-adic unramified dimensions equal the corresponding local $H^0$ dimensions. For the trivial diagonal the global $H^0$ term cancels the real-place contribution. [Wiles, `1, dual Selmer dimension formula](https://www.williamstein.org/edu/Fall2003/252/references/wiles-flt/Wiles-Modular_Elliptic_Curves_and_Fermats_Last_Theorem.pdf)

The dual Selmer dimension is zero in seven rows. It is **two** for the even lower line $C=z^{-1}$: its actual strict dimension is one and the right side of the formula is $-1$. The dual character is $\omega z$. In it there is one everywhere unramified line, from the order-577 minus class group, and one additional local degree of freedom at 577. The obstruction to lifting the pure lower class to coefficient order $577^2$ persists even with the local condition at 577 relaxed. The radicand character is nontrivial on inertia there, forcing all its valuations to be multiples of $577^2$ even in that test. The [unit-times-577 divisor multiplier](2026-11-24-the-577-class-on-paper.md#prove-the-upper-bound) then excludes such a lift. Thus the integral obstruction is visible already to the dual strict line.

There is also a class-field count of the two-dimensional dual space. The Kummer character for the odd dual coefficient $\omega z$ is $z^{-1}$, an even character with nontrivial inertia at 577. In its faithful real field of degree 576 the valuations of a relaxed eigen-radicand are all multiples of 577. The even global-unit eigenspace has rank one by the real unit theorem. The class-group quotient supplies one more dimension: its $z^{-1}$ component modulo 577 is dual to the already computed original strict group by class field theory, since the prime classes at 577 have nontrivial stabilizer on this character. The order-577 parts of the class group killed by 577 and reduced modulo 577 have the same dimension in that component. Exactness of Kummer theory on the prime-to-577 character component gives $1+1=2$ independently. In the dual strict condition the faithful CM field is the previous degree-64 mirror. Its only 577-primary minus direction is detected by the Jacobi radicand’s divided divisor, which generates the order-577 class of character $\omega z$.

The local matrix conditions used here are liftable across small coefficient extensions. At 2 and 7 fixed inertia has order prime to 577. It lifts by conjugation, and its Frobenius transporter is a torsor under the smooth centralizer (a torus for the three distinct characters at 2, and the orthogonal group for the outer involution at 7, with 2 invertible). At other primes away from 577 the unramified condition lifts by choosing Frobenius. At 577 lift the unramified changes of the diagonal first. Then lift the triangular matrices. The strictly upper Lie algebra has zero local $H^2$ as in the [width check](2026-11-24-the-companion-with-the-same-full-trace.md#check-the-width-locally). The stable ordered inertia flag is unique. After putting it in the standard basis, an element on the nonidentity multiplier coset also preserves the corresponding upper flag because the reversed dual flag has the same ordered inertia characters. Thus one can use triangular matrices in the semidirect product on the full decomposition group. Conjugating the chosen basis back adds formally smooth frame variables.

For clarity on the relation bound, choose arbitrary global matrix lifts across a square-zero extension and choose permissible local lifts. Their multiplication defect is a global two-cocycle, locally a coboundary. After correcting a trivial global class, the remaining mismatches are local one-cocycles modulo the allowed tangent conditions and global changes. Poitou–Tate supplies the exact sequence from the cokernel of this local restriction map through the dual Selmer space to the global $H^2$ classes vanishing locally. This gives the usual obstruction space, of total dimension at most two here. All maps split according to the residual coefficient lines. Normalizing $c$ and the mark after a lift adds no obstruction: a near-identity change fixes $c$ by the order-two equation, then the separated spectral lines fix the mark. This argument uses framed matrices and remains applicable to the reducible residual representation. [Local-to-global presentation method](https://www1.iwr.uni-heidelberg.de/fileadmin/groups/arithgeo/templates/data/Gebhard_Boeckle/LocalGlobalRev3.pdf)

Indeed, if $I$ is the kernel of the minimal presentation $A=O[[X,U,V]]\to R$, the dual of $I/\mathfrak m_A I$ injects into the obstruction space. Push out the universal small extension $A/\mathfrak m_A I\to R$ along a functional on its kernel. A lift changes the chosen coordinates only by kernel elements. All relations lie in $\mathfrak m_A^2$ because the coordinates are a relative tangent basis and vanish on the integral base point. Their images cannot change under those adjustments, so a zero obstruction forces the functional to vanish. One may take finite truncations first, with $I\cap\mathfrak m_A^n\subset\mathfrak m_A I$ by Artin–Rees. The construction and the local duality sequence are equivariant under the diagonal torus and the duality involution.

## The equations

The pure-axis relation which restricts to $577X$ is nonzero in $I/\mathfrak m_A I$. Both possible relation generators have its same torus weight $-1$ and positive duality sign, because the obstruction space has just the one coefficient line with multiplicity two. This identification holds after every unramified residue extension, so it determines the integer torus weight. Choose homogeneous generators by the projections in the [ramification proof](2026-11-24-why-the-trace-field-must-ramify.md#a-forced-integral-relation) and Nakayama. There are at most two. They have the form
$$I=\big(X f_1(a,b),\;X f_2(a,b)\big),\qquad
f_i(-a,-b)=f_i(a,b),\quad f_1(0,0)=577,\quad f_2(0,0)=0.$$
A redundant second generator may be zero. The constant terms follow from the exact pure-axis quotient, after an integral change of generators.

In characteristic 577 the first *possible* terms in the original parameters are $X^3U^2$, $X^4UV$ and $X^5V^2$, of degrees five, six and seven. No term through degree four can impose a relation there. The Lie algebra allows such higher terms. With $L=E_{21}-E_{32}$, $P=E_{12}+E_{23}$ and $Q=E_{13}$, one has $[L,Q]=P$ and $[[P,[[P,L],L]],L]=3L$. Replacing one or both occurrences of $P$ by $[L,Q]$ gives the other two monomial types. A nonzero Lie bracket does not compute the global obstruction coefficient. [Monomial and Lie bracket replay](marked-directions-577/matrix_checks.py)

There is a finite-order test before either alternative. In characteristic 577 the simultaneous assignment $X=U=V=t$ already gives an ordinary representation over $k[t]/(t^5)$, and $a=t^2$ detects a directed trace difference there. Setting $X=V=t$ and $U=0$ works through $k[t]/(t^7)$. For a sharper probe set $X=t$, $U=c t^2$ and $V=t$. Then $a=c t^3$ and $b=t^3$. The first test at order $t^7$ is the pair $Q_i(c,1)$, where $Q_i$ is the homogeneous quadratic part of $\bar f_i$. For the direction at infinity use $U=t^2,V=0$. A computation of the obstruction vector in a common dual basis at $c=0,1,\infty$ would determine both quadratics by polarization. A yes/no lifting test instead needs to account for common roots over the algebraic closure. A common root of two binary quadratics is already defined over an extension of degree at most two unless both vanish identically. A finite Artin ring with nilpotents is outside the DVR hypothesis of `FinEnd`.

## A falsifiable alternative

Consider $D=O[[a,b]]/(f_1,f_2)$.

* If $D/577$ has positive dimension, take a one-dimensional domain quotient, normalize a branch and complete. This gives $a,b$ in $t k'[[t]]$, at least one nonzero, for a finite residue extension $k'$. Substitute $t^3$ for $t$, then put $x=t$, $u=a(t^3)/t$ and $v=b(t^3)/t^2$. All are valid local coordinates and the two relations vanish. The resulting characteristic-577 representation is irreducible over the algebraic closure of $k'((t))$ by the marked-subspace test. It contradicts `FinEnd`. In fact, shrinking its purported finite-index subgroup to a normal core inside $G_K$ and the residual kernel would make every image on that core unipotent. A group of unipotent matrices has a common fixed vector over the algebraic closure, and normality and irreducibility make the core act trivially. A finite image in characteristic 577 has constant traces. The invariant pair/triple trace test gives a nonzero directed difference of positive $t$-adic order because at least one of $a,b$ is nonzero. Such a trace difference cannot come from a finite image. The symmetric mark can be returned to the source mark over the same complete DVR. * If $D/577$ is zero-dimensional, $D$ is finite flat over $O$ of rank at least four. Completeness and finite special fiber give finiteness. Two equations in the three-dimensional regular local ring leave dimension at least one. Finiteness forces height two, hence a complete intersection with no vertical associated prime. The special fiber has no linear relations and at most two independent initial quadratics, so at least one quadratic survives in addition to $1,a,b$. A characteristic-zero point of $D$ has $a,b$ in the maximal ideal and at least one is nonzero because $f_1(0,0)=577$. After a finite ramified extension choose $x$ of sufficiently small positive valuation that $a/x$ and $b/x^2$ remain in the maximal ideal (ignore zero entries in the valuation minimum). These coordinates give an irreducible ordinary Galois point. Rank counts multiplicity and does not assert four distinct characteristic-zero systems.

For the second alternative the ordinary point also meets the released selected-component condition at 577 for this residual datum. The integral triangular local functor with the fixed inertia diagonal is formally smooth over its unramified diagonal variables by the vanishing of the indicated local $H^2$. It has a framed power-series ring. Add a near-identity local change of basis, which is again smooth. This integral domain contains the diagonal base and specializes to any such ordinary local point. On it the source's inertia characteristic-polynomial and ordered-product equations vanish. The discriminant used in saturation is nonzero as a power series: specialize to diagonal $\operatorname{tor}(\Psi\nu_T)$ with $\nu_T(\mathrm{Fr})=1+T$ unramified. The infinite-order inertia element separates the three roots in characteristic zero, and the designated Frobenius has positive residue degree, so both required adjacent differences are nonzero functions of $T$. A minimal prime of the saturated radical inside the family kernel therefore passes through the base, and the selected ideal vanishes at the given specialization. This is the same ideal argument as in the [earlier marked example](2026-11-24-the-two-tangent-signs.md#does-it-lie-on-a-component-through-the-base-point-at-5). Conversely the ordinary inertia equations already force the local flag here. With an inertia matrix diagonalized at three unit-separated roots, placing it in the two outside factors of the ordered product kills the three lower entries of any other inertia matrix. Using it twice on one side isolates the first column or the last row and fixes the diagonal. The decomposition group preserves the resulting ordered flag. ## Unresolved

Determining the common zero set of $\bar f_1,\bar f_2$ is the missing arithmetic calculation. Relatively prime initial homogeneous quadratics would prove a finite special fiber. A shared initial direction alone would leave higher terms to test. An actual nonconstant characteristic-577 arc would refute the finite-index claim on this in-window datum.
