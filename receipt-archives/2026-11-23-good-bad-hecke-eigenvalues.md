# One residual Hecke system contains both good and bad rows

At either host prime $p=q,d$, the same loss appears in the split Hecke measurements of the release's full rank-$r$ row space $W$. At a sufficiently large trivial-character indefinite stage, assume the release's regular-embedding and bad-rank estimates and its stated rationality and lattice preservation for the row Hecke operators. A single residual system then contains distinct characteristic-zero eigenforms on both sides of the good-type cut. On the family constructed below one can compute **exactly** the denominator required to select the good eigenforms using the integral Hecke algebra. A one-entry diagonal matrix explains why transporting these forms to the larger $V$ space by the same twist fails.

## From local shifts to automorphic forms

Write $F=E^+$ for the real subfield of the host and $T$ for the norm-one torus of $E/F$. For a finite place $v$ of $F$, write $U_v$ for its integral units in $T(F_v)$. Put $U=\prod_v U_v$. At a marked split place $U_v$ is identified with the units of $F_v$ by choosing the marked half. Let $K_T$ be the subgroup of $U$ reducing to $1$ at every marked half. The principal row level in `datWp` has determinant in $K_T$, and the Whittaker subgroup used to form the type lattice has unipotent reduction, hence determinant $1$ there. The quotient $C=T(F)\backslash T(\mathbb A_f)/K_T$ is finite. Modulo $U$, the valuations of a norm-one idele give a fractional ideal with opposite exponents at conjugate split primes and exponent zero elsewhere. Each such ideal is $J/\bar J$ for a fractional ideal $J$ of $E$. Passing to principal ideals sends $(a)$ to $(a/\bar a)$, with $a/\bar a\in T(F)$. Thus the quotient modulo $T(F)U$ is an image of the finite ideal class group of $E$, and $U/K_T$ is finite. The image of $U$ in $C$ is $U/(K_T\mu(E))$, since a norm-one global unit in a CM field has every conjugate on the unit circle. The prior host calculation gives $\mu(E)=\{\pm1\}$. Every product of the even local determinant characters therefore descends to this subgroup. Each extends to $C$ by adjoining one generator at a time and taking the requisite root. A character of $p$-power order can be extended with $p$-power order by taking roots in $\mu_{p^\infty}$. The global order may exceed the local order. [Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Theorem 4.4](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANTc.pdf)

On finite level translates it induces precisely the prescribed determinant twist, up to reversing every shift if inverse translates are used. ## What the split Hecke operators actually measure

Use the release's normalized split operators $T_{w,k}$ for $0\le k\le r$. Omit any finite set of places containing those above $p$, the levels and the exceptional primes, together with their conjugates. Take a common coefficient DVR $O$ over $p$ containing the eigenvalues and character values. A finite extension suffices since the rational operators act on one finite-dimensional space. If $a_{w,k}$ is the eigenvalue on $f$ and $h_{w,k}$ a double coset representative, the function of $\xi\in B$ measured by this operator is

$$
\xi\longmapsto a_{w,k}\,\xi(\det h_{w,k}).
$$

The determinant character is constant through the double coset, so this follows term by term. All $a_{w,k}$ are integral by the release's local lattice bound. All values of a $p$-power character reduce to $1$ at every place above $p$. Thus every twist in this family has the same residual system at all the retained split operators. The central operator $k=r$ is one translate. At $w$ it is represented by the scalar $z_v I_r$ for the norm-one idele $z_v$ which has a uniformizer on the chosen half and the reciprocal on the other. The conjugate prime gives its inverse, up to compact units away from the level. Their **normalized** operators have product $(\mathrm Nw)^{-r n_V}$ times the identity. Let $\widehat B$ denote the characters of the finite group $B$. Evaluation at the $z_v$ for all retained split $v$ generates $\widehat B$. To see this, take $\xi\in B$ trivial on these elements. It is already unramified there. At every other place outside the omitted set the local torus is compact, so $\xi$ is trivial there too. The diagonal $T(F)$ is dense in the product of the local tori at the omitted places. Indeed each specified local norm-one element is $y_v/\bar y_v$ by Hilbert 90, also directly at a split pair. Weak approximation in $E$ approximates the finitely many $y_v$ simultaneously. Since $\xi$ is continuous and trivial on $T(F)$, its remaining local values are trivial. This proves the generation by finite abelian duality. The determinant of $z_v I_r$ is $z_v^r$, and $p>r$ makes the $r$-th power map invertible on $\widehat B$. [Milne, *Class Field Theory*, II.1.23](https://jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf) · [Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Chapter 7](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANTc.pdf)

Consequently the image of the $O$-algebra generated by the split operators on this family of eigenvalues is exactly the group ring $O[\widehat B]$ in its character-evaluation representation inside $O^B$. One inclusion follows from the double coset formula. For the reverse inclusion the integral algebra contains every generating central character function, since its scalar $a_{w,r}$ is a unit. Finite-order powers give their inverses. Character independence in characteristic zero makes the evaluation faithful. In particular the $|B|$ systems are pairwise distinct in characteristic zero even after any further finite omission of primes. Their common residual eigenvalues have an entire family above them.

## How much the good selector costs

Each fibre of $B\to\Theta$ has $|\ker(B\to\Theta)|$ elements. Exactly $|\ker(B\to\Theta)|\prod_v(n_v-b_v)$ systems are good. In the fraction-field group ring the selector is the indicator of the preimage of the good subset of $\Theta$. Its Fourier coefficients vanish outside the embedded $\widehat\Theta$. On $\widehat\Theta$ they have denominators dividing $|\Theta|$, since their numerators are sums of roots of unity. Its identity coefficient is

$$
\prod_v\frac{n_v-b_v}{n_v}.
$$

Every numerator is a $p$-unit. The clearing ideal of this selector **in the measured Hecke algebra on this family** is exactly $|\Theta|O=p^{\sum_v a_v}O$. This agrees with the previously forced lattice exponent on the selected slices. The kernel of the global-to-local character map adds multiplicity to the family and cancels in the normalized Fourier averages.

For just two systems with quotient character of order $p^h$, the image is $\{(a,b)\in O^2:a-b\in(1-\zeta_{p^h})O\}$. Every character function has difference in that ideal, and some character function has a primitive quotient value, giving the reverse inclusion after adding constants. The clearing cost for this pair has $p$-valuation $1/\varphi(p^h)$, normalized by $v_p(p)=1$. Restoring the whole family can cost much more. For a hand check take $B=C_{25}\to\Theta=C_5$ with zero the only bad shift. In $O[t]/(t^{25}-1)$ the good selector is $(4-t^5-t^{10}-t^{15}-t^{20})/5$. Evaluation gives zero at the five characters indexed by multiples of five and one at the other twenty. Multiplying its numerator by itself gives five times the numerator. Two independent copies have exact denominator $25$.

## The fixed larger level stops the same twist

The larger $V$ space has rank $r+t$ and fixes a character on an extra $t$-dimensional block at every marked split place. In the release's `datVt` the block count is one and the block determinant is read through the prescribed $\chi_{i+1,v}^{-1}$. The full rank-$r$ row has block count zero. In the chosen split integral frame take $\operatorname{diag}(I_r,a,1,\ldots,1)$, with $a$ an arbitrary local unit in the extra block. Self-duality of the framed lattice makes the inverse adjoint on the conjugate half integral. Put identity at every other place. This element belongs to the $V$ level since its top block is the identity and its reduction is block diagonal. Its prescribed scalar is $\chi_{i+1,v}(a)^{-1}$. Twisting a $V$ form on the whole determinant would multiply that scalar by $\xi_v(a)$.
