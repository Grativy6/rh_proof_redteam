# The Gaussian height bound for the actual jet construction

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Chris Pang’s public audit request.** There are two ways an infinite or unnecessarily fine expansion could hide a height in this row. The fine local level could contribute more coefficients than the bound pays for, and high derivatives could grow faster than its factorial allowance. The finite Fourier calculation and a Gaussian integral test both losses. This derives the analytic bound with its stated local input $\beta$ and the volume factors of the actual matrix entries. The arithmetic estimates for the particular data assigned to $\beta$ and to the finite places remain separate.

## Follow the change of level

Use the [coset notation and lift calculation](2026-11-23-coset-jets-over-one-field.md). The local coefficient $\Xi$ is supported on $H_S$ and is constant modulo $cH_S$. The entry is initially formed at the finer level $k=cc'$. For $x\in H_S$, choose $t$ in the integral period lattice $P$ lifting $x\bmod cP$ at all selected primes; the Chinese remainder theorem and the local unit condition allow this. Since $W_{-t}e_{Y,Y'}=e_{Y,Y'}$, the formula for the indicator is
$$
 D_c(t)=\frac1{n_c}\sum_{y\in B(c)/(P\cap B(c))}
  e^{2\pi i e(t,y)}W_y e_{Y,Y'},\qquad n_c=|H_S/cH_S|.
$$
Indeed the index in the denominator has contributions only at $\mathfrak p\in S$, of sizes $[P_{\mathfrak p}:cP_{\mathfrak p}]$. The quotient indexing $y$ has the dual contributions at the conjugate primes. The trace pairing of $P_{\mathfrak p}$ with $P_{\bar{\mathfrak p}}$ is perfect by self-duality, so these are exactly the finite characters of $H_S/cH_S$. Different integral lifts of $t$ have the same character values. Components at unselected primes contribute only integral pairings. Under the surjection $H_S/kH_S\to H_S/cH_S$, summing a fine character on one fiber vanishes unless it comes from the coarse group. In the latter case it is the coarse character times $n_k/n_c$. For these common frequencies choose the same rational representative $y$ in the two period sums: prescribe its class at the conjugate primes, take it in $kP$ at the selected primes and integral elsewhere. These local requirements are compatible by the Chinese remainder theorem. This ensures the same $W_y e_{Y,Y'}$ occurs at both levels. The factor $n_k/n_c$ cancels against the fine denominator, giving $\sum_{t'\mapsto t}D_k(t')=D_c(t)$. Thus the possibly large $c'$ leaves no factor in this particular entry. This uses the actual support and period of $\Xi$; taking absolute values before regrouping would lose it. If $|\sigma\Xi(t)|\le\beta$ at every relevant embedding, the normalized finite Fourier coefficients of $\Xi$ have sum of squares at most $\beta^2$ by orthogonality. Their sum of absolute values is at most $\beta\sqrt{n_c}$. Under a field embedding all roots of unity of the finite pairing are raised to a power prime to their order. The resulting characters are still distinct. Also $n_c\le c^{[E:\mathbb Q]rs}$ by the local index formula on the rank $rs$ Hom module. The release charges $\beta c^{2[E:\mathbb Q]rs}$, which covers this coefficient cost. Here $r$ and $s$ are the source and target ranks. ## Bound the actual translated function

The same absolute norm survives conjugating a move of the vacuum at nonstandard $Y,Y'$. To justify this extension of the [standard-vacuum calculation](2026-11-23-theta-transport-at-a-place.md), write $F_*$ for the elliptic product in fourfold coordinates, repeated for the $rs/4$ groups; both ranks are even. Use its product period lattice $P_*$. Every arithmetic lattice used in constructing $e_{Y,Y'}$ is a finite sum of columns over $K$ times the elliptic period lattice in these coordinates. For large $m$ all are between $mP_*$ and $P_*/m$. For any section $G$ formed by the finite averages, $R_G(w)=G(mw)/F_*(mw)$ is an algebraic rational function on the elliptic product, by the finite torsion expression. For a rational translating vector $x$ one has the exact identity
$$
 \frac{(U_x G)(mw)}{F_*(mw)}
   =R_G(w+x/m)\,\frac{(U_x F_*)(mw)}{F_*(mw)}.
$$
The first factor transports by the algebraic point $[x/m]$ and the second by the elliptic translation calculation, up to a root of unity. This gives one phase for the *whole* translate of $G$, independent of the summands used to represent $G$. Integral columns on $m^2$-torsion transport all of the lattices and their inclusions simultaneously. The period-eigenvalue identity for $G$ transports by the same ratio, so its Gaussian square is periodic on the corresponding receiving lattice. In the next normalized average the distinct translation classes remain orthogonal on a common cover; every coefficient still has absolute value $1/\sqrt n$. Starting at $F_*$ and applying this argument through the two moves shows the receiving norm of $e_{Y,Y'}$ equals the receiving standard-vacuum norm. The determinant-character and $\kappa$ factors have absolute value one. No comparison of unrelated choices of phase in different terms is needed. [Divided torsion and the rational-function argument](2026-11-23-theta-transport-at-a-place.md) · [Finite field of the determinant character](2026-11-23-phase-of-a-moved-theta-row.md)

In coordinates normalized by $H$, write $D=frs$ for the complex dimension. A nonzero receiving period vector has $q(x)=\sqrt d\,H(x,x)/2$ a positive integer. Indeed the receiving CM lattice comes from an invertible ideal of $K$ with its scale, and the same trace-dual calculation applies in the conjugated embeddings. Thus $H(x,x)\ge 2/\sqrt d\ge 1/v$ for $v=\operatorname{rd}_E\ge\sqrt d$. The symplectically self-dual lattice has Euclidean covolume one in these coordinates. The ball of radius $R=1/(2\sqrt v)$ is injective in the torus up to its boundary. Translate the holomorphic section to any chosen center using $U$, apply the submean inequality to its squared absolute value, then restore its Gaussian weight. Since $\operatorname{vol} B_R=\pi^D R^{2D}/D!$, this bounds the supremum of the weighted square by
$$
 e^{\pi/(4v)}D!(4v/\pi)^D\,\|e_{Y,Y'}\|^2
 \le 3(2Dv+1)^D\,\|e_{Y,Y'}\|^2 .
$$
The same bound holds for every translate because the weighted density is just shifted. In particular the Gaussian-growth prefactor for the actual regrouped sum at an embedding is bounded by the release's $\beta c^{2[E:\mathbb Q]rs}\sqrt{3(2Dv+1)^D}\exp(\operatorname{logThAt}/2)$. This supplies a frame for the entire function before taking its derivatives. ## Take the derivative without dropping its factorial

Consider one complex block of an $s$-by-$r$ matrix variable, with $r<s$ on the actual edges. Let $\nu_1\ge\cdots\ge\nu_r\ge0$ be the integer row weights, $n=\sum\nu_i$, and $\phi(y)=y\log y-y$ with $\phi(0)=0$. The input dominant weight differs from $\nu$ by a constant shift and the output differs from padded $\nu$ by a shift. The polynomial part of the released operator first multiplies by the determinant to the power $\nu_r$, then substitutes $Z^{\mathsf T}X$ in the first $r$ columns of its frame. Finally it contracts the monomials in $Z$ with the rescaled derivatives. Write $T(Z)$ for the polynomial substitution operator at a fixed $Z$. [Weights and claimed allowance](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/DivisorBase/PaddedLastEntry.lean#L10-L35) · [Determinant, substitution, jet contraction and retraction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/KernelLattices/ThDistC.lean#L20-L39)

For the Fock norm divided by the squared norm of the highest-weight vector, one has
$$
 \|T(Z)\|\ \le\ \prod_{i=1}^r s_i(Z)^{\nu_i}
$$
for the decreasing singular values of $Z$. Here are the representation-theoretic details behind this inequality. The characteristic-zero module of products of flag minors of shape $\lambda^\natural$ is the irreducible polynomial Schur module of that highest weight. Multiplication by the full determinant shifts all its weights by $\nu_r$ and maps its highest vector to the one of shape $\nu$. Its two normalized unitary-invariant norms agree under this map by irreducibility, since the determinant character is unitary on $U(r)$. Padding with zero rows and extending the polynomials to $s$ variables preserves the Fock inner product and the norm of that vector. Substituting a general matrix gives a vector of the target Schur module by Cauchy–Binet for each flag minor. Thus the arbitrary complementary projection in `retrVV` acts as the identity on these outputs.

The singular-value decomposition now reduces the norm to positive diagonal substitution. The weight spaces are orthogonal under the compact diagonal torus. For each semistandard tableau of shape $\nu$, entries up to $j$ can occupy only the first $j$ rows, by strict increase down columns. Hence the weight has at most $\nu_1+\cdots+\nu_j$ entries with labels at most $j$. For decreasing positive singular values this bounds its diagonal monomial by $\prod_i s_i^{\nu_i}$, by summation by parts; zero singular values follow by continuity. These are classical characteristic-zero representation facts. The source's Gram normalization is essential in the comparison between ranks. After rescaling the coordinates, suppose $|G(Z)|\le S\exp(\|Z\|^2/2)$. The derivative contraction is the ordinary Gaussian integral of $G(Z)T(\bar Z)$ against $e^{-\|Z\|^2}dZ/\pi^{rs}$. Angular integration at fixed radii selects the Taylor coefficient of each monomial, and radial integration supplies its multi-index factorial. This uses the Taylor expansion only on compact tori. The integral of a polynomial times $e^{-\|Z\|^2/2}$ controls the full integral. Thus no reordering of an uncontrolled tail is involved.

For $n>0$ take $h=1/(n+1)$. Keep $e^{-h\|Z\|^2/2}$ for integration. The other part times the bound for $T$ is at most
$$
 (1-h)^{-n/2}\exp\left(\tfrac12\sum_i\phi(\nu_i)\right)
 \le\sqrt e\,\exp\left(\tfrac12\sum_i\phi(\nu_i)\right),
$$
by maximizing each $s_i^{\nu_i}e^{-(1-h)s_i^2/2}$ separately. The remaining normalized Gaussian integral equals $(2/h)^{rs}=(2n+2)^{rs}$. For $n=0$ take $h=1$ and the polynomial factor is constant. The proof multiplies over the embedding blocks even for a nonproduct $G$, since the growth majorant and the test operators factor there. Each block has fixed degree $n_\tau$. Its argument and derivative scales contribute $|c_{D,\tau}r_\tau|^{2n_\tau}=\Omega_\tau^{-n_\tau}$. Squaring the bound gives precisely
$$
 S^2\prod_\tau \Omega_\tau^{-n_\tau}
      e(2n_\tau+2)^{2rs}\exp\left(\sum_i\phi(\nu_{\tau i})\right).
$$
The factor $e^{-\|Z\|^2/(2(n+1))}$ retained in the integral is the explicit decay margin for the tail. ## An adversary that saturates the growth condition

Take one block, $\nu=(m,\ldots,m)$, trivial source polynomial, and $G(Z)=\exp(-r\phi(m)/2)\det(Z_{\rm top})^m$. Its optimal Gaussian-growth constant is one by Hadamard's inequality, with equality on a diagonal of singular values $\sqrt m$. Only the top $r$ rows in the substitution contribute. The output is the target's highest vector times
$$
  e^{-r\phi(m)/2}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{(m+j)!}{j!}.
$$
For the factorial product, integrate $|\det Z|^{2m}$ against a normalized complex Gaussian on $r$-by-$r$ matrices. Successive independent Gaussian columns projected off their predecessors have squared lengths with gamma shapes $r,r-1,\ldots,1$. Iterated conditional expectations give the displayed product. For $r=2$ the polynomial contraction also gives $(m+1)(m!)^2$ directly by the binomial theorem. For fixed $r$, Stirling's formula leaves an excess $r^2\log m+O_r(1)$ in the log squared norm over the main $r\phi(m)$ term. This disproves a bound that keeps only the main entropy term and a constant. The released polynomial allowance pays for it, including at growing ranks by the preceding integral proof. ## One local factor of the terminal entry

At a type prime $\mathfrak p$ of norm $q$, the actual terminal `ftY` factor is zero if any coordinate of the displayed covector $\eta$ is nonintegral. Otherwise it is $\psi(\epsilon\,\eta(y))$. Membership of $Y$ in the selected coset set puts $qy$ in the local integral lattice. Therefore $q\eta(y)$ is integral. The local additive law is needed only on the denominator-$q$ inputs: adding the argument to itself $q$ times proves $\psi(\epsilon\eta(y))^q=1$ from triviality on integral arguments. Consequently every complex embedding gives absolute value one on its support. Changing $y$ by an integral vector leaves it unchanged. Changing $\eta$ by $q$ times an integral covector also leaves it unchanged because $qy$ is integral. Thus this individual Fourier factor earns its size and period at the actual allowed representatives. The product entry also contains the independently defined moved local matrix test. ## Unresolved

This is an archimedean bound for the specified kernel operator under its actual support, periodicity and conjugate-size hypotheses on $\Xi$. It checks exactly where the fine-level count and derivative factorial go. The special weights in the stress example test the universal analytic statement; the general bound above covers the weights on the selected rows. The scale and support actually attained by the *local arithmetic test functions* and the integral denominators of the coupled map still have to fit their own allowances. This calculation supplies no counterproof of the RH implication.
