# The split Gram fibre loses integrability one row below the release rank

**23 November 2026.** This is a local test of the singular-mass argument in the [downward theta receipt](2026-11-23-downward-residue.md). At a good split finite place, fix a unimodular first Gram block of rank $r$. Its orthogonal complement in the enlarged space has dimension $m=(r+2t)-r=2t$ in each split field factor. Choose dual integral bases of the two complements. The residual Gram equation on $t$ columns becomes $A^\top B=0$, with $A,B$ independent $m$ by $t$ matrices. We test the standard compactly supported lattice function. [Enlarged dimension](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/RegularisedSiegelWeil/ResidueVanishing/Rethread.lean#L19-L25) · [Gram equation after subtracting the first block](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/RegularisedSiegelWeil/SingularNullSets/WeakApproximationFinitePlace.lean#L60-L75)

Let $O$ be a local field's ring of integers, $\pi$ a uniformizer and $q$ the residue cardinality. Give $O$ volume one. For $m\ge t\ge1$ put

$$
 Z_N=q^{Nt^2}\Pr(A^\top B\equiv0\bmod\pi^N).
$$

The normalization imposes $t^2$ scalar equations. With an additive character of conductor $O$ it also has a Fourier interpretation. Integrating the Gram character over $\pi^{-N}M_t(O)$ gives $q^{Nt^2}$ times the same congruence indicator. For the lattice function, the Fourier integral over $A,B$ at each frequency is nonnegative because integrating over $B$ first imposes a system of linear congruences on $A$. Thus these truncated masses also test absolute integrability of the local Fourier transform for this function.

## Exact density by conditioning

For full-rank $A$, write $\pi^{a_1},\ldots,\pi^{a_t}$ for its elementary divisors, up to units. Changing integral bases preserves volume. Each column of $B$ must satisfy one congruence of index $q^{\max(N-a_i,0)}$ for each $i$. Its $t$ columns are independent. The normalized conditional mass at $A$ is therefore

$$
 q^{t\sum_{i=1}^t\min(a_i,N)}.
$$

Let $S(A)=\sum_i a_i$, the valuation of the ideal generated by the maximal minors. Reveal the columns of $A$ one by one. Their rational span intersects $O^m$ in a direct summand of rank $j-1$. Choose an integral basis adapted to it. The new column projected to the quotient has $m-j+1$ independent uniform coordinates. Their common minimum valuation $D_j$ satisfies

$$
 \Pr(D_j\ge h\mid\hbox{earlier columns})=q^{-(m-j+1)h}\qquad(h\ge0).
$$

This law is independent of the earlier columns. The projection is nonzero with probability one, which also proves full rank almost surely. Wedge the new column with the preceding ones in the adapted basis. The valuation of the ideal of maximal minors increases by $D_j$, so $S(A)=\sum_j D_j$.

For $m\ge2t$, each geometric moment $\mathbb E(q^{tD_j})$ is finite. The conditional masses increase to the integrable function $q^{tS(A)}$ almost everywhere, and

$$
 Z_\infty=\prod_{j=1}^t
 \frac{1-q^{-(m-j+1)}}{1-q^{-(m-j+1-t)}}.
$$

In particular, the marginal measures on $A$ converge in total variation to a measure with this pointwise density $q^{tS(A)}$ relative to ordinary matrix volume. The same holds for $B$ by symmetry. A weak limit of these Gram measures can give neither matrix's deficient-rank locus positive mass.

## The boundary

At the release value $m=2t$ the answer is $\prod_{k=t+1}^{2t}(1-q^{-k})/\prod_{k=1}^t(1-q^{-k})$. At $m=2t-1$, the event that the first $t-1$ columns are independent modulo $\pi$ has positive probability. On this event $t-1$ elementary divisors are units, and the final valuation $D_t$ has tail $q^{-th}$. The conditional mean of the normalized mass is exactly

$$
 \mathbb E\big(q^{t\min(D_t,N)}\big)=1+(1-q^{-t})N.
$$

This gives a linearly diverging lower bound. For $t=1$ one can check both sides directly. Two rows give $Z_N=1+q^{-1}(1-q^{-N})$. One row gives $Z_N=1+(1-q^{-1})N$. For instance at $q=5$, $N=2$ the answers are $149/125$ and $13/5$.

## What happens if more primes are included?

At $m=2t$ the factor begins $1+q^{-1}+O_t(q^{-2})$. Even at $t=1$, a product of raw factors $1+1/p$ over the rational primes diverges. Indeed $1+1/p=(1-p^{-2})/(1-p^{-1})$ and Euler's product for the harmonic series diverges. Multiplying unnormalized local densities over growing sets of primes is therefore a different operation with a different answer. The release first allows a scalar depending on the finite set of places and the choice of Haar measure. To obtain one scalar for all tests, it evaluates any one nonzero reference pairing and the proposed new test on a finite set containing the places of **both**. Their ratio fixes the scalar. If every reference pairing is zero, the finite-set proportionality forces all the corresponding pairings to vanish.
