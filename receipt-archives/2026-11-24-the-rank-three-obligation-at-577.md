# The marked rank-three obligation at 577

**24 November 2026.** Fix $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, $\ell=577$, the [even character of conductor 256 with $\psi(5)=510\bmod577$](2026-11-24-a-class-inside-the-zeta-windows.md), and $j=296$. The [nonzero group](2026-11-24-the-577-class-on-paper.md) forces a positive-length input to the marked-point argument. We tried to account for it by the rank-two corner and by a self-dual rank-three point. Both fail modular-form constant-term tests. The tests work at the actual weight 593 for the corner, above $\ell+1$. The same exclusions hold at every $j=296+576u$, $u\ge0$.

## What the marked points have to preserve

Write $\eta=\chi_{-7}$ and $z=\bar\psi\omega^{j}$. On $G_K$ the residual multiset of characters is $z,1,z^{-1}$. The field cut out by $z$ has conductor supported at 2 and 577 and is disjoint from $K$. Its character has order $\operatorname{lcm}(64,72)=576$. The weight of the desired characteristic-zero flag at 577 is $j,0,-j$. At 2 the prime splits in $K$ and the prescribed inertia characters are $\psi,1,\psi^{-1}$. Their orders are prime to 577. The source kills all prime-to-577 inertia elements in its residual kernel. Consequently the characteristic-zero inertia actually factors through this cyclic group of order 64 and has these eigencharacters, distinguished by reduction. At the two arbitrarily large host primes the marked representation is unramified. At 7 it is unramified on $G_K$. These restrictions explain why increasing the host level cannot rescue the two particular alternatives below. The larger trace catalogue used at the end of the argument has a weaker level bound. ## An elementary obstruction at the tame modular level

Suppose a normalized ordinary cuspidal eigenform of weight $k$ on $\Gamma_1(D)$, with $577\nmid D$, has residual semisimplification $1\oplus\chi\omega^{k-1}$ for a primitive nebentypus $\chi$. For the two levels below, exactly one of the two tame inertia characters is nontrivial at every prime dividing $D$, and the inertia order is prime to $\ell$. Frobenius on the remaining line reads 1 modulo $\ell$, since the two global residual characters are distinguished on inertia. Inertia invariants commute with reduction by averaging. Thus the bad-prime Fourier coefficient also reads 1. At 577 the ordinary unramified quotient is the residual trivial character, singled out by the nontrivial inertia character of the other constituent. Hence $a_{577}=1\bmod577$. At good primes away from 577 the residual trace already gives $a_p=1+\chi(p)p^{k-1}\bmod577$. Hecke recurrences and multiplicativity determine **every positive Fourier coefficient**. This checks the bad primes that a bare good-prime comparison leaves open.

Take $k_0\ge2$ with $k\equiv k_0\bmod576$, $k\ge k_0$. Let $E=E_{k_0}^{1,\chi}$ with constant term $-B_{k_0,\chi}/(2k_0)$. Its integral Fourier coefficients have the required residues, including the coefficients at 577 and at $p\mid D$. Multiply its reduction by the Hasse invariant $H$ to the power $(k-k_0)/576$. This preserves the $q$-expansion and matches the weight. If the stated constant is nonzero modulo 577, subtracting the hypothetical cusp form would give a modular form $G$ with nonzero constant $q$-expansion. Now $G^{576}$ and a nonzero scalar times $H^k$ have the same weight and entire $q$-expansion, hence agree on the connected tame modular curve. At a supersingular point the Hasse invariant has a simple zero. Orders of vanishing would give $576\,\operatorname{ord}(G)=k$. For $576\nmid k$ this is impossible. This proof permits $k$ to exceed the coefficient prime. The stated levels are fine tame levels, and their character orders are prime to 577. ## Apply it to the two alternatives

**Type II, the corner plus a line.** After the standard Tate normalization the classical newform would have weight $2j+1=593$. On $G_K$ the two residual characters of the classical representation are $\bar\psi^{-1}$ and $\bar\psi\omega^{2j}$. The inertia characters at 577 distinguish the weight-zero character. Both characters extend to $G_\mathbb Q$ and are fixed separately by conjugation. Each extension has just the additional quadratic choice $\eta$. Before the Tate normalization the determinant is finite, unramified away from 7 and odd, hence $\eta$. At 2 its inertia determinant is one. At 7 the inertia quotient has order at most two and oddness forces the nontrivial quadratic factor. At 577 the successive local characters are unramified twists of opposite cyclotomic powers. Their distinct slopes differ by $2j$, which excludes special monodromy there. The finite inertial Weil–Deligne type is trivial. This gives the primitive tame conductor via local-global compatibility, as in the [corner calculation at 571](2026-11-24-the-rank-two-alternative-at-571.md). [Saito, local-global compatibility at the coefficient prime](https://www.ms.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~t-saito/pp/suams.pdf#page=8)

Twist off the weight-zero residual character, lifting it by its finite character $\psi^{-1}$ and the appropriate quadratic choice at 7. The resulting inertia at 2 consists of $1,\psi^2$. The latter has order 32 and conductor 128. At 7 the primitive quadratic inertia has conductor 7. Thus $D=896$, $\chi=\eta\psi^2$, and the residual characters are $1,\chi\omega^{2j}$. The reciprocal convention replaces $\psi^2$ by $\psi^{-2}$. Since $593=17+576$, use $k_0=17$. The respective Eisenstein constants are **438 and 246 modulo 577**, both nonzero.

**A rank-three rational adjoint.** A weight-$j+1$ classical form on $\mathrm{GL}_2/\mathbb Q$ whose adjoint restricts to the marked point would have projective residual ratio $z$ on $G_K$. The residual representation is reducible. On $\ker z$ all projective elements are unipotent because the residual adjoint trace is three. A nontrivial normal 577-group of projective translations fixes a unique line. If that group is trivial, the projective image over $K$ is cyclic. Either way there are two character constituents on $G_K$ with ratio $z$ or its inverse. Conjugation cannot interchange them because $z$ extends and has order greater than two. Thus the residual characters extend separately over $\mathbb Q$.

Remove scalar ramification by a finite Dirichlet twist. At 2 the projective inertia is cyclic of order 64, with distinct residual eigencharacters. The globally stable residual characters ensure that Frobenius preserves their inertia lines. At 7 the projective inertia has order at most two. These finite inertia characters extend on local units, and the Chinese remainder theorem realizes the chosen scalar-removing twists globally. At 577 the ordinary adjoint flag has trivial finite Weil–Deligne inertia and excludes special monodromy. In particular the potentially crystalline two-dimensional representation becomes crystalline after removing a finite scalar inertial twist. Its inertia constituents have weights $0,j$. One can see ordinarity from the full adjoint flag. A nilpotent invariant line gives an invariant line on the two-dimensional representation. A semisimple line gives two eigenlines. Interchanging them would give an irreducible two-dimensional root-space subquotient, since their ratio on inertia has infinite image. Distinct weights also preserve the ordering after unramified quadratic descent. These are the same local arguments as in the [rational adjoint reduction](2026-11-24-which-rank-three-representations-remain.md).

Normalize the weight-zero residual character to be trivial by the finite twists at the ramified primes. At 2 the remaining inertia ratio is $\psi$ or its inverse, of conductor 256. At 577 the orientation is fixed by its cyclotomic power $j$, distinct from $-j$ modulo 576. At 7 oddness of the classical representation supplies the additional $\eta$ in the ratio because $\psi$ and $\varepsilon^j$ are even. The remaining nebentypus is $\eta\psi$ or $\eta\psi^{-1}$, of conductor $D=1792$. There is no extra character of 577-power order there since $577\nmid\varphi(1792)$. Thus $k_0=j+1=297$ works for the first twist and remains the reduced weight at the later twists. The respective constants are **10 and 48 modulo 577**.

## The remaining representation

An essentially self-dual rank-three point of determinant one could differ from its dual only by a cubic character. Reduction would permute the character multiset $\{1,z,z^{-1}\}$. The only character there of order dividing three is 1 because $z$ has order 576. A cubic character trivial modulo 577 is trivial. Thus the point would be self-dual. Its conjugate self-duality would then give quadratic invariance. Cyclic base change and its description of fibers give a rational cuspidal descent, whose quadratic ambiguity is fixed by asking for determinant one. Self-duality of that descent follows from its central character. Ramakrishnan's adjoint characterization would give the weight-$j+1$ rational adjoint just excluded. [Clozel–Rajan, cyclic descent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.02513) · [Ramakrishnan, Theorem A and Corollary B](https://math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/Ramakrishnan.pdf)

A monomial three-line induction also cannot have this residual trace. On the coset of a three-cycle its trace would vanish identically. The permutation kernel has index at most six, so the image of $z$ on that coset contains at least $576/6=96$ different elements. The trace $1+x+x^{-1}$ can vanish at only two of them. The characteristic-zero point, if it exists, must be genuinely non-self-dual and primitive. At the split prime 11 its normalized trace must reduce to **537** in both directions, which singles out the one live character among the 32. At the split prime 3329 all labels require **384**. A surviving form must meet these conditions on the same global system. The level away from 577 of a marked point on $G_K$ is unramified outside the two places over 2. Its inertial characters there have conductors $256,1,256$ in rational local coordinates. [Orbit collision at that prime](2026-11-24-one-of-thirty-two-characters.md)

## Unresolved

This puts both excluded alternatives inside the final window. The assertion of a suitable remaining ordinary cuspidal rank-three point stays open in this independent audit. In this example its Hodge–Tate interval has width $592>576$. Any argument restricted to an interval shorter than $\ell-1$ would need another input here. The release states its ordinary automorphy conclusion for a strongly irreducible marked point, and obtaining or excluding such a point with the actual nonzero residue is the outstanding step.
