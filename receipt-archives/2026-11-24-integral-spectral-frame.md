## The common Lean target

Let $C$ be an $n$-by-$d$ matrix, $L$ a $d$-by-$n$ matrix, and $LC=I_d$. If $Cx=Cy$, applying $L$ gives $x=y$. This works over every semiring, including the natural numbers. The proof uses associativity of matrix action and the identity action. The APCI left-inverse lemma needs only two functions and the equation that their composite fixes every input. ## Two integral maps on the same line

Work over $\mathbb Q$ with the usual dot product, or locally over $\mathbb Q_5$ with lattice $A=\mathbb Z_5^2$. Put $v=(1,t)^T$, $C(x)=vx$ and $s=1+t^2$ for an integer $t$. First-coordinate projection $L_0=(1,0)$ is an integral left inverse. The orthogonal coordinate projection is

$$P=\frac{(1,t)}{s},\qquad PC=1,\qquad P(1,0)^T=\frac1s.$$

The physical vector on the line is $CPa$ for input $a$. Orthogonality forces the denominator: $v^T(a-vx)=0$ gives $x=v^Ta/s$. The vector $v$ is primitive for every $t$ because its first coordinate is 1. The distinction is present even for an integral symmetric operator. Take $T=\lambda I+vv^T$ with $\lambda\in\mathbb Z$. Its two eigenvalues are $\lambda+s,\lambda$, its spectral projector onto $v$ is $(T-\lambda I)/s$, and the upper left entry of this projector is $1/s$. In the two eigenvalue coordinates the algebra $\mathbb Z_5[T]$ consists exactly of pairs $(a,b)$ with $a-b\in s\mathbb Z_5$. Polynomial evaluation gives the inclusion. The linear polynomial $b+((a-b)/s)(X-\lambda)$ supplies the converse.

| $t$ | Observed $t\bmod5$ | $s$ | Power of 5 in $s$ | Index of the cut lattice in the projected lattice |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 2 | 5 | 1 | 5 |
| 7 | 2 | 50 | 2 | 25 |

The frame column $v$ and the entire matrix $T$ also coincide modulo 5 for these two inputs. The yes/no question “does 25 divide the orthogonal denominator?” has opposite answers. The APCI criterion says any answer computed from an observation must agree on inputs with the same observation. Applying it here gives a concrete impossibility proof, with the two input values exposed in [the Lean control](SpectralFrameControls.lean). [APCI factorization implication](https://github.com/Grativy6/PAL-Lean-Audit/blob/5d5f88d283cd3fdab27aa2c7d91ceb0745653cd0/projects/APCI/APCILeanAudit/Interface.lean#L34-L41)

Every fixed observation depth has this defect. Start at $2\bmod5$. If $1+t^2=5^m u$, replacing $t$ by $t+c5^m$ changes $u\bmod5$ by $2tc$. Since $2t$ is a unit modulo 5, exactly one of the five lifts reaches the next depth. Choose one of the other four for exact valuation $m$, and take a lift of the distinguished branch of exact valuation $m+1$. The two inputs agree modulo $5^m$. This argument works for every $m\ge1$. The [integer check](check_inert_spectral_frames.py) also constructs the witnesses through depth 16.

## Where the actual estimate puts the denominator

The released `zE` includes the subspace in frame coordinates. Its `Pq` is the specific orthogonal inverse $(C^*HC)^{-1}C^*H$. Positive definiteness at the designated embedding gives an invertible Gram matrix over the field. At the archimedean metric step the caller uses the field identity $PC=I$. The integral definitions then distinguish the image under projection from the preimage under inclusion. [Orthogonal inverse](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/GoodPartHermitianSpec.lean#L61-L63) · [Frame and three lattices](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/RowLat.lean#L136-L159) · [Callers of the field calculation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/RowLatLemmas.lean#L51-L74)

Write $O=\mathbb Z_5$ and measure in the coordinate of $v$. In the rank-one model take both ambient input lattices equal to $A=O^2$, with the level maps on the ambient space equal to the identity. The projected lattice is $T_{\rm pr}=P(A)=s^{-1}O$, and the cut lattice is $B=C^{-1}(A)=O$. Both copies of the cut agree, so the exponent of their quotient, `JexpW`, is zero. The quotient $T_{\rm pr}/B$ has length $j=v_5(s)$, exactly the release's separate quantity `etaW` in this model. The restricted Gram matrix is $(s)$, and the dual of $s^{-1}O$ for the pairing $sxy$ is $O$. Consequently the released signed index convention gives $j_T=j$, with a **positive** sign. This is a local model of the indicated maps and indices. Instantiating the whole global arithmetic `BlockHyp` is a further condition. There is also a basis-free explanation. In a unimodular Hermitian lattice $A$ over a DVR with conjugation preserving the ring, take a nondegenerate subspace $V$ and $B=A\cap V$. For $x\in V$, $h(x,P_V a)=h(x,a)$. Hence $(P_V A)^\#=A^\#\cap V=B$. Dualizing again gives $P_V A=B^\#$. The projected-to-cut quotient is the discriminant module $B^\#/B$. Its length can grow even for a primitive summand. The release explicitly states both duality formulas. [Duality between projection and cut](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/QuotientMetricDivisors/ClassSumDualLe.lean#L43-L54)

The actual upper bound includes $\eta_W$ with coefficient $+1$, alongside a separate term for the exponent between the two cuts and the stated arithmetic factors. The family above can make $\eta_W$ arbitrarily large while the cut exponent is zero. Dropping $\eta_W$ at this step would lose the entire local index in the model. The actual proof of inclusion uses $P C=I$ to put each cut element into the projected image. Its downstream family estimate still displays $\eta_W$.
