# Two explicit base-unit targets for the 577 norm test

**24 November 2026.** We computed a unit of the original real degree-576 coefficient field $M$ whose local coefficient, after taking its genuine global 577th root, is **171 modulo 577**. A second explicit unit has local coefficient **81**. Cyclic norm lifts of these units into the real affine field give the first of the two required real rows and the correction that removes its unwanted upper entry. A separate finite-field calculation at two rational primes checks the division by 577 and the local normalization.

## Construct the global units

Let $p=577$, $z=\bar\psi\omega^{296}$, $M=\mathbb Q^{\ker z}$ and $T_0=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{256p})$. The character $\psi$ has conductor 256 with $\psi(5)=510\bmod577$. For $\chi=z^{-1}$ or $z$, form
$$C_\chi=\prod_{g\in\operatorname{Gal}(T_0/\mathbb Q)}g(1-\zeta_{256p})^{w_\chi(g)},$$
where $w_\chi(g)$ is the least nonnegative representative of the inverse Teichmüller lift of $\chi(g)$ modulo $p^2$. These are global units because the conductor has two prime factors. They lie in $M$ since the integer weights are constant on the kernel. Modulo $p^2$th powers their character is $\chi$. Norm across the prime-to-$p$ extension $T_0/M$ preserves the power-class assertion.

Use the earlier local embedding $\zeta_{256p}\mapsto YZ$ with $Y^4=[20]$, $Z$ a primitive $p$th root, and the tame eigenparameter $\pi$ with leading term $Z-1$. For $\chi=z^{-1}$ all coefficients of $C_\chi^{576}-1$ are divisible by $p$ in the full local cyclotomic integer ring. After division by $p$ the first nonzero coefficient is at degree 280. It is **$78Y^3=406 Y^{-1}$** modulo $p$. For $\chi=z$ the first nonzero coefficient before division is **$496Y$** at degree 296. These are leading coefficients in the $\pi$ parameter. The finite replay extracts them using the truncated variable $X=\log Z$ below degree 576, whose linear term is also $Z-1$. It checks every earlier coefficient.

For a short description of the computation write the tame index as $(\pm5^i,5^t)$ modulo $(256,p)$. The exponent of $z$ in the generator 5 is $351i+296t\bmod576$. The kernel has $i=8k$ and $t=45k+72j$, $0\le k,j<8$, with either sign. Forming its 128 factors requires only rotations for multiplication by $Y^v Z^t$. The rest comprises the 72 inertia automorphisms in each of eight cosets. All products are exact in the cyclic group ring modulo $p^2$, followed by $1+Z+\cdots+Z^{576}=0$. The $\chi=z$ reading is also $31\cdot16Y$ by the separate finite Gauss-sum and Bernoulli computation. [Local projection and independent sums](boundary-577/real_norm_units.py)

There is a reason the local division for $z^{-1}$ gives a **global** unit. The $z^{-1}$ component of the maximal abelian pro-$p$ group of $M$ ramified only at $p$ has order exactly $p$, and its unramified quotient already has that order. To see this at finite Teichmüller weight, an order-$p^2$ character would give a relaxed lift at $j=296p$, where the [actual divisor multiplier](2026-11-24-the-cyclotomic-module-of-the-fixed-class.md#an-exact-divisor-test-at-every-coefficient-order) is $298p\bmod p^2$. This excludes the lift. The residual rank and the unramified class are given by the [real class calculation](2026-11-24-the-affine-resolvent-and-its-class-groups.md#check-all-576-real-sectors). The ray exact sequence now says that global units project onto the entire $z^{-1}$ part of the semilocal principal units. Both $p$-completed modules are free of rank one in this character, by Dirichlet's unit theorem and the local logarithm in the tame degree-72 completions. Hence the map on these components is an isomorphism. No index theorem for circular units is needed.

The computed valuation gives a local $p$th power at every conjugate place, since the first remaining term after $p$ has positive valuation. The other character components are already trivial modulo $p$th powers by the projector. Consequently $C_{z^{-1}}=e_-^p$ for a unique unit $e_-\in M$. This follows by reducing the preceding isomorphism modulo $p$ and using finite generation of the units. In the $u_-$ convention the leading coefficient of $\log e_-$ is $406/576=171$ modulo $p$. For $e_+=C_z$ the corresponding $u_+$ coefficient is $496/576=81$. Both are nonzero. All character assertions about these individual units used below are modulo $p$th powers.

At a rational prime $r\equiv1\bmod256p^2$ evaluate the entire product for $C_{z^{-1}}$ in $\mathbb F_r$. Its $(r-1)/p^2$ power reads the $p$th power-residue exponent of its global root $e_-$. We use the least primitive root of each finite field and the original global $\mu_p$ generator.

There is a reciprocity check **over $M$**. At $j=296p$ the coefficient modulo $p^2$ is the finite Teichmüller character $z^{-1}$, so each [order-squared Jacobi character](2026-11-24-two-independent-obstruction-primes.md#actual-tame-inertia-at-the-lift-from-p-to-p2) becomes additive over $M$. The divided tame inertia at its own prime is $k(j)=298$. Its local residue at $p$ is $h=406u(r)+178v(r)$, equal to 179 and 164 in the two cases. By the local Hilbert calculation the character on $u_-$ has divided exponent $446h$ over the unramified quadratic extension of $M_v$, or **$223h$ over $M_v$** by the norm. There are eight such places. At the 576 split conjugates of $r$, the tame Hilbert symbol on a unit has the opposite sign to the Frobenius evaluation on the radical. All conjugate contributions agree by equivariance. The two sides of the product formula are consequently
$$8\cdot223h\cdot171=576\cdot298\cdot\operatorname{exp}_r(e_-)\pmod{577}.$$
They give **330 and 557** respectively. This check uses a finite product in two different residue fields and the earlier Jacobi characters independently of the new circular-unit multiplication at 577.

## What is still needed upstairs

Every global unit of $M$ is a norm from the unramified cyclic $L_y/M$ as an element, by the cyclic Hasse norm theorem. Modulo $p$th powers it can be lifted to a unit. Indeed if the ideal of a norm preimage has norm one, it is a difference of conjugate ideals since the extension is unramified. The $p$-class number of $L_y$ is one. Raising a correcting ideal to a prime-to-$p$ power principalizes it, and its conjugate ratio removes the divisor. In the group modulo $p$th powers undo that prime-to-$p$ exponent.

Choose unit norm lifts in $L_y$ of $e_-,e_+$ and take their complement projections of weights $z^{-1},z$. Summing the lowest local component on the first lift over the translation orbit takes its norm down to $e_-$. The eight places downstairs then give $\Lambda_-=8\cdot171=214$. The [ordinary-unit relation](2026-11-24-four-norm-kernels-for-the-two-quadratics.md#why-the-two-missing-readings-really-are-independent-tests) gives $\Lambda_0=16$. Likewise the highest component on the second lift is $8\cdot81=71$. Its lower and diagonal components vanish by weight. Apply the square of the translation logarithm to this second unit class. On its even output it takes $A_2$ to $2A_0$, so it gives the row $(0,0,142)$. Subtracting its appropriate multiple from the first lift removes the upper entry. The resulting first test row is **$(214,16,0)$**, a known unit multiple of $(495,188,0)$ used in the four-slope determinant. The same two corrections can be used when normalizing the $S$-prime principalizer for the other row.

The two base units themselves lie in the abelian field $M$. Directly using them upstairs repeats each local sum 577 times with polynomial translation weights and gives zero. The nonzero row 214 appears after taking a cyclic **norm preimage**. A global preimage in the real affine field and a generator above the specified $S$-prime still have to be represented to evaluate the four higher norm kernels. We have not inferred one of their values from the base-unit calculation.
