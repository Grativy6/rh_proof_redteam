# The order-nine carry in a nonsplit degeneration

**24 November 2026.** Hearthline, pursuing Chris Pang’s question about a residue surviving the lattice handoff. In a principally polarized abelian threefold with two toric directions and one good elliptic direction, we remove the good period labels. A primitive root of unity remains in the resulting torus periods. At level three it changes a polarization kernel from four order-three generators in the split control to **two order-nine generators**. Both groups have 81 elements. The source’s actual eighth-power factor comparison can be tested on the surviving phase.

## One elliptic calculation over five

Use the good elliptic curve $B:y^2=x^3+1$ at residue characteristic 5. Let $P=(0,1)$ and $Q=(x_0,y_0)$ with $x_0^3=-4$, $x_0=1\bmod5$ and $y_0^2=-3$. Their tangent lines meet the cubic with multiplicity three, so $P,Q$ have order three. Their reductions have distinct first coordinates and are independent. In the residue field $\mathbb F_{25}$ we write $s^2=2$ and $\bar y_0=s$.

The functions of these tangent lines, normalized by the same coefficient of $y$, compute the Weil pairing. The tangent at $P$ is $y-1$. The tangent at $Q$ evaluated at $P$ is $1-y_0+3x_0^3/(2y_0)=1+y_0$. With the convention $e_3(P,Q)=-f_P(Q)/f_Q(P)$ this gives
$$\zeta=\frac{1-y_0}{1+y_0}=\frac{-1-y_0}{2},\qquad \bar\zeta=2+2s.$$
The reader can check $\bar\zeta^2+\bar\zeta+1=0$ by multiplication and $\bar\zeta\ne1$. Reversing the pairing convention inverts $\zeta$ throughout. [Finite-field replay](check_triangle_and_nonsplit_carry.py)

## The two power maps on the same fibre

For the general calculation fix $k\ge2$ prime to the residue characteristic and a symplectic basis $P,Q$ of $B[k]$. Work after finite extension to realize the needed points and roots of unity. Let $\mathcal P$ be the rigidified Poincaré biextension under the elliptic principal polarization. A nonzero vector in its fibre at $(P,Q)$ has two $k$th-power readings: multiply in the first variable and rigidify at $(0,Q)$, or multiply in the second variable and rigidify at $(P,0)$. Their ratio is the Weil pairing, with orientation chosen as above. Scaling the starting vector multiplies **both** readings by the same $k$th power.

One can see the ratio without any valuation. Over the complex elliptic torus take $P=1/k,Q=\tau/k$. For the Poincaré bundle the period shift $(u,v)\mapsto(u+a+b\tau,v+c+d\tau)$ multiplies a fibre coordinate by $\exp[-2\pi i(bv+du+bd\tau)]$. Starting with coordinate 1 at $(P,Q)$, addition in the first variable $k$ times reads 1 at $(0,Q)$. Addition in the second reads $\exp(2\pi i/k)$ at $(P,0)$. The algebraic pairing specializes to the preceding flex calculation for $k=3$, up to the stated orientation. Choose the off-diagonal vector with its first power reading 1 and use its symmetric image for the reversed pair. The second reading is necessarily $\zeta$. A vector with first power 1 is algebraic in this torsion fibre and can be chosen after finite extension. [Biextension and Weil pairing](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~damienrobert/pro/publications/notes/2023-08-thetagroup.pdf)

## A genuine principally polarized ambient variety

Take $T=\mathbb G_m^2$ and the extension $G$ of $B$ by $T$ classified in the two directions by $P,Q\in B^\vee[k]$. Each direction is the multiplicative torsor of the corresponding rigidified translation-invariant line. The extension does not split since the classes are nonzero. Map the period lattice $Y=\mathbb Z^2$ to $B$ by $e_1\mapsto P,e_2\mapsto Q$. To lift the periods to $G$, choose the off-diagonal fibre vectors just described and diagonal vectors with first $k$th power 1. Diagonal symmetry is compatible because the self Weil pairings are 1. Multiply both diagonal vectors by $q^2$ for a local parameter $q$. Then the two lifted periods $y_1,y_2$ satisfy, **in the ordinary torus**,
$$ k y_1=(D,1),\qquad k y_2=(\zeta,D),\qquad D=q^{2k}.$$
The symbol $k y_i$ denotes multiplication in the extension. Thus $k^2y_i$ are pure diagonal periods.

To check algebraicity, pull $G$ back along $[k]:B\to B$. The two extension classes vanish there, giving an isogeny $T\times B\to G$ with the identity on $T$. Its composite to the analytic quotient $A=G^{\rm an}/Y$ descends to an isogeny from the product of two Tate curves of parameter $D^k$ and $B$. Its finite kernel gives an algebraic abelian quotient. The symmetric biextension elements above, the identity from periods to characters, and the ordinary principal elliptic line give polarization data on $A$. Concretely a multiplier on $ae_1+be_2$ is obtained by multiplying the two generator multipliers to powers $a,b$ and the diagonal and off-diagonal biextension multipliers to powers $\binom a2,\binom b2,ab$. Exact symmetry in the fibre makes this a cocycle. The valuation pairing is positive diagonal and the period-to-character map has determinant one. Hence the induced polarization is principal. The generator multipliers may be chosen symmetrically after adjoining square roots of the diagonal rigidified fibre data. [Raynaud line data and polarization, Theorem 3.10 and Proposition 3.24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08212#page=12)

The torus image $C\subset A$ is $T^{\rm an}/(Y\cap T)$. Independence of $P,Q$ makes $Y\cap T=kY$. Its periods are exactly $(D,1),(\zeta,D)$. Discarding the unit entry leaves the valuation matrix unchanged.

## The two kernels with the same count

The polarization on $C$ obtained by restricting the ambient one has lattice map $kI$: the period basis just changed from $Y$ to $kY$ while the torus characters stayed fixed. The **target** dual torus has transposed periods $(D,\zeta),(1,D)$. The lifted map on tori raises both coordinates to the $k$th power. Choose $\alpha^k=D$ and $\xi^k=\zeta$. Two elements of its geometric kernel are represented by $u=(\alpha,\xi)$ and $v=(1,\alpha)$. In $C$ itself,
$$ku=(1,\zeta),\qquad kv=(\zeta^{-1},1).$$
Every kernel point reduces by its valuations to a combination of $u,v$ and a torus $k$-torsion point. Their $k$th powers generate that entire torus subgroup. Their images in the quotient by it are independent of order $k$. Hence the kernel is $(\mathbb Z/k^2)^2$. For the phase-free period matrix, the analogous polarization is ordinary multiplication by $k$ on a product of Tate curves. Its kernel is $(\mathbb Z/k)^4$.

In explicit coordinates represent a point by $(D^{a/k}\zeta^i,D^{b/k}\xi^a\zeta^j)$ for $0\le a,b,i,j<k$. On adding another point let $A_1=a+a'$ and $B_1=b+b'$. The new torus coordinates are
$$i+i'-\lfloor B_1/k\rfloor,\qquad j+j'+\lfloor A_1/k\rfloor\pmod k.$$
Removing the floors preserves the count and changes the group law. At $k=3$ the true kernel has eight elements of exact order 3 and 72 of exact order 9. The phase-free control has 80 of exact order 3.

There is an independent geometric check on the cost of splitting. The lift $j:B\to G$ of $[k]$ restricts on $B[k]$ to the two Weil characters for $P,Q$, up to inversion, hence is injective. This also follows by presenting $G$ as $(T\times B)/B[k]$ with $B[k]$ acting by its two Weil characters and by translation on $B$. Its image stays embedded in $A$ because its intersection with the torus consists of torsion, whereas nonzero periods in $kY$ have nonzero valuation. The composite $B\to A\to B/B[k]$ is $[k^2]$ upon identifying the quotient via $[k]$. Therefore $j(B)\cap C=j(B[k^2])$. The addition map $C\times j(B)\to A$ identifies opposite copies of this intersection. Projecting onto $C$ and multiplying by an integer $d$ descends to $A$ precisely when $k^2\mid d$. A different rational retraction cannot lower this denominator: there is no nonzero homomorphism from the good elliptic curve to $C$, which is isogenous to a product of elliptic curves with split multiplicative reduction.

## What the unit changes, and what the release actually uses

An arbitrary polarization of the pure toric surface must have a positive-definite integral matrix $L=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\b&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$: symmetry of valuations forces symmetry of the matrix. Equality of the two off-diagonal **scalar** periods further imposes $\zeta^a=1$. A principal one thus requires $ad-b^2=1$ and $b^2=-1\bmod k$. Conversely if this congruence has a solution, $a=k$ and $d=(1+b^2)/k$ work. The symmetric bicharacter supplies the cocycle by the same binomial formula on the free lattice. At $k=3$ there is **no principal polarization at all** on $C$, even after extending the field. The least positive determinant is 2, attained by $(a,b,d)=(3,1,1)$. The phase-free surface has the product principal polarization. [Classification of line bundles on a totally degenerate abelian variety, Example 3.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08212#page=9)

This defect also distinguishes a fixed matrix from existence on a power. On $C^h$ write $L=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}U&J\\J^t&V\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ in the two blocks of $h$ toric directions. Exact symmetry of the unit periods gives $U=0$ and $J=J^t$ modulo 3. Thus $\det L=(-1)^h(\det J)^2\bmod3$. No odd power can be principally polarized. For $h=2$, put $U=3I$, $V=I$ and $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The matrix is positive definite of determinant one, by $J^2=2I$ and the Schur complement. Direct sums cover the even powers.

The released torus-quotient count concerns ordinary points **killed by** $N$ and explicitly lifts such points along a quotient with divisible torus kernel. It does not identify the kernel of the restricted polarization with $C[k]$. Its principal factor theorem requires an **integral** inclusion and retraction with composite the identity and an integral inverse condition on the chosen corner of the coefficient ring. Our $C$ fails the retraction condition by exactly the $k^2$ just computed. ## Unresolved

This is a counterexample to recovering the integral polarization, its kernel exponent, or the cost of an integral retraction from the toric valuation matrix and cardinality.
