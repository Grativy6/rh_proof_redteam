# The golden angle and the unit in the lattice sum

**24 November 2026.** Chris Pang asked whether the Alpöge–Claude release collapses the golden angle and then carries it incorrectly. The golden-ratio polynomial really does occur on a proof-body path to `rhOfAll`. The release uses one of its roots to **change a summand without changing its $p$-adic size**. Elsewhere the proof doubles a complex angle. These are different operations, and we tested them both. No incorrect use of the golden angle to establish the released conclusion was identified. ## The actual golden-ratio substitution

A search for the words “golden” and “Fibonacci” in the release’s 175,187 Lean files gave no matches. Searching for the polynomial found a declaration that chooses $u$ in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_p$ satisfying $u^2=u+1$. Its two possible values are $(1\pm\sqrt5)/2$. Both $u$ and $u-1$ are algebraic integers and $u(u-1)=1$. Hence $|u|_p=|u-1|_p=1$ at every finite prime. The proof needs both equalities. It makes no choice of a positive real root or a rotation angle. The shortest proof-body path I found from `rhOfAll` to this declaration has 33 edges. Equivalent expressions can evade a text search. [Polynomial and exact use of its roots](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/LatticeClassSumsOfTermsPrescribedNorms.lean#L26-L50)

The consumer has a lattice class, a target sum $G(\kappa)$ and prescribed bounds on individual terms. It starts with terms $s^{n(\chi)}$ and assigns the distinguished term the difference between the target and the sum of its companions. If two indices tie for largest allowed magnitude and that difference comes out too small, it multiplies a companion by $u$. Its magnitude stays fixed, while the change $(u-1)s^{n(\chi)}$ has the **full** leading magnitude. The ultrametric inequality makes the adjusted distinguished term attain its bound. With a unique largest term the release instead imposes a separate equality condition on the target. At $p=2$ the distinction is visible without Lean. Two units of $\mathbb Q_2$ both reduce to 1, so their sum cannot be 1. In $\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt5)$ the two units $u$ and $1-u$ **do** sum to 1. Modulo 2 their residues are the two other nonzero elements of $\mathbb F_4$, since $X^2+X+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_2$. The residue of $u$ has order three, but the actual element satisfies $u^3=1+2u$. The separately named root of unity $U(\kappa)$ in the geometric caller is obtained **before** this substitution. The integrality proof applies the root-of-unity relation to $U(\kappa)$. ## A lost distinction a finer lattice actually reads

Take $Q(x,y)=x^2+xy+y^2$ on $\mathbb Z^2$, an odd modulus $m\ge5$, and $a=(m-1)/2$. In the class of $(a,1)$ modulo $m$ there are exactly two shortest vectors, $v_+=(a,1)$ and $v_-=(a-m,1)$, both of value $h=(m^2+3)/4$. Indeed $Q=(x+y/2)^2+3y^2/4$. For $y=1$ the two indicated $x$ values give the minimum. For every other allowed $y$, $3y^2\ge3(m-1)^2>m^2+3$.

Give every other vector $v$ its term $2^{Q(v)}$ and write $H$ for their sum within that class. This converges in $\mathbb Q_2$ and $H/2^h$ is even. Set the class target to $2^h+H$. On the other residue classes take the usual sums of $2^{Q(v)}$. These data satisfy the numerical lattice lemma, with $s=2$, $q(v)=Q(v)\log2$ and one $\kappa$. No choice of terms in $\mathbb Q_2$ can achieve the specified norm at each vector and that target. After division by $2^h$ the two leading units sum to zero modulo 2. The release’s allowed coefficient field admits the pair $2^h u,2^h(1-u)$, in either order.

Swapping the pair preserves the full coarse sum and **every individual norm**. Modulo $3m$ the two vectors separate. In the refined class of $v_+$ the two assignments differ by $2^h(2u-1)$, of norm exactly $2^{-h}$ because $(2u-1)^2=5$. For $m=5$ that is $1/128$, precisely the size of the whole leading term. Thus the coarse data cannot determine an individual coefficient, its refined class sum, or a realization over the starting field. This is a counterexample to those stronger readings of the numerical lemma.

In the released geometric application the target is an evaluation of a geometric weight part. The term family is existential. The all-level statement chooses the depth and the terms **after** the requested cutoff. At one chosen depth each allowed tuple of residue classes lifts independently in its coordinates, so a product of class sums uses the same coarse sums after our swap. The approximation consumer chooses one depth, uses the term family from that depth throughout, and uses a separate uniqueness argument for the leading term at its selected trait. I found no use there of a unique coefficient carried unchanged to a finer level. The independent geometric obligations are establishing the bounds on the actual weight parts and isolating the required leading term. ## Carry the angle itself

In Pang’s six-axis code write $\beta=(3-\sqrt5)/2$ turns, $\alpha=2\pi\beta$, $K_n=\lfloor6n\beta+1/2\rfloor$ and $\epsilon_n=2\pi n\beta-K_n\pi/3$. One golden step has label 2 and residual about $17.51^\circ$. Doubling the label gives 4. Doubling the **actual** angle gives about $275.02^\circ$, which lies in sector **5**. The two residuals cross the half-sector threshold. Exactly, $1/4<6\beta-2=7-3\sqrt5<1/2$, so $K_2=2K_1+1$. The first six labels starting from zero are $0,2,5,1,3,5$. Each sector’s limiting frequency is $1/6$ even though this finite sample misses sector 4. [Author’s sector and residual](../corpus/GOLD_v0.1.pdf) · [Fibre criterion](../corpus/Compactification_Costs_v0.2.pdf)

There is a real angular quotient in the release. At a complex embedding it uses $A(z)=z/\bar z$. For $z=e^{i\alpha}$ this is $z^2$, and $A(-z)=A(z)$. Reconstructing $z$ by the principal square root would return **$-z$** for one golden step because $\pi/2<\alpha<\pi$. The character used at this step is trivial on the real ideles, including a sign at one infinite place. On $\mathbb C^\times$ its angular character is $z/|z|$ to some integer power $n$. Triviality on $-1$ forces $n=2k$, giving exactly $A(z)^k$. The source records winding $2k$. When the construction later needs an odd winding, it explicitly multiplies by the inverse auxiliary character of winding one, changing $2k$ to $2k-1$. It therefore retains the sign in the place where the next row can read it. Two further checks locate the boundary. In the determinant square-root cover, an element is a pair $(g,w)$ with $w^2=\det g$, and multiplication keeps the product of the two $w$ values. For one golden rotation in a single coordinate, composing its exponential lift twice retains $e^{i\alpha}$ over $e^{2i\alpha}$. Re-taking the principal square root of the final determinant would flip its sign. In the separate volume argument the proof pushes a restricted Haar measure to the circle and verifies invariance by left multiplication with **every** unit phase. Its exact arc mass is a continuous measure calculation. The six finite sample values above do not satisfy that invariance. ## Unresolved

The golden-ratio construction really changes residue information, and the finer lattice detects exactly how much. The angular maps tested here carry the sign required by their stated outputs. A break in the published inference would need a later obligation that depends on the discarded individual residue or on a coherent choice across levels beyond what the class sums guarantee. The earlier [disappearing-tail counterexample](2026-11-23-independent-theta-check.md) uses the same number $\beta$ as a coefficient whose conjugate is $1/\beta$. That is a separate operation from rotating through $2\pi\beta$.
