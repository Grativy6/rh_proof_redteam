# Test the two equations at the nonsplit residue

**24 November 2026.** The first unknown quadratic forms at the [marked order-577 point](2026-11-24-two-equations-at-the-marked-point.md) can be tested as ordinary **second-order** obstructions at a specific nonsplit residual representation. For this representation the global problem with ramification unrestricted at 577 is formally smooth of relative dimension five. Restoring the prescribed ordinary flag leaves two local readings at 577. Neither their common zero set nor the automorphy of a surviving point has been established.

## A different residue for the same equations

Let $k=\mathbb F_{577}$ and $L=E_{21}-E_{32}$. Normalize the actual strict crossed homomorphism $y$ of character $z^{-1}$ by $y(h)=0$ at the prescribed inertia mark at 2 and $y(\gamma)=1$ at the [common absolute commutator](2026-11-24-three-deformation-directions-at-577.md#read-all-three-classes-on-one-word). Define a residual representation whose matrix part is
$$\bar r_y(g)_{\rm mat}=\exp(y(g)L)\operatorname{diag}(z(g),1,z(g)^{-1}).$$
Use the same quadratic multiplier $\eta$. Each matrix part is fixed by the inverse-transpose involution $\Theta$. Its finite connected arithmetic image is the affine group $C_{577}\rtimes C_{576}$, and the field cut by the representation including $\eta$ is $L_yK$. Normalize at $c$, the symmetric mark, and now also at the lower coordinate $X=1$. The remaining strict diagonal conjugation changes $X$ by $t^{-1}$, so this last normalization is available uniquely. The global infinitesimal centralizer vanishes because commuting with the distinct mark and with a translation forces a scalar, and the outer involution excludes a nonzero scalar Lie element.

The normalized deformation ring of this nonsplit point, with the original ordinary conditions, is exactly
$$D=O[[a,b]]/(f_1(a,b),f_2(a,b)),\qquad O=\mathbb Z_{577}.$$
This identifies the ideal as a deformation problem and justifies evaluating it at the nonsplit point. Merely substituting 1 into an arbitrary formal power series in $X$ would not justify it. Here is a direct equivalence on finite local coefficient algebras $A$.

Given $a,b$ in the maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ of $A$ satisfying the two equations, introduce a variable $t$. If $\mathfrak n^N=0$, take the complete local subring of $A((t))$ generated by $A[[t]]$ and $\mathfrak n/t^3$. It is finite over the coefficient power-series ring and has nilpotent negative-degree coefficients. Its residue field is $k$ when $A$ has residue $k$, and $t$ is a nonzero divisor. Use the original split presentation with $X=t$, $U=a/t$, $V=b/t^2$. Every monomial in an entry of torus weight $w$ has $-i+j+2k=w$. The entry therefore becomes $t^{-w}$ times an element of $A$. Only finitely many powers of $a,b$ survive. Conjugating by $\operatorname{diag}(t,1,t^{-1})$ therefore gives a representation over $A$, reducing to $\bar r_y$ and with $X=1$. The local ordinary conditions also descend. They can be expressed by the unit-separated spectral projectors on inertia, with homogeneous lower-block and diagonal equations, as in the [marked presentation](2026-11-24-why-the-trace-field-must-ramify.md#a-complete-local-ring-with-three-coordinates).

Conversely, conjugate a normalized lift of $\bar r_y$ by the inverse diagonal matrix. The upper entries were nilpotent and become elements of the maximal ideal of the indicated subring. The lower entries gain positive powers of $t$, and the new residual representation is split. The local flag reduces at the nonsplit point to a lower-unipotent change of the prescribed diagonal one because $y$ is locally a coboundary. This change tends to the identity after rescaling. Equivalently the spectral projector equations give the correct ordered flag. The split presentation now imposes $t f_i(a,b)=0$. Cancelling the nonzero divisor and using $A\subset A((t))$ gives both equations in $A$. The same homogeneous entries show that the two constructions are inverse. This also works over finite residue extensions.

## Where the global obstruction goes

The even part of the determinant-one residual Lie algebra under $\Theta$ is three-dimensional. It is the space $z^{-1}P_{\le2}$, where $P_{\le d}$ denotes polynomials of degree at most $d$ on the affine line over $k$. The action before twisting is $F(t)\mapsto F(z(g)(t-y(g)))$. The odd part is $\eta z^{-2}P_{\le4}$. This follows either by differentiating the matrix conjugation or by successively applying $\operatorname{ad}(L)$ to the upper matrix units. [Matrix and bracket checks](boundary-577/boundary_bracket.py)

Write $E$ for the even part. Its first two filtration steps are $k(z^{-1})$ and an extension of $k$ by that line with extension class $y$. The third graded quotient is $k(z)$. For relaxed cohomology the lower line has dimension one, and the top quotient has zero $H^1$ by the multiplier 428. At the first extension, the connecting map on $H^0(k)$ supplies all of $k y$. On $H^1(k)$ it is cup product with $y$. This last map is nonzero. Indeed the unique relaxed trivial-character direction over $\mathbb Q$ is the principal cyclotomic character. Changing the coefficient lift between twists 296 and 265256 changes the connecting obstruction to lifting $y$ by a nonzero scalar times that cup product. At the first twist even a relaxed order-$577^2$ lift is impossible, and at the second we explicitly constructed one. The two cochain problems factor through the same group allowing the prescribed inertia away from 577. Thus **the relaxed $H^1$ of $E$ is zero**. The nonvanishing follows from the two different arithmetic outcomes at the additional coefficient digit.

For the odd part $S$, the five graded characters are $\eta z^i$ for $-2\le i\le2$. Each has zero strict $H^1$ over $\mathbb Q$, including $\eta$ by the class number of $K$. The graded characters have no local invariants at 577, including the nontrivial unramified $\eta$. The filtration splits locally there. Induction in the cohomology sequences therefore gives zero strict $H^1$ for $S$ itself. Local full $H^1$ has dimension five, and the full local contribution in global duality is five. With no real fixed vectors and no global invariants on either side, the relaxed dimension is at least five. Localization on the odd part is consequently an **isomorphism** of five-dimensional spaces. It has zero dual strict Selmer group.

For $E$, local $H^1$ has dimension four with one local invariant line. The three-dimensional real-place contribution cancels the full local difference. Its zero global relaxed $H^1$ therefore also gives zero dual strict Selmer group. The local unconstrained second cohomology at 577 vanishes on both parts by local Tate duality. At the other places the specified finite prime-to-577 inertia conditions lift smoothly. Hence the complete global problem with the coefficient prime relaxed is unobstructed. After the basis normalizations its ring is a power-series ring in five odd parameters over $O$. This uses the same small-extension and Poitou–Tate argument as the [original relation count](2026-11-24-two-equations-at-the-marked-point.md#count-the-available-obstructions), now on the actual nonsplit module.

The ordinary subproblem at 577 cuts the odd local tangent dimension from five to two. Its three missing directions may be eliminated globally by the formal implicit function theorem, since odd localization is an isomorphism. One can choose the equations equivariantly because 2 is a unit. Locally the full and the ordinary framed conditions are smooth because the root ratios have no local $H^2$, the ordered flag is separated on inertia, and the remaining diagonal choices lift. The even local tangent condition cuts dimension four to two. The two residual readings it removes are **the strict lower extension and the ramified change of the diagonal character**. There is no global even tangent available to adjust either. Thus the quadratic problem is the response of the global relaxed lift in precisely these two local coordinates. Locally the two allowed odd upper matrices $E_{12}+E_{23}$ and $E_{13}$ commute. They can be lifted together with zero local commutator correction. That local fact cannot decide the global response.

There is an independent integral check on the origin of this relaxed family. The [rank-one relaxed cyclotomic module](2026-11-24-the-cyclotomic-module-of-the-fixed-class.md) supplies an $O$-valued pure lower lift at its unique 577-adic weight $j_*$. It is fixed by $\Theta$. In odd power-series coordinates the fixed locus is just this $O$-point. Its top diagonal weight differs from weight 296 by $117\bmod577$ on the first principal cyclotomic digit. At its own weight the lower class also fails the local split condition at the next digit. With $X=1$ the local residue is $231\cdot177=497\bmod577$. A convention using the opposite diagonal character negates the weight digit. These are constant-term controls on the two local readings. The response to mixed odd directions remains to be computed.

## The second-order arithmetic test

For any one of the two global ordinary odd tangent classes, or a linear combination $C$, the primary multiplication defect in the even Lie module is represented (up to the overall cochain sign) by
$$\tfrac12[C(g),\,g\cdot C(h)].$$
This is the ordinary square-zero cup obstruction to lifting from $k[\delta]/\delta^2$ to $k[\delta]/\delta^3$. Use the local representatives and local lifts for the prescribed flag and finite inertia conditions. The relaxed global even correction is unique up to a coboundary because its $H^1$ and its obstruction group are zero. Its lower and diagonal local outputs decide ordinarity. Equivalently, allow last-step even tame corrections at the [two measured auxiliary primes](2026-11-24-two-independent-obstruction-primes.md#why-these-primes-work-at-the-nonsplit-point-too) and test whether both required corrections vanish. Neither an unrestricted cup computation nor a local bracket alone is that test.

In polynomial coordinates $A_i$ (degree $i=0,1,2$ in the even module) and $B_i$ (degree $i=0,\ldots,4$ in the odd module), choose the basis with $\operatorname{ad}(L)B_i=-iB_{i-1}$ and top $B_4=E_{13}$, and similarly $A_2=E_{12}-E_{23}$. The six nonzero $[B_i,B_j]$ for $i<j$ are, in the order $(0,3),(0,4),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,3)$,
$$-A_0/8,\quad -A_1/2,\quad A_0/16,\quad A_1/16,\quad -A_2/8,\quad A_2/16.$$
They are exact rational matrix identities. Determining the coefficients of the *global cocycles* $C(g)$ at the necessary elements is the arithmetic step still needed. Each choice of direction gives two scalars in a common basis. Evaluating the two basis directions $U,V$ and their sum gives six scalars in total. For either local coordinate $Q$, its mixed coefficient is $Q(U+V)-Q(U)-Q(V)$. These values determine both binary quadratics. A common zero of their initial forms could still be cut off by higher terms. [Consequences of the different outcomes](2026-11-24-two-equations-at-the-marked-point.md#a-falsifiable-alternative)

There is an abelian route to each first-order odd cocycle. Embed the quartic polynomials into all $k$-valued functions on the affine 577-point set. Shapiro's lemma then evaluates the class at the stabilizer of its origin, the absolute group of the [degree-577 real resolvent](2026-11-24-the-affine-resolvent-and-its-class-groups.md). Injectivity on $H^1$ follows because the quotient of the function module by the quartics, twisted by $\eta z^{-2}$, has no global invariants because inertia at 7 fixes the affine set and acts by $-1$ on the twist. The output is a degree-one cohomology class on the resolvent with scalar coefficient $\eta z^{-2}$. Over its coefficient field that becomes an abelian ray-class character. Above 577 the resolvent has ramification indices 1 and 72, and adjoining this scalar coefficient field adds no larger ramification index there. Principal units at sufficiently high depth, in particular those congruent to 1 modulo $577^2$, are 577th powers locally. Thus the first-order computation needs a finite ray modulus. Identifying the two scalar ray characters, or equivalently the global primitive of their bracket, has not been performed. Restriction to this degree-577 field and division by its degree would not justify a computation of $H^2$.

## Unresolved

The primary forms now have a genuine second-order interpretation with explicit dual detecting primes. If the two invariant series share a nonconstant characteristic-577 curve, it still contradicts the [source finite-index claim](2026-11-24-two-equations-at-the-marked-point.md#a-falsifiable-alternative). Finite special fiber gives ordinary characteristic-zero Galois lifts with the existing ramification bound, followed by a separate automorphy obligation. The computed Bockstein and class groups determine neither alternative.
