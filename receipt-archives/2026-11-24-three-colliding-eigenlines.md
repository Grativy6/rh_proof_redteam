# Counting three colliding eigenlines on both sides of the transfer

**24 November 2026.** Hearthline, under Chris Pang’s continuing request. The released proof uses an identity between a twisted orbital integral on two blocks and a signed orbital integral on their direct sum. Reducing a matrix at a finite place can merge three distinct eigenspaces. We computed both sides directly when this happens. In the first 3-adic case there are 61 contributing lattices on the full space. Of these, 48 glue all three lines together. The signed count is 45. Including the released normalizations gives $5/9$ on each side. The [exact enumeration](check_inert_spectral_frames.py) also checks a deeper collision that reverses the sign, and a dyadic example. ## Which assertion is being tested?

At a nonsplit finite place of a CM field unramified over its real subfield at every finite place, let $F$ denote the real-field completion, $E/F$ its unramified quadratic extension, $\pi$ a uniformizer in $F$, $q$ the residue size of $F$, and $O$ the integers of $E$. For $a+b=n$, depth $h\ge1$, and monic conjugate-reciprocal $Q_1,Q_2$ of degrees $a,b$ with separable product, the assertion is

$$\operatorname{stTw2}(Q_1,Q_2;\phi_h)
 =(-q)^{-v(\operatorname{Res}(Q_1,Q_2))}
    \operatorname{kapOrb}(Q_1,Q_2;1_{K(h)}).$$

On the twisted side $\phi_h$ is $(-q)^{-hab}$ times the indicator that each block is integrally invertible and its **twisted norm** is congruent to the identity modulo $\pi^h$. On the unitary side the sign is the parity of the valuation of the Gram determinant on $\ker Q_1(\gamma)$. A change of basis multiplies this determinant by a norm, which has even valuation. The proof uses this match in its base-change frame. The declaration dependency graph has an eleven-edge route through `frameEx0`, `baseChangeN`, `everyCharacter` and `rhOfAll` to `riemannHypothesis`. We test $a=1,b=2$. Fix an integral $\alpha\in E$ with $\alpha-\bar\alpha$ a unit and distinct $t_i\in\mathbb Z$. Set
$$\lambda_i=\frac{1+\pi^h\alpha t_i}{1+\pi^h\bar\alpha t_i}.$$
Each root has norm one. Its linear polynomial is conjugate-reciprocal. The product is separable since the $t_i$ are distinct. With $D_0=\mathrm{diag}(t_i)$ and $\gamma=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)$, a lattice $L$ satisfies $(\gamma-I)L\subseteq\pi^hL$ exactly when $D_0L\subseteq L$. Indeed $Z=(\gamma-I)/\pi^h=(\alpha-\bar\alpha)D_0(1+\pi^h\bar\alpha D_0)^{-1}$. Conversely $D_0=((\alpha-\bar\alpha)I-\pi^h\bar\alpha Z)^{-1}Z$. Each inverted factor is a unit times identity modulo $\pi$ on the lattice in the direction where it is used. In particular
$$v(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)=h+v(t_i-t_j).$$

These examples also have number-field realizations satisfying the theorem's global host hypothesis. In each row below $F^+$ is the real subfield of the displayed biquadratic CM field. The product of the two displayed imaginary quadratic discriminants equals the discriminant of $F^+$. The products of their integral basis elements span an order whose discriminant is the square of that product, by the trace determinant. The discriminant tower gives the same square times the positive integer norm of the relative discriminant for the full field. Since the full discriminant divides the order discriminant, the relative discriminant is the unit ideal. In each case the rational prime splits in $F^+$ and is inert in the chosen imaginary quadratic subfield. Thus $F=\mathbb Q_p$, $q=p$, and we take $\pi=p$. The global eigenvalues have complex absolute value one at every embedding.

## Count the twisted side from its own definition

Write $J$ for the integral signed antidiagonal matrix of a block of rank $m$, so $J^*=\epsilon J$ with $\epsilon=(-1)^{m-1}$. Here $*$ is conjugate transpose. Put $D=yJ$. Under twisted conjugation by $g$, $D$ becomes $g^{-1}Dg^{-*}$. The norm is $\gamma=\epsilon DD^{-*}$, where $D^{-*}=(D^*)^{-1}$. Fix an eigenbasis with the distinct $\lambda_i$ in a specified order. The equality $D=\epsilon\gamma D^*$ forces $D_{ij}=\lambda_i\bar\lambda_jD_{ij}$. Thus $D$ is diagonal. [Twist and norm matrices](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/TwConj.lean#L11-L22) · [Group involution](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/AutomorphicMembership/ContinuousConjFin.lean#L390-L397)

Take a unit $s$ with $s/\bar s=\epsilon$ (use $1$ or $\alpha-\bar\alpha$), and an integral $u$ of unit trace. Such a $u$ exists since the trace on the quadratic residue extension is onto, including at 2. The diagonal coefficients of $D$ are precisely $s(u+\lambda_i\bar u)a_i$ with $a_i\in F^\times$. Their ratios to their conjugates are $\epsilon\lambda_i$. Diagonal changes of eigenbasis change $a_i$ by norms. Norms of units are all units of $F$: the norm on the finite residue fields is onto, and the successive principal-unit corrections solve a surjective residue trace equation at each depth. Norm valuations are even. Thus the classes have independent parities $d_i\in\{0,1\}$.

For $L=gO^m$, write $L^\vee=g^{-*}O^m$. Integral invertibility of the twisted representative means $DL^\vee=L$. When $ZL\subseteq L$, $uI+\bar u\gamma$ is an automorphism of $L$ since it is a unit scalar modulo $\pi^h$. Removing this factor and $s$ identifies the condition with self-duality for the Hermitian matrix $\mathrm{diag}(a_i^{-1})$. Representatives can be chosen with entries $\pi^{d_i}$. The twisted centralizer is the compact product of the norm-one unit groups. The right compact average in the source is constant on each contributing coset, and its stabilizer index counts every lattice in the orbit once. The twisted sum therefore counts stable self-dual lattices **separately on the two blocks**, with positive weight before the prefactor. [Compact average](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/SimpleTwistedTraceFormulaStable.lean#L32-L42) · [Double-coset weight and class sum](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/SimpleTwistedTraceFormulaStable.lean#L59-L68)

## Count the unitary side on the full space

Distinct norm-one eigenvalues force the eigenlines to be pairwise orthogonal. After choosing eigenvectors, each line has Gram coefficient $\pi^{d_i}$ up to norm. For the standard ambient form the total parity is even. Conversely, two odd lines form a hyperbolic plane just as two even lines do. In each plane $-1$ is a unit norm and gives an isotropic vector. Pair it with a vector of pairing 1 and subtract a multiple of the isotropic vector to make the second vector isotropic, using surjectivity of the field trace. The two resulting hyperbolic bases give the isometry. Thus total parity classifies the ambient form here. A unimodular integral Hermitian lattice has an orthonormal basis: its nonsingular residue form has a vector of nonzero norm (use the nondegenerate residue trace pairing if needed), which lifts to a vector of unit norm. Normalize by a unit norm and split off the line; induction finishes. Hence the hyperspecial cosets on this side account for all self-dual lattices. Again the centralizer is compact, and the weight counts the lattices themselves. If $Q_1$ selects line $i$, each contributes $(-1)^{d_i}$. [Unitary group, hyperspecial compact and sign](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/SimpleTwistedTraceFormulaStable.lean#L133-L143)

For each coordinate, write the projection of $L$ as $\pi^{a_i}O$. Self-duality then makes the pure-axis intersection $\pi^{-d_i-a_i}O$. Their length difference is $r_i=-d_i-2a_i\ge0$. After normalizing projections to $O$, the Hermitian coefficients are $\pi^{-r_i}$. The pure-axis ideals become $\pi^{r_i}O$, and $d_i\equiv r_i\bmod2$. The integral operator $\prod_{j\ne i}(D_0-t_jI)$ acts by $\prod_{j\ne i}(t_i-t_j)$ on the $i$th axis and by zero on the others. Consequently
$$r_i\le\sum_{j\ne i}v(t_i-t_j).$$
This bound makes the enumeration finite without supposing the individual spectral projectors are integral.

In two coordinates every glue between the two cyclic projected-to-cut quotients is the graph of an isomorphism. Both gaps equal $r$. The normalized lattice has $y\equiv ux\bmod\pi^r$ and self-duality requires $u\bar u\equiv-1\bmod\pi^r$. It is stable exactly when $r\le v(t_1-t_2)$. At $r=0$ there is one lattice. At positive $r$ the norm fibers have size $(q+1)q^{r-1}$ by the residue norm and successive trace lifts. A singleton block admits one self-dual lattice across its two rational parity classes.

## The three-line contribution

Suppose all three slope differences have valuation 1 and the reduced slopes after subtracting a common value and dividing by $\pi$ are distinct $s_1,s_2,s_3\in\mathbb F_q$. Every $r_i$ is at most 2. The sum $\sum r_i$ is even by self-duality. A single largest positive gap cannot occur, since a vector projecting to a unit there has a norm term of strictly lower valuation than its other terms. With one coordinate of gap zero the remaining pair is the binary calculation, so two gaps of 2 with a zero are also impossible. The surviving patterns are $000$, the three placements of $110$, and $222$. The first contributes one lattice, and each $110$ contributes $q+1$.

For $222$, work modulo $\pi^2$ on all three axes. The reduction of a self-dual glue is a nonzero isotropic line $\ell=\langle v\rangle$ in the residual Hermitian three-space, with all coordinates nonzero. Its intersection with the $\pi$-multiple subspace is $\pi\ell^\perp$ by self-duality. Lifting a fixed generator $v$ amounts to choosing a correction from the one-dimensional residue quotient $k^3/\ell^\perp$, where $k=\mathbb F_{q^2}$. One surjective trace equation imposes isotropy of the lift, leaving $q$ choices. Multiplying a lift by $D_0$ shows stability exactly when $\sum_i s_i N(v_i)=0$. Isotropy already imposes $\sum_i N(v_i)=0$. The allowed triples of norms are precisely the nonzero multiples of $(s_2-s_3,s_3-s_1,s_1-s_2)$. Each nonzero norm has $q+1$ preimages. Dividing the number of vectors by $q^2-1$ gives $(q+1)^2$ lines and **$q(q+1)^2$ lattices**.

For any singleton choice, two of the $110$ patterns have negative sign and one positive. The signed full-space count is
$$1-(q+1)+q(q+1)^2=q^2(q+2).$$
The same-block pair on the twisted side has $1+(q+1)=q+2$ lattices. The cross resultant has valuation $2h+2$. Both sides therefore give $(q+2)/q^{2h}$. This derives equality for this collision family over each indicated local field. No matching theorem was used in either count.

## A finite observation that provably misses the answer

Here is a test of the APCI factorization criterion on the transfer value itself. Fix the singleton $t_1=0$. At $q=3,h=1$, the polynomials from $(0,3,6)$ and $(0,3,12)$ agree coefficient by coefficient modulo 9: all the norm-one roots above are $1\bmod9$. Both cross resultants have valuation 4. Nevertheless the normalized answers in the first-singleton rows are $5/9$ and $17/9$. The change is in $v(t_2-t_3)$ within the second block.

There is an all-depth version admitting a two-line lattice count. Fix $h\ge1$ and take slopes $(0,1,1+\pi^k)$ for $k\ge1$. Compare this input with the same slopes at $k+1$, using $\pi=p$ in any of the displayed number fields. Corresponding individual roots, hence both block polynomials, agree modulo $\pi^{h+k}$. Both cross resultants have valuation $2h$. Yet the signed count for singleton zero is
$$M(k)=1+\sum_{r=1}^{k}(q+1)q^{r-1}.$$
Indeed the spectral projector onto the singleton is integral, since $(0-1)(0-1-\pi^k)$ is a unit. Every stable self-dual lattice therefore splits as that singleton and the binary block. The singleton has even parity and the binary count above gives $M(k)$. The two twisted blocks give the same unsigned count independently. The normalized answers differ by $(q+1)q^k/q^{2h}$. Thus for **every fixed precision**, even recording the cross-resultant valuation as well, there are admissible inputs with identical observations and different transfer values. The Python control counts these full three-dimensional lattices at $p=3$ through $k=4$.

The release retains both complete polynomials in its orbital integrals. Its matching condition does not replace either side by a function of the two reduced polynomials and the cross-resultant valuation alone. This distinguishes an exact failure of the proposed compressed observation from an invalid inference in the stated match. A nearby local-constancy theorem in the release fixes a regular invariant $a_0$ with nonzero discriminant **before** taking a neighborhood of $a_0$. The family $k\to\infty$ above tends to a singular repeated root. A single precision on this whole family would be stronger than the quantifiers in that theorem. [Local constancy at each regular invariant](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/InertiaEigenvalues/JacquetRallisOrbitalIntegralsInvariantLocallyConStatement.lean#L5-L7)

A global restriction could supply a finite precision. For example, suppose one positive integer $D$ clears every coefficient of every allowed degree-$n$ polynomial over a fixed number field $K$, and every conjugate root has absolute value one. Two distinct degree-$j$ elementary coefficients $a,b$ then satisfy $|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(D(a-b))|\le (2D\binom{n}{j})^{[K:\mathbb Q]}$. Since $D(a-b)$ is a nonzero algebraic integer, this norm is divisible by $q_w^{v_w(D)+v_w(a-b)}$, where $q_w$ is the residue size. Thus the common denominator and the archimedean bound together give a separating precision. Applying this argument to a varying global family would require a common denominator or an explicit bound with the right parameter dependence. The local matching lemma supplies its own full inputs.

## Unresolved

The calculation establishes the equality for the stated equal-depth three-line family and the isolated-singleton family at every depth. Exact enumeration covers the listed nested cases. The general claim remains under audit. A regular polynomial can have an irreducible ramified factor over $E$. Then a coordinate lattice in that factor is a module over a field order and the separate-eigenline classification used here is unavailable. This is a concrete next test of the local matching input.
