# The exact cost of powering a Frobenius matrix

**25 November 2026.** Christopher Daniel Pang asked what residue survives repeated measurements. His PAL atlas and APCI work led to comparing the same local arithmetic map at successive powers and precisions. Hearthline derived the exact count below. Alpöge and Claude supply the matrices, the paired coefficient space and the order of the limits in their patching argument. Smith normal form over a discrete valuation ring is the classical arithmetic input. [PAL atlas](../corpus/PAL_v2.4-M_Mathematical_Realization_Atlas.docx) · [APCI](../corpus/APCI_Declared_Interface_v1.0.0.docx) · [Pang’s shadow-lattice paper](../corpus/Shadow_Lattice_and_Dispersal_Lattice.pdf)

The release bounds how many matrices commute with a fixed Galois element modulo $t^m$. Even when the eigenvalues are distinct in $k[[t]]$, several can agree through many coefficients. The smallest additive error in the counting exponent admits an exact formula. On replacing the element by its $p^s$th power, one part of the error grows by $p^s$. An index measuring the integral lattice stays fixed. We trace precisely which of these constants the release uses.

## The operator in the released bound

Write $A=k[[t]]$ for the power-series ring over the finite field of $q$ elements and characteristic $p$. At a selected element $\sigma$, let $P=\rho(\sigma)=\operatorname{diag}(P_1,P_2)$ with block sizes $a,b$, $a+b=n$. Assume its characteristic polynomial splits into $n$ distinct roots in $A$, as in the local bound. Write $g$ for the number of odd extension coordinates. The release’s coefficient multiplication has $e_i f_j=\delta_{ij}\eta$, with every other product between these positive-degree basis elements zero. Its first-order matrix and its dual variables are exactly
$$
 R=P+\sum_{j=1}^g e_j X_j,\qquad
 Y=\eta Z+\sum_{j=1}^g f_jY_j ,
$$
where $Z$ is block diagonal, and $X_j,Y_j$ are off block. Here $X_j=\kappa_j(\sigma)P$. The space $W$ of all $Y$ is free of rank $a^2+b^2+2gab$. Its reduction is the entire dual coefficient space used at finite precision. Let $L$ be the endomorphism $Y\mapsto RY-YR$ on $W$. Multiplication in the displayed ring gives its odd outputs $[P,Y_j]$ and its even output $[P,Z]+\sum_j[X_j,Y_j]$. Over the fraction field, the operator $U\mapsto[U,P]$ is invertible on the off-block matrices because the spectra of $P_1,P_2$ are disjoint. Choose off-block $U_j$ with $[U_j,P]=X_j$. Conjugation by $1+\sum e_jU_j$ takes $P$ to $R$ and preserves $W$ over that field. The inverse is $1-\sum e_jU_j$. Diagonalizing $P_1,P_2$ now shows that $L$ is diagonalizable over the fraction field and has a kernel of dimension $n$. The integral cost of that change of basis can be nonzero.

## The exact finite count and its integral index

Write $F=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, $I=W\cap\operatorname{im}(L_F)$ and $K=W\cap\ker(L_F)$. The intersection defining $I$ is inside $W\otimes_A F$. Let $s_1,\ldots,s_h$ be the valuations of the nonzero Smith factors of $L$, including those of valuation zero. For every $m\ge0$,
$$
 \log_q|\ker(L\bmod t^m)|=nm+\sum_{j=1}^{h}\min(m,s_j).
$$
Indeed each zero diagonal in Smith form contributes $m$ free field coefficients, and multiplication by $t^{s_j}$ has a kernel of length $\min(m,s_j)$. Thus the **least** uniform additive constant after fixing $\sigma$ is $C(\sigma)=\sum_j s_j=\operatorname{length}_A(I/LW)$.

Put $f_i=\operatorname{charpoly}(P_i)$, use valuation $v(t)=1$, and take the discriminant of an empty or linear block to be one. Set
$$
 \delta=v(\operatorname{disc}f_1)+v(\operatorname{disc}f_2)
               +2g\,v(\operatorname{Res}(f_1,f_2)),\qquad
 D=\operatorname{length}_A\big(W/(K+I)\big).
$$
For an empty block the resultant is one. The first number is $v(\det(L_F|_{\operatorname{im}L_F}))$. Each ordered pair within a block contributes one eigenvalue difference. Each cross-block pair occurs in both directions in each of the $g$ odd copies. The determinant is unaffected by the rational conjugation above. The second number measures the index of the lattice generated by the integral kernel and the saturated image. Though they span the fraction-field space together, their integral sum can be smaller than $W$.

**Power formula.** For $k_0=p^s$ with $s\ge0$,
$$
                   C(\sigma^{k_0})=k_0\delta-D .
$$
In particular $C(\sigma)=\delta-D$. To see this, $L$ preserves $I$ and is injective there. The index of $LI$ in $I$ has length $\delta$, and $LW/LI\simeq W/(K+I)$. More generally, $L^{k_0}W/L^{k_0}I$ still has length $D$ and $I/L^{k_0}I$ has length $k_0\delta$. Left and right multiplication by $R$ commute, so in characteristic $p$ the operator $L^{p^s}$ equals $Y\mapsto R^{p^s}Y-YR^{p^s}$. This identity first holds on the full matrix algebra and restricts to $W$. Since $R$ is the value of a representation, the latter is exactly the operator for $\sigma^{p^s}$. Its saturated image and kernel remain $I,K$. This proves the formula.

There is also an exact second count. On the space of ordinary off-block matrices, the release bounds the commuting subspace at a second obstruction estimate. Its fraction-field kernel is zero and its determinant has valuation $2v(\operatorname{Res}(f_1,f_2))$. This is its least uniform additive exponent. It scales by $p^s$ under the same powers. The scalar comparing Hecke modules uses a different product. At limiting norm 1 it is $(\operatorname{disc}(f_1f_2))^2$. Its value can agree for integral matrices whose first-order counting constants differ. ## Readings with the same characteristic polynomial

In a $1+1$ block write $r=v(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)$. With $g\ge1$, let $u$ be the smallest valuation of any off-diagonal entry among the $X_j$, with $u=\infty$ if all vanish. The $2g$ odd variables are each killed by the same eigenvalue difference. There is one additional scalar relation on them, with coefficients the off-diagonal entries of the $X_j$ up to signs. At precision $m$, set $d=\min(m,r)$. Each odd variable has $d$ free coefficients, and the scalar relation removes $\max(0,d-u)$ of them, by the entry of lowest valuation. Thus the exact counting exponent is $2m+2gd-\max(0,d-u)$, with $D=\max(0,r-u)$. For $g=0$ only the two diagonal variables remain.

For a $2+1$ example over $\mathbf F_5[[t]]$ take $P_2=1+2t^r$ and compare
$$
 P_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1+t^r\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad P_1'=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1+t^r\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Use one odd copy with $X_1=0$. Both choices give $\delta=6r$. For the diagonal choice all six nonzero Smith valuations equal $r$, so $D=0$. For the triangular choice the two nonzero within-block commutator factors are units. Indeed its reduction is a single Jordan block, whose ordinary centralizer has dimension two, so the commutator has rank two even after reduction. In each cross-block direction the two-by-two difference matrix has a unit entry and determinant of valuation $2r$. Its Smith valuations are $0,2r$. Altogether $C(\sigma)=4r$ and $D=2r$. At $r=2$ the optimal constants at the fifth power are 60 and 56. The integral index still accounts for the difference of four, although all the eigenvalue gaps have grown fivefold.

These are controls on the local matrix calculation. The general formula applies to the actual $X_j$ of every input satisfying the released eigenvalue hypotheses. The [PARI/GP replay](check_frobenius_centralizer_cost.gp) also tests a $2+1$ matrix with two nonzero mixed extension coordinates. It computes both the Smith valuations and the rank of the full coefficient matrix over $\mathbf F_5$ at precisions 1 through 13. The arbitrary-precision and power claims follow from the proof.

## What happens at the primes the proof really selects?

Even for the arithmetic objects, a constant uniform over **all choices of separating element** would generally fail. At a rank-three nice input choose a local element in the cyclotomic kernel on which all the diagonal ratios have infinite order. This choice can be made from the pair witnesses, as shown in the [finite separation calculation](2026-11-25-the-centroid-that-hides-four-character-readings.md). Its eigenvalues are the flag characters. In a normalized model some positive power makes $R$ the identity modulo $t$, because the reduced matrix belongs to a finite group. The eigenvalue ratios stay of infinite order. Call the powered element $\tau$. For every $k_0=p^s$, the binomial identity gives $R(\tau^{k_0})=1\bmod t^{k_0}$. Every vector of $W/t^{k_0}W$ now commutes. Its counting exponent is $k_0(a^2+b^2+2gab)$. In rank three, $a^2+b^2\ge5>3$, so a bound with slope three and one common additive constant for all these choices is impossible. The local-input hypotheses stay true through all the powers. The published sequence has a different order of choices. It first takes finitely many points and fixes the elements $\sigma_j$ simultaneously, then constructs the prime levels. For each fixed $m$ the eventual Frobenius matrices agree with these same fixed elements modulo $t^m$. To see the arithmetic, lift each $\sigma_j$ to the absolute group. Its cyclotomic character is 1. In each finite quotient intersect the observing kernel with the kernel of the cyclotomic character modulo $p^N$. Chebotarev realizes the specified inverse class away from the finite set of forbidden places. A choice of prime above it fixes the representative, and inversion gives the source’s geometric Frobenius. The construction uses the cyclotomic congruence to choose the prime. It does not raise $\sigma_j$ to $p^N$. At precision $m$ the first-order representation also has finite image. On the subgroup used for the pro-$p$ quotient, adjoining its first-order coordinates extends the ordinary finite $p$-group image by a subgroup of the additive off-block matrices, again a $p$-group. The finite first-order test is therefore one of the allowed observations. Equality there puts its invariant matrices inside the fixed centralizer counted above. One may sum the constants for the fixed finite tuple. At the passage to the limit the proof fixes $m$, intersects the relevant sets of levels, and selects one $N$ in that intersection. It makes this selection separately for each $m$. This independently explains why the growing error of the powered family misses this particular estimate. A separate symplectic construction really does take the limit of powers $p^{n!}$ of a Frobenius lift. In its residual representation the matrix is diagonal, and every residual eigenvalue belongs to a finite field. For $n$ large, the residue degree divides $n!$, so this power fixes each residual eigenvalue by finite-field Frobenius. The published normalization uses differences already distinguished in that residue. This comparison does not transport the preceding finite-precision constant through those powers.
