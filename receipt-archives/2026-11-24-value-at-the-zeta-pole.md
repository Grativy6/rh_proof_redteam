## Point value, residue and finite part

The classical meromorphic zeta function has a simple pole of residue 1 at $s=1$. A Lean function of type $\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ returns an element there. In the pinned Mathlib construction that number is $(\gamma-\log(4\pi))/2$, where $\gamma$ is Euler’s constant. Its sign can be checked on paper from $\gamma<1<\log(4\pi)$. The uncompleted zeta function has Laurent finite part $\gamma$. The two numbers measure different expressions. [Mathlib’s value and nonvanishing proof](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Harmonic/ZetaAsymp.lean#L395-L447) · [Laurent expansion](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2#E4)

For the precise normalization, put $A(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$. Then $A(1)=1$ and $A'(1)=(-\log\pi-\gamma-2\log2)/2$, using the digamma value at $1/2$. The constant term in $A(s)\zeta(s)$ is $\gamma+A'(1)=(\gamma-\log(4\pi))/2$. The completed finite part explains the assigned value. The residue remains 1 in both expansions. [Digamma special values](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.4#E13) · [Mathlib’s limiting formula](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Harmonic/ZetaAsymp.lean#L395-L427)

## Change just one value

Let $Z_c(s)=\zeta(s)$ for $s\ne1$ and $Z_c(1)=c$. Set $g_c(s)=(s-1)Z_c(s)$ off 1 and $g_c(1)=1$. Every $g_c$ is the same entire function. Every $Z_c$ has the same punctured germ and the same meromorphic order $-1$ at 1. At every other point multiplication by $s-1$ preserves nonvanishing. At 1, $g_c(1)\ne0$ and $Z_c(1)\ne0$ means precisely $c\ne0$. Hence

$$\big[\forall s,\;g_c(s)\ne0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Z_c(s)\ne0\big]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\ne0.$$

The released `poleData_char` constructs exactly this regularization, proves that $g(1)=1$ and $g'(1)=\gamma$ from the punctured limit, and invokes `riemannZeta_one_ne_zero` in the $s=1$ case of this zero-set comparison. The converse test above requires no knowledge of a single nontrivial zero. For the third row only the usual nonvanishing on $\Re(s)\ge1$ away from the pole enters. The boundary point is supplied separately in Mathlib’s own proof. For the fourth row the quantified zero comes with $s\ne1$, so substituting $Z_c$ into the formula leaves each application unchanged. The [APCI control](PoleValueControl.lean) also proves that no exact decoder of the full punctured function can decide from those values whether the assigned point is zero, expressed as $Z_c(1)=0$. [Pinned nonvanishing theorem](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/Nonvanishing.lean#L405-L418) · [Corpus interface](../corpus/Interface.lean)

The boundary nonvanishing used in that third row also has a direct check for zeta. For real $\sigma>1$ and $t\ne0$, the Euler product gives $|\zeta(\sigma)^3\zeta(\sigma+it)^4\zeta(\sigma+2it)|\ge1$ because its logarithm is a sum with nonnegative weights multiplying $3+4\cos x+\cos(2x)=2(1+\cos x)^2$. If $\zeta(1+it)$ had a zero of order $m\ge1$, the same product would be $O((\sigma-1)^{4m-3})$ as $\sigma\downarrow1$, since $1+2it$ is regular. This gives the contradiction. Absolute convergence of the Euler product handles $\Re(s)>1$.

## Follow the residue into the window argument

The modulus-one prime ramp subtracts $y\log y$ and adds $(1+\gamma)y$. Its Mellin transform for $\Re(s)>1$ depends on the ordinary off-pole values of $\zeta'/\zeta$. The release evaluates this transform initially still farther right. Replacing a single assigned value at 1 leaves that prime ramp and its transform there identical. [Actual ramp](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/CharFamily.lean#L10-L22) · [Mellin identity](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/mellinGchiModOne.lean#L115-L122) · [Initial half-plane](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/poleData.lean#L204-L221)

One can also calculate what survives the pole subtraction. With $h=s-1$ and first Stieltjes constant $\gamma_1$ in the convention $\zeta(1+h)=h^{-1}+\gamma-\gamma_1h+O(h^2)$, one has $g(1+h)=1+\gamma h-\gamma_1h^2+O(h^3)$. The regularized Mellin expression in the source is
$$-\frac{g'(s)/g(s)-s\gamma+(1-s)}{s(s-1)}.$$
Its numerator before the outside minus sign has coefficient $-2\gamma_1-\gamma^2-\gamma-1$ at $h$. The analytically continued transform therefore has **zero residue** and finite value $1+\gamma+\gamma^2+2\gamma_1$ at 1. Every $c$ gives this same limit. [Regularized expression](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/poleData.lean#L22-L42) · [Principal normalization](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/poleData.lean#L149-L175) · [Stieltjes convention](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2#E4)
