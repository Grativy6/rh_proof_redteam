# Two opposite ideal-class readings at the original 577 input

**24 November 2026.** The [explicit real unit $e_-$](2026-11-24-two-real-unit-norm-targets-at-577.md) is a global field norm from $L_y/M$ and from the cyclotomic layer $M_1/M$ separately. It is **not** a global norm from their compositum. For every $b\in M_1^\times$ with norm $e_-$, the divisor of $b$ is a difference of conjugate ideals, and the intervening ideal reads **2 modulo 577** on the original unramified character. No cyclotomic norm preimage can be a global unit. A second explicit circular construction gives a divided ideal with reading **$-2$**. Together they give base targets for each of the two missing ordinary coordinates separately.

## The simultaneous norm on the named unit

Put $T=L_yM_1$, where $M_1$ is the first rational cyclotomic $p$-layer composited with $M$, $p=577$. Its two cyclic degree-$p$ factors over $M$ are independent. The $y$ extension is everywhere unramified and split at $p$, while the cyclotomic layer is totally ramified at each place above $p$. Away from $p$ the compositum is unramified. Every local decomposition group is cyclic, so the Hasse norm defect of $T/M$ has order $p$ by the exterior-square calculation. The unit $e_-$ is everywhere a local norm. At $p$ its class has nontrivial tame character $z^{-1}$ and the cyclotomic norm character on units is trivial on this eigenspace. The cyclic Hasse theorem supplies each separate global preimage.

Here the central extension which reads the missing bit of information is already known. Use the [actual relaxed order-$p^2$ lower lift at twist 265256](2026-11-24-the-last-577th-power-digit.md). Over $M$ its image is generated by a translation $x$ of order $p^2$ and a lift of the principal cyclotomic generator $\tau$. Its abelianization field is $T$, and its commutator group is the order-$p$ line $x^p$. In fact the lower coefficient action of $\tau$ is $(1+p)^{-413}\bmod p^2$, so $[\tau,x]=x^{-413p}$. Here $y$ is normalized by $y(\gamma)=1$ and the generator $x^p$ corresponds to $pL$ in the matrix, $L=E_{21}-E_{32}$. The local groups over $M$ are abelian. At $p$ the residual translation splits, so in a local lower frame the matrices reduce to the identity and multiplication of their last digits is additive. Elsewhere this extension is unramified over $M$.

The product of the local Artin symbols of an everywhere-local norm gives its global defect in this central line, as in the [surjectivity argument for mixed tangents](2026-11-24-which-norm-tests-see-the-cube.md#the-obstruction-the-global-bracket-measures). Both groups have order $p$. Use local reciprocity taking a uniformizer to arithmetic Frobenius. The lower reading of $u_-$ is $94L$ after division by $p$ over $F_v$ and $47L$ in the real completion $M_v$ by the quadratic norm. Each of the eight coefficient places contributes $47\cdot171$ for $e_-$ in the common global convention. All other places contribute zero. Therefore
$$\Psi_{\rm int}(e_-)=8\cdot47\cdot171=249=2\cdot413\bmod577.$$

One can locate the same number in the ideal of any cyclotomic norm preimage. If $N_{M_1/M}b=e_-$, its ideal has norm one. Hence $(b)=(\tau-1)I$, using additive notation on fractional ideals and taking $I$ prime to $p$. This follows prime by prime by taking successive partial sums of valuations. At $p$ itself $b$ is a unit because there is only one place above each place of $M$. Over $M_1$ the central extension just used is cyclic with translation group $\langle x\rangle$ of order $p^2$. On the ideal $I$ the residue of its Artin exponent is $y(I)$, the unramified reading on $L_yM_1/M_1$. Thus the Artin value on $(\tau-1)I$ is $-413p\,y(I)$. The $p$-adic contributions for $b$ equal the previous ones on $e_-$ by local norm functoriality. Global reciprocity gives **$413y(I)=249$, hence $y(I)=2$**. A unit $b$ would permit $I$ to be the unit ideal. Adjoining $\mu_p$ to put this in a Kummer field preserves the failure of a unit preimage by norm back across prime-to-$p$ degree.

This is a direct test of a distinction in the source's own norm theorem. The input is an $S$-unit. Its `kNorm` conclusion takes the norm of a unit **of the field algebra**, whose unit group consists of all nonzero elements in the field case. It does not require an integral unit or give a bound on the support of a norm preimage. ## Recover a second global target in the abelian field

Raise the conductor of $C_-$, the circular unit $C_{z^{-1}}=e_-^p$, from $256p$ to $256p^2$. Take the same tame weights modulo $p^2$, using the Teichmüller lifts of the $p$-part indices to $(\mathbb Z/p^2)^\times$, with initial principal-cyclotomic index zero. Call the resulting unit $C_1\in M_1$. Use compatible standard roots with $\zeta_{256p^2}^p=\zeta_{256p}$. Taking the product over the wild conjugates of a factor $1-\zeta_{256p^2}^a$ gives its original factor downstairs. Thus **$N_{M_1/M}C_1=e_-^p$**. The nonzero reading 2 already implies $C_1$ cannot be a $p$th power in the real field $M_1$.

There is an explicit descent of the high translation difference. For $v=C_1/e_-$ set $v_j=\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}\tau^k v$, with $v_0=v_p=1$. In $M_1$ take
$$w=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}v_j^{-1},\qquad R=N_{M_1/M}w.$$
The circular products and the unique real odd root are totally positive. Hence $w\ne0$ and $\tau w/w=v$. The class of $R$ modulo $p$th powers in $M_1$ is $(\tau-1)^{p-2}C_1$ in multiplicative group-ring notation. To verify the sign, let $J=\sum j\tau^j$, so $(\tau-1)J=p-\sum\tau^j$. Modulo $p$, $(\tau-1)^{p-2}=-\sum(j+1)\tau^j$. Substituting $C_1=e_-\,w^{\tau-1}$ gives precisely the norm of $w$ modulo powers. Restriction of power classes from $M$ is injective because this totally real cyclic extension has no nontrivial $p$th roots of unity. The class has character $z^{-1}$. Every valuation is divisible by $p$. Away from $p$ this follows because the ideal of $w$ is invariant in an unramified extension. At $p$ the valuation is fixed by an inertia element acting nontrivially on the power class. Write $J_R=\operatorname{div}(R)/p$.

Evaluate all 577 wild translates at the following split primes. Each row uses a single final degree-$p$ power-residue exponent for each of the 577 products. Summing those exponents gives zero, as required by the norm $e_-^p$. Weighting by $-(j+1)$ recovers the exponent of $R$.

## The second divided ideal reads minus two

Let $s_R$ be the local coefficient of the $p$-unit class $R$ on $u_-$ and let $j_R=y_1(J_R)$, where $y_1$ has exponent one at 2363393. Finite primes where we evaluate the class can be cleared of $p$th-power valuations by approximation. This preserves the divided ideal class and the displayed power-residue test. In particular the circular derivative is a unit representative at each split test prime.

Use the same order-$p^2$ abelian reciprocity over $M$ as in the [base-unit check](2026-11-24-two-real-unit-norm-targets-at-577.md#check-the-division-using-other-places). Outside the coefficient and test primes, a valuation $p\,v(J_R)$ now contributes the mod-$p$ Frobenius of the original lower class, whose scale is $u(r)y_1$. Including the unramified part at the test primes, reciprocity gives
$$8\cdot223h_r s_R+u(r)j_R=576\cdot298\operatorname{exp}_r(R)\pmod p,\qquad h_r=406u(r)+178v(r).$$
The first two rows give $255s_R+507j_R=508$ and $37s_R+101j_R=313$. Their determinant is 72, hence $s_R=488$ and $j_R=567$. The third prime checks the same equation. In the commutator normalization $y=231y_1$, this says
$$y(J_R)=231\cdot567=-2\bmod577.$$
The class of $R$ is therefore a genuine nonunit-type Kummer class. The explicit radicand $R_0=R\,e_-^{95}$ has **zero** local coefficient at $p$ and the same divided ideal class. In the preceding table of duals, $R_0^{376}$ represents the unramified dual.

The local contributions of $R$ to the simultaneous norm test for $T/M$ total $8\cdot47\cdot488=2$ at $p$. At other primes the $p$-fold valuations contribute precisely $y(J_R)=-2$, since the $p$th power of a lifted Frobenius reads its residual translation in the center. At $p$ one can clear its valuations by a $p$th power, whose total effect on this test is zero by reciprocity for the unramified character $y$. Thus **$R$ is a global norm from $T$**, and **$R_0$ is an everywhere-local nonglobal norm of defect $-2$**. For the latter the two separate cyclic norm equations still have solutions. This computation gives both a cancellation and a failure on named elements of the same field.

## Feed these results into the original test

Actual $S$-unit representatives modulo powers can be taken with support over $r=12407809$ only. Indeed $y(\mathfrak r)=296$ at the chosen prime of $M$, so the divided class $-2$ is $269[\mathfrak r]$ on the entire $p$-primary class group. One can change a prime-to-$p$ power of $R$ or $R_0$ by a $p$th power to put its divisor on this prime, then undo the prime-to-$p$ scaling modulo powers. The same applies with the complement idempotent for $z^{-1}$. These elements are local norms from the unramified $L_y/M$ by their valuations, and hence field norms. The trivial $p$-class group of $L_y$ permits $S$-unit norm preimages modulo $p$ as in the preceding receipt.

For a weight-$z^{-1}$ real $S$-unit $b$ of $L_y$, the simultaneous norm defect of $N_{L_y/M}b$, in the translation convention of this receipt, is **$268\,\Lambda_0(b)$**. Indeed its contribution at $p$ is $47\Lambda_-(b)$. Each real $S$-valuation contributes its normalized $y$-Frobenius times that valuation. In the integral global reciprocity relation for $b$ there are two primes of $F=L_yK$ at each of these real places and the basis element is $A_0=-L/2$. Hence four times this latter sum equals $389\Lambda_-(b)+495\Lambda_0(b)$. Adding the two contributions cancels $\Lambda_-$ and gives $495/4=268$. This also checks $268\cdot16=249$ on a norm preimage of $e_-$.

It follows that corrected norm preimages of **$R$ and $R_0$** give two separate axes for the [four-field determinant](2026-11-24-four-norm-kernels-for-the-two-quadratics.md#four-fields-and-one-determinant). Their rows are **$(442,0,0)$** and **$(0,366,0)$** respectively in lower, diagonal, upper order. The lower reading is eight times the local coefficient of the target. The diagonal reading is its simultaneous norm defect divided by 268. The upper correction uses the explicitly normalized unit of reading 142 in the [base-unit construction](2026-11-24-two-real-unit-norm-targets-at-577.md#what-is-still-needed-upstairs).

There is also a direct check on **using the target at the wrong level**. As elements of $F$, all three $e_-,R,R_0$ are global norms from each entire mixed first-order field $B_s/F$. Away from $p$ their valuations are divisible by $p$ and the local extensions are unramified of exponent $p$. At $p$, remove local $p$th powers and use reality to kill the odd first-order characters. On the even value, summing over the $p$ translates above a place of $M$ gives zero because the residual translation acts on quadratics. The class-field isomorphism $\Psi_{N_s}$ then proves global norm membership. In particular the four tests on $R$ or $R_0$ **before taking the prescribed norm preimage** all return yes, even though the two base targets have different simultaneous norm answers over $M$.
