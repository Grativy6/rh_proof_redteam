# An order-five class and a hundred missing coefficient levels

**24 November 2026.** On the zeta branch, take the odd character of conductor 571 and twist 1. Its global strict Selmer group over $\mathbb Q$ has exactly five elements. For an explicit permitted host prime $d$, the nonzero classes fail the local equation with coefficients of orders $5,5^2,\ldots,5^{100}$ and first pass at order $5^{101}$. Another host prime with the same $v_5(d+1)=100$ passes at order 5. The finite local test and the global class can both be checked without Lean. The released reduction at the same coefficient level uses a different local condition away from 5. ## A class built from one quadratic number

In $B=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2855})$ take
$$ \beta=3657-50\sqrt{-2855},\qquad \beta\bar\beta=29^5.$$
Its trace $7314$ is prime to 29. Consequently the entire divisor of $\beta$ is five times one prime above 29. If $\beta$ were a fifth power, an algebraic integer of $B$ would have norm 29. Writing it as $(a+b\sqrt{-2855})/2$ excludes $b\ne0$ by size and $b=0$ since 29 is nonsquare. At either embedding into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_5$,
$$v_5(\beta-2^5)=5/2>2v_5(5\cdot2^4)=2.$$
Indeed $3657-32=125\cdot29$. Hensel's lemma makes $\beta$ a fifth power locally at 5.

Put $M=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5,\sqrt{-571})$. It contains $B$ because it contains $\sqrt5$. Its degree over $B$ is four, so taking norms shows $\beta$ remains a non-fifth-power there. For $s^5=\beta$, the extension $M(s)/M$ is cyclic of order five, split above 5 and unramified elsewhere. Away from 5 one removes a local fifth power of a uniformizer and adjoins a fifth root of a unit, which lies in an unramified extension.

Write $\omega=\varepsilon_5\bmod5$. An automorphism $\delta$ of $M/\mathbb Q$ has a lift taking $s$ to $s$ or to $29/s$ according to $\chi_B(\delta)$. These lifts form a subgroup. Conjugation on the translation $s\mapsto\zeta_5s$ is by $\omega\chi_B=\psi^{-1}\omega^{-1}$, since $\chi_B=\psi\omega^2$. In the resulting semidirect product the translation coordinate is a crossed homomorphism into $A_1[5]$. Its restriction to the translation subgroup is nonzero, where every coboundary is zero. It is locally a coboundary at 5 since the local image has order prime to five. On inertia away from 5 the same holds because the relative degree-five extension is unramified. This proves the global lower bound directly.

The faithful quotient acting on the five numbers $\zeta_5^is+29/(\zeta_5^is)$ has order 20. Their polynomial is
$$ f(X)=X^5-145X^3+4205X-7314.$$
This follows by expanding $X=s+29/s$. The group acts by the affine transformations of $\mathbb F_5$. The fixed field of its translations is the real quartic field cut out by $\psi^{-1}\omega^{-1}$. This supplies an independent factorization test for every Frobenius used below.

## Why the whole group has order five

For $j=1$ restrict to $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-571})$. Restriction is injective on 5-primary cohomology by corestriction and the degree two. Over $F$ the coefficients become $(\mathbb Q_5/\mathbb Z_5)(-1)$. The group unramified away from 5 and locally zero at 5 has dual the étale wild kernel in degree two. To match the conditions, take $S$ to consist of the places over 5 and the archimedean places, and apply Poitou–Tate duality to the localization kernels in degrees one and two with dual Tate twists $-1$ and $2$. At $v\nmid5$ the unramified local group already vanishes on the divisible coefficients, since multiplication by $(Nv)^{-1}-1$ is surjective. The degree-two wild kernel embeds in the 5-part of $K_2(\mathcal O_F)$ by Tate’s theorem. Belabas and Gangl computed $K_2(\mathcal O_F)=C_5$. Their entry 571 is unstarred and belongs to the proven table. Thus the explicit class generates the entire group over $\mathbb Q$. [Jaulent and Michel, introduction and Definition 1](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~jjaulent/Articles/J44.pdf#page=2) · [Belabas and Gangl, Section 7.1 and entry 571](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~kbelabas/research/K2.pdf#page=26)

One can check the twist sign by a separate class-group calculation. In the real quartic field cut out by $\psi^{-1}\omega^{-1}$ take $x=\sqrt{-q}(\zeta_5-\zeta_5^{-1})$. Its polynomial is $x^4-5qx^2+5q^2$. The element fixing $\sqrt{-q}$ and sending $\zeta_5$ to $\zeta_5^2$ sends $x$ to $x^3/q-3x$. A full PARI class-group certification gives $C_5$, with this generator acting by **3**. For $q=347$ the same computation gives $C_5$ with action **2**. The fields for the two inverse order-four residual characters coincide for each $q$.

Here is why the matrix action matters. In the original $V$ condition the global and local maps from $H^1(A_j[5^n])$ to $H^1(A_j)$ are injective wherever they must test coboundaries. Globally and on decomposition at 5 a residual scalar differs from 1 by a unit, so the invariants vanish. On inertia at other primes the invariants are either the whole divisible module or zero, because $\psi$ is quadratic. The kernel in the multiplication-by-$5^n$ sequence is the invariants modulo $5^n$ in each case. At $n=1$, restriction to the real quartic field of the residual character therefore identifies $S(A_j)[5]$ with the homomorphisms from its class group to $\mathbb F_5$ transforming by that residual character and vanishing at the primes above 5. The degree four and the local degrees are prime to five, so restriction and descent are exact on these classes. The unique prime above 5 is fixed by the quartic group and contributes zero to a nontrivial eigenspace. Ordinary versus narrow classes have the same 5-part.

For $q=571$ the order-five subgroup is present at every $j\equiv1\bmod4$. The whole group is zero at $j\equiv3\bmod4$, since any nonzero 5-primary torsion group contains an element of order five. For $q=347$ these two residual outcomes are reversed. For $q=571$ and $j\equiv1\bmod20$, the loose-order-25 conditions are identical to those for $j=1$ since $\varepsilon_5$ modulo 25 has exponent 20. Thus the full group is exactly $C_5$ in that entire congruence class. This argument bounds the order at these twists without a growth assumption. [Certified field and action replay](check_odd_class_fields.gp) · [PARI’s class-group certificate](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html-stable/General_number_fields.html#bnfcertify)

Over every $E_m$ on these hosts the class survives, by restriction and the 2-power degree. On removing the $\psi^{-1}$ coefficient twist the class is in the $\psi$ component of the action on cohomology. Complex conjugation acts there by $\psi(-1)=-1=(-1)^j$ for the indicated odd twists. It consequently contributes to the released **plus** summand, at least $\lfloor(t+2)/4\rfloor\log5$ over $1\le j<t$ when $q=571$. No claim about equality for the other character components over $E_m$ is required. ## Move only the prime where splitting is tested

Choose an odd prime $d=8\cdot3\cdot7\cdot571\,k5^b-1$ with $k$ odd and prime to 5. Then $\psi(d)=-1$, $d\equiv-1\bmod5$, and the residual action at $d$ is the identity. In $M$ its Frobenius is complex conjugation. The image of Frobenius in the degree-20 affine quotient is therefore a translation. It is nontrivial precisely when $f$ is irreducible modulo $d$, and trivial precisely when $f$ splits. Equivalently, in the quadratic residue field of $B$ one tests $\beta^{(d^2-1)/5}\ne1$. Going to that residue field squares Frobenius, which preserves nontriviality of a translation of order five.

At a detecting $d$ the value of our cocycle on Frobenius has order 5. At twist 1 the scalar minus one is $-d^{-1}-1$, of valuation $b$. In $A_1[5^n]$ solving the local coboundary equation means solving $( -d^{-1}-1)y=h$ with $h$ of order 5. A solution exists exactly when $n\ge b+1$. In the divisible group it always exists. The global cohomology map from finite coefficients is injective as above, so changing the global representative cannot lower this threshold. At 2 the residual Frobenius scalar is a primitive fourth root and the local equation already has a solution modulo 5. At $q$ the inertia has an element acting by $-1$, so a cocycle zero on inertia in cohomology is zero on decomposition as well. At 5 the explicit extension splits. These cover all primes dividing $5N$.

For twist 1 these describe **the whole stricter finite-coefficient group**. On the detecting side it is zero for $n\le b$ and $C_5$ thereafter. On the split side it is $C_5$ from the first level. For other $j\equiv1\bmod4$ the same nonzero residual class has first level $b+v_5(j)+1$ on the detecting side, by lifting the exponent in $d^j+1$. Global lifting under multiplication by 5 is a different operation. At $j=1$ the order-five class has no global strict lift of order 25, even though its local coboundary can require $5^{101}$.

The [integer replay](check_odd_selmer_precision.py) proves all twelve primes, tests the Kummer residue and quintic factorization separately, and solves the finite linear congruences. For the larger primes its certificate uses a norm-one element $z$ modulo $d$ with $z^{d+1}=1$ and $z^{(d+1)/5}-1$ of norm prime to $d$. For each prime divisor $r$ this forces $5^b\mid r-1$ or $5^b\mid r+1$. Since $5^b-1>\sqrt d$, $d$ is prime. The smaller cases use trial division.

There is no uniform coefficient level as $d$ varies. For each $b$ fix the displayed congruences with $v_5(d+1)=b$ in an abelian cyclotomic extension containing $M$. Its intersection with $M(s)$ is exactly $M$. Indeed the commutator subgroup of $\operatorname{Gal}(M(s)/\mathbb Q)$ is the translation group $C_5$, by the faithful order-four action. A compatible automorphism in the compositum can therefore have either zero or nonzero translation. Chebotarev supplies primes for each choice and allows any finite additional exclusions.

Even on the **fixed field $\mathbb Q$** the order of taking limits matters. At every finite coefficient level a class that vanishes on every decomposition group vanishes globally. To see this, restrict its finite affine Galois quotient to the kernel of the coefficient action. Any nonzero translation there would be detected by a Frobenius by Chebotarev. The remaining class inflates from the finite cyclic scalar image, whose cohomology vanishes because it contains a central scalar with scalar minus one a unit. For the divisible $A_1$, the everywhere locally zero group is $C_5$. At unramified primes the Frobenius coboundary exists, at $q$ inertia invariants vanish, at 5 the class is strict, and the infinite place has order prime to five. This is a counterexample to commuting the *all-prime* kernel with the coefficient limit on this number field. It is distinct from the [infinite-field example](2026-11-24-every-place-splits-in-the-limit.md).

## What the released proof uses

For each fixed $N$ the divisible-coefficient `V` and `V'` agree here. At an unramified $p\ne5$ the nonzero scalar $\psi(p)^{-1}p^{-j}-1$ is surjective on $A_j$. At a ramified $p\ne5$ the inertia invariants are zero. The release uses this equality before it counts the character summand. In `sha_inj` it then takes the coefficient bound $a=b$ for a class killed by $b$. Its finite matrix must be a coboundary on decomposition **at 5**. Away from 5 it must vanish on specified **inertia elements**. An unramified but nonzero Frobenius at $d$ satisfies that latter condition. The paper derivation, the actual subgroup specified in the source, and the independently certified $C_5$ all give the same verdict here.
