# Can a nonzero theta coefficient disappear?

**23 November 2026.** The released proof contains a conversion that returns zero when a complex coefficient lies outside the chosen number field. We exhibit a nonzero input it erases, prove an exact period error for the same arithmetic example, and follow the conversion to the theta-lift operator used in the RH argument. The operator’s degree and field-of-definition conditions rule out this failure for the coefficients it reads. We have not found a failure of the RH conclusion.

## Where zero enters

Fix an embedding of a number field $K$ in $\mathbb C$ and identify $K$ with its image. The release defines $D_K(z)=z$ for $z\in K$ and $D_K(z)=0$ otherwise. Its `jetK` applies this rule to a character-normalized Taylor coefficient of a theta function. For $K=\mathbb Q(i)$ one immediately has $D_K(\sqrt2)^2=0$ and $D_K((\sqrt2)^2)=2$. Thus even a nonzero product in the target field gives no assurance that its individual factors survived conversion. [Definition and use in `jetK`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/KernelLattices/ThDistC.lean#L56-L104)

Chris’s PAL interface question gives a direct test here. Zero and $\sqrt2$ have the same readout in $\mathbb Q(i)$. Squaring before taking the readout distinguishes them. The mathematical obligation is field membership **for each complex coefficient the operator reads, before applying $D_K$**. [Public audit key](../RH-PROOF-WORK-KEY.md)

## A test inside the period calculation

The November 22 reconstruction gives $d=23,\ q=3,\ N=4$ as a field where the integral line may have no generator. Its supporting class-group calculation was run without certification. Here is an unconditional proof for that case. This tests the auxiliary field construction. We do not assume this small level realizes a full prime window or that the elliptic curve descends to the small field. [Reconstruction, p. 130](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/zeta-paper_v2.pdf#page=130)

Set $k=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})$ and $E=k(\sqrt{-3})$. Write $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-23})/2$. The polynomial $X^2-X+6$ has distinct roots $0,1$ modulo 3. Write $\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_1$ for the two primes of $k$ over 3 in that order. Each ramifies tamely in $E/k$, with residue degree one, since adjoining $\sqrt{-3}$ at either prime is a ramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q_3$. No other prime ramifies in this relative extension since $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ has discriminant $-3$. Thus the relative different is $\mathfrak Q_0\mathfrak Q_1$, where $\mathfrak p_i\mathcal O_E=\mathfrak Q_i^2$. The two choices of half-different give the two conjugate prime ideals as integral lines. [Definition of the line and trace-dual calculation, pp. 128–129](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/zeta-paper_v2.pdf#page=128)

Neither line has a generator in $E$. A generator of $\mathfrak Q_i$ would have norm to $k$ generating $\mathfrak p_i$. An element $x+y\omega$ generating $\mathfrak p_i$ would have norm 3, but

$$
N_{k/\mathbb Q}(x+y\omega)=(x+y/2)^2+23y^2/4.
$$

For $y\ne0$ this exceeds 3, and for $y=0$ the norm is a square. In fact $a=1+2\omega=2+\sqrt{-23}$ has norm 27, reduces to zero at $\mathfrak p_1$ and to one at $\mathfrak p_0$. Hence $(a)=\mathfrak p_1^3$ in $k$ and $(a)=\mathfrak Q_1^6$ in $E$. Both $\mathfrak p_i$ have ideal class of order three. If a finite extension $M/E$ makes $\mathfrak Q_i$ principal, taking its ideal norm all the way to $k$ shows that $3$ divides $[M:E]$.

Now take $g^6=a$ in $\mathbb C$. Unique factorization of ideals gives $(g)=\mathfrak Q_1\mathcal O_{E(g)}$, by taking sixth powers of the ideals. In particular $g\notin E$. This gives $D_E(g)=0$ while $D_E(g^6)=a\ne0$. It also gives an explicit working field and a generator of the other line, $\sqrt{-3}/g$. This is a test input for the conversion. It does not identify $g$ as one of the selected theta coefficients.

**The size of the error.** In $E$ the vector $z=\sqrt{-3}$ lies in $\ell=\mathfrak Q_1$. Its principal ideal is $\mathfrak Q_0\mathfrak Q_1$, so $[\ell:z\mathcal O_E]=3$. The squared-period formula adds $2\log[\ell:z\mathcal O_E]/[E:\mathbb Q]$ to the average of $\log(A_\rho/(\pi|\rho z|^2))$, for archimedean areas $A_\rho$. Calling $z$ a generator would lose exactly $\tfrac12\log3$. Upstairs $|\rho g|^2=\sqrt3$ for every embedding, whereas $|\rho z|^2=3$. The corrected value downstairs and the value from a generator upstairs agree, embedding by embedding for the denominator correction. The same calculation works after adjoining the field of definition of the areas. [Squared-period formula, p. 128](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/zeta-paper_v2.pdf#page=128) · [Release’s integral-index identity](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/GlobalTwisting/ExistsPowPrincipalFractionalIdeal.lean#L225-L251)

## What the theta lift actually reads

1. **Start with the complex numbers.** The set from which the proof constructs a coefficient field consists of the original character-normalized derivatives in $\mathbb C$. The set is defined before `desc`. At each fixed stage and degree, equivariance reduces the lattices to finitely many representatives, the translations to finite quotients and the derivatives to finitely many monomials of bounded degree. For those representatives, the normalized analytic function is expressed as a finite algebraic linear combination of torsion translates. Passing from Taylor coefficients to derivatives multiplies by a product of ordinary factorials, which preserves algebraicity. This yields one finite extension containing all the coefficients needed at that stage. [Set being enclosed](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/FinAlgOfFinite.lean#L49-L59) · [Coefficient field and basis changes](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/FinAlgJetSet.lean#L53-L106) · [Derivatives of the normalized function](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/VacuumEntireAndNormalizedJetsAlgebraic.lean#L339-L357) · [Factorial identity](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/DerivAtZeroReindexEqTaylorCoefficient.lean#L30-L43) · [Where analytic algebraicity enters](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/DegreeEqTotDeg.lean#L193-L226)
2. **Try a monomial the field construction might omit.** The union used to select the field explicitly filters for sets contained in a finite algebraic extension. For each actual step, `finAlg_step` supplies that condition. Each eligible monomial gives its original complex value as a member of the set. The multidegree is checked at every weight place. On the polynomial side, homogeneity forces every monomial contributing to the operator to have that multidegree. The comparison applies $\sigma(D_K(z))=z$ with a field preimage for each contributing coefficient. An off-degree coefficient may be discarded without changing that operator. 3. **Change the generator while keeping the ideal fixed.** The real unit $\epsilon=(25+3\sqrt{69})/2$ belongs to $E$ and has conjugate $\epsilon^{-1}$. Replacing $g$ by $\epsilon^t g$ changes a local log squared period by $-2t\log|\rho\epsilon|$. Its absolute change at a single embedding is unbounded, and the mean change is zero. The local estimate keeps the individual period. A Taylor coefficient of degree $n$ in the changed block acquires the factor $(\rho\epsilon)^{nt}$, so its squared norm changes by the same factor $|\rho\epsilon|^{2nt}$ as the exponentiated bound with term $-n\log\Omega_\rho$. The global step takes the mean of the logarithms. Every infinite place of the totally complex field $K$ receives exactly $2[M:K]$ embeddings of the common extension, so the extension degree cancels. There really is no fixed bound on the field degree in this auxiliary family. Keep $q=3,N=4$ and take primes $d\equiv23\bmod24$. If an extension of degree $h$ over $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d},\sqrt{-3})$ principalizes the line above $\mathfrak p$, then $\mathfrak p^h$ is principal in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. A rational integer cannot generate it because its valuations at the two primes over 3 are equal. The same norm calculation therefore gives $3^h\ge(d+1)/4$. For $d=983$, degrees one through five already fail. Dirichlet’s theorem gives arbitrarily large such $d$. The release explicitly adjoins roots of generators of ideal powers and then takes a compositum with the other coefficient data. This obstruction defeats a fixed-degree shortcut. The normalized mean above needs only finiteness. ## Boundary sections and the second period

The previous receipt asked whether the cusp-form count loses a section. We checked both inclusions on the same component at the same tensor weight. Restricting a section that vanishes on the boundary gives a cusp form supported on that component. The Fourier test uses a ray with every coordinate positive. Frequencies in its support have nonnegative coordinates, so only the zero frequency can have zero pairing with that ray. The converse uses extension with boundary vanishing and uniqueness on the dense interior. The growth comparison uses the fixed reduced boundary. This attempt produced no lost section. ## Unresolved

The remaining mathematical target at the covolume comparison is the whole lattice of cusp forms. Equality of the rational spaces and the normalization of an individual period still leave the finite index of a sublattice undetermined. A discrepancy could arise in the identification of the row lattice with integral cusp forms, or in the bound on covolume after that identification. The scalar tests settle neither question.
