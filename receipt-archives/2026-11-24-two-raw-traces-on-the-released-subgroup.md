# Two raw traces on the released finite-index subgroup

**24 November 2026.** Two ordinary arithmetic directions give trace differences **$341a$ and $394b$ modulo 577** on three group elements in the actual finite-index subgroup chosen by the release. Here $a,b$ are the [local tangent amplitudes](2026-11-24-four-local-units-at-577.md). The determinant is 490 modulo 577. These two trace differences generate the maximal ideal at the nonsplit point. Either one can be followed all the way from an explicit ordinary finite truncation to the domain criterion for a break.

The conclusion named `FinEnd` permits an arbitrary finite-index subgroup. In the Case I producer the source chooses **$G_L$**, the absolute subgroup of its linked abelian extension $L/\mathbb Q$. Thus every absolute commutator belongs to this particular subgroup. The link also identifies $G_L$ with the frame subgroup intersected with $\ker\eta$. Choose an inertia element $s'$ at 577 in $G_L$ with $z(s')=4$. Such an element at the chosen prime was [checked on the actual frame](2026-11-24-the-unramified-character-that-local-data-miss.md#does-the-published-frame-make-this-substitution) by raising an inertia element to a $577$-power. One can even make the choice independent of the auxiliary exponent. Start with $s$ with $\omega(s)=5$ and take its prime-to-577 component inside the procyclic closure of $\langle s\rangle$. Equivalently take the limit of $s^{577^{n!}}$. The exponent tends to zero on the pro-577 part and to one at every finite order prime to 577. The resulting $s'$ belongs to the required quadratic subgroup by the local intersection condition and maps trivially to the auxiliary 577-group quotients. Its residual value remains 4.

Put $\gamma=[h,\mathrm{Frob}_{12407809}]$, using the marked inertia at 2 and the [normalization $y(\gamma)=1$](2026-11-24-three-deformation-directions-at-577.md#read-all-three-classes-on-one-word). Write $c$ for complex conjugation. Choose local reciprocity lifts $g_i$ of the explicit $u_i$, $i=1,2$, in $G_{F_v}$ at the coefficient prime. Define
$$H=\gamma s',\qquad k_i=[c,g_i],\qquad H_i=k_i H.$$
All three $H,H_1,H_2$ belong to $G_L$ and have multiplier one. The original torus representation $r_0$ has identical matrices on all three, even integrally, since its diagonal characters kill each $k_i$. The source's equality of characteristic polynomials on $G_L$ therefore implies equality of the three traces whenever its coefficient and ordinary-point hypotheses hold. The choices of absolute lifts at the local Artin inputs may be fixed once. Their first-order readings depend only on local reciprocity.

## Compute the two first-order differences

Use the local origin with $y=0$ on the chosen decomposition group. In the actual nonsplit residue,
$$\bar r_y(H)=\exp(L)\operatorname{diag}(4,1,4^{-1}),\qquad L=E_{21}-E_{32}.$$
Its entries in positions $(2,1),(3,2),(3,1)$ are $4,-1,-2$. Each $g_i$ has trivial residual image. Complex conjugation negates the odd tangent matrices, so the first-order part of $k_i$ is $-2C(g_i)$, independently of the choice of first-order frame at $c$. The two inputs are $C(g_1)=35aB_3$ and $C(g_2)=387bB_4$, with $B_3=-(E_{12}+E_{23})/4$ and $B_4=E_{13}$.

For a lift modulo $\delta^2$, subtract $\operatorname{tr}r(H)$ from $\operatorname{tr}r(k_iH)$. Its first-order correction on $H$ cancels. In this order the two differences divided by $\delta$ are
$$35a(4-1)/2=341a,\qquad 387b\cdot4=394b\pmod{577}.$$
Each is an ordinary matrix trace on a multiplier-one word. Their determinant $341\cdot394=490\bmod577$ is a unit. [Direct matrix arithmetic](boundary-577/quadratic_words.py)

Let $D$ be the complete local ring for the [nonsplit ordinary problem](2026-11-24-the-obstruction-at-the-nonsplit-point.md#a-different-residue-for-the-same-equations). The two trace differences give elements $d_1,d_2$ of its maximal ideal. Their derivatives evaluated above form a basis of the relative cotangent space. The [even integral relation](2026-11-24-two-equations-at-the-marked-point.md#count-the-available-obstructions) has constant term 577 and all other terms of degree at least two in the invariant coordinates. Thus $577\in\mathfrak m_D^2$, so those derivatives already form a basis of the full cotangent space. Nakayama gives
$$\mathfrak m_D=(d_1,d_2),\qquad 577\in(d_1,d_2)^2.$$
In particular every nonconstant characteristic-577 branch at this point is detected by one of the **same two** comparisons. At a characteristic-zero specialization, with $v(577)=1$, at least one has $0<v(d_i)\le1/2$. The weight-zero trace functions agree at the corresponding split marked lift by diagonal rescaling. For an irreducible split lift its lower coordinate is nonzero, so the [reduction to $D$](2026-11-24-why-the-trace-field-must-ramify.md#a-forced-integral-relation) applies. A ramified trace field is already required by these raw traces on the linked subgroup.

For any particular finite-coefficient test of these traces, Chebotarev also gives oriented good Frobenius representatives. Use the finite quotient containing $L$ and the reductions of the representation and the torus characters at the desired precision. Select places with the specified Frobenius elements. The three rational primes split in $L$. The choices may depend on the representation and precision. ## An ordinary finite truncation where the trace equalities fail

There is an explicit construction over $k[t]/t^4$ for each of the two directions. Start with its actual first-order ordinary representation at the nonsplit residue. Choose the local representative upper on the chosen decomposition group and write a matrix part as $\bar r_y(g)+\delta M(g)\bmod\delta^2$. Replace $\delta$ by $t^3$ in these matrix representatives over $k[[t]]$. The multiplicative defects, including the inverse-transpose action, are divisible by $t^6$. Conjugate by $\operatorname{diag}(t,1,t^{-1})^{-1}$ on the left and its inverse on the right. Every original residual lower entry gains positive $t$-order. Every first-order entry retains at least one factor of $t$. The conjugated matrices are integral and reduce to the split representation. Each defect loses at most two powers of $t$, so they give an exact representation modulo $t^4$. The diagonal rescaling is fixed by the group involution. The prescribed killed inertia stays trivial and the local upper flag and fixed inertia characters persist. The original mark can be normalized by the [separated-eigenvalue change of basis](2026-11-24-why-the-trace-field-must-ramify.md) without changing traces.

In that finite ordinary lift the differences are **$341a\,t^3$ and $394b\,t^3$**. Thus the trace equality fails on the release's own chosen subgroup if its quantifier is enlarged to include these rings. For an actual use of `FinEnd` the release requires a complete discrete valuation **domain**. Its later finiteness test explicitly obtains that domain and DVR structure before applying the endpoint. A surviving nonconstant branch over a characteristic-577 DVR would give a failure at the required scope. The construction works with $\delta$ replaced by $t^m$, $m\ge3$, modulo $t^{2m-2}$. The first trace difference detected then has order $m$ and its square vanishes. These finite lifts can go arbitrarily deep. At every fixed precision their matrices converge, in the indicated frame, to the pure lower family $\exp(t y(g)L)\operatorname{diag}(z(g),1,z(g)^{-1})$. Its lower entry at $\gamma$ still reads $t$, and its traces agree with the undeformed traces. Moving the first visible difference farther out in the truncation does not produce a nonconstant limiting trace.

## A numerical milestone for the next obstruction

Use the original split coordinates $X,U,V$, with invariant pair $A=XU$, $B=X^2V$. The raw trace functions have integer torus weight zero, so in characteristic 577 they are power series in $A,B$ with zero constant. Their linear terms are independent by the trace calculation at the nonsplit point. Hence they generate exactly $(A,B)$ in the split ring too.

Two finite jets already have a **nonzero square** of a specified raw trace difference. The conversion of tangent normalizations can be checked at the same Frobenius that defines $\gamma$. In the earlier global convention the residual exponents of the raw upper circular units on $\gamma$ were 468 and 95. In the present local convention the primitive radicand root is $Y_DZ$, the final power is $577^{12}-1$, and the Kummer exponent is relative to $Z$. Relative to the earlier radicand this applies the automorphism equal to 1 modulo $D$ and $D/256$ modulo $p$. Together with the change of Kummer generator the multipliers are $-256(D/256)^{1-296j}$ for $j=1,2$, giving **86 and 189**. Direct evaluation of the conjugates at 12407809 gives 53 and 306, and hence 435 and 68 on $\gamma$. The fourth-degree coefficient of the pure first tangent is zero globally after the $c$ normalization. Its third-degree coefficient on $\gamma$ contributes $-435/4=324$ to the old upper-12 coordinate. For either tangent only the fourth-degree coefficient goes into the upper-13 coordinate, with value $68b$. Thus $B=68b$ to first order and $A=324a$ along the pure first direction. No value of the remaining off-diagonal change-of-basis coefficient is needed.

Here is a fixed finite target. **A split ordinary jet on which either raw trace difference has a nonzero cube exists if and only if the two unknown homogeneous quadratics have a common projective root.** To prove the forward implication, work in $k'[t]/t^N$ for a finite residue extension. Let $r=\min(v_t A,v_t B)=\min(v_t d_1,v_t d_2)$, with zero assigned valuation $+\infty$. A nonzero cube gives $3r<N$. Write $x=v_t X$. The marked coordinates all lie in $(t)$, so $x<r$. If some initial quadratic were nonzero on the leading pair $(t^{-r}A,t^{-r}B)\bmod t$, its relation would start at $x+2r<N$, a contradiction.

Conversely a common direction $(\alpha,\beta)$ is defined over a field extension of degree at most two, since both forms are binary quadratics. Set $X=t,\ U=\alpha t^2,\ V=\beta t$. Then $(A,B)=(\alpha t^3,\beta t^3)$ and both relations vanish modulo $t^{13}$. Their quadratics vanish on this direction and the next possible invariant degree is four by evenness. One raw trace has order three, so even its **fourth** power survives there. No cube on the actual arithmetic input has yet been exhibited.

There is also a criterion for the full branch. For a nonzero trace pair at length $N$, its ideal in the truncated polynomial ring has nilpotency index $\lceil N/r\rceil$. **Unbounded indices on actual jets at this fixed input are equivalent to a nonconstant characteristic-577 branch.** If the nonsplit special fiber is Artinian, some $(A,B)^e$ lies in $(\bar f_1,\bar f_2)$ in the invariant power-series ring. The split relations give $X(A,B)^e=0$, hence $(A,B)^{e+1}=0$ there, uniformly in the jet and its residue field. Conversely a positive-dimensional special fiber gives a nonconstant DVR branch by the [normalization and rescaling argument](2026-11-24-two-equations-at-the-marked-point.md#a-falsifiable-alternative). Its truncations have bounded first trace order and unbounded $N$. A single nonzero cube settles the quadratic-direction question. Higher terms still decide whether its orders can grow without bound.

## Unresolved

The two ordinary quadratic conditions at 577 determine whether any first-order direction can extend to the next order at the nonsplit point. A common quadratic direction may still be cut off later. The raw trace tests above already detect any surviving branch and give the integral bound on every surviving characteristic-zero witness. The existence of such a branch or of the required automorphic system remains unresolved.
