# The local norm that does not compose

**24 November 2026.** The full zeta host $E/\mathbb Q$ has exactly one bit of failure of the Hasse norm principle. It persists as the two selected primes and the cyclotomic level move. Already on $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{16})^+(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{-23})$, **31 is a norm at every completion and is not a global norm**. The same 31 is separately a global norm for each arrow $E\to E^+\to\mathbb Q$. The two arrows need compatible preimages to compose. The theta scale in the release uses the first arrow on a specified rational input.

## The two nonsquares at 23

Put $C=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and $B=C(\sqrt{-23})\subset E$. The prime 31 splits completely in $E$. It is $-1\bmod16$, a square modulo 3, and a square modulo 23. At every unramified finite prime different from 31 the norm on units is surjective. At 3 and 23, take a square root of 31 in the rational local field. In the maximal unramified local subfield choose a unit of that norm. In the full completion the additional ramification degree is two, so this unit has norm 31. At infinity 31 is positive. At 2, write $\theta=\zeta_{16}+\zeta_{16}^{-1}$. Its polynomial $x^4-4x^2+2$ gives $N(1+\theta)=-1$. The unit $-31$ is a fourth power in $\mathbb Q_2$ since it is $1\bmod16$. This gives 31 as a unit norm from the real cyclotomic completion. The rest of the dyadic extension is unramified and its unit norm is onto.

Suppose $31=N_{B/\mathbb Q}x$. The intermediate value $y=N_{B/C}x$ is totally positive. Put $\pi=7+3\sqrt2$, also totally positive, with $N_{C/\mathbb Q}\pi=31$. Writing $\sigma(\sqrt2)=-\sqrt2$, we have $y/\pi=t/\sigma t$ for some $t\in C^\times$. One can take $t=1+y/\pi$. Thus
$$y=\pi t^2/n,\qquad n=N_{C/\mathbb Q}t>0.$$

Every rational prime $\ell\ne23$ which is a quadratic nonresidue modulo 23 has even valuation in $n$. Indeed 2 and 31 are residues. If $\ell$ is inert in $C$, its residue degree gives the assertion. If it splits in $C$, quadratic reciprocity gives $(-23/\ell)=(\ell/23)=-1$. At a prime above $\ell$ the extension $B/C$ is unramified quadratic. The valuation of its norm $y$ must be even, and $\pi$ is a unit there. The displayed equation for $y$ gives the required parity, including negative valuations. Consequently the 23-unit part of the positive rational number $n$ is a square residue modulo 23.

At the two places of $C$ above 23, $\sqrt2$ reduces to $5$ and $-5$. The residues of $\pi$ are **22 and 15**, both nonsquares modulo 23. Removing powers of 23 from $y=\pi t^2/n$ therefore leaves a nonsquare unit in both completions. In $\mathbb Q_{23}(\sqrt{-23})$, $\sqrt{-23}$ has norm 23 and the residue of the norm of every unit is a square. After imposing the valuations away from 23, both places over 23 exclude that same $y$. This proves the contradiction. A norm from the full host would have been a norm from $B$ by transitivity.

The scalar 31 itself has the following relative CM preimage.
$$N_{E/E^+}\big((16+\sqrt{-23})/3\big)=(256+23)/9=31.$$
There is likewise an explicit preimage for $E^+/\mathbb Q$. Set $s=(1+\sqrt{69})/2$, $v=1+3\theta+2\theta^2-\theta^3$. The norm of $v+s\theta$ to $\mathbb Q(\theta)$ is $v^2+v\theta-17\theta^2=-1+15\theta+\theta^2-4\theta^3$. Its quartic norm is 31. These two successful scalar checks do not make the latter preimage a relative CM norm. [Integer and polynomial replay](check_host_norm_defect.py)

## What survives in every zeta host

Take the trivial outer character, $m\ge2$, primes $q\equiv3\bmod8$, $d\equiv7\bmod16$, and $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^{m+2}})^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$. Its three conductor parts are disjoint. Write their generators as $e_0,e_q,e_d$, of orders $2^m,2,2$. Tate's norm-obstruction calculation identifies the everywhere-local norms modulo global norms with $\bigwedge^2G$ modulo the images of $\bigwedge^2D_v$ from the decomposition groups. Here $G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$. Unramified and infinite groups are cyclic and contribute zero. The exterior square has three coordinates of order two, ordered $0q,0d,qd$. At 2 the cyclotomic factor is totally ramified, $-q$ gives the unramified quadratic extension, and $-d$ is a square. The local row is $(1,0,0)$. At $q$, inertia is $e_q$ and the cyclotomic Frobenius has odd coordinate because $(2/q)=-1$. The row is $(1,0,b)$, with $b=1$ precisely if $(-d/q)=-1$. At $d$ the cyclotomic Frobenius has even coordinate since $(2/d)=1$, and inertia is $e_d$. The row is $(0,0,c)$, with $c=1$ precisely if $(-q/d)=-1$. Quadratic reciprocity gives $b+c=1$. Thus the quotient is **$C_2$**, represented by $e_0\wedge e_d$. This calculation includes both possibilities for the cross-symbol. In the real subfield $E^+=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^{m+2}})^+(\sqrt{qd})$ the dyadic decomposition group is already its entire Galois group, giving zero obstruction.

There are prime witnesses even when $m,q,d$ are fixed arbitrarily late. Use narrow ray class theory in $C=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ to select the witness directly. Require a totally positive prime generator $\pi$ congruent to 1 at the places over $2^{m+2}q$, and to a nonzero nonsquare at **both** places over $d$. The CRT and positivity give this ray class. It contains primes of residue degree one, by the positive density of primes in each ray class and the density-zero contribution of degree greater than one. For $r=N\pi$ we get complete splitting in $E$ and the same nonsquare obstruction. At 2 the real cyclotomic norm contains $1+2^{m+2}\mathbb Z_2$ by taking a $2^m$th root. The parity proof above works with $d$ in place of 23. The witness prime may depend on the host. [Ray class existence and prime distribution](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf)

## Which norm the source uses

The curve ideal belongs to $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. For its ratio with the reference ideal the code fixes the **particular positive rational number** $x=N\mathfrak c/N\mathfrak c_0$ as an element of $E^+$. The extended ideal identity $I\bar I=x\mathcal O_E$ forces even valuations at every inert finite place. The extension $E/E^+$ is quadratic and unramified at all finite places. Complex conjugation is $e_q+e_d$ and belongs to none of the three finite inertia subgroups generated by $e_0,e_q,e_d$ separately. Its local unit norm is surjective at inert places and arbitrary at split places, and $x$ is positive at every real place. The cyclic Hasse norm theorem therefore gives $s\bar s=x$. The source constructs $s$ as $a+b\sqrt{-d}$ over $E^+$. The separate cyclic-tower lemma requires cyclicity of the full extension. At $d=23$, take $\mathfrak c/\mathfrak c_0$ to be the prime over 31 at which $\sqrt{-23}=-16\bmod31$. This is an allowed curve-ideal change. The displayed $s=(16+\sqrt{-23})/3$ gives exactly the required scale. Its fractional ideal in $K$ has, in addition to that prime, exponents $+1,-1$ at the two primes over 3. In fact the numerator has norm $31\cdot3^2$ and all its 3-divisibility is at the prime where $\sqrt{-23}=-1\bmod3$. The curve prime has ideal class of order three. The three reduced forms of discriminant $-23$ are $[1,1,6],[2,1,3],[2,-1,3]$, and $x^2+23y^2=124$ has no integer solution. Extension to the host preserves the nontrivial class since its relative degree is a power of two. A scalar with the right norm cannot identify these two ideal lattices. The [actual transported lattice](2026-11-24-the-self-dual-lattice-with-no-unit-vector.md) retains the difference.
