## Test the discarded rank on paper

Let $d_g,d_b$ be the retained and discarded ranks, $\mu,\mu_g$ the full and retained slopes, and $\ell$ the logarithmic cost of clearing the projector. The release's scalar calculation starts with
$$ (d_g+d_b)\mu\le d_g\mu_g+d_b(m+\ell),\quad
d_b\le3\delta(d_g+d_b),\quad m,\ell\le Q,\quad \mu\ge-Q.$$
With $0\le\delta\le1/12$, $0\le\ell$ and positive total rank, division gives
$$\mu-\mu_g\le\frac{d_b}{d_g}(m+\ell-\mu)
 \le\frac{9\delta Q}{1-3\delta}\le12\delta Q.$$
The scalar constant is sharp. Take $d_g=3,d_b=1,\delta=1/12,Q=m=\ell=1,\mu=-1,\mu_g=-2$. Both ends agree. This checks the arithmetic after the projector, rank and slope hypotheses have been supplied. For fixed $c>0$ and fixed exponents $A,B$, $e^{-x^c}x^A$ and $e^{-x^c}x^B$ tend to zero. Allowing $A(x)=x^c/\log x$ would erase that decay. The source takes the exponents before choosing the common large-$x$ threshold. ## The rank comparison worth pressing

In the live regular-representation argument, the rank at Hodge shift $+2c_0$ is bounded by $3/2$ times the rank at $-c_0$ **on the same selected deep cover**. One input provides a lower bound by the component degree times $\binom{T-1}{d}$ on the cusp-section space at the lower weight. This is a finite-weight assertion. An asymptotic Hilbert polynomial by itself permits a poor estimate at a particular weight. A smooth degree-40 hypersurface in $\mathbb P^4$ shows how much the bundle matters. In degree 5 the hyperplane bundle gives 126 sections, while $40\binom43=160$. The actual abstract rank lemma counts sections of $K_X\otimes A\otimes L^T$. For the same hypersurface and $A=L=\mathcal O_X(1)$, adjunction gives $K_X=\mathcal O_X(35)$. The required degree is then 41, with $\binom{45}{4}-5=148990$ sections. This is a check on the rank formula’s bundle, outside the automorphic cover construction. More generally, with a basepoint-free $L$ giving proper successive hyperplane cuts, higher-cohomology vanishing for $K_X\otimes A\otimes L^j$, $j\ge1$, makes the forward differences of its Hilbert polynomial at 1 the nonnegative section dimensions on the cuts in the correspondingly shifted degrees. The last difference is the length $L^d$. The binomial expansion gives the stated lower bound.
