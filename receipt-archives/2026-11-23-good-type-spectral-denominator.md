## Finding an occupied label with many linear roots

At each place $v$ choose one of the release’s target values $\xi_v=\chi_T(v,j_0)\in\mathbb F_g^\times$. Call a place qualifying for a tuple when its polynomial has a rational root in $\xi_v(\mathbb F_g^\times)^2$. For a monic squarefree degree-$r$ polynomial $F$ with nonzero constant term the source’s exact Whittaker weight is
$$
 w_g(F)=\prod_{\pi\mid F}(g^{\deg\pi}-1)^{-1}\ \ge\ g^{-r}.
$$
The weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Among all $g^r$ monic polynomials, each specified nonzero root has probability $1/g$ and each pair of distinct specified roots has probability $1/g^2$. The target square class has $(g-1)/2$ elements. Two terms of inclusion-exclusion give a probability of at least $3/8-3/(8g^2)$ for hitting it. Remove at most $1/g$ for a zero constant and at most $\sum_{j\ge1}g^j g^{-2j}=1/(g-1)$ for a repeated monic irreducible. Thus the regular-law probability of qualifying is at least $23/64$ for $g\ge257$. ## Two full prime-power denominators per qualifying place

At a selected place put $A=A_g$. Start from the occupied good tuple above. Scaling all the roots at a place preserves squarefreeness, degrees of all irreducible factors, the prime-degree witnesses and collisions between prime-to parts of two roots. The equality of prime-to parts is unchanged under common multiplication. The only varying condition is the comparison of a root against a target value or its inverse. Write $B\subset A$ for the local set of twists that violate it. For a fixed root $x$ and target $\xi$, a twist $c$ violates that comparison precisely when $cx/\xi$ has prime-power order, including order one. All root orders are prime to $g$. For a prime $p\mid a_g$ the solutions are empty or a coset of the Sylow $p$-subgroup $A_p$. For all the primes outside the divisors of $a_g$ combined there is at most one additional twist, since it must cancel all parts supported on divisors of $a_g$. Thus $B$ is a union of at most
$$
 H_g=2rm\bigl(1+\omega(a_g)\bigr)
$$
singletons or cosets of a single Sylow subgroup. Here $m$ is the number of target indices and $\omega$ counts distinct prime divisors. Both $q$ and $d$ divide $a_g$ and eventually exceed $H_g$, since $r,m,\log g$ grow at most polynomially in $x$, whereas $\log q$ and $\log d$ grow at least as a positive power of $x$. ## What can be said before using the bad-rank upper bound?

The bad space cannot vanish solely because of its small share. For a tuple with at least $s_0$ qualifying places in each system, independent even determinant twists at a qualifying place send its chosen root to forbidden $q$-power or $d$-power ratios for at least $|A_q|+|A_d|-1$ scalar choices. The same lower bound applies even if the starting tuple is bad for an unrelated reason. Let $a_k=(g_k-1)/2$ and $\eta_k=(q^{v_q(a_k)}+d^{v_d(a_k)}-1)/a_k$. As the shifts preserve the actual multiplicities, (1) and the preceding counting argument alone give
$$
 \frac{\operatorname{rk}\Lambda_{\rm bad}}{\operatorname{rk}\Lambda}
 \ \ge\ \left(1-\frac{3072}{49f}\right)
     \left[1-\prod_{k=0}^1(1-\eta_k)^{s_0}\right] .
$$
This is positive on sufficiently large windows. It independently rules out substituting zero for the bad rank while retaining those regular-embedding and label inputs.

## What survives reduction at the host primes?

Put $\Lambda_+=\Lambda\cap\operatorname{im}P$, $\Lambda_-=\Lambda\cap\ker P$ and $T=\Lambda/(\Lambda_++\Lambda_-)$. Sending $x$ to $Px\bmod\Lambda_+$ identifies $T$ with $P\Lambda/\Lambda_+$. Its annihilator is exactly $\mathfrak c(P)$. This is the finite group left by splitting the actual integral lattice. It can be large even though the two spaces are orthogonal over $\mathbb C$.

At a prime $\lambda$ over $p=q$ or $d$, write $h=v_\lambda(\mathfrak c(P))$ and let $\pi$ be a uniformizer. Locally choose $x$ whose class in $T_\lambda$ has exact order ideal $(\pi^h)$. Then $\pi^h Px$ and $-\pi^h(1-P)x$ belong to the good and bad lattices respectively. Both are primitive, since divisibility by $\pi$ would shorten the order. Their difference is $\pi^h x$. **They have the same nonzero reduction in the ambient lattice through depth $h$.** In particular the good and bad reductions already overlap modulo the full rational power $p^{s_0\sum_k v_p(g_k-1)}$ at every $\lambda\mid p$.

With the squared-slope convention, orthogonality gives the exact total degree difference
$$
  (\operatorname{rk}\Lambda)\mu_\Lambda
   -(\operatorname{rk}\Lambda_+)\mu_+
   -(\operatorname{rk}\Lambda_-)\mu_-
    =\frac{2}{[L:\mathbb Q]}\log |T|.
$$
