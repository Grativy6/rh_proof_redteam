# Does a good window control the part of the integral outside it?

**24 November 2026.** Hearthline followed Chris’s question about the infinite tail into the analytic step that turns late prime windows into nonvanishing. For the published exponent $3/2$ the tail admits an explicit bound independent of frequency. It works even when the prime sum can be very negative on the good window. The bound is conditional on the window estimate for the actual arithmetic sum. The [prime-pair receipt](2026-11-24-prime-pair-readouts.md) tests an input to that estimate. ## The exact test

A finite family of admissible Dirichlet characters has positive weights $a_i$. Write $G(y)$ for its weighted real sum of the specified prime ramps and $g(u)=G(e^u)$. The analytic argument uses a transform $\Phi$ equal to the Laplace transform of $g$ for $\Re(s)>2$. It requires $|g(u)|\le C_0 e^{2u}$ for all $u\ge0$, local finiteness of the candidate poles in $\Re(s)>0$, and at each one a genuine simple pole, of positive residue if real. [Family and ramp](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/CharFamily.lean#L10-L45) · [Analytic hypotheses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauZeroFreeCriterion.lean#L9-L30)

The elementary growth bound can be seen directly. For $y\ge1$ each summand of the raw ramp has magnitude at most $y\log(n)/n$ since $|\chi(n)|\le1$. Summing over $2\le n\le y$ gives $O(y(1+\log^2y))$, hence $O(y^2)$. The explicit principal correction is $O(y(1+\log y))$, and the nonprincipal correction is linear in $y$ for each fixed character. A finite sum has a fixed $C_0$. At a zero $\rho$ with $0<\Re(\rho)<1$, the residue in the symmetrized transform is $S/[\rho(1-\rho)]$, where $S=\sum_i a_i(m_i+m'_i)/2$ and $m_i,m'_i$ are the orders of the character and its inverse there. Every summand has the same complex factor and the positive multiplicities cannot cancel. At a real zero the denominator is positive. The [pole-value control](2026-11-24-value-at-the-zeta-pole.md) checks the separate exceptional point at 1. [Residue and its sign](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/poleData.lean#L336-L383)

Fix $0<\theta<1$. The input gives arbitrarily late intervals $[Y,Y^{3/2}]$. On the logarithmic interval $I=[U,3U/2]$, $U=\log Y$, the more general form says that for each $\eta>0$ there is a nonnegative envelope $R\ge g$ on $I$ with $\int_I R e^{-\theta u}\,du\le e^{\eta U}$. The pointwise estimate $G(y)\le Cy^\theta$ implies this condition by taking $R(u)=\max(C,0)e^{\theta u}$ and comparing the resulting linear function of $U$ with $e^{\eta U}$. No lower bound for $g$ on $I$ is required. [Window envelope](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/CharFamily.lean#L38-L45) · [How the exponent is used](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/TopWin.lean#L68-L81)

## Price of the omitted tail

Specialize the release’s parameters to $\delta=1/2$. They give $\ell=14$, $M=21$, $\sigma=336$ and $k=\lceil420U\rceil$. For every real $U\ge1$ direct integration gives
$$336^k\int_{(0,\infty)\setminus I}\frac{u^k e^{-334u}}{k!}\,du
\ \le\ \frac{e^{-14U/29}}{348}+\frac{21}{6400}e^{-U/2}.$$
For the low tail insert $e^{14(U-u)}\ge1$, and for the high tail insert $e^{14(u-3U/2)}\ge1$. Extending each integral to the full positive half-line and integrating by parts gives respectively $e^{14U}/348^{k+1}$ and $e^{-21U}/320^{k+1}$. After multiplication by $336^k$, use $420U\le k\le420U+1$. The first exponent is at most $[14-420\log(29/28)]U<-14U/29$ by $\log(1+x)>x/(1+x)$. For the second, $\log(1+x)\le x-x^2/[2(1+x)]$ follows by integrating $1/(1+t)\le1-t/(1+x)$ for $0\le t\le x$. At $x=1/20$ this gives $420\log(21/20)-21\le-1/2$. The extra unit from the ceiling gives the factor $21/20$. [Released parameters and ceiling](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/landauTails.lean#L80-L105)

## Why one derivative order works for every frequency

For $\gamma\in\mathbb R$ put
$$A_k(\gamma)=\frac{(-1)^k\Phi^{(k)}(\sigma+i\gamma)}{k!}
 =\int_0^\infty \frac{u^k e^{-\sigma u}}{k!}g(u)e^{-i\gamma u}\,du.$$
The growth bound permits differentiation here. In particular $A_k(0)$ is real. Rotate $A_k(\gamma)$ by a unit complex number so its real part is its absolute value. Then $|A_k(\gamma)|+A_k(0)$ integrates $g$ against the same kernel times $1+\cos(\alpha-\gamma u)$, which lies in $[0,2]$. On $I$ replace $g$ by $R$ and use $t^k/k!\le e^t$ with $t=(\sigma-\theta)u$. Off $I$ use $2C_0$ times the tail bounded above. This yields
$$|A_k(\gamma)|+A_k(0)
\le \frac{2e^{\eta U}}{(\sigma-\theta)^k}
 + \frac{2C_0}{336^k}\left(\frac{e^{-14U/29}}{348}+
                      \frac{21e^{-U/2}}{6400}\right).$$
Only the auxiliary angle $\alpha$ depends on $\gamma$. The window $U$ and integer $k$ work simultaneously for every frequency. [Quantifiers and one-sided moment estimate](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/landauWindow.lean#L40-L73)

For completeness, take any proposed pole with $\Re(\rho)>\theta$. The initial Laplace half-plane puts all poles to the left of $\sigma$. Write $E=\sigma-\theta$. If a real pole $\beta$ is uniquely nearest to the real center at distance $d=\sigma-\beta<E$, its positive residue makes $A_k(0)$ grow at least as $c/d^{k+1}-O(b^{-k})$ for a radius $b>d$. This follows by removing the simple pole and bounding the Taylor coefficients of the holomorphic remainder on a circle of radius $b$. Use $\gamma=0$ in the upper estimate. [Pole subtraction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/LandauPoleAsym.lean#L19-L32) · [Real-pole case](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/landauWindow.lean#L134-L164)

In the other case take $D$ to be the smaller of $E$ and the distance from the real center to its nearest pole, using $E$ if the closed disk contains none. The real disk of radius $D$ has no pole in its interior, and some pole has horizontal separation less than $D$. Indeed a nearest nonreal pole has smaller horizontal separation than its distance. If the only nearest pole is real, the preceding case applies when its distance is strictly below $E$. At distance $E$ the proposed pole supplies the strict horizontal separation. A center $\sigma+i\gamma$ close to the corresponding ordinate then has a pole strictly within distance $D$. Only finitely many poles can matter locally. Two distinct points to the left of the line can be equally near for at most one ordinate, so a small perturbation of $\gamma$ eliminates ties. Choose $d<b<D$ so the nearest shifted pole is alone in a slightly larger disk than radius $b$. Cauchy estimates at the real center give $|A_k(0)|=O(b^{-k})$. Pole subtraction at the shifted center gives $|A_k(\gamma)|\ge |c|/d^{k+1}-O(b^{-k})$. This argument requires no globally rightmost pole. [Real-center dichotomy](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/LandauDichotomy.lean#L24-L42) · [Shifted center](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/LandauNearest.lean#L36-L51) · [Common radii](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/landauWindow.lean#L88-L119)

In either case choose $0<\eta<\log(E/d)$. Since $U\le k$ and admissible $U$ are arbitrarily large, both terms of the moment upper bound vanish relative to $d^{-k}$. The lower bounds give a contradiction. This establishes the window-to-pole inference with the actual exponent and the stated pole and growth hypotheses.

## Two attacks that separate the hypotheses

* **Discard the sign at real poles.** Take $g(u)=-e^{\beta u}$ and $\theta<\beta<1$. All upper windows work with $R=0$. The transform $-1/(s-\beta)$ has a real pole of negative residue. The released arithmetic-family calculation enforces a positive residue at such a pole.
* **Keep windows of fixed multiplicative length in the original variable.** For any fixed $H>0$, choose $\rho=\beta+i\omega$, $\theta<\beta<1$, $0<\omega H<\pi$, and $c=1/[\rho(1-\rho)]$. The real signal $g(u)=2\Re(c e^{\rho u})$ has the permitted paired simple poles with residues $c,\bar c$. There are arbitrarily late whole intervals $[U,U+H]$ on which $g\le0$. Those are $[Y,e^H Y]$ intervals in the original variable. The released windows have logarithmic width $U/2$, which eventually exceeds each fixed half-period. This is a counterexample for the weakened analytic criterion, using a model signal.

## Unresolved

The analytic deduction genuinely bounds the infinite complement once it receives the stated good windows and pole data. Proving that the specific character families supply those windows is the arithmetic burden.
