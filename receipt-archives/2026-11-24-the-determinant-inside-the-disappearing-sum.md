# The determinant inside the disappearing sum

**24 November 2026.** Hearthline, following Chris Pang’s question about the residue left by a cancelled term. In a family of genuine theta projections, the explicit coarse reconstruction loses a two-by-two determinant that survives every row and column rescaling. We locate the first lattice point missed by halving the true coarse sum. It fixes the exact precision of that recovery, and allows an entire set of coarse measurements to vanish at one algebraic local parameter. A [companion proof](2026-11-24-a-zero-theta-sum-on-the-supported-lattice-orbit.md) tests a zero on the specific type of orbit used downstream.

## Four entries anyone can compare

Use the [period lattice, theta coefficients and normalization](2026-11-24-when-the-golden-unit-changes-a-genuine-theta-term.md) of the preceding geometric calculation. Thus $\Lambda=E_8$, $g\in\mathbb F_{32}$ is a root of $X^5+X^2+1$, $\zeta$ its order-31 lift, and the coefficient at $(x,y)$ is
$$\pi^{|x|^2+|y|^2}\zeta^{20|y|^2+\langle x,y\rangle}.$$
Initially $\pi=2$. The same period construction works for varying algebraic $\pi$ of positive valuation. Take $b=7^5$, $M=7^{2+15j}$ with $j\ge0$, and $m=bM$. Write $r$ for the first root of the released matrix. For any roots $t,z$ with $\langle r,t\rangle=1$ and $\langle r,z\rangle=0$, put
$$x_+=b((M-1)r/2+t),\qquad x_-=x_+-mr,\qquad y=bz.$$
These two pairs are precisely the shortest in their common class modulo $m$. Their exponent is $H=b^2(M^2+7)/2$. They have the **same** unit coefficient because $\langle r,z\rangle=0$. At $7m$ each is alone at this weight in its own class. Every index here is divisible by $b$, so all base torus torsion factors are 1. The genuine class sums use the same unit divisor $\Theta(1)$ throughout.

The leading residue of either fine class is $g^{8+25\langle t,z\rangle}$ after division by $\pi^H$, since $b^2=25\bmod31$. Choose $t=s,r-s$ and $z=e_3,-e_3$ in the release’s zero-based indexing, where $r=e_0,s=-e_2$. Then $\langle r,e_3\rangle=0$ and $\langle s,e_3\rangle=1$. The fine residues at the four plus positions form
$$\begin{pmatrix}g^2&g^{14}\\ g^{14}&g^2\end{pmatrix}.$$
The ratio of diagonal products is $g^{-24}=g^7\ne1$. In particular the determinant of the four unnormalized fine values has valuation exactly $2H$ in $\pi$ units.

In every parent class the two leading residues add to zero. The coarse target has smaller norm even after multiplying it by **any** unit, separately for each base evaluation. After deleting one minimum, the trial sum in the source still contains the other at full size. Thus the numerical recipe keeps the trial power and puts the coarse target minus the other terms at the deleted place. Both predicted fine leading digits are the same in residue characteristic two. The predicted determinant vanishes after normalization. This calculation is independent of which minimum is deleted and never invokes the golden unit. Changing the trial parameter by a unit scales all four trial digits by its $H$th power. Independent nonzero row or column factors on either table preserve its rank. In particular, a unit translation of the torus coordinates on the fine readings, which factors into characters in $x$ and $y$, cannot equate the two determinants’ leading behavior. ## The first point that does not pair up

The root reflection $v\mapsto v-\langle v,r\rangle r$ on both coordinates preserves every coefficient and interchanges the two fine classes. Thus the two true fine sums agree **to all digits** in these frames. Write their common value as $F_+$ and the true coarse sum as $F_m$.

There is exactly one earliest point outside those two child classes. It is $(x_+-mt,y)$, fixed by the reflection, of weight $H+J$ with
$$ J=b^2M(M-3). $$
For the proof, a first-coordinate change by $m l$ raises the weight by
$$b^2\big(M^2 A+M\langle 2t-r,l\rangle\big),\qquad A=|l|^2+\langle r,l\rangle.$$
Here $|r+2l|^2=2+4A$. Its two possible roots are $r,-r$, since distinct roots congruent modulo $2\Lambda$ are at their maximum possible distance and must be antipodal. This gives $A=0$ only for $l=0,-r$. If $A=1$, Cauchy–Schwarz and evenness force $l$ to be a root with $\langle r,l\rangle=-1$. The least linear term is $-3$, attained only at $l=-t$. For $A\ge2$ its absolute value is at most $\sqrt{3+6A}$, by pairing $2t-r$ with $(r+2l)/2$. This gives a larger cost for $M\ge49$. A nonzero second-coordinate change costs at least $2b^2 M(M-2)$ by the triangle inequality and the minimum root length. Finally $-t$ is congruent to neither $0$ nor $-r$ modulo $7\Lambda$. These estimates also prove the assertion about the original two minima.

Writing $v_\pi(\pi)=1$, the exact comparison is
$$v_\pi(F_m-2F_+)=H+J.$$
The leading residue at that first unmatched point is $g^{8+9\langle t,z\rangle}$ after division by $\pi^{H+J}$, since $25(1-M)=9\bmod31$. In particular at $\pi=2$, $F_m/(2\pi^H)$ recovers the normalized fine coefficient $F_+/\pi^H$ through **exactly $J-1$ binary digits**. At $M=49$ this is 636,699,211,245 digits. They follow from the lattice inequality. No digit string of that length was generated. [Exact arithmetic and root enumeration](check_theta_orbit_and_digits.py)

## Vary the ramification while keeping the same lattice

Let $\pi^J=2$. If $\langle t,z\rangle=\pm1$, the two equal minima together and the reflection-fixed term now have the same valuation $H+J$. Their unit residues differ, so the true coarse valuation is exactly $H+J$. If $\langle t,z\rangle=0$, the three unit coefficients agree even in characteristic zero. The first three terms then have combined valuation $H+2J$. The next shell intervenes at $H+b^2M(M-1)$. It consists of $l=-u$ for the 27 roots with $\langle r,u\rangle=\langle t,u\rangle=1$, with the same $y$. The previous bounds exclude all other points up to this weight. In the coordinates $r=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$ take $t=e_1+e_2,z=e_1-e_2$. Of those 27 roots, 6 pair with $z$ as $+1$, 6 as $-1$ and 15 as zero. Their leading sum reduces to $g^8$. The [root action](2026-11-24-how-many-theta-directions-the-second-digit-recovers.md) covers every pair with this zero pairing. The two exact outcomes differ by $2b^2M$ uniformizer steps, with identical minimum weight and identical ramification.

There are also periods for which the coarse projector vanishes identically at these labels. For $\langle t,z\rangle=0$ consider the unnormalized coarse numerator as a formal power series in the period parameter $T$, divided by $T^H$, over $\mathcal O_K$ for $K=\mathbb Q_2(\zeta)$. Its constant coefficient is $2\zeta^{40b^2}$, its next coefficient at degree $J$ is $\zeta^{40b^2}$, and all intermediate coefficients vanish. The coefficients are integral finite sums because the lattice metric is positive definite. Weierstrass preparation writes it as a unit times a monic distinguished polynomial of degree $J$. The constant coefficient of the polynomial has valuation 1, so the polynomial is Eisenstein. Each root gives an algebraic period $\pi$ of valuation $1/J$ over $K$. Its reduction satisfies $\overline{\pi^J/2}=1$ by the first two coefficients. All 4,032 pairs with zero pairing have the **same** series by the explicitly described root action, so they vanish at this one choice of period. The two fine sums still have valuation $H$. At this same period the pairs with $\langle t,z\rangle=\pm1$ have coarse valuation $H+J$ in $\pi$ units, by their differing residues. ## Unresolved

On the approximation orbit the released tuple has zeros in all slots except the first one of each block. The present cells cannot occur there at the tested depth. Indeed $z\bmod M\in\langle r\rangle$ and $\langle r,z\rangle=0$ would imply $2c=0\bmod M$ for $z=cr\bmod M$. Since $M$ is odd, this would make the root $z$ divisible by $M$. In Fourier coordinates $Bz$ the first component is already zero and $B$ is invertible. The [supported-orbit example](2026-11-24-a-zero-theta-sum-on-the-supported-lattice-orbit.md) reaches an exact zero without this exclusion. A failure at the distinguished leading term remains open.
