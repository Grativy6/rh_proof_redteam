# A strict class inside the zeta windows

**24 November 2026.** Take the coefficient prime $\ell=577$, a primitive even character $\psi$ of conductor 256, and twist $j=296$. The strict Selmer group in the published Case I argument is **cyclic of order 577**. Its nonzero class restricts injectively to the plus group of every sufficiently deep zeta host field. The two moving primes can go beyond every fixed cutoff. The fixed conductor 256 is what makes this example different from the [two earlier finite hosts](2026-11-24-all-odd-twists-at-two-conductors.md). ## The same object after the primes move

Let $\varepsilon$ be the arithmetic 577-adic cyclotomic character, and let $\omega$ be its reduction. In $\mathbb F_{577}$ the integer 5 has order 576. Define $\psi(-1)=1$ and take $\psi(5)$ to be the Teichmüller lift of $5^{9\cdot39}=510\bmod577$. Here the *argument* 5 generates $(\mathbb Z/256)^\times/\{\pm1\}$, of order 64. The *value* of the character also has order 64, so the conductor is exactly 256. All values lie in $\mathbb Z_{577}$. The coefficient module is
$$A_j=(\mathbb Q_{577}/\mathbb Z_{577})(\psi^{-1}\varepsilon^{-j}).$$
The strict local condition at 577 and the unramified class conditions elsewhere come from the arithmetic module in the source. At 2 inertia acts through a nontrivial scalar modulo 577, giving zero invariants. At every other $p\ne577$ the nonzero scalar $\psi(p)^{-1}p^{-j}-1$ is surjective on the divisible module. Thus the class is locally zero on decomposition as well. In particular enlarging the set of level primes by the moving host primes leaves the group on this character unchanged. Write $m$ for the host exponent, to distinguish it from the cohomological twist $j$. On the trivial outer character the host field is
$$E_m=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^{m+2}})^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d}),\qquad N=2^{m+2}qd.$$
As soon as $m\ge6$, $\psi$ factors through its real cyclotomic factor and inflates to the required character modulo $N$. This follows directly from `Host.H`, whose two-primary unit condition is membership in $\{\pm1\}$. The character is even and $j=296$ is even. The exceptional primes for the trivial outer character are 3 and 7. Indeed the auxiliary quadratic field is $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, of class number one, and `pOne` is 3. We have $576\nmid592$, so 577 is in Case I. At 577 the finite-character component is unramified. The quadratic field $K$ cannot lie in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{577},\zeta_{256})$ because it ramifies at 7. This verifies the independence in `GD`. The actual window uses $x^{2+8\epsilon}\le2^{m(x)}$ and $t(x)=\lfloor x^{C_t}\rfloor$. The character sum runs over $0<j<t(x)$. For all sufficiently large $x$ both $m(x)\ge6$ and $t(x)>296$ hold. ## Check the group upstairs

The degree $[E_m:\mathbb Q]$ is a power of two. Restrict the class to $G_{E_m}$, where $\psi=1$. Corestriction after restriction is multiplication by this degree and is injective on the 577-primary group. Local vanishing survives restriction. The resulting class is unramified outside the primes above 577 and locally zero at all these primes. It belongs to the source's actual `Sha` with coefficient $\varepsilon^{-296}$. Complex conjugation acts by $\psi(c)=1$ on this restricted isotypic class, equal to the required sign $(-1)^{296}$. This proves a lower bound on the **upstairs plus group**, independently of the one-directional upper bound used in the release. The residual module is identical at every $j=296+576u$ with $u\ge0$. Global invariants and the local invariants at 577 vanish because the residual inertia character has order 72 there. Consequently the residual Selmer class embeds in the divisible-coefficient group at every one of these twists. At every host with $m\ge6$, each such $j<t$ contributes at least one to the 577-adic plus length. For $t>296$ there are $1+\lfloor(t-1-296)/576\rfloor$ such indices. These explicit finite subgroups account for that many length units. Under the finiteness clause of the estimated sum their contribution is at least this number times $\log577$. An infinite length would already violate the separate finiteness clause. The stated upper bound has room for linear growth. ## How the candidate was found

The discovery search considered primes $\ell\le2200$ outside the zeta exceptional set, even primitive two-power characters of orders $2,\ldots,256$ that divide $\ell-1$, and positive even twists represented below $\ell-1$ with $\ell-1\nmid 2j$. There were 1,180,660 combinations. A Jacobi-divisor multiplier left 1,271. A split-prime power test on a projected circular unit left two after the first prime and one after four. This last test was a search heuristic. The proof of the displayed order constructs a class and proves its upper bound directly. None of the excluded candidates is needed for it. ## Unresolved

On this input the required rank-three witness must be genuinely non-self-dual if it arises by the published marked-point route. Its tame level and residual character stay fixed when the host primes move. The construction of an admissible cuspidal point, or an independently proved failure of a necessary step producing it, remains open.
