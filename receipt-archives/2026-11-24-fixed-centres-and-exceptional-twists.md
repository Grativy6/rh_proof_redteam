## Growth on a module that can have torsion

Let $X$ be generated by $R$ elements over $\mathbb Z_2$. Let $\gamma$ be unipotent modulo 2 and $u\in1+4\mathbb Z_2$ with $c=v_2(u-1)$. For twist $k$ put $T=u^k\gamma$ and $\Delta_m=T^{2^m}-I$. The source measures the module length of $X/\Delta_m X$. If $R=0$, $X=0$. Otherwise $\dim_{\mathbb F_2}X/2X\le R$, so the nilpotent part of $T\bmod2$ has $R$-th power zero. For $2^j\ge R$ this gives $T^{2^j}=I\bmod2X$. Squaring once more gives $I\bmod4X$ because $S(X)\subseteq2X$ implies $(2S+S^2)(X)\subseteq4X$. This argument also applies to nonfree modules.

For $m\ge j+1$, the image of $I+T^{2^m}$ lies in $2X$ and surjects onto $2X/4X$ by reducing $2I+(T^{2^m}-I)$ modulo $4X$. Nakayama’s lemma gives $(I+T^{2^m})X=2X$. Therefore $\Delta_{m+1}X=2\Delta_m X$ exactly. A finite cokernel length can increase by at most $R$ per subsequent layer, since $\Delta_m X/2\Delta_m X$ has dimension at most $R$. For smaller $m$ the images decrease with $m$ by factorization of $T^{2^m}-I$. The release’s anchor $j_0=\lfloor\log_2R\rfloor+3$ is large enough. ## What a determinant retains

At $j_0$ choose a surjection from a free module of rank $R$ onto $X$, then lift $\gamma^{2^{j_0}}$ to one integral matrix $S$ through this surjection. Every specialized matrix cokernel surjects onto the corresponding cokernel on $X$. Its determinant gives an upper bound, interpreted as infinite if singular. Write $z_i$ for the eigenvalues of $S$, with multiplicities. For each unit $z_i$ minimize $|u^{2^{j_0}t}z_i-1|_2$ over $t\in\mathbb Z_2$ and choose a minimizer $s_i$. The exponent map extends continuously from the integers and satisfies
$$|u^{2^{j_0}t}-u^{2^{j_0}t'}|_2=2^{-(c+j_0)}|t-t'|_2.$$
This follows by repeated squaring and the isometry for multiplication of an exponent by an odd integer. The compact domain is $\mathbb Z_2$. The eigenvalue can lie in a ramified extension. The ultrametric inequality and minimality then give
$$2^{-(c+j_0)}|k-s_i|_2\le|u^{2^{j_0}k}z_i-1|_2.$$
A nonunit integral eigenvalue gives a right-hand side of 1. Multiplying the inequalities and adding the layer-growth estimate gives exactly the required upper bound with the **same centres** for all $k,m$. No integral eigenbasis is required. How much can a spectral reading conceal? On $(\mathbb Z/2^h)^2$ compare $\gamma=5^{-1}I$ with $\gamma'=5^{-1}I+2^b E_{12}$. They agree modulo $2^b$ and have the same characteristic polynomials and determinant functions for every twist and layer. At $k\ne1$ put $r=2+m+v_2(k-1)$ and $s=\min(r,m+b)$. For the scalar operator the two Smith exponents on a free lift are $(r,r)$. On the other lift they are $(s,2r-s)$ because the off-diagonal entry has valuation $m+b$. Reduction modulo $2^h$ caps each exponent at $h$. With $h=18$, $b=3$, $m=4$, $k=1+2^{20}$, the measured lengths are **36 and 25**. The difference can grow without bound at fixed observation depth $b$. The release only uses the free determinant in the upper direction at this step. ## A fixed window exponent costs exponentially in the number of exceptional twists

For $1\le k\le Z$, the strict inequality $|k-s_i|_2<2^{-\lfloor\log_2Z\rfloor}$ can hold for at most one integer per centre. Two such integers would differ by a multiple of $2^{1+\lfloor\log_2Z\rfloor}>Z$. Fix the resulting exceptional set before choosing the subwindow. Write $U=\log X$, $y=\log Y$ and $B=C_t/\eta>1$. For an exceptional $k>1$ set $a_k=\log k/C_t$. A bad running point has $a_k\le\log x<Ba_k$. Thus the **whole** interval $[Y,Y^2]$ works precisely when $y$ avoids every half-open interval $[a_k/2,Ba_k)$. Zero and one are harmless. If $B=1$ the goodness condition is automatic.

Permitted starts are $[U/J,U/2]$. On a logarithmic scale each excluded interval has length $\log(2B)$. For at most $R$ exceptions, $J=2(2B)^R$ suffices, including equality because a finite union of left-closed, right-open intervals of exactly the total length cannot cover a closed target of that length. The source uses $4(2B)^R$. With smaller $J$ and $B>1$ an overlapping chain of $R$ such intervals can cover the candidate starts, and integer $k$ approximate the required positions after dilation in $\log X$. In the exact control $X=2^{3100}$, $C_t=2$, $\eta=1$, and $k=2^{400},2^{1599}$. All starts are covered at $J=31$. At $J=32$, $\log_2Y=99$ works. ## Why the separate arithmetic estimate matters

On the genuine algebraic domain take $X=\mathbb Z_2$, $u=5$ and $\gamma=5^{-s}$, with $s=\sum_j2^{n_j}\in\mathbb Z_2$ an infinite series of binary digits. For $k_j=\sum_{i\le j}2^{n_i}$ choose the next position $n_{j+1}>4\exp(k_j^{2j})$. The series is not a positive integer. Every positive integer twist has finite length $2+m+v_2(k-s)$, but the length at $k_j$ is $2+m+n_{j+1}$. Fix any $0<\epsilon<1/52$ and $J\ge1$, and take $X_j=k_j^{2j}$. For all sufficiently large $j$, throughout $X_j^{1/J}\le x\le X_j$ the twist $k_j$ is already below $\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor$ and its length alone exceeds $\exp(x^\epsilon/2)/\log2$. Even a fixed rank-one module can have no usable late subwindow at these scales. This is an operator satisfying the finite-generation, unipotence and centre hypotheses. It has no asserted number-field realization.

On the actual exceptional branch, `Good` ensures $k\le x^{\epsilon/(2C_K)}$. The fallback is a bound on $H^2$ over a **fixed-height** cyclotomic layer. For the zeta base that anchor has the form $L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^c},\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$ with $c=2+j_0$. Its degree is $2^{c+1}$ and
$$|\operatorname{disc}L|=2^{(c-1)2^{c+1}}(qd)^{2^c}.$$
The two odd quadratic discriminants are coprime to each other and to the cyclotomic discriminant, so the integral tensor bases give this formula. Hence $\log(2|\operatorname{disc}L|)\ll_c x^{\epsilon/4}$ when $\log(qd)\le x^{\epsilon/4}$. The stated `LemmaK` price $\exp((2k[L:\mathbb Q])^{C_K}\log(2|\operatorname{disc}L|))$ is then at most $\exp(C x^{3\epsilon/4})$ at an exceptional twist. The slack between $3\epsilon/4$ and $4\epsilon/5$ eventually covers the polynomial number of twists. This calculation verifies the use of `LemmaK` conditional on its arithmetic content.
