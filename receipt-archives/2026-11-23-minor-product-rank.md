# Independent lists of minors can lose rank when multiplied

**23 November 2026.** At a selected embedding the released proof multiplies two lists of polynomials and counts independent target directions. We kept the prescribed number of factors, made each list an integral direct summand, and kept the products independent over the fraction field. At degree two, **four of the 30 products can still acquire an arbitrarily large common prime-power loss in independent combinations**. The native proof imposes an additional restriction on the leading monomials. We checked why it excludes the example. ## The relation that changes the answer

Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\pi$. Take $k\ge4$, $d\ge1$, and two columns of independent variables $x_i,y_i$. Put $\Delta_{ij}=x_i y_j-x_j y_i$. At an even selected slot of weight $(d+1,1,0,\ldots,0)$ the flat polynomials have degree $d$ in $x$. The residual weight is $(1,1,0,\ldots,0)$. The release counts $k-1$ residual factors. Its leading-monomial restriction here selects the minors $\Delta_{12},\ldots,\Delta_{1k}$, up to the signs used to order minors. The restriction fixes the first entry of the first column of the residual tableau. [Vector and cofactor coordinates](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GroupLattices/FockModels/FlatModels/Letter.lean#L12-L22) · [The selected indices](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/DualFockLinear/IsMultiHom.lean#L11-L17) · [Unit leading coefficients and minors](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GradedWeylDivisibility/StatementD.lean#L24-L30) · [The chosen family](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/DualFockLinear/TheoremIIClosedOfTestBound.lean#L23-L42)

With these $k-1$ minors the products are integrally independent. In $\sum_{j=2}^k P_j(x)\Delta_{1j}$ the coefficient of $y_j$ is $x_1P_j$. Extracting those coefficients and dividing their monomials by $x_1$ recovers every $P_j$ using only operations over $O$.

Change just the last minor to $\Delta_{23}$. Each list still has the right cardinality and spans a direct summand. Nevertheless
$$
 x_1\Delta_{23}-x_2\Delta_{13}+x_3\Delta_{12}=0.
$$
Multiplication by every monomial of degree $d-1$ gives an independent relation. In graph terms the selected minors are the edges of a star. Replacing its last edge by $23$ introduces a triangle.

There is an exact count for any edge set. Grade by the total occurrence of each row index. For a multidegree $u$ of size $d+2$, write $S$ for its support and $v_i=x^{u-e_i}y_i$ for $i\in S$. A minor $ij$ contributes the source monomial $x^{u-e_i-e_j}$ precisely when $i,j\in S$. Its image is $v_j-v_i$. This block is the oriented incidence matrix of the graph on $S$. A spanning tree in each component supplies an integral basis of the image, and each extra edge supplies one independent relation. This calculation works over every residue characteristic. In the modified star a block loses one rank exactly when $1,2,3\in S$. The number of such multidegrees is $\binom{d+k-2}{k-1}$.

## Keeping the rational rank still loses integral precision

Replace the last minor instead by $\Delta_{23}+\pi^N\Delta_{1k}$ for any $N\ge1$. This residual list is a direct summand even modulo $\pi$. Use the coefficient belonging to $\Delta_{23}$ as its last pivot. The products now have full rational rank. Their exact Smith exponents are

| Exponent | Multiplicity |
| --- | ---: |
| $N$ | $\binom{d+k-2}{k-1}$ |
| $0$ | $(k-1)\binom{d+k-1}{k-1}-\binom{d+k-2}{k-1}$ |

To see the positive exponents, write the last coefficient polynomial uniquely as $P_0+x_1P'$ with $P_0$ independent of $x_1$. Subtracting columns for $\Delta_{12}$ and $\Delta_{13}$ clears $x_1P'\Delta_{23}$ by the displayed relation. Those columns now give exactly $\pi^N x_1P'\Delta_{1k}$. After removing the indicated powers, the whole list admits integral pivots. The retained columns have the $x_1$-divisible coefficients of $y_j$ for $2\le j<k$. The new $P_0$ columns are read from the $y_3$ coefficient at $x_1=0$, namely $x_2P_0$. The remaining columns are read from the $x_1^2$-divisible part of the $y_k$ coefficient, namely $x_1^2P'$. This proves the table.

For $k=4,d=2,N=3$ over $\mathbb Z_5$, a primitive combination can thus be invisible modulo $125$ in four distinct directions. The elementary divisors of the genuine star products in this model are all units. This tests the full local product space. The smaller global source family is chosen by a further counting theorem.

## How the release prevents the collision

The native proof checks products after reduction to the residue field. At an even marked slot, the residual leading monomial has first-column exponents $(\nu_0,0,\ldots,0)$. Subtracting that fixed vector from the first column of a product's leading monomial recovers the exponents $\alpha_i$ of its flat factor. At an odd marked slot the flat coordinates are signed maximal minors. In zero-based indices, deleting row $i$ contributes one to diagonal position $(t,t)$ exactly when $i>t$. The residual exponent there is zero. Its tableau excludes row zero and strict increase down a column forces the entry in position $t$ to exceed $t$. Thus the diagonal entries give the tail sums $\sum_{i>t}\alpha_i$; consecutive differences and the fixed total recover $\alpha$. In either parity the remaining exponents determine the residual label. Distinct slots use disjoint variables. The common polynomial multiplying the flat input has nonzero reduction. Its determinants use distinct frame variables, so a determinant has a monomial of coefficient $\pm1$ that survives reduction. The polynomial ring over the residue field is a domain. Multiplication by this common factor preserves independence there. The flat basis spans a direct summand, so a primitive integral combination of it cannot reduce to zero. The release uses both properties on the same chosen inputs. At the next use the proof constructs both actual product lists, including the list of approximating symbols. It tests every coefficient combination with a unit entry and controls the difference between the two lists on the selected edge. Across different edges, the other pairings are multiples of $p^{D+1}$ while the selected pairing divides $p^D$. Their sum has the selected valuation. Smith reduction then constructs one integral right inverse after scaling by $p^D$. This argument permits the separating test to depend on the combination. ## Reproduce

Run `python receipt-archives/check_minor_product_rank.py`. It expands the generic determinants and computes the elementary divisors in the truncated coefficient ring. At $k=4,d=2$ it checks the 30 products in both the vector and cofactor conventions. At $k=5,d=3$ it finds 15 exceptional directions among 140 vector products in the modified family. A zero modulo the checking precision is reported at that precision. The incidence argument proves the exact kernel in the unperturbed triangle example. ## Unresolved

This counterexample defeats independent factor counts, saturation of both input lists and rational independence of all their products. The released proof also fixes the residual leading labels, which block the cancellation. The remaining quantitative burden is the size of the complemented flat space *before* these products are taken, including any collisions caused by identifying positions around a Frobenius orbit.
