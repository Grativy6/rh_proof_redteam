# Two arithmetic actions indistinguishable at a full level

**24 November 2026.** Hearthline, under Chris Pang’s request to press the distinction between a lattice and the arithmetic transported on it. The [doubling argument](2026-11-24-reconstructing-a-marked-lattice-by-doubling.md) recovers the entire marked metric at level at least four. Here is a genuine arithmetic ambiguity. On one principally polarized abelian variety two actions of the **full ring of integers** agree on all the marked torsion points and preserve the same metric. There is a sharp size comparison that prevents this for small discriminant.

## A discriminant bound that uses the metric

Let $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_K$ for an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant $\Delta$. Suppose two actions on an integral positive-definite metric lattice agree modulo $M$ and are balanced, meaning that complex conjugation acts by taking metric adjoints. Then
$$M>\sqrt{|\Delta|}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rho_1=\rho_2.$$
This applies after transporting the metric by any isometry with the stated marking.

Write $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\omega]$ with $\omega=(t+\sqrt{\Delta})/2$, $t=0$ or $1$. The difference $D=(\rho_1(\omega)-\rho_2(\omega))/M$ is integral and skew-adjoint. Each $\rho_i(\omega)-tI/2$ is skew-adjoint and has operator norm $\sqrt{|\Delta|}/2$ because its square is $\Delta I/4$. Thus $\|D\|\le\sqrt{|\Delta|}/M$. If this is less than one, every eigenvalue has modulus less than one. An integer matrix with that property is nilpotent: if its characteristic polynomial is $X^k h(X)$ with $h(0)\ne0$, the product of the nonzero eigenvalues would have to be a nonzero integer of absolute value less than one. A skew-adjoint nilpotent operator is zero. In particular, at $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ the congruence and metric already determine the action at $M\ge3$.

## Failure only two units of discriminant away

Take any odd $M\ge3$ for which $d=M^2+2$ is squarefree. Then $d=3\bmod4$ and $\Delta=-d$. In the Hamilton quaternions use the Hurwitz order $\Lambda$ with integral basis $1,i,j,(1+i+j+k)/2$. Give it the positive form $G(x,y)=2\operatorname{Re}(x\bar y)$, whose Gram matrix in that basis is
$$\begin{pmatrix}2&0&0&1\\0&2&0&1\\0&0&2&1\\1&1&1&2\end{pmatrix}.$$
The pure quaternions $I_\pm=i\pm Mj+k$ both square to $-d$. The two Hurwitz elements $\alpha_\pm=(1+I_\pm)/2$ give actions of $\mathcal O$ on $\Lambda$ by left multiplication. Their metric adjoints are multiplication by their conjugates. Their difference is $M$ times multiplication by $j$, an integral automorphism whose square is $-I$.

Consequently the two arithmetic actions agree modulo $M$, while every nonzero discrepancy of this last automorphism is visible one suitable level deeper. No integral isometry congruent to the identity modulo $M$ interchanges them. The integral isometry group of $G$ is finite, and its subgroup congruent to the identity at $M\ge3$ is torsion-free. The only candidate is the identity, which sends $1$ to the two different values $\alpha_\pm$ under the two actions. The small example is $M=9$, $d=83$. It misses the discriminant threshold by only $|\Delta|-M^2=2$. For an odd $M$, this is the first possible fundamental imaginary quadratic discriminant magnitude above $M^2$: such magnitudes are 0 or 3 modulo 4, and $M^2=1\bmod4$.

For a size-admissible full level take $M=3^{28}$, $N=3^4$, $r=3^{24}$, so $M=rN$. In dimension four all three released \(\mathrm{Big}\) inequalities hold. The required squarefreeness can be checked with small integer trial division:
$$3^{56}+2=249737\cdot14947671577\cdot140195420227.$$
All three factors are distinct primes. Since $M$ is odd and $d=3\bmod4$, this field has odd discriminant. Also $5\nmid3d$, as required for the local example below. The degree-four integral actions, their adjoints, the factorization and the level bounds are checked by the [independent integer replay](check_marked_lattice_and_torsion.py). ## One abelian variety realizes both actions

Work at residue characteristic 5, prime to the displayed level and discriminant. Let $T=\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb G_m)$ and fix $q=5$. The period corresponding to $y\in\Lambda$ is the character evaluation $x\mapsto q^{G(y,x)}$. The analytic quotient is an abelian fourfold. Indeed $G\Lambda$ contains $4\mathbb Z^4$ in the displayed coordinates since $\det G=4$. The quotient is thus a finite quotient of the product of four Tate curves with parameter $q^4$. The identification of the period lattice with the character lattice is an isomorphism, and the pairing is symmetric with positive-definite valuation matrix. It defines a principal polarization. The determinant four occurs in the monodromy pairing.

For either action $\rho$, let $a\in\mathcal O$ operate on torus points by precomposition with $\rho(\bar a)$ on characters. It sends the period of $y$ to the period of $\rho(a)y$ by the adjoint identity, hence descends. The principal polarization carries this endomorphism to its conjugate under Rosati. On the four-dimensional tangent space each complex embedding has multiplicity two, since the characteristic polynomial of the generator is the square of its quadratic polynomial. The difference between the two generators as endomorphisms of the fourfold is $M$ times the automorphism induced by $j$. Thus the actions agree on **all of $A[M]$**, including the period torsion, after any field extension used to mark it. A point of exact order $3M$ distinguishes them. All theta readings depending just on the underlying polarized variety and the same chosen level frames also agree. [Split-torus uniformization and polarization data](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08212)

A polarization-preserving isomorphism respecting the marking would induce an isometry on the toric lattice that is the identity modulo $M$ and intertwines the two actions. The preceding calculation excludes it. This gives two distinct marked arithmetic structures on one geometric object.

## Where the release asks for the missing datum

The unitary setup has a specified map to the **unitary** moduli space on the generic fibre. Its comparison witness identifies the group over that very point with the pullback of the universal group. The fibre argument extends those specified endomorphisms of the common generic group and then uses their action on the toric character lattice. The definition of a comparison map in the closed-immersion argument also includes exact commutation with every ring element. In a separate criterion that does determine an endomorphism from torsion, the quantifier is over **every** $n^j$. Our example fails this premise explicitly at the next power of 3. The deeper setup also fixes the unitary point itself by base change. In the construction the new map is the composite of the extension map with the old unitary moduli point. ## Unresolved

This is a counterexample to reconstructing a marked CM action from the positive metric and a single large torsion level without a discriminant restriction. The pair has different generic endomorphisms. To break the traced unitary argument one would have to lose the action even with its **same generic pullback**, or find a downstream assertion that silently discards that datum. The fourfold calculation does not establish either. The example also does not assert membership in a particular global Shimura component of the release.
