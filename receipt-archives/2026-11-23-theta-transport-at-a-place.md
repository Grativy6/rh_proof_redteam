## Does the conjugated series converge?

At an elliptic torsion point $t$ the previous calculation expresses $U_t F_{\rm odd}$ using an algebraic divisor, an algebraic leading coefficient and the normalized odd section. Under an extension of $\rho$ to the algebraic numbers this becomes the Gaussian translate at **any lift of the transported point**, times a root of unity. At period lifts one uses the period sign directly. This works for translates of the normalized half section as well. Indeed $U_xU_t=e^{-\pi i E_H(x,t)}U_{x+t}$, with a root of unity for torsion lifts, and its nonzero normalizing value also transports up to a root of unity. For the unshifted normalized half section the normalization at zero determines it exactly. The unkilled condition survives because the algebraic $\mathcal O_K$ endomorphisms transport with their differentials. The two idempotent tests are exchanged if $\rho$ reverses $K$. [Derivation of the torsion scalar and the normalized half section](2026-11-23-cm-theta-torsion-trace.md)

Every expansion in the finite sum for $G$ therefore becomes a product of entire, one-variable receiving translates, with a constant of absolute value one and the common factor $1/\sqrt n$. The possible sign change of $\sqrt n$ is also a phase. Call the sum $G'$. All its Taylor coefficients are the images of the source coefficients. Multiplication by $\kappa$ and substitution by $g$ give $(\rho\kappa)G'\circ g'$, where $g'$ is obtained by applying $\rho$ to the entries of $g$. The entries lie in the normal CM field $E$. Complex conjugation commutes with every embedding on this field, so $g'$ is unitary too. This constructs a receiving entire function **before** invoking any convention for summing a divergent series. The univariate recursions, finitely many algebraic torsion coordinates and normalizations, matrix entries, $\sqrt n$ and $\kappa$ generate one number field for all coefficients.

If $\theta=\mathrm{thMM}$ is a chosen fourth-tensor root, write $c_\nu$ for a nonzero Taylor coefficient of $\theta$. Then $c_\nu^4$ and every $c_\mu c_\nu^3$ are coefficients of $\kappa G\circ g$ in independent blocks. Adjoining $c_\nu$ suffices for every $c_\mu$. On the receiving side take the coefficient indexed by $\nu$ in each of the last three **blocks of variables** of $(\rho\kappa)G'\circ g'$, and divide by $\rho(c_\nu)^3$. Those partial derivatives at fixed zero arguments define an entire function of the remaining block. Its coefficients are precisely $\rho(c_\mu)$, and taking its fourth tensor recovers the whole function by Taylor uniqueness. For the actual even adjacent ranks $a,b$, $4\mid ab$, so one can also group the $ab$ independent blocks of `thZero` into copies of the known fourfold expression. ## Why the right domain of integration survives

Put $P'=(\Lambda')^{4f}$. Write
$$
 S=\sum_j A_j\Lambda,\qquad S'=\sum_j A'_j\Lambda',\qquad
 A_j\in K^{4f},\quad A'_j=\rho(A_j).
$$
The columns are obtained by taking a finite $\mathcal O_K$-generating set of $(\mathfrak b^{-1})^4$ and applying $g_0$. This formula uses the original rational isometry and ideal on both sides. In the receiving block coordinates it says $S'=g'(M'^4)$ with $M'$ generated by $w\,(\rho\circ\Phi)(a)$, $w\in\Lambda'$, $a\in\mathfrak b^{-1}$. For standard source and target modules the integral homomorphisms are the matrices with entries in $\mathcal O_E$, so multiplying by $\mathfrak b^{-1}$ gives all matrices with entries in that ideal, one entry at a time. Hence the lattice appearing in `La` for ranks $a,b$ is precisely $(M')^{ab}$, after arranging the coordinates. [Arithmetic matrix entries](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L25-L39) · [Lattice and Gaussian form](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L68-L85) · [Embeddings used in the receiving coordinates](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L125-L136)

Choose an integer $m$ clearing all the column denominators and such that $mP\subset S\subset m^{-1}P$. For the reverse inclusion one can express $m$ times every standard basis vector in the $\mathcal O_K$-span of the $A_j$, since these generate a full module. Applying $\rho$ gives the same inclusions on the receiving side. The exact finite subgroups of the elliptic products are
$$
 \frac{S/m}{P}=\sum_j (m A_j)E_\Lambda[m^2],\qquad
 \frac{S'/m}{P'}=\sum_j (m A'_j)E_{\Lambda'}[m^2].
$$
Indeed $E_\Lambda[m^2]$ consists of the classes of $\lambda/m^2$ for $\lambda\in\Lambda$. The integral columns $mA_j$ act by algebraic endomorphisms, and their differentials transform under $\rho$. The equalities identify the two subgroups under the torsion-point bijection. Thus for **every** $x'\in S'$ there is $x\in S$ such that the point of $x/m$ transports to that of $x'/m$. Remembering $x$ only modulo $P$ loses this information.

Here is a control for that loss. Use $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ and $g=\frac15\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-4\\4&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ on two coordinates, extended by the identity on two more. On the active coordinates record the two integer parts followed by the two $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-7})/2$ parts, on $25$-torsion. The numerators $(3,4,0,0)$ and $(8,4,0,0)$ agree modulo $5$. Their reductions modulo $25$ land on opposite sides of the subgroup test since the determinant $3v-4u$ is respectively $0$ and $-20$. This is a counterexample to recovery from the coarser level. The integral-column formula above supplies the missing level from the released input. [Exact finite control](check_theta_lattice_transport.py) · [PAL and APCI interface tests](../corpus/README.md)

We must still show that the Gaussian square of $G'$ is periodic on this *particular* $S'$. Arbitrary phases on the transported summands would preserve orthogonality on a common cover but need not give an eigenfunction on $S'$. For $mx\in P$ define
$$
 R_x(w)=\frac{(U_x F_0)(mw)}{F_0(mw)},\qquad
 R_G(w)=\frac{G(mw)}{F_0(mw)} .
$$
$R_x$ is a product of meromorphic elliptic functions of the individual coordinates of $w$. Translation by a period multiplies its numerator and denominator by the same factor since $mx\in P$. Its zeros and poles are torsion points, and its Taylor or Laurent leading factors are algebraic by the univariate calculation. A meromorphic elliptic function with that divisor is rational in Weierstrass coordinates. The divisor equations have algebraic coefficients and fix the function up to scale, and the algebraic leading coefficient fixes the scale. Thus it is an algebraic rational function on the product. The finite sum gives the same assertion for $R_G$. The coefficient calculation gives
$$
 \rho(R_x)=\eta_x R'_{x'},\quad |\eta_x|=1,\qquad
 \rho(R_G)(w)=G'(mw)/F'_0(mw)
$$
whenever $[x']$ is the image of $[x]$. Equality can be checked on Taylor germs and then as rational functions. In these expressions $\rho$ acts on algebraic data, never on values of a transcendental function at arbitrary complex points.

If $x\in S$, $U_xG=\beta(x)G$. Dividing this identity gives an equality of rational functions
$$
 R_G(w+x/m)R_x(w)=\beta(x)R_G(w).
$$
Translation by the torsion point $[x/m]$ is an algebraic morphism. Transport the equality and use the divided-point identification above for a prescribed $x'\in S'$. Clearing denominators gives $U'_{x'}G'=\rho(\beta(x))\eta_x^{-1}G'$, first off the zeros of $F'_0$, then everywhere by holomorphic continuation. Its scalar has absolute value one. This proves the required periodicity of the weighted square on the actual receiving lattice.

## What the norm reads

Over the common cover $mP'$, distinct source summands still have distinct character labels on $P'$. Their labels are the images of distinct elliptic torsion points in $S/(S\cap P)$, and $P'$ is self-dual. Orthogonality and the unit magnitudes now give $\|G'\|^2=\|F'_0\|^2$. The periodicity just proved permits the same normalized mean on $S'$, for every measurable fundamental domain allowed by the release. The tensor product and the split Gaussian give the product of the one-block means by integration in independent variables. Periodicity of the one-block density also follows by fixing three arguments with nonzero values in the fourfold identity. The factor $|\rho(\kappa)|=1$ drops out. Consequently at **each** embedding
$$
 \operatorname{logThAt}_\rho(a,b)
     =ab\, f\,\log\|\mathrm{th0}_{\Lambda',t'_0}\|^2 .
$$
The positive norm and all-orders convergence rule out the disappearing-tail mechanism at this standard vacuum. The rank multiplier is the number of independent entries, and the same $g_0$ determined both the finite sum and its receiving period lattice.
