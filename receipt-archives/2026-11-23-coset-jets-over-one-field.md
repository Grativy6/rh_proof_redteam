# Do the actual coset jets land in one number field?

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Chris Pang’s public audit request.** The selector for a matrix entry chooses a rational lift from infinitely many possibilities. At a selected prime, two lifts can agree on the observed coset and still differ by a nontrivial phase. The opposite-prime condition in the released lift prevents that failure. We derive the condition and the existence of one coefficient field for the fixed family of jets. The cost of the resulting derivatives is a separate quantitative question.

## Test the lift itself

Use the arithmetic period module $P=J\operatorname{Hom}(Y,Y')$ and the form of the [unitary calculation](2026-11-23-unitary-phase-with-fixed-directions.md), writing rational vectors in place of their scaled CM embeddings. Let $S$ be the selected primes of $E$, containing at most one of any conjugate pair. On $S$ the scalar ideal $J$ is locally the unit ideal for the input to the indicator. Write $H_S$ for rational linear maps integral from $Y$ to $Y'$ at those primes. For $c>0$ the lattice $B(c)$ is $cP$ at $S$, $c^{-1}P$ at their conjugates and $P$ elsewhere; $A(c)$ uses the opposite exponents. Each is the original lattice times a norm-one fractional ideal, hence is self-dual for the alternating form. The source asks for a lift $t$ of $x$ with $t-x\in cH_S$ and $t\in A(d_t)$ **for a single positive integer** $d_t$. Such lifts exist for every $x$ in the required range. Pick a positive $D$ clearing its denominators at $S$. The lattice $A(Dc)$ permits the specified residues $x\bmod cP$ at $S$, imposes divisibility by $Dc$ at each conjugate, and integrality elsewhere. A fractional lattice over a Dedekind ring maps onto its product of finite local congruence quotients by the Chinese remainder theorem, after clearing a common denominator. Choose $t$ in this lattice with the required residues. A common integer can accommodate all the primes, including several over the same rational prime.

For the rational translation use $W_x=e^{\pi i q(x)}U_x$. With $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$ its multiplication law, including the sign, is
$$
 W_xW_y=e^{-2\pi i j(x,y)}W_{x+y},\qquad j(x,y)=e(\omega x,y).
$$
This follows from $2e(\omega x,y)=e(x,y)+\sqrt d\,\Re H(x,y)$. In particular $W$ is an ordinary additive action on each of the displayed integral period lattices. A rational fractional lift uses the exponential of the unrounded quadratic value in this selector. Put $\Lambda=P$ in arithmetic coordinates. In the inner finite Fourier sum for the indicator, only the zero class of $(\Lambda\cap A(c))/(\Lambda\cap A(c)\cap B(c))$ contributes. Its representative belongs to $B(c)$ so its pairing with every $y\in B(c)$ is integral. The two inverse square roots of the local volume cancel the volume in the numerator exactly. What remains is
$$
 \operatorname{thInd}(c,x)=
 \frac{1}{[(\Lambda\cap A(c)):(\Lambda\cap A(c)\cap B(c))]}
 W_{-t}\sum_{y\in B(c)/(B(c)\cap\Lambda)}W_y e_{Y,Y'} .
$$
All groups are commensurate full lattices, so the positive index and the quotient are finite. The sum is fixed by $W_b$ for $b\in B(c)$ by permuting cosets. The local volume is a product of ratios of finite positive lattice indices. These observations justify both cancellations even if the integer readouts have wildly different sizes. Let $s,t$ be any two permitted lifts of the same $x$. Then $h=t-s$ belongs to $B(c)$ by comparing the local conditions just listed. To identify $W_{-t}W_h$ with $W_{-s}$ one also needs $j(t,h)\in\mathbb Z$. Locally at a selected $\mathfrak p$, let $k$ be the least nonnegative exponent required to make $t_{\mathfrak p}$ integral in $P_{\mathfrak p}$. Congruence modulo $cP_{\mathfrak p}$ gives the same $k$ for $s_{\mathfrak p}$. The denominators $d_t,d_s$ in their lift tests must therefore each have valuation at least $k$ at $\mathfrak p$. They are rational integers and have the same valuations at the conjugate prime. Both $t_{\bar{\mathfrak p}}$ and $s_{\bar{\mathfrak p}}$ consequently lie at depth at least $k$ in $P_{\bar{\mathfrak p}}$. This is enough to pair $t_{\mathfrak p}$ integrally with $h_{\bar{\mathfrak p}}$. In the other order $t_{\bar{\mathfrak p}}$ and $h_{\mathfrak p}$ are both integral. Away from the selected pairs both lifts are integral. Multiplying by $\omega\in\mathcal O_K$ preserves the bounds. The trace duality of the period lattice now gives $j(t,h)\in\mathbb Z$ at every rational prime, hence in $\mathbb Z$. Thus the chosen lift and any transported lift give exactly the same function. [The two-lift comparison](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/ThetaMovedCosetIndicatorVacuumShape.lean#L241-L282)

Here is an exact failure if the conjugate condition is dropped. In $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, put $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-7})/2$, $\pi=1+2\omega$ of norm $11$, $S=\{(\pi)\}$ and $c=1$. Take $t=\bar\pi/\pi=(1-8\omega)/11$ and $s=t+\pi$. Both give the same coset at $(\pi)$. With period lattice $\mathcal O_K$, $e(a+b\omega,c+d\omega)=bc-ad$, so
$$
 e(\omega t,\pi)=-39/11.
$$
The two readouts at the selected prime hide a nontrivial eleventh root of unity in the translation law. The lift $t$ has depths $(-1,+1)$ at the selected pair, whereas $s$ has depths $(-1,0)$. A single rational integer denominator in $A(d_s)$ cannot admit $s$. This is a counterexample to the one-prime test and a direct check of its exclusion by the actual definition. ## A finite field for one family

For $Y\mapsto gY$, $Y'\mapsto g'Y'$, conjugation of a linear map $x\mapsto g'xg^{-1}$ takes $P,H_S,A(c),B(c)$ and the lift congruence to their counterparts. Indices are invariant under that bijection. The rational form $j$ and all translation factors are invariant under the corresponding unitary change of arguments. We can therefore use the transported representatives in the finite sums, and the preceding two-lift calculation permits the transported $t$. The [phase of the normalized vacuum](2026-11-23-phase-around-three-lattices.md) gives the exact equivariance of the indicator function for the same moves. [Source and target indicator](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/GmapToAddSubgroup.lean#L256-L285) · [Covariance used for the jets](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/FinAlgJetSet.lean#L80-L105)

For fixed $Y,Y'$ and $D$, the quotient $D^{-1}H_S/cH_S$ is finite for each $c\mid D$. Indeed the local integrality conditions identify it with a subgroup of the product of the finite quotients $D^{-1}\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{\mathfrak p},Y'_{\mathfrak p})/c\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{\mathfrak p},Y'_{\mathfrak p})$ at the selected primes. The local congruences are exactly its kernel. On each class the admissible lift predicate is unchanged, so there are finitely many functions. Every one is a finite linear combination of torsion translates with algebraic coefficients. To see this all the way back to elliptic functions, expand the two moves defining the vacuum, then group its even rank product into blocks of four. The rational isometry in the fourfold formula sends each rational translating vector into period-rational elliptic coordinates. The translation product contributes roots of unity because $j$ is rational there. The [torsion divisor calculation](2026-11-23-cm-theta-torsion-trace.md) supplies an algebraic Taylor expansion of each receiving elliptic section. This proves algebraicity for all derivatives here. The coordinate scale factors $c_D$ are algebraic as well. The coordinate-axis ideals cut out of the full span of the CM embedding vectors are nonzero fractional ideals over the embedded number field. Fullness follows from the invertible embedding matrix of a field basis, and clearing its denominators gives a nonzero multiple of each axis. If $I^h=(a)$ for the class number $h$, adjoining a root of $X^h-a$ makes $I$ principal because the group of fractional ideals is torsion-free. Do this for finitely many coordinates. Intersecting the module with a coordinate axis commutes with extension to the larger integer ring by flatness over a Dedekind domain. This produces a finite field for the scales. Every choice in the released generator predicate is already an element of a number field. It remains to control infinitely many possible $Y,Y'$. A positive self-dual Hermitian $\mathcal O_E$-lattice of fixed rank $r$ gives a positive integral $\mathbb Z$-lattice by the absolute trace form. Its trace dual is its multiple by the inverse different, so its Gram determinant is $|\operatorname{disc} E|^r$ in positive trace normalization. There are finitely many such Euclidean lattices up to isometry. For example their minimum squared length is at least one, their covolume is fixed, and Minkowski's second theorem bounds all successive minima. Independent vectors at these bounded lengths give finitely many integral Gram matrices and bounded-index overlattices. Each fixed integral generator of $\mathcal O_E$ acts with operator norm bounded by the largest absolute value of its conjugates, so there are finitely many choices for its integral matrix on any one such lattice. A trace isometry respecting these generators is $E$-linear on the rational span and preserves the full Hermitian pairing by trace nondegeneracy. There are therefore finitely many rational unitary orbits. At the finitely many representative pairs take the finitely many cosets, divisors of $D$, and monomials up to the prescribed total degree. Adjoin their algebraic derivatives, the scales, the host embeddings and the values of the fixed determinant character to one finite extension. For a transported pair the multivariate chain rule expresses each derivative as a polynomial in entries of $g,g'$, their inverses, and the same-degree representative derivatives. The entries are in the embedded host field. The determinant character has values in a fixed finite extension by the [principal-ideal calculation](2026-11-23-phase-of-a-moved-theta-row.md). This gives one finite field containing the entire requested jet set, including the normalized coefficients. The source's `FinAlg` filter therefore admits these actual families. The calculation does not assert a degree bound as the modulus or the host varies. The distinction between “some finite field” and the chosen coefficient field matters. The construction explicitly demands that the latter contain the finite generators. Its second list can depend on the *set of twists*. For fixed underlying arithmetic data, frame and twist characters, these are finite lists of algebraic numbers. Take the normal closure of their compositum with the previous coefficient field inside $\mathbb C$. Embed the old distinguished subfields by inclusion. One can retain the same twist at every new place by pulling it back along restriction of places; each old place has an extension and the residue characteristic is unchanged. In the displayed existence construction the twist is chosen as a function of the **rational** residue prime, trivial outside a finite set, so its range is identical after enlargement. Thus this particular field-adjunction step has an explicit order and can be performed. It still leaves substantive arithmetic premises on which twists and initial host data exist. ## Unresolved

A field containing the normalized coefficients gives no numerical bound on their denominators or on the sharp norm of the resulting jet operator. Also, conjugation on a normal coefficient field need only agree with ordinary complex conjugation at its distinguished embedding. The [height-field test](2026-11-23-conjugation-in-the-height-field.md) checks the latter issue where the actual matrix norm is read. The specified high-derivative estimate is a distinct remaining quantitative target.
