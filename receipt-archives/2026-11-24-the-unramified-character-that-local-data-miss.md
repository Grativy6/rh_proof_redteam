# An unramified character that matches every local reading at 577

**24 November 2026.** On the degree-577 affine field behind the live input, there are at least 577 distinct scalar ray characters with the same prescribed local restrictions at **all nine places** above 577. They give the same discriminant for the associated degree-332929 fields. On a specified translate of a projected Frobenius above **3360449**, the genuine quartic character gives **0**, and an explicitly normalized competitor gives **457 modulo 577**. Four global evaluations isolate the genuine character. This is a counterexample to recovering the quartic from those local data alone. The published frame uses an abelian auxiliary field and tests its global cocycles separately. [Comparison with the source](#does-the-published-frame-make-this-substitution)

## What the nine places observe

Use $p=577$, $k=\mathbb F_p$, $M=\mathbb Q^{\ker z}$, $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, $M_0=MK$, and $F=L_yK$. The lower cocycle gives the [actual affine action](2026-11-24-the-affine-resolvent-and-its-class-groups.md) with translation group $P=\langle\sigma\rangle=C_p$ and multiplier $z^{-1}$. Fix the translation $\sigma$ by $y(\sigma)=1$. The complement $C=\Delta\times\langle c\rangle$, $\Delta=C_{576}$, fixes the origin, and $c$ is complex conjugation. Write $E=F^C$, the real degree-$p$ point field. Choose the origin at the fixed point of the local decomposition group at 577. This is possible since $y$ is locally a coboundary. Translation by one is unchanged by that choice of origin.

In the [nonsplit tangent problem](2026-11-24-the-obstruction-at-the-nonsplit-point.md), an odd cocycle takes values in $\eta z^{-2}k[T]_{\le4}$ on this affine line. Restriction to $G_E$ makes the residual action diagonal. Its coefficient of $T^j$ is a scalar cocycle of character $\eta z^{j-2}$, $0\le j\le4$. For $j=0$ this is the scalar in the Shapiro construction. Restriction further to $G_F$ gives additive characters. Restriction from $E$ to $F$ preserves their cohomology classes because $[F:E]=1152$ is prime to $p$.

At $p$, the multiplier has order 72. Its orbits on the affine line are the origin and eight nonzero orbits with representatives $t=5^i\bmod577$ for $0\le i<8$. These are exactly the nine local factors of $E$. ## Every complement weight occurs in the upper minus class group

Put $A=\mathrm{Cl}(F)_p^-$ and $B=\mathrm{Cl}(M_0)_p^-$. The lower [Bernoulli census and four ideal classes](2026-11-24-the-affine-resolvent-and-its-class-groups.md#what-changes-on-adjoining-the-imaginary-quadratic-field) give $B=k^4$, one copy in each weight $\eta z^a$ for
$$a=276,\ 336,\ 407,\ 447.$$
The extension $F/M_0$ is cyclic unramified of degree $p$. Both fields are CM and $\mu_p$ is absent from $F$. Indeed its abelian subfields over $\mathbb Q$ lie in $M_0$. The character $\omega$ is odd but unramified at 7, so it cannot be a product of the even $z$ and $\eta$ in that abelian quotient.

Extension of ideals $j:B\to A^P$ is an isomorphism. Every invariant fractional ideal is extended since the ramification indices are one. For global units $u$, $u/\bar u$ is a root of unity. Their $p$-completed minus part vanishes. Applying cohomology to units, nonzero field elements and principal ideals, and using Hilbert 90, gives both descent of invariant principal ideals on the minus part and zero minus $H^1$ for the principal ideals. This proves the claim for ideal classes. The norm onto $B$ is surjective. In the Hilbert class field of $M_0$, any minus class fixes $F$ because $P$ is an even quotient. It has a representative prime split in $F$, which is a norm. Hence $N_P A=jN(A)=A^P$. For finite $A$, its $P$-invariants and coinvariants have equal order, giving $N:A_P\simeq B$. [Class field theory](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf) · [Lemmermeyer, Theorem 5 on minus capitulation](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa84/aa8415.pdf)

In $\mathbb Z_p[P]$ put
$$D=\frac1{576}\sum_{s=1}^{576}[s]^{-1}(\sigma^s-1).$$
It has complement character $z^{-1}$. Dividing by $\sigma-1$ leaves an element of augmentation $1\bmod p$, a unit of this local group ring. Thus $\ker D=A^P$ and $\operatorname{coker}D=A_P$. Write $A_b$ for the integral $\eta z^b$ part under the prime-to-$p$ complement. The map $D:A_b\to A_{b-1}$ has kernel of order $p$ exactly at the four listed source weights, and cokernel of order $p$ exactly when its target is a listed weight. Consequently
$$\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_p}A_b=m+\mathbf 1_{\{276,336,407,447\}}(b)$$
for a single integer $m$. If $m=0$, then $|A|=|A_P|=p^4$ would force trivial $P$-action and $A=k^4$. Its group norm would be zero, contradicting $N_P A=A^P\ne0$. Thus $m\ge1$. In particular **the upper minus $p$-rank is at least 576 and its order is $p^{4+576m}\ge p^{580}$**.

The weight-zero minus part is the $p$-class group of $EK$ by prime-to-$p$ descent. Its order is $p^m$ since $E$ has $p$-class number one. Multiplication by $D$ twice identifies this group with $A_{-2}$. In the scalar coefficient field $E\,\mathbb Q^{\ker(\eta z^{-2})}$, the 144 odd complement characters are $\eta z^{-2j}$ for odd $j\bmod288$. None is one of the four exceptional weights. The entire $p$-class number of this field is $p^{144m}$, since its real subfield embeds in $L_y$ with prime-to-$p$ index. The exact value of $m$ remains unknown.

The original even class changes in the opposite direction under this same restriction. Its cocycle $y$ vanishes on the stabilizer of its origin. In fact $S_E(z^{-1})=0$ because over its coefficient field $L_y$ a strict class would be an everywhere unramified character, and the $p$-class group there is trivial. Restriction from $E$ to $L_y$ is injective by its prime-to-$p$ degree. The five scalar odd strict groups over $E$ all have the same positive dimension $r=\dim_k(\mathrm{Cl}(EK)_p/p\mathrm{Cl}(EK)_p)$, while all five downstairs groups are zero. Indeed $D$ gives isomorphisms between consecutive nonexceptional weight parts from $2$ to $-2$. Prime-to-$p$ descent identifies their unramified characters over $E$, which are locally zero at the nine places above $p$. The free cover below gives $1\le r\le4$. This is an actual loss of the original order-$p$ class and a gain of new scalar alternatives in the very same field change.

## Isolate an excess character of degree 278

Let $V=A/pA$ and $X=\log(\sigma)$ in $k[P]=k[X]/(X^{577})$. Both logarithm and exponential truncate at degree 576, with unit denominators. The reduction of $D$ is $X$. Choose four integral generators $e_a$ of definite weights lifting a basis of $A_P$. They give a surjection onto $A$ from a free $\mathbb Z_p[P]$-module with one generator at each weight. Its kernel is a lattice of the same integral rank. Since $H_1(P,A)=A^P/N_P A=0$, the coinvariants of this kernel inject into those of the free module, with image $p\mathbb Z_p^4$. Lift the four indicated eigenvectors back to the kernel using the complement idempotents. Nakayama gives a surjection from four free copies to the kernel, and equality of integral ranks makes it an isomorphism. We therefore have four relations, of the same four weights, whose constant terms vanish modulo $p$.

For $b=-2\bmod576$, the $b$ part of the free cover of $V$ has basis $X^{d_a}e_a$ with
$$d_a=(a-b)\bmod576=278,\ 338,\ 409,\ 449.$$
In this weight each relation must itself be multiplied by $X^{d_a}$ before contributing. Every resulting term has degree at least 279, because each relation has positive augmentation degree. Therefore $X^{278}e_{276}$ survives in the weight-$b$ quotient by $X^{279}$. It spans that quotient. The same argument in each gap between consecutive generator weights gives minimum chain lengths **405, 60, 71, 40**. [Finite weight and relation checks](boundary-577/unramified_layers.py)

This identifies a particular unramified functional $\lambda$ of weight $\eta z^{-2}$. On the one-dimensional quotient just obtained, define $\lambda(X^{278}e)$ to be the lower unramified character of weight $\eta z^{276}$ evaluated at the norm of $e$. Set it to zero on other weights and on $X^{279}V$. This definition is independent of the lift of $e$. Via class field theory and prime-to-$p$ descent it gives an everywhere unramified scalar class over $E$. Its restriction is locally zero at every place over $p$ by the local stabilizers computed above.

## Four actual global tests

For each of the four lower weights form the circular-unit Kummer character in the [original compatible Frobenius convention](2026-11-24-three-deformation-directions-at-577.md#representatives-for-the-upper-directions). Its leading local digit at $577$ is zero in all four cases. There is a second check from the primitive Bernoulli values. In the defining first Bernoulli sum write the input as $h+577d$, $1\le h<577$. The term proportional to $h$ sums to zero over $d$ by the nontrivial tame character. Dividing the rest by the conductor gives exactly $n!$ times the circular-unit digit modulo 577, with $n=1-296a\bmod576$. The four Bernoulli values were already measured to have valuation one. The radicand is a global unit and the prime-to-$p$ tame projection selects a single positive degree below 576. To see that this suffices for local splitting, pass to the fixed field of the radicand character, whose inertia order divides 576. The four relevant odd inertia exponents $1-296a$ are respectively $97,193,489,169$ modulo 576. For ramification index less than 576 the logarithm gives the local power criterion directly. For index 576, the full cyclotomic comparison has only one additional exceptional degree for a unit modulo $p$th powers, namely 577, of inertia character $\omega$. Neither that degree nor the degree 576 trivial character matches these exponents. After the selected digit vanishes the class is a $p$th power. One can see the exceptional degree directly from $p/\pi^{576}=-1\bmod\pi$ and the Artin–Schreier computation in the [Hilbert-symbol receipt](2026-11-24-four-local-units-at-577.md#compute-the-first-order-numbers). All four normalized characters are therefore unramified class-field characters over $M_0$ after descent. Each is nonzero by the following finite-prime tests. Write $g_a$ for its complement projection of weight $\eta z^a$ in the elementary abelian ray quotient of $G_F$. This projection means a specified $k$-linear combination of conjugates, equivalently a product of integral powers of words in the abelian quotient. A genuine quartic cocycle restricted to $G_F$ has only the five complement weights $\eta z^{-2},\ldots,\eta z^2$. It vanishes on each $g_a$, and by equivariance also on $\sigma g_a\sigma^{-1}$. For an unramified scalar ambiguity $\lambda'$ of weight $\eta z^b$,
$$\lambda'(\sigma g_a\sigma^{-1})=\lambda'(X^{d_a}g_a)/d_a!.$$
This follows by expanding $\sigma=\exp X$ and selecting the unique matching weight. Those four vectors span $V_b$, so vanishing of the four readings kills the whole ambiguity. For coefficient $j$ of a quartic take $b=j-2$ and replace every displayed $d_a$ by $d_a-j$. The same four translated, projected words work for all five coefficients.

Thus the ramification conditions, the nine local prescriptions, and these **four global zeros** uniquely specify the two first-order Shapiro ray characters. Existence follows from the [relaxed odd localization isomorphism](2026-11-24-the-obstruction-at-the-nonsplit-point.md#where-the-global-obstruction-goes). In the comparison of two putative characters, the difference is unramified everywhere over $F$, so the preceding argument on $V$ gives uniqueness. This singles out the characters without requiring an exact upper class number.

For the particular extra $\lambda$ fixed above,
$$\lambda(\sigma^t g_{276}\sigma^{-t})=346\,t^{278}/278!=534\,t^{278}\pmod{577}.$$
The second split prime gives $275\,t^{278}$ in the same normalization. There is also a test starting with an individual prime. Take $r=3360449=13\cdot448\cdot577+1$, with least primitive root 3. It splits in the conductor of the weight-276 circular unit and gives raw exponent **389**. Here $z(r)=24$ has order four and $\eta(r)=1$. The Frobenius has linear part different from one in the affine group, so its fourth power has zero translation by the geometric sum. Every prime above the indicated place in $M_0$ therefore splits in $F/M_0$. Choose such a prime in $F$ and project its Frobenius class to weight $\eta z^{276}$ using the same complement. The lower character on its norm is $4\cdot389=402\bmod577$. On its translate by $\sigma$, the extra character reads
$$402/278!=457\pmod{577},$$
whereas the genuine polynomial character reads zero. On this prime the competing character contributes the polynomial $457T^{278}$. Its constant through fourth coefficients at zero all vanish. After translating the same input by one, those five coefficients are **457, 106, 256, 472, 138**, by the binomial theorem. Thus truncating at four and translating give different answers in the two possible orders on the actual class. ## Even the discriminants agree

Adding any multiple of $\lambda$ to either genuine scalar character preserves all nine local restrictions. A nonzero multiple changes its global value at the tested Frobenius. These at least 577 choices give distinct cyclic extensions over the scalar coefficient field. Proportionality between two characters defining the same kernel would have scalar one by their common nonzero local reading. Their affine degree-$p$ point extensions over the marked field $E$ also have distinct normal closures.

The resulting degree-$p^2$ fields over $\mathbb Q$ have discriminant
$$2^{2324709}\,7^{166176}\,577^h,\qquad h=503400\text{ for the first tangent},\quad h=342120\text{ for the second}.$$
At 2 the original point field has discriminant exponent 4023. Its unique inertia-fixed sheet has scalar inertia order 32. The additional contribution is $18\sum_{i=1}^{5}(i+2)2^{i-1}=3438$. On all other sheets the old inertia stabilizer is trivial. At 7 inertia is reflection on each fiber, contributing $577\cdot288$. At 577 the original exponent 568 is multiplied by 577. The origin contributes 16 tame degree-36 orbits. At each of the eight other places, passing through the unramified quadratic coefficient extension gives a cyclic totally ramified extension of degree 577 with single upper break $d=37$ or $2$. The contribution there is $576(d+1)$. The discriminant is positive because the number of complex pairs is $577\cdot288$. These counts depend only on the inertia and the measured local readings. Also the source's strict-class condition quantifies over every decomposition place above the coefficient prime.
