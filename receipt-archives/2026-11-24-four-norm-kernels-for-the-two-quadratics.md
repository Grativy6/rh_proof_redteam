# Four global norm kernels decide the two quadratic forms

**24 November 2026.** On the fixed conductor-256 input, one can decide whether an ordinary trace cube survives by testing **four first-order abelian fields** on the same two-dimensional quotient of real $S$-units. The fields correspond to slopes $1,2,3,4$. Each required extension has degree $577^4$ over the residual splitting field. This test avoids constructing its much larger second-order extension and also detects common roots defined only over $\mathbb F_{577^2}$. We have determined the two independent local measurement rows on which to do the test. We do not yet have the four norm kernels.

Let $\mathcal U$ denote the real $S$-units of $L_y$ modulo $p$th powers. For each of the three even coordinates define a local sum $\Lambda_-,\Lambda_0,\Lambda_+:\mathcal U\to E_{\mathrm{Lie}}$. At our chosen place, extract respectively the three unit characters of tame type $z^{-1},1,z$, quadratic-involution sign plus. Normalize each to one on its own unit and zero on the other characters. The units are $u_-=\exp(Y^{-1}\pi^{280})$, $u_0=1+p$, $u_+=\exp(Y\pi^{296})$. Their depths in $F_v$ are 35, 72, 37. The symbols $Y,\pi$ and the local Artin convention are fixed in the [unit calculation](2026-11-24-four-local-units-at-577.md). The identity $Y^p/Y=553\bmod p$ and $\psi(p)=553$ give the indicated character of $u_+$. Each character occurs once in the indicated local unit quotient by the logarithm and the distinct tame powers. Attach $A_0,A_1,A_2$ to the respective readings. At any other place over $p$ transport the input and the output by the same Galois element, then sum over places of $F$. The local maps are $D_p$-equivariant, so the result is independent of coset representatives. All three maps are $G_0$-equivariant.

Every element of $\mathcal U$ is a local norm from the mixed first-order field $B_s/F$ for $C=C_U+sC_V$. The local characters above $p$ all have odd sign while the units are real. Complex conjugation stabilizes each of those places, so the characters vanish there. Outside $p$ the extension is unramified. At the possible support over $r$, even the relaxed second-order field $N_s$ splits over $F$. Indeed Frobenius in the residual group over $M_0$ is a nonzero translation of order $p$. The first two are the homogeneous binary quadratics of the ordinary problem in a common choice of obstruction coordinates. One may choose the equivariant relaxed lift over the two-variable degree-two truncation first and then specialize to each slope.

## Why the two missing readings really are independent tests

The three $\Lambda$ maps are linearly independent on $\mathcal U$. Here is the class-field argument. A linear combination vanishing on real $S$-units extends to an equivariant, even abelian $k$-valued-vector character of $G_F$, unramified away from $p$ and split over $r$. Indeed the real field $L_y$ has $p$-class number one by the [class-group calculation](2026-11-24-the-affine-resolvent-and-its-class-groups.md#all-finite-layers-and-the-degree-577-field). The ray exact sequence with the $S$-primes killed identifies the unit relations with these $S$-unit relations. At $p$ the extension from the real field to $F$ is unramified quadratic. Norm on real units is squaring, so one can first divide the prescribed real local character by two downstairs and then restrict. Equivalently take the plus part of the ray sequence over $F$. The local characters with plus sign kill the minus units.

For descent from $F$ to $\mathbb Q$, the transgression of this invariant character is detected on the cyclic translation subgroup $P$ of order $p$ since the complement has order prime to $p$. For its generator lifted as a suitable power of Frobenius at $r$, the obstruction is its $p$th power evaluated by the character, modulo the group norm on $E_{\mathrm{Lie}}$. This norm operator is zero by the length-three translation action. The chosen splitting over $r$ makes the reading zero. Inflation–restriction now extends the character to a global class on $E_{\mathrm{Lie}}$. Away from $p$ any remaining inertia in the residual field has prime-to-$p$ order, so the class satisfies the relaxed local conditions. The [arithmetic calculation of relaxed even $H^1$](2026-11-24-the-obstruction-at-the-nonsplit-point.md#where-the-global-obstruction-goes) gives zero. Thus the original linear combination already vanishes on unrestricted local units, which by the three distinct unit lines makes all coefficients zero.

On the weight-$z^{-1}$ part of $\mathcal U$ keep the scalar coefficient on $A_0$ for each sum. These three scalar rows remain independent. The image on $\mathcal U$ of any equivariant linear combination is an affine-group submodule. A nonzero submodule of the translated quadratics contains the lowest polynomial $A_0$, by taking finite differences. The complement idempotent for $z^{-1}$ then supplies a preimage of that weight. In particular the subspace with $\Lambda_+=0$, modulo the common kernel of all three rows, is exactly **two dimensional**. Call it $V$.

For clarity, the extra prime supplies information that ordinary global units alone cannot give. On genuine global units there is just one relation among the three rows. Restricting invariant ray characters as above gives an injection into $H^2(G_0,E_{\mathrm{Lie}})$ by the same zero relaxed $H^1$. This obstruction space has dimension one. On $P$ it is the fixed line $kA_0$ modulo the zero norm, with the reciprocal translation-generator twist making its complement action trivial. The [actual integral relaxed lower lift](2026-11-24-the-cyclotomic-module-of-the-fixed-class.md) gives the nonzero example by restricting to $G_F$ and dividing its difference from the identity modulo $p^2$ by $p$. This character has nonzero value on a lift of the translation raised to $p$.

Its lower reading on $u_-$ is $94L$, or **389** on $A_0=-L/2$. Its top diagonal on $u_0$ is $-144\cdot413\bmod577$, or **495** on $A_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,-1)/2$. Here 413 is the full relaxed exponent modulo 577. The comparison with the prescribed weight in the earlier receipt subtracts 296 and answers a different question. $$389\Lambda_-+495\Lambda_0=0.$$

For a second coset take a prime of $L_y$ over the specified place at $r$, principalize a prime-to-$p$ power, divide by that power in the $k$-space, and project to weight $z^{-1}$. Divide also by two for the two places in $F$. ## Four fields and one determinant

For each $s=1,2,3,4$ take the mixed first-order field and its subfield
$$D_s=B_{C_U+sC_V}^{\,kB_4}.$$
It is an elementary abelian extension of $F$ of degree $p^4$. Equivalently retain the scalar coefficients of degrees zero through three on $G_F$. Their first-order characters have the [nine local prescriptions and four global normalizations](2026-11-24-the-unramified-character-that-local-data-miss.md#four-actual-global-tests). The [rank-four norm theorem](2026-11-24-which-norm-tests-see-the-cube.md#why-every-three-generator-quotient-misses-it) proves that a weight-$z^{-1}$ element of $\mathcal U$ is a global norm from $D_s$ exactly when the $A_0$ coordinate of $\Psi_{N_s}$ is zero. Therefore the global norm test on $V$ descends to the quotient and its kernel is precisely the kernel of $h_-(s)\Lambda_-+h_0(s)\Lambda_0$.

If a single one of these kernels is all of $V$, that slope supplies a cube. Otherwise choose a nonzero row $(x_s,y_s)$ annihilating each kernel in any one common basis of $V$. The rows are determined only projectively, which suffices. Form the four-by-four matrix with rows
$$(y_s,\ s y_s,\ -x_s,\ -s x_s).$$
**A cube exists if and only if this determinant vanishes.** Its kernel is a pair of linear polynomials whose projective ratio agrees with the sampled quadratic pair. Cross multiplication gives a polynomial of degree at most three with four distinct zeros, hence an identity, including the homogeneous value at infinity. If the two binary quadratics are coprime, no nonzero linear pair can satisfy it by divisibility. Conversely a common factor leaves a pair of degree at most one, which can be padded homogeneously in the constant case. A nontrivial linear change of the common basis of $V$ preserves having a common factor. This also detects a common irreducible quadratic over $k$, without running a separate nonrational-slope norm calculation. The [finite-jet theorem](2026-11-24-two-raw-traces-on-the-released-subgroup.md#a-numerical-milestone-for-the-next-obstruction) then gives the equivalent nonzero-cube test.

There is a genuine negative control on these four fields. Every real global unit from the abelian base $M$, including $1+\sqrt2$, has all three local sums zero. Indeed at $p$ the degree-$p$ affine extension splits, and on a base unit the sum over translates is the sum of a polynomial of degree at most two over $k$. That sum is zero. Here $\sqrt2\in M$ because $z^{72}$ cuts out the degree-eight real cyclotomic subfield of conductor 32. Such units are everywhere-local norms, and the Artin-product isomorphism proves that they are **global** norms from each of the four mixed fields $B_{C_U+sC_V}$ themselves. Testing arbitrarily many of those base units would give the same answer independently of the missing quadratics. The two independent rows above require the larger real field.

## Evaluate a rank-four test through a bicyclic preimage

There is a smaller field for *computing* the obstruction from the four-dimensional extension. In $D_s/F$ take $P_s$ cut out by the coefficients $\chi_0,\chi_1$ of the mixed tangent. Its group over $F$ has dimension two. On the four retained coordinates the central alternating form is
$$\Omega(e_0,e_3)=-1/8,\qquad\Omega(e_1,e_2)=1/16$$
and their alternating reversals, with all other basic pairs zero. The kernel $H=\langle e_2,e_3\rangle$ for $D_s/P_s$ is isotropic. At a nonzero affine parameter $t$, the determinant on $\chi_0,\chi_1$ of the two local generators is $35\cdot387\,s\,t^6\ne0$. All the ramified directions there have therefore already been absorbed by $P_s$, and $D_s/P_s$ splits at those places. At the origin $P_s$ splits and only $\chi_3$ can ramify further. Its local input is the pure cubic line. Thus $\chi_2$ over $P_s$ is an everywhere-unramified character, split also above $p$. All finite contributions away from $p$ below are ordinary ideal valuations and Frobenius readings.

For a test input $x\in\mathcal U$ as above, the Hasse norm theorem **for this specific bicyclic quotient** gives a global $b\in P_s^\times$ with $N_{P_s/F} b=x$. Here the justification is the full two-dimensional decomposition group at any nonzero $t$, or the all-small-quotients computation above. An element preimage suffices. At each place $v$ of $F$, choose an origin among the places of $P_s$ over it. Label the other places $w$ by translates $(i_w,j_w)$ in the first two coordinates modulo the local decomposition subgroup. Let $b_w$ be the local component. The actual $A_0$ obstruction is given by the finite sum
$$\sum_{v}\sum_{w\mid v}\left(\frac{i_w}{8}\,\chi_{3,w}(\operatorname{rec}b_w)
     -\frac{j_w}{16}\,\chi_{2,w}(\operatorname{rec}b_w)\right).$$
Use one common orientation on the four global first-order characters. This expression has no unknown second-order character. It uses the same first-order ray characters previously specified by their local data and four global zeros.

To prove it, use the central quotient with pairing $\Omega$ from the rank-four argument. Over $P_s$ its Galois group is abelian because the inverse image of $H$ is abelian. The product of the ordinary Artin symbols of the **same global $b$** over all places of $P_s$ is therefore one. In the local Artin symbol for $x$ over $F$, all symbols at $w\mid v$ must instead be carried back to the chosen place over $v$ before multiplication. Conjugation back by a lift of $(i_w,j_w)$ changes a symbol of projection $h$ by the central scalar $-\Omega((i_w,j_w),h)$. Its two terms give precisely the displayed sum. The abelian local group over $F_v$ makes this independent of the lift and of the representative of the place. Changing the origin also leaves the sum at $v$ unchanged since $x$ is already a local norm from $D_s$. Global reciprocity proves the value is independent of the global norm preimage. In particular a preimage which is a local norm from $D_s/P_s$ everywhere suffices. For a separately chosen preimage the sum computes the actual discrepancy.

This procedure uses a global bicyclic norm solve and local compatibility between its conjugate preimages. It explains how a degree-$p^4$ failure can be recovered through a smaller field even though every smaller norm test taken on $x$ alone succeeds.
