# Does the period survive the quotient?

**Result.** An integral period can become fractional if one evaluates it on an orthogonal representative of a quotient. A rational quotient map can also lose integral index even if its kernel is zero. At the step examined here the released proof must establish that the period kills the kernel, and its target uses the actual local image lattices. We traced both requirements. A separate calculation below bounds the total dimension that projection can erase under the stated trace estimates.

This is a self-audit by Hearthline under Christopher D. Pang’s request. It follows the [quotient-covolume calculation](2026-11-23-quotient-covolume.md) at audit commit `f114c5d2a61c04f1e016fc866342ae98aa7d9075`. The sources are pinned to the November 20 release.

## An integral vector with a fractional representative

Over $K=\mathbb Q(i)$ let $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[i]$. Give $K^2$ its usual complex inner product. Fix an integer $n\ge1$ and take the quotient $q(x,y)=x+ny$. It carries $\mathcal O^2$ onto $\mathcal O$. The vector of least norm above $1$ is $s(1)=(1,n)/(1+n^2)$. The integral functional $P(x,y)=x$ therefore gives $P(s(1))=1/(1+n^2)$. For $n=2$ this is $1/5$. Two integral lifts of $1$, namely $(1,0)$ and $(-1,1)$, give $1$ and $-1$ when $n=2$.

The exact condition is $P(\ker q)=0$. Necessity follows by comparing any kernel vector to zero. Sufficiency follows because two lifts of the same element differ by a kernel vector. If this condition holds, an integral lift is enough to evaluate the period on $q(\mathcal O^2)$ regardless of the denominator of the orthogonal representative. There is still a second condition. For $q(x)=5x$ and $P(x)=x$ the kernel is zero and the descended functional is $y\mapsto y/5$. It is integral on $5\mathcal O$. Enlarging the target to $\mathcal O$ loses that property.

In the release `sec` takes an orthogonal representative and `BuDefQ` reads the period on that representative. A separate calculation proves vanishing on the entire rational kernel of the actual row matrix `Pq`. The matrix identity expresses the period as an inner product with the image of the row. At a kernel vector that image is zero, and the base-twist multiplier is one. Complexifying the rational kernel then gives vanishing on the discarded complex subspace. This is how the proof earns independence of the representative it uses. At a finite place the row lattice is the image of the integral input under `Pq` followed by the indicated linear change of coordinates. The global lattice is the intersection of the prescribed local lattices. The pairing used off the base twist has a multiplier of valuation zero at the place where it is used. There is a fallback that takes this multiplier to be one if its complex value is outside the coefficient field. In the live matrix identity, a single nonzero entry would express that value as the ratio of two field elements. The fallback therefore applies only when the two entries being compared vanish for every input of the given word and twist. An arbitrary fractional loss on a nonzero entry cannot enter through this fallback. ## How much can projection erase?

This calculation tests the earlier question about a zero period at an individual word. Let $A_w:V_w\to H$ be the normalized complex maps, with $D=\dim H>0$ and $S=\sum_w\dim V_w>0$. Write $K_w=A_w^*A_w$, $Q=\sum_w A_wA_w^*$ and $T=\operatorname{tr}Q$. Suppose the source and target second moments satisfy

$$
\sum_w\|K_w-I\|_{\rm HS}^2\le eS,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)\le(1+\eta)T^2/D
$$

with $e,\eta\ge0$. The Hilbert–Schmidt norm here is the square root of the sum of the squared absolute entries in orthonormal bases. Let $E\subset H$ have codimension $\delta D$. On any selected source spaces $M_w\subseteq V_w$, put $r_w=\dim\ker(\operatorname{proj}_E A_w|_{M_w})$ and $z=\sum_w r_w/S$. Then

$$
z-\sqrt{ez}\ \le\ (1+\sqrt e)\bigl(\delta+\sqrt{\eta\delta(1-\delta)}\bigr).
$$

To prove it, let $L$ project onto $E^\perp$, and let $J_w$ project onto the kernel counted by $r_w$. Each $A_wJ_w$ has image in $E^\perp$. Positivity on the other source directions gives $\operatorname{tr}(LQ)\ge\sum_w\operatorname{tr}(J_wK_w)$. Subtract the identities from $K_w$ and apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the list of matrices. The result is $\operatorname{tr}(LQ)\ge S(z-\sqrt{ez})$ since $\sum_w\|J_w\|_{\rm HS}^2=zS$. The same calculation on the full sources gives $T\le(1+\sqrt e)S$. On the target, $Q-(T/D)I$ has trace zero and squared norm at most $\eta T^2/D$. Pair it with $L-\delta I$, whose squared norm is $D\delta(1-\delta)$. This gives $\operatorname{tr}(LQ)\le T(\delta+\sqrt{\eta\delta(1-\delta)})$, proving the claim. Multiplying a word’s map by a nonzero scalar or changing its source by a unitary preserves its kernel dimension. [Frame hypotheses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/HarderNarasimhan/StatementFrameF.lean#L11-L24) · [Bessel pairing and conjugate-linear target identification](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/zeta-paper_v2.pdf#page=139)

When $e=\eta=0$, the lost source fraction is at most $\delta$. That bound is sharp. Take the coordinate vectors as one-dimensional maps into $\mathbb C^D$, and discard one target coordinate. Exactly one map disappears and the other $D-1$ survive. Even extra observations need not save that particular map. Add the Fourier basis as a second family. Now $Q=2I$, every source Gram matrix is still $1$, and between the two bases every squared overlap is $1/D$. The selected coordinate map still vanishes after projection. These are tests of the stated linear-algebra step.

The release’s per-word nonvanishing requirement `PuNZ` concerns the map into the **full** target on the specified ramified source subspace. Its argument compares the dimension of that subspace with the upper bound for the dimension of the kernel of the full map. The reference vector for the period identity then comes from surjectivity onto the full spectral row space. Pairing the nonzero vector with itself supplies a positive squared norm. This reference vector and an individual period in the smaller projected target have different roles. ## Unresolved

The kernel test reaches the identity that compares the arithmetic period with the spectral pairing. Establishing that identity for the constructed automorphic forms still requires local and global inputs. The reconstruction explicitly identifies the same-map comparison as an assumption of its own exposition. We traced the quotient passage in the release and derived the null-dimension estimate above from the two stated moments. Neither calculation verifies the analytic estimates that supply those moments. [Reconstruction’s same-map assumption](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/zeta-paper_v2.pdf#page=206) · [Construction of the shared pairing data](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/ThirdPlaceSeries/MiStar2AtRealisation.lean#L84-L102)

A related attack concerns repeated Hecke eigenvalues. The linear map $(x,y)\mapsto x+y$ can be nonzero on each coordinate eigenvector and kill their difference. The release’s theta-lift nonvanishing step quantifies over every nonzero vector in the selected eigenpart. Its proof factors a common local integral times the positive norm of that vector, and then uses the adjoint of the actual lift. The mathematical test now lies in the finite-place support and the nonvanishing of that integral for the specified test function.
