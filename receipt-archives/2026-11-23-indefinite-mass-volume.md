# One arithmetic example separates zero mass, unit fallback and quotient volume

**23 November 2026.** Over $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3,i)$ with real subfield $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$, take $O_E^3$ with Hermitian matrix $J=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)$. On this lattice the release's **lattice mass is zero**. With an inconsistent signature in the input record its **`trueVol` value is one**. With the correct signature and the release's normalization, its **adelic quotient volume is $1/10368$**. We obtained the last number by an external Tamagawa-number comparison with the definite group. These three calculations test which value the argument is entitled to carry across a change of signature. ## Why the indefinite mass is exactly zero

Let $e=(1,0,1)^t$ and $N=ee^*J$. Then $N^2=0$, $N\ne0$, and $N$ is self-adjoint for $J$. For every integer $t$, the matrix $I+itN$ is a rational isometry with inverse $I-itN$. Its powers give all integer multiples of $t$. Taking $t$ divisible by $13\cdot37$ makes the matrix equal to the identity modulo both auxiliary levels in the [split class example](2026-11-23-split-class-count.md).

Every full lattice $B$ in this rational space has infinite automorphism group. Choose an integer $d>0$ with $d O_E^3\subseteq B\subseteq d^{-1}O_E^3$ by clearing denominators in both directions. Taking $d^2\mid t$ gives $itN B\subseteq B$, as well as the inverse inclusion needed for $I+itN$ to preserve $B$. Thus the formula using `Nat.card` of the automorphism group returns zero in every summand, even when there are finite class representatives. If no finite representatives exist the mass definition also returns zero. The release's strict mass positivity theorem requires positivity of the trace of $h(x,x)$ for every nonzero $x$. Here $e$ is an explicit isotropic vector. ## Why one can be written into the raw volume record

The `HSpace` record contains a signature function as data alongside a basis, form and self-dual lattice. `SigOK` separately checks the signature against the Gram matrix. Keep all the data above and enter $(3,0)$ at every embedding. This is a well-formed raw record. The volume-measure predicate requires a frame satisfying $A^*I_3 A=J$. Its left side is positive semidefinite and $J$ has a negative direction. The frame set is empty. `AllSome` includes existence, so the putative measure cannot meet the predicate and the definition returns exactly one. The theorem asserting that the raw value is positive holds on this input. ## Measuring the correctly labelled example

Let $G_-=U(J)$ and $G_+=U(I_3)$ over $F$. At every finite place normalize the stabilizer of the standard lattice to have measure one. At both real places use the release's `CDHaar` normalization, which gives the compact group probability mass one and ties the density at the identity of $U(2,1)$ to that of $U(3)$. These choices realize the product normalization in the released volume definition. The norm-one center has no split $F$-character, so the quotient in the Tamagawa number is the full adelic quotient. [Identity density and compact calibration](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataCD.lean#L42-L61) · [The product measure on the arithmetic quotient](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/StandardLattice.lean#L241-L263)

At a finite place of $F$, the algebra $E\otimes_F F_v$ is split or unramified quadratic. The unit norm map is surjective in either case. In the unramified case, first take a norm in the residue field, then lift successively using the surjective residue trace. This includes residue characteristic two. Choose a local integral unit $b_v$ of norm $-1$. Then $S_v=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,b_v)$ identifies the two Hermitian forms and their lattice stabilizers.

For the measure comparison choose a rational invariant top differential on $G_+$ and transport its value at the identity to $G_-$ by the $F$-linear map $X\mapsto JX$ on the Lie algebras. At a finite place both this map and conjugation by $S_v$ take the lattice of integral Lie algebra matrices isomorphically to the corresponding one for $G_+$. Their determinants differ by a local unit. The measures from the two rational differentials therefore assign the same volume to the two compact stabilizers.

At an infinite place the same map preserves the Frobenius norm. More explicitly, write a Lie algebra element using its imaginary diagonal entries and the independent complex upper triangular entries. For a pair of Lie algebra elements $A,B$, the map $(A,B)\mapsto A+iB$ has real Jacobian one on each diagonal pair and four on each off-diagonal pair. The signs of $J$ do not affect the absolute Jacobian. Hence the additive measure `IsOmegaLeb` in the source is exactly Frobenius Lebesgue measure in either signature. The exponential has derivative the identity at zero. Equality of the infinitesimal Haar densities in `CDHaar` gives the same factor between the chosen Haar measure and the rational differential for both groups. In rank three that density relative to Frobenius volume is $1/(32\pi^6)$. Projecting the compact group onto its first unit column gives the recurrence $\operatorname{vol} U(n)=\operatorname{vol} U(n-1)\,2^{n-1}\operatorname{vol} S^{2n-1}$ for this metric. Of the tangent directions on the sphere, the $2n-2$ real directions off the chosen complex line have length scale $\sqrt2$.

Finally $G_+$ and $G_-$ are inner forms of the same connected reductive group. Kottwitz's invariance of the Tamagawa number under inner twisting applies. The character modules governing the convergence factors are identical, since the algebraic characters over an algebraic closure are generated by the determinant with the same Galois action. With the finite and infinite factors matched above, the two quotients have equal volume in the release's normalization. For $G_+$ the quotient volume is the [rank-three genus mass](2026-11-23-hermitian-mass.md), $1/10368$. There is one finite lattice class, the archimedean factor is compact with probability measure, and its finite stabilizer has order 10368. This proves the quoted value for $G_-$ as well. The comparison uses Kottwitz's theorem and the cataloged rank-three class number as mathematical inputs. [Kottwitz, *Tamagawa numbers*, Annals of Mathematics 127 (1988)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1988/127-3/p05) · [Zhang, unitary Tamagawa-measure normalization, p. 542 n. 1](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/271/8715-jams784_off_print.pdf)

## Unresolved

A positive fallback on a raw record can conceal the absence of any compatible frame. The indefinite lattice's raw mass can also vanish while its arithmetic quotient has positive volume. On the example above the signature check, finite lattice stabilizers used for the definite comparison, and the normalization of Haar measure distinguish the values. What remains for the broader argument is to test its measured comparison when the finite lattice or the rational embedding changes, including whether a determinant alone pays for the induced integral change.
