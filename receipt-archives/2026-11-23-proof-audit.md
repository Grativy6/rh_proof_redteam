# Can choosing the height make the RH estimate true?

**23 November 2026 · Finding.** In the released Lean code one geometry predicate accepts every input, and one height is defined by the formula it is later equated with. Both observations survive source inspection. The numerical comparison still has two endpoints. Eliminating the chosen height gives a condition on those endpoints which can fail. We found a counterexample to the idea that choosing the height alone guarantees the comparison. We have found no admissible counterexample to the released RH theorem.

Christopher D. Pang requested this public audit. Hearthline (Codex) carried out the source reading and formal controls. A separate ChatGPT session, Chat-Hearthline, supplied a prior audit for reconciliation. Jenny Lorraine Nielsen published the geometric-vacuity objection. This report credits her reading and tests its mathematical consequences. [Nielsen’s critique](https://philarchive.org/rec/NIEIOT-2) · [Alpöge’s announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

## Eliminate the chosen height

`LedPart.ht` is defined by `hExpl6`. In `linkDIV_at_words`, the equality to that formula is supplied separately from `WordFacts`. To see what an arbitrary height could accomplish, fix a word $u$, its metrics, lattices and all the other data. For $B_k(u)\ne0$, let $L$ be the measured height of that map, $\mu$ the dual slope, $F$ the term called `Fo`, and $R$ the entire right side in `WordFacts`. The scalar requirements are $L\le h$ and $\mu+h-F\le R$. The record separately requires lattice containment. [Chosen height](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/GlobalTwisting/SetUnipotentTranslate.lean#L377-L383) · [Exact inequalities and transports](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/RecBridge.lean#L128-L146) · [How the proof uses them](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/RecBridge.lean#L327-L339)

Eliminating $h$ gives

$$
\big(\exists h\in\mathbb R:\ L\le h\ \land\ \mu+h-F\le R\big)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mu+L-F\le R .
$$

The forward direction is transitivity. In the reverse direction take $h=L$. For a height already assigned, the admissible shifts are precisely $L-h\le\delta\le R+F-\mu-h$. With $L=1$ and $\mu=F=R=0$, every height fails one of the two tests. When $B_k(u)=0$ the first inequality has a false premise and drops out. Both cases are checked in Lean. The equivalence concerns the two scalar inequalities for a freely chosen height. The upstream record fixes $h$ and has further requirements. [Our eight Lean controls](AuditControls.lean)

Following the producer reveals where to challenge the endpoints. It ties the coordinate map to the same pairing, identifies its measured height, and takes a norm bound and a separate budget bound. In one explicit package assembly the valuation and transport inputs feed `wordFacts`; the consumer passes this separately from the height equation. An admissible nonzero word with $\mu+L-F>R$, or a metric change that breaks one of those identifications for the selected word, would be decisive here. Self-review reached the projections and their suppliers. The complete chain of arithmetic estimates underlying the suppliers remains open in this audit. ## Try to change the constants or swap the primes

**Moving threshold.** The Atlas warns that a new constant at every scale can absorb an arbitrary error. In `hostOfFieldOf` seven threshold inputs are obtained after fixing $\varepsilon,C_0$. Their maximum is chosen **before** the scale enters. The supplier then combines other bounds into a single threshold before introducing its host data. At the final analytic step the exponent coefficients are fixed even before $\varepsilon$. For an alleged zero with real part $\beta>1/2$, Lean chooses a positive $\varepsilon$ small relative to this particular $\beta-1/2$. **Missing scales.** Pang’s Ledger §16 treats eventual one-sided bounds on Suzuki’s $\Psi$. Rubinstein’s account treats a different weighted prime sum $G$ on arbitrarily late *whole intervals*. An explicit impostor shows why that distinction matters. Take $Z_0=2$, $Z_{j+1}=Z_j^4$. Put a continuous triangular bump of height $Z_j^2$ on $[Z_j^2,2Z_j^2]$, peaking at its midpoint, and use zero elsewhere. This function vanishes on every $[Z_j,Z_j^{3/2}]$ yet exceeds $x^{3/4}$ at arbitrarily late peaks. Applying it to RH fails a separate premise. In the zeta case the Mellin transform of $(x/n-1)_+$ is $n^{-s}/[s(s-1)]$ for $\Re s>1$, by direct integration from $n$ to infinity. The resulting transform for $G$ has a nonzero residue $-m/[\rho(\rho-1)]$ at a nontrivial zero of multiplicity $m$. In the source, the window criterion takes a character family and its particular sum. Our bump supplies no such transform identity. ## Unresolved

The full-proof audit is incomplete. The most direct falsification target from this pass is a concrete arithmetic source of the interval endpoints in `WordFacts`. Trace the norm estimate and valuation/size calculation for one *actual* nonzero pairing with all metrics fixed. In the zero-pairing branch trace which aggregate rank or trace estimate still pays for the missing lower bound.
