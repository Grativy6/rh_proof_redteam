# The ramification cost in passing from theta sums to a divisor

**24 November 2026.** Chris’s question about carrying the golden-angle residue led to a [theta projector whose two leading coefficients change at refinement](2026-11-24-when-the-golden-unit-changes-a-genuine-theta-term.md). At a fixed trait there is a sharp absolute error bound. On an explicit family of increasingly ramified traits the same error stays near $1/\sqrt2$. I followed both quantifiers into the released ideal-sheaf argument and computed its two precision requirements exactly on an arithmetic surface.

## Absolute and relative error on the same lattice

Fix a positive-definite lattice $(L,Q)$, a uniformizer parameter $\pi$ with $|\pi|_p<1$, and genuine coefficients $A_v$ bounded by $|\pi|_p^{Q(v)}$. Suppose that at modulus $m$ another family $t_v$ with the same bounds gives the exact same sums over every residue class in the same frame. A fixed torsion evaluation can be absorbed into these coefficients. For any $v$, subtract the two class sums and isolate that term. Writing $q'=\min_{w\ne v,\ w\equiv v\bmod m}Q(w)$ on a nonzero-rank lattice gives
$$ |t_v-A_v|_p\le|\pi|_p^{\max(Q(v),q')} .$$
The bound using $q'$ comes from all the other terms. The bound using $Q(v)$ is the individual term bound. If $\lambda_1$ is the minimum nonzero lattice length for $\sqrt Q$, then $\max(\sqrt{Q(v)},\sqrt{Q(w)})\ge m\lambda_1/2$ for every distinct congruent pair. Consequently
$$\sup_{v\in L}|t_v-A_v|_p\le |\pi|_p^{m^2\lambda_1^2/4}.$$
This also bounds the difference between witnesses at nested levels when the fine class sums regroup to the coarse ones. Convergence only needs the cofinite tail bound, which makes the finite exceptional sets in the source’s class sums sufficient. [Bounds on terms and sums](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/ClassSums.lean#L44-L73)

For the genuine $E_8\oplus E_8$ test at $\pi=2$, $\lambda_1^2=2$ and the observed error is $2^{-m^2/2-7b^2/2}$, where the base level $b=7^5$ stays fixed. This proves sharpness of the quadratic exponent along infinitely many allowed prime-power levels in the numerical reconstruction. It also explains how a relative discrepancy of the full leading size can coexist with uniform absolute decay on one trait.

Now let $\pi_m^{m^2}=2$ and use the same period formula and residue units over this finitely ramified field. Compare levels $m$ and $7m$ on that one trait before moving to the next. The normalized leading residues are unchanged. The absolute error becomes
$$|\pi_m|_2^{(m^2+7b^2)/2}
=2^{-(1/2+7b^2/(2m^2))}\longrightarrow 2^{-1/2}.$$
This is a counterexample to taking the previous uniform absolute limit simultaneously over all traits with the normalized $2$-adic absolute value. The release puts the trait’s positive scale inside the exponent. Its approximation chooses one reference trait before the large level and allows a different nonunit error at other traits. ## Test the descent on a complete set of arithmetic probes

Use the identity proper morphism of normal models of the arithmetic line, at its closed point with local ring $A=\mathbb Z[T]_{(2,T)}$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(2,T)$, effective Cartier divisor $(2^a)$ with $a\ge1$, and open $D(2)$. The function field is $\mathbb Q(T)$. In this example the fibre-divisibility ideal is $(2^a)$. The source tests centred maps to valuation rings of algebraic $p$-adic numbers. A map through this closed point has $p=2$. Every allowed specialization $T=t$ has $|t|_2<1$, and every such algebraic $t$ supplies a test. Every denominator of a germ is a unit there. [Correction with the full quantifier order](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsChevalleyCorrectionOverProperFibreStatement.lean#L8-L9)

At a fixed test $t=2^{1/R}$ the least positive exponent $k$ guaranteeing the source’s close-generator inference is $aR+1$. Indeed $b\in\mathfrak m^k$ implies $|b(t)|_2\le2^{-k/R}$. Once $k>aR$, equality $|G(t)+b(t)|_2=2^{-a}$ forces $|G(t)|_2=2^{-a}$. If $G/2^a$ is regular at the point, it is a unit there because its value is a unit at this centred test. At $k\le aR$, take $G=0$ and $b=T^{aR}$. The norm equality still holds. The source chooses $k$ **after** fixing $t$. No single $k$ works across arbitrary ramification for this implication. [Exact local-equation assertion](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/GeneratesOfCloseLocalEquationClassicalPointStatement.lean#L8-L9)

For the other direction let $I_e$ consist of germs satisfying the released approximate-divisibility test at every such $t$. For $e\ge1$ this becomes
$$|F(t)|_2\le\max\bigl(2^{-d},|t|_2^e\bigr),\qquad d=\min(a,e).$$
Indeed the largest possible norm of a nonunit at $t$ is $\max(1/2,|t|_2)$, attained by 2 or $T$. A monomial $2^uT^v$ passes exactly when $u/d+v/e\ge1$. Sufficiency follows by comparing valuations on the two sides of $v_2(t)=d/e$. Necessity already follows at this one slope. For a polynomial, choose $t=2^{d/e}\xi$ with $\xi$ an unramified root of unity whose residue avoids the zeros of the nonzero initial polynomial. Its terms at the least weighted degree have distinct powers of $\bar\xi$, so cancellation can be avoided in a finite residue extension. Clearing a unit denominator gives the same result for germs. This computes the whole ideal $I_e$.

The least $e$ with $I_e\subset (2^a)+\mathfrak m^k$, for $k\ge1$, is
$$e_{\min}(a,k)=
\begin{cases} k,&k\le a,\\ a(k-a)+1,&k>a.\end{cases}$$
For $e<a$ the ideal is just $\mathfrak m^e$. For $e\ge a$ the least ordinary order of any passing monomial whose coefficient is not divisible by $2^a$ is $a-1+\lceil e/a\rceil$. It occurs at $u=a-1$ because $e/a\ge1$. As a concrete boundary test take $a=4,k=8$. The germ $2^3T^4$ passes at $e=16$ even though its ordinary order is only 7. At $e=17$ the evaluation $T=2^{4/17}$ rejects it, and the inclusion holds for all germs. If one restricted every probe to ramification index at most $R$, even the case $a=1$ would admit $T^R$ for **every** $e$ while missing the required inclusion at $k=R+1$. ## Why the general descent has a completion hypothesis

The producer in the release takes $k$ at the reference point, obtains a correction exponent for that $k$, then asks for an approximate germ. In the general Chevalley step it checks what happens in the **completion** of the local ring. For a noetherian local ring alone, a descending sequence of ideals can have zero intersection and still retain low-order elements forever. For example choose $A_0=\mathbb Q[X,Y]_{(X,Y)}$ and a transcendental power series $h(X)\in X^2\mathbb Q[[X]]$. Let $h_n$ be its polynomial truncation below degree $n$. The ideals $(Y-h_n(X),X^n)$ have zero intersection in $A_0$ by substitution, but none for $n\ge2$ is contained in $(X,Y)^2$. In the completion the element $Y-h(X)$ survives their intersection. [Where the release tests the completed limit](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsChevalleyCorrectionOverProperFibre.lean#L12-L31)

There is a direct check of one general step. After localization at the base point, let $Y$ be the proper model over the noetherian local ring $A$, and let $D$ be its effective Cartier divisor. The base ideal of functions divisible by its local equations along the special fibre is $\ker(A\to H^0(D,\mathcal O_D))$. Indeed the support of a nonzero restriction is closed, and properness makes its image meet the closed point of the local base. The target module is finite over $A$. Flat completion identifies the extended kernel with the kernel on completed modules. The theorem on formal functions identifies the latter target with $\varprojlim_n H^0(D\times_A A/\mathfrak m^n,\mathcal O)$. If a proposed formal function vanishes in the completed local rings of $D$ at every closed point over $\mathfrak m$, then at each thickening its value is zero in every such completed stalk. Faithfulness of local completion gives zero in the stalks, and the support condition gives the zero section. This proves membership in the extended kernel, with precisely the local vanishing as an input. ## Unresolved

The explicit surface pays the two different precision costs in the order the release asks for them. The check by formal functions explains the passage from vanishing in completed stalks to base-ideal membership. A [singular arithmetic surface and a twice-blown-up fibre](2026-11-24-which-traits-detect-a-divisor-at-a-singular-point.md) give explicit cutoffs and an independent separation argument for normal local rings. What remains is the construction of the approximate base germs with the required simultaneous bounds. The geometric theta example exposes a genuine coefficient mismatch, while the traced descent requests norms and ideals.
