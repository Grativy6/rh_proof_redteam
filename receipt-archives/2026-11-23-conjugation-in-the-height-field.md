# A negative squared length in an allowed coefficient field

**23 November 2026 · Hearthline, under Chris Pang’s public audit request.** An allowed field enlargement can make the real part of a transported algebraic “squared length” negative. This is a paper counterexample to exchanging field conjugation and complex conjugation at arbitrary places. The specific norm used for the theta jet avoids the exchange. The Gram matrix on invariant forms needs a further condition, which can be checked on its basis.

## A witness in an enlargement of any starting field

The coefficient field $L$ in the construction is a normal number field with a chosen involution agreeing with complex conjugation at one distinguished embedding. It contains the CM host. It can also contain the splitting field of $X^3-2$. Starting with the prescribed embedded field, take its compositum with this splitting field inside $\mathbb C$, use the restriction of ordinary complex conjugation as its involution, and retain the old distinguished subfields by inclusion. This is again normal, totally complex and satisfies the specified compatibility at the distinguished embedding. The construction explicitly permits adjoining any finite set of algebraic numbers this way. [Field and involution requirements](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/RootOfUnityEightQMemCoeffGaloisSubfield.lean#L261-L281) · [Quantifier on the coefficient-field involution](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Columns/AbelianHostFieldConditions.lean#L100-L107) · [Enlargement operation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ToMapSurj.lean#L40-L73)

Let $a=\sqrt[3]{2}>0$, let $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}$ and extend the splitting-field automorphism $a\mapsto\zeta a$, $\zeta\mapsto\zeta$ to an embedding $\rho$ of the enlarged field. The extension exists by algebraic extension of embeddings. At the chosen place $\star a=a$. Then
$$
 \Re\,\rho((\star a)a)=\Re(\zeta^2a^2)=-a^2/2,\qquad
 \overline{\rho(a)}\,\rho(a)=a^2.
$$
In particular one cannot define the metric at all places by transporting the distinguished algebraic square of an arbitrary coefficient. Even in dimension one the two formulas disagree on an element allowed in the coefficient field. The order of the two automorphisms is checked exactly by their permutations of the three roots. ## Which expression the arithmetic row actually uses

In `sqAt` the squared length of a vector with coordinates $x_i$ and Gram matrix $H$ is
$$
 \Re\sum_{ij}\overline{\rho(x_i)}\,\rho(H_{ij})\,\rho(x_j).
$$
The variable coefficients are conjugated after embedding. For $x=av$ this multiplies the previous value by $|\rho(a)|^2$, as required. At the coefficient-polynomial level the basis consists of products of minors with integer coefficients. At the identity Gram matrix the Fock pairing adds the products of the polynomial coefficients with factorial weights and divides by the positive squared norm of a highest-weight vector. Its matrix in that basis is rational positive definite, at every embedding. Thus a change of $\rho$ cannot introduce the negative factor into these Gram entries either. There is a second possible entry point for the same error. The class-sum lattice constructs an algebraic Gram matrix from a basis of forms fixed by stabilizers, using $\star$ before embedding. Define
$$
 L_0=\{x\in L:\rho(\star x)=\overline{\rho(x)}\ \text{for every embedding }\rho\}.
$$
This is a subfield, denoted `cmMax` in the release; the cubic element above is excluded. Every embedded host element is in $L_0$, since the host is a normal CM subfield on which complex conjugation is central. Every root of unity is in $L_0$ since $\star\zeta=\zeta^{-1}$ and every conjugate is again on the unit circle. Rational numbers lie there as well. [Exact definition of the compatible subfield](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/QuotientMetricDivisors/RatHermLcvTwist.lean#L16-L30) · [Conditional basis selection and Gram construction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/ClassSum.lean#L63-L84)

For the fixed-class forms the action matrices come from substitution by a host unitary matrix or its inverse transpose in the polynomial representation, times a finite-character value. Substitution has integer structure constants in the minors presentation, or one can solve for its coordinates in the rational polynomial basis by rational elimination. The arithmetic matrix entries lie in embedded copies of $E$, and the finite-character factors are roots of unity. Their products and sums are therefore in $L_0$. The invariant forms solve linear equations with coefficients in $L_0$. Row reduction there produces a basis that still spans precisely the invariants after extending to $L$. Thus `HasRatBasis` really holds at this step, and *every* basis chosen under that predicate has its relevant evaluation coordinates in $L_0$. For such basis vectors the class-sum Gram entries may be evaluated using the usual complex conjugates at every place. The weights are positive reciprocals of finite stabilizer orders, and evaluation on class representatives is injective. The resulting Hermitian Gram matrix is positive definite. For a general vector the complex Gram formula then applies to its arbitrary embedded coordinates. The actual class-sum height retains the order of operations that the cubic control distinguishes.
