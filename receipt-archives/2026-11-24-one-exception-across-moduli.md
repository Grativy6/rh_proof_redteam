## Transcript of the arithmetic requirement

Fix $Z\ge3$, and take two real nonprincipal Dirichlet characters of moduli $M_1,M_2\le Z$. If both have real zeros $\beta_j$ in $(1-c_0/\log Z,1)$, the source needs the characters to agree on every integer prime to $M_1M_2$. We obtain this with $c_0=1/40000$. Its size is inessential because the selection requires existence of a positive absolute constant. The original source uses a same-modulus zero-free-region result after lifting both characters to the product modulus. [Exact cross-modulus proposition and lift](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/JacobiCharacterSums/MSqPage.lean#L37-L65)

This is the real-zero uniqueness part of the classical Landau–Page theorem. Ford, Green, Konyagin, Maynard and Tao record the primitive-character form for conductor at most $Q$ in Lemma 7.1. The calculation here verifies the particular imprimitive, common-unit conclusion used by the release. [Ford et al., *Long gaps between primes*, Lemma 7.1](https://www.ams.org/journals/jams/2018-31-01/S0894-0347-2017-00876-2/S0894-0347-2017-00876-2.pdf)

### A uniform logarithmic derivative bound

Let $M=M_1M_2\le Z^2$. For any nonprincipal character $\chi$ at modulus $M$, write $F(s)=L(s,\chi)$. Its partial character sums have absolute value at most $M$ by periodicity and zero sum over a complete period. Partial summation gives $F(s)=s\int_1^\infty (\sum_{n\le u}\chi(n))u^{-s-1}\,du$ in $\Re(s)>0$. On $|s-2|\le7/4$ this gives $|F|\le15M$. Use $F_0(s)=(s-1)\zeta(s)$ for the principal factor. From $\lfloor u\rfloor=u-\{u\}$ one gets $F_0(s)=s-s(s-1)\int_1^\infty\{u\}u^{-s-1}\,du$, also bounded there by $50Z^2$. Set $A=50Z^2$ for all four functions below. At $s=2$ each has absolute value at least $1/2$, by the absolutely convergent Euler product and $\zeta(2)<2$. Every zero has real part at most 1 by that same Euler product.

For $1<\sigma\le3/2$ we claim
$$-\Re(F'/F)(\sigma)\le350\log Z-\sum_{\rho}\Re\frac1{\sigma-\rho},$$
where the sum can retain any chosen zeros with $|\rho-2|<3/2$, including multiplicities. This applies to $F_0$ too. Here are the radius and constant checks. Jensen’s formula on the outer disk bounds the number of zeros inside radius $13/8$ by $\log(2A)/\log(14/13)$. Choose a zero-free boundary radius $3/2<R<13/8$, use coordinate $\xi=s-2$, and remove all its interior zeros with the disk Blaschke factors
$$b_v(\xi)=R(\xi-v)/(R^2-\bar v\xi),\qquad v=\rho-2.$$
The remaining holomorphic nonvanishing $H$ satisfies $|H|\le A$ on the disk and $|H(0)|\ge1/2$. For $|\xi|\le1$, Harnack’s inequality gives $u(\xi)\le5u(0)$ for $u=\log A-\log|H|$. The disk Poisson estimate $|\nabla u(\xi)|\le 2u(\xi)/(R-|\xi|)$ then bounds $|H'/H|$ by $20\log(2A)$. In $b_v'/b_v$ the correction to $1/(\xi-v)$ has absolute value at most 2. Thus the total error is at most $(20+2/\log(14/13))\log(2A)\le350\log Z$, since $\log(14/13)>1/14$ and $100Z^2\le Z^7$. Each omitted zero has $\Re(1/(\sigma-\rho))\ge0$, giving the indicated one-sided bound.

### Why two distinct exceptions are impossible

Inflate the two characters to modulus $M$. Their real zeros in the specified interval persist because the extra Euler factors $1-\chi(p)p^{-\beta_j}$ are nonzero for $\beta_j>0$. If the inflated characters differ on the unit group, their product is nonprincipal. For real $\sigma>1$ the negative logarithmic derivative of $\zeta L_1 L_2 L_{12}$ is nonnegative. Its Dirichlet series has coefficients $\Lambda(n)(1+\chi_1(n))(1+\chi_2(n))\ge0$. Using the preceding bounds for $F_0,L_1,L_2,L_{12}$ and restoring the zeta pole gives
$$0\le \frac1{\sigma-1}+1400\log Z-\frac1{\sigma-\beta_1}-\frac1{\sigma-\beta_2}.$$
At $t=\sigma-1=1/(10000\log Z)$ both zeros have $\beta_j>1-t/4>1/2$ and hence lie in the inner disk. The right-hand side is less than $(1+0.14-1.6)/t<0$. This contradiction proves the required equality on units without assuming any Riemann hypothesis or consulting the released zero-free proof.

Now suppose two distinct squarefree odd numbers $s_1,s_2$ prime to the fixed modulus $m_0$ were both exceptional labels. The corresponding characters agree on common units by what we just proved. On $u\equiv1\bmod m_0$, each must equal $(u/s_i)$. Choose a prime dividing exactly one of the two squarefree numbers. By the Chinese remainder theorem take $u$ nonsquare at that prime and $1$ at all other primes in $s_1s_2$, and $u=1\bmod m_0$. It is a common unit, and its two Jacobi signs differ. Thus at most one label can require the exception. The [finite CRT control](check_prime_window_readouts.py) illustrates why labels containing an extra prime cancel on a full residue-class fiber. ### The scale remaining after the exception

For an exceptional term too far from 1 to enter that uniqueness argument, $\beta\le1-c_0/\log Z$. For large $U$ and $\beta\ge1/2$,
$$\mathrm{li}(U^\beta)/\mathrm{li}(U)
 \le (2/\log2)(\log U)\exp[-c_0\log U/\log Z],$$
using $\mathrm{li}(x)=\int_2^xdt/\log t$, an upper bound by $x/\log2$ and a lower bound for $\mathrm{li}(U)$ from $[\sqrt U,U]$. In the application $Z=m_0Y^A$, and admissibility gives $\log m_0\le4\log U/(\log Y)^2$ and $Y^{\epsilon/6}\le\log U\le Y^{\epsilon/5}(\log Y)^2$. For fixed $\epsilon,A>0$, eventually $\log Z\le 5\log U/(\log Y)^2$. Thus the ratio is $o(Y^{-B})$ for every fixed $B$. The main-term treatment can indeed use a single exception at these scales. ## Unresolved

The prime-counting input still has to control errors relative to the **actual surviving main term** on each progression class. In a class where an exceptional character is $+1$, that term is
$$\frac{\mathrm{li}(U)-\mathrm{li}(U^\beta)}{\varphi(M)}
   =\frac1{\varphi(M)}\int_\beta^1\frac{U^t}{t}\,dt.$$
Putting $a=(1-\beta)\log U$, the numerator is between $U(1-e^{-a})/\log U$ and $U(1-e^{-a})/(\beta\log U)$. When $a$ is small, the full-magnitude scale $U/\log U$ overstates the surviving amount by a factor of order $1/a$. Uniqueness of the exceptional label has no bearing on this amplification. The release explicitly asks for an error proportional to the surviving term. Its assembly passes through a zero sum, zero-density estimates and an exceptional-zero repulsion estimate. Checking that the small factor survives those steps is a sharper remaining audit than counting the possible labels.
