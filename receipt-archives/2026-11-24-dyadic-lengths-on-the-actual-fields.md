# The actual two-dimensional operator on the dyadic zeta fields

**24 November 2026.** Testing an arbitrary integral matrix left open whether the bad twist values could occur on the arithmetic module used by the release. A theorem of Yasushi Mizusawa identifies the Galois group for exactly the two required prime congruences. It gives a much sharper check than a generator-count bound. The dyadically split module has **rank two**, and its characteristic polynomial can be tested at the genuine cyclotomic twists. For $(q,d)=(3,23)$ every positive twist has length $2+2m$ at full cyclotomic layer $m$. For $(3,167)$ the lengths already vary from 4 to 14 at layer zero on the three twists $1,3,35$. The [exact character sums](check_dyadic_L_values.py) use neither the released proof nor Lean. [Mizusawa, Theorem 2.2 and Section 4.2](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.707.pdf#page=5)

## Identify the same module before measuring it

Write $k_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-qd})$, $K=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$, $K_m=K(\zeta_{2^{m+2}})$ and $K_\infty=\bigcup K_m$. These are coprime odd prime conductors with $q=3\bmod8$, $d=7\bmod16$. The genus field $k_0(\sqrt q,\sqrt d)$ in Mizusawa is exactly $K$. Its cyclotomic extension over $K$ is precisely the tower in the release following the index-two extension adjoining $i$. The paper’s chosen generator acts by 5 on the real cyclotomic coordinates and fixes the Hilbert 2-class field of $k_0$, hence fixes $K$. As it fixes $i$, its 2-adic cyclotomic value in this tower is $+5$. The paper defines the action by left conjugation. The release acts by inverse conjugation on characters and by the dual action on their dual, recovering this same left conjugation. Here is the passage from the paper’s *unramified* module to the *dyadically split* module required by the release. In Mizusawa’s group $G$ the commutator subgroup is generated topologically by $a^2$. The subgroup for $K_\infty$ is $H=\langle b^2,c,a^2\rangle$, abelian and free of rank three. The decomposition subgroup in $G$ at a place above 2 is $\langle a\rangle$, normal in $G$. Its intersection with $H$ is $\langle a^2\rangle$. The maximal unramified pro-2 extension above $K_\infty$ is covered by this computation. Taking the normal closure of any finite unramified Galois 2-extension over the unramified, degree-four extension $K_\infty/(k_0)_\infty$ still gives an unramified 2-extension. Thus killing the dyadic decomposition groups gives
$$X'=H/\langle a^2\rangle
  \ \simeq\ \langle 2\bar b,\bar c\rangle
  \ \subseteq\ G/\langle a\rangle\simeq\mathbb Z_2^2.$$
The inclusion has index two. This quotient is the compact dual of the locally constant characters that vanish on inertia everywhere and on decomposition at 2, the definition used in the source. An index-two change of lattice can affect individual invariant factors. The characteristic polynomial of the rational linear operator is identical on these two lattices. ## From that operator to the exact length

Let $A_j=(\mathbb Q_2/\mathbb Z_2)(-j)$, $j\ge1$, and let $\ell_m(j)$ be the length of the release’s strict `Sha` over $K_m$. Let $B$ denote left conjugation by the generator of cyclotomic value 5 on $X'$. Over the infinite field all cyclotomic coefficients are trivial. On $\Gamma_m=\operatorname{Gal}(K_\infty/K_m)\cong\mathbb Z_2$ a generator acts on $A_j$ by $5^{-j2^m}$. Its difference from 1 is a nonzero 2-adic scalar, hence surjective on $A_j$, and $H^1(\Gamma_m,A_j)=0$. Also $H^2(\Gamma_m,A_j)=0$ by the cohomological dimension one of $\mathbb Z_2$ on discrete 2-primary modules. Inflation-restriction therefore gives the equivariant homomorphism description. At each dyadic place the same argument applies because the cyclotomic extension has full local degree. Away from 2, inertia is already in the kernel of the cyclotomic character. The local restrictions on the equivariant homomorphisms give exactly $X'$.

Dualizing gives the cokernel of $(5^jB)^{2^m}-1$. Since $X'$ is free, its finite length equals the valuation of the determinant. Use $v_2(2)=1$, extended to the algebraic closure. With
$$P(T)=\det((1+T)I-B)=T^2+C_1T+C_0$$
one obtains
$$\ell_m(j)=\sum_{\omega^{2^m}=1}v_2\big(P(5^{-j}\omega-1)\big).$$
The powers of 5 that arise on factoring the determinant are units. Both $C_0$ and $C_1$ are even, as $P$ is distinguished. The interpolation below proves nonvanishing of the factors in use. This verifies the sign and the coefficient-group interpretation of the released tower formula for these fields. ## Independent values at the two signs

Let $\psi$ be the primitive even quadratic character of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{qd})$. Mizusawa records that $P$ generates the same ideal of $\mathbb Z_2[[T]]$ as a series $\Phi$, with $L_2(s,\psi)=2\Phi(5^s-1)$. Consequently $P$ and $\Phi$ differ by an integral unit series, which stays a unit at all the arguments above. For a character $\eta$ of the cyclotomic $\mathbb Z_2$-extension, the finite-character interpolation shifts the argument to $\eta(5)^{-1}5^s-1$. There is no principal-character denominator here, as the odd conductor $qd$ remains nontrivial. We use the main-conjecture identification as recorded by Mizusawa and the Kubota–Leopoldt interpolation theorem as external inputs. [Mizusawa, printed p. 119](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.707.pdf#page=6) · [Greenberg, *On p-adic L-functions and cyclotomic fields*, equation (11)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/DAF2660E9D75B56D7D90263CA4CC9281/S002776300001638Xa.pdf/on-p-adic-l-functions-and-cyclotomic-fields.pdf)

At $s=-j$, set $n=j+1$ and take the primitive representative $\theta=\psi\eta\omega_2^{-n}$, where $\omega_2$ is the sign character modulo 4. Interpolation gives
$$L_2(-j,\psi\eta)=-(1-\theta(2)2^j)B_{n,\theta}/n.$$
The Euler factor is a 2-adic unit, including when $\theta(2)=0$. The parity is precisely the nonvanishing parity of the classical special value. The functional equation and the Euler product at $n\ge2$ give this without any hypothesis on the critical strip. At $\omega=1$ use $\eta=1$. At $\omega=-1$ use the even quadratic character of conductor eight, with $\eta(5)=-1$. [Interpolation and the modulo-four convention](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/DarmonCM/2011Notes/dirichlet.pdf)

For $q=3,d=23$ the two untwisted values at $j=1,3$ are $-72$ and $178200$. Thus $\Phi(-4/5)=-36$, $\Phi(-124/125)=89100$. Their divided difference is $-464250$, of valuation one. For $q=3,d=167$ the corresponding $L_2$ values are $-1440$ and $183928320$. Again the divided difference for $\Phi$ has valuation one. In the first case $v_2(C_0)=2$, because $T\in4\mathbb Z_2$ gives $P(T)\equiv C_0\bmod8$. In the second case $C_0$ is divisible by 8. In both cases the divided difference is congruent modulo 4 to the linear coefficient of $\Phi$. Writing $\Phi=UP$ gives $U(0)C_1+U'(0)C_0$ for this coefficient, so $v_2(C_1)=1$. For $d=167$, $P(-4/5)$ has valuation four, forcing $C_0\equiv8\bmod16$ from the other two terms.

* For $d=23$, the polynomial $P(2Z)/4$ reduces to $Z^2+Z+1$ modulo 2. Both $P(5^{-j}-1)$ and $P(-5^{-j}-1)$ have valuation two for every $j\ge1$. At a primitive fourth root argument $T=\pm i\,5^{-j}-1$ the leading term has strictly smallest valuation, one. Each higher cyclotomic shell contributes two to the *sum* of valuations since a primitive $2^h$-th root minus 1 has valuation $1/2^{h-1}$. Therefore $\ell_m(j)=2+2m$ for every $m$ and $j\ge1$.
* For $d=167$ the Newton polygon has two distinct slopes, with root valuations one and two. Equivalently, $P(4Z)/8$ reduces to $Z+1$ with simple derivative modulo 2, so it has an odd 2-adic root. Dividing $C_0$ by the resulting root of $P$ gives the other root in $\mathbb Q_2$. The eigenvalues can be written uniquely as $5^{-a}$ and $-5^{-b}$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z_2$. The exact formulas are $\ell_0(j)=3+v_2(j-a)$ and, for $m\ge1$, $\ell_m(j)=2m+4+v_2(j-a)+v_2(j-b)$. At the negative argument the corresponding valuation is $3+v_2(j-b)$. Nonvanishing of the interpolated values excludes a positive integer root at a tested twist or any other positive twist.

The integral form of the two-dimensional action gives one more check. With columns indexed by $2\bar b,\bar c$, its matrix is

This follows by reducing the presentation's formulas for $\gamma b$ and $\gamma c$ modulo $\langle a\rangle$. In both numerical fields $C_0$ is divisible by four. ## Do the arithmetic centres allow arbitrarily rapid approximation?

For the upper bound it suffices to consider $\omega$ of order at most four. For order $2^h$, $h\ge3$, the argument $T=5^{-j}\omega-1$ has valuation $1/2^{h-1}<1/2$. In the distinguished quadratic $P(T)$ the leading term has strictly smaller valuation than both remaining terms. Hence every shell $h\ge3$ adds exactly two. This gives $\ell_m(j)=\ell_2(j)+2(m-2)$ for $m\ge2$ and $\ell_m(j)\le\ell_2(j)$ for $m\le2$.

The four low-order characters $\eta$ have conductor dividing 16 and values in $\mathbb Q(i)$. Thus $\theta$ has conductor $f\le16qd$. Put $n=j+1$. From $B_{n,\theta}=f^{n-1}\sum_{b=1}^f\theta(b)B_n(b/f)$, the element $D B_{n,\theta}/n$ with $D=n f(n+1)!$ is integral. Indeed the ordinary Bernoulli denominators through $n$ divide $(n+1)!$ by von Staudt–Clausen. At any complex embedding its absolute value is at most $3(n+1)!n! f^{n+1}$, using $|B_i|\le i!$ and the binomial formula for $B_n(x)$ on $[0,1]$. There is only one prime of $\mathbb Q(i)$ above 2. Taking the norm of this **nonzero** algebraic integer thus bounds its normalized 2-adic valuation by the base-two logarithm of the same archimedean bound. The factor $D$ is an integer and the Euler factor a 2-adic unit. Combining the four terms proves, for an absolute constant,
$$\ell_m(j)\ll (j+2)\big(1+\log(qd)+\log(j+2)\big)+2m.$$
This independently rules out the [arbitrarily fast generic centre example](2026-11-24-fixed-centres-and-exceptional-twists.md) on these actual zeta fields.

The field in `VAt` is $E_m=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^{m+2}})^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$. Its strict group injects on restriction to $K_m=E_m(i)$, because the global and dyadic cyclotomic images over $K_m$ agree and the dyadic decomposition group maps onto the quadratic quotient. Restriction also gives a lower bound on the strict groups over the original fields $E_m$. Let $\tau$ be the involution fixing $E_m$, with cyclotomic value $-1$ and acting trivially on all the real cyclotomic coordinates. On $(k_0)_\infty$ it acts by complex conjugation, since it fixes $\sqrt{qd}$ and changes the sign of $i$. Its action on $X(k_{0,\infty})$ there is $-I$. To see that each finite real cyclotomic layer $\mathbb Q_n$ has odd class number, suppose it had an unramified quadratic extension. The normal closure would be a 2-extension of $\mathbb Q$ ramified only at 2, with a proper inertia subgroup at 2. A maximal subgroup containing inertia would give a quadratic field unramified at every finite prime. Thus for any 2-primary ideal class of $(k_0)_n$ its norm to $\mathbb Q_n$ is trivial. Extending that norm back shows that the conjugate class is its inverse. This passes to the limit. Two lifts that agree on $(k_0)_\infty$ differ by inner conjugation, trivial on $G/\langle a\rangle$. Its stable sublattice $X'$ therefore has $\tau=-I$. On the twisted strict group over $K_m$ the induced action is $(-1)^{j+1}$.

For completeness, compare $S(E_m,A_j)$ with $S(K_m,A_j)^\tau$. The global invariants $M=A_j^{G_{K_m}}$ and those at each dyadic place of $K_m$ are the same cyclic group, since the global and local cyclotomic images are both $1+2^{m+2}\mathbb Z_2$. The obstruction to lifting an invariant class to global $H^1(E_m,A_j)$ lies in $H^2(C_2,M)$ by inflation-restriction. Restriction to either dyadic place gives the identity comparison of those $H^2$ groups. A strict class over $K_m$ is locally zero, so this obstruction vanishes. At odd primes inertia is unchanged under the unramified quadratic base change. At the two dyadic places a chosen lift has two errors in $H^1(C_2,M)$, a group of order two. A global inflation adjusts both errors diagonally, leaving an obstruction of size at most two. The same local comparison gives injectivity on strict classes. Hence the image of $S(E_m,A_j)$ has index at most two in the invariant subgroup. For odd $j$ this gives
$$\ell_m(j)-1\le\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_2} S(E_m,A_j)\le\ell_m(j).$$
For even $j$ the invariant subgroup consists only of two-torsion and its length is at most two. This comparison does not specify which of the two possibilities for the odd-twist index occurs.

On $X'/2X'$ the matrix of $5^jB$ is unipotent of size two. Its square is identity modulo 2, its fourth power is identity modulo 4, and successive squaring increases that guaranteed precision by one bit. Thus $\ell_m(j)\ge2m$ for $m\ge2$. Every positive odd twist on the original $E_m$ contributes **at least $2m-1$**. At the [certified 656-bit input](2026-11-24-dyadic-class-enters-late.md), where $m=86$, a *single* odd twist contributes at least $171\log2$ to a budget less than 3. This stronger bound uses Mizusawa’s arithmetic result. The original one-bit witness has its own elementary proof.

Summing over $1\le j<t$ gives the uniform upper bound $O(t^2(1+\log(qd)+\log(t+2))+mt)$ for the dyadic length sum in `VAt`. On the released scales $t\le x^{401}$, $m=O(\log x)$ from the page condition and $\log(qd)\le x^{\epsilon/4}$. For every fixed $\epsilon>0$ this quantity eventually fits within $\exp(x^{4\epsilon/5})$, even before the final dyadic coefficient $\log2$ is incorporated. The calculation covers the 656-bit prime pair at the level of the uniform inequality. The numerical evaluations use the two stated small conductors.
