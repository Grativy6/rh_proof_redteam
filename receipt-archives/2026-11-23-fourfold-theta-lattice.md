# Can the fourfold theta average cancel?

**23 November 2026.** Chris Pang asked Hearthline to follow the actual arithmetic maps to their height bound. In the selected fourfold kernel the translates can be recovered individually from their responses to period shifts. The normalized sum has exactly the mean-square norm of the input. Here is the arithmetic that makes that assertion apply. The use of this norm after moving the coefficients is tested in the [transport calculation](2026-11-23-theta-transport-at-a-place.md).

## What is being averaged?

Write $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$, choose $\sqrt{-d}=i\sqrt d$ at the base embedding, and let $E$ be the host CM field. Write $\Phi$ for the tuple of its $f=[E:K]$ embeddings over this embedding of $K$. The labels in the release enumerate the kernel of the sign acting on $\sqrt{-d}$. Since $E/\mathbb Q$ is Galois, these are exactly the required embeddings, each occurring once. The elliptic input is $\Lambda=\delta\mathfrak c$, where $\mathfrak c$ is an integral ideal of odd norm prime to $d$, and $\Delta(\Lambda)=1$. We use $\mathfrak c$ also for its embedded image. The argument works for every such ideal, including the selected `ccK`. The release uses $\mathfrak b$ for one half of the relative different, $\mathfrak D=\mathfrak D_{E/K}$. This factor can be recovered by factoring ideals. Outside the primes of $Q_S qd$ the ambient cyclotomic field is unramified. At $d$ its inertia acts trivially on the prime-to-$d$ part, and the defining character sends it into $\{1,\mathrm{iod}\}$. The second element negates $\sqrt{-d}$, so the relative inertia over $K$ is trivial. Every possible remaining prime of the different lies over a rational prime dividing $Q_S q$. Each splits in $K$ by the specified quadratic symbols, including $2$ since $-d\equiv1\bmod8$. Complex conjugation therefore pairs the prime factors of $\mathfrak D$ over distinct primes of $K$, with equal exponents. Choosing one side over each such pair of base primes and taking every factor there with its full exponent gives $\mathfrak b\bar{\mathfrak b}=\mathfrak D$. This is the side selection in the host construction. Either choice at any split pair gives the calculation below. Set $I=\mathfrak c\mathcal O_E\,\mathfrak b^{-1}$. The block lattice is $M=\delta\Phi(I)$, because the additive generators in `lamMM` are precisely products of a period with an embedded element of $\mathfrak b^{-1}$. Write $A=|\delta|^2 N\mathfrak c\,\sqrt d/2$ for the covolume of $\Lambda$, and $H(z,w)=\sum z_j\bar w_j/A$, linear in $z$. The release uses a $K$-linear isometry $g_0$ from four copies of the positive Hermitian trace form on $E$ to the standard form on $K^{4f}$. Its complex realization $g$ is fixed by its action on the embedding vectors. These span by separability. In an $E/K$ basis the matrix of $g$ is the product of the matrix of $g_0$ and the inverse embedding matrix, in four blocks. Its entries lie in the embedded normal field $E$, and the trace identity makes it unitary. The two lattices in the average are
$$
 P=\Lambda^{4f},\qquad S=g(M^4).
$$
Both are full lattices in the same rational space $\delta K^{4f}$ and are commensurable. [Rational isometry](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/KernelLattices/LateralTheory.lean#L73-L89) · [Product lattice and its complex realization](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/KernelLattices/LateralTheory.lean#L119-L149) · [Exact finite average](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/QuadraticSemicharacters/ProductThetaMem.lean#L52-L69)

A rational isometry actually exists. Diagonalize the positive Hermitian form over $K$. Each diagonal entry is a positive rational $s$. Write $s$ as a sum of four rational squares by applying the four-square theorem to the product of its numerator and denominator. Multiplication by the resulting rational quaternion has a four-by-four matrix $C$ with $C^t C=sI_4$. Apply these matrices to the four copies of each diagonal coordinate. This argument also explains why a single diagonal entry need not itself be the norm of an element of $K$.

## Which period shifts distinguish the translates?

Let $E_H=\Im H$. On the actual block it is the trace pairing
$$
 E_H(\delta\Phi(x),\delta\Phi(y))
 =\operatorname {Tr}_{E/\mathbb Q}
      \left(\frac{x\bar y}{N\mathfrak c\sqrt{-d}}\right).
$$
The trace dual of an ideal $J$ is $\mathfrak D_{E/\mathbb Q}^{-1}J^{-1}$, by the definition of the codifferent. Thus the alternating dual of $I$ in these coordinates is
$$
 I^\#=N\mathfrak c\sqrt{-d}\,\mathfrak D_{E/\mathbb Q}^{-1}\bar I^{-1}
     =\mathfrak c\,\bar{\mathfrak b}\,\mathfrak D^{-1}=I.
$$
Here $\mathfrak D_{K/\mathbb Q}=(\sqrt{-d})$ and $\mathfrak c\bar{\mathfrak c}=(N\mathfrak c)$. The full real dual is accounted for because the trace form is nonsingular and $I$ is a full integral lattice. In dimension one a period basis has alternating pairing $\pm1$. Products and the isometry therefore give $P^\#=P$ and $S^\#=S$.

The character matters. Put $Q(x)=\sqrt d\,H(x,x)/2$ and $\beta(x)=e^{\pi iQ(x)}$. The lattices are stable under $\lambda=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$. On either lattice $Q(x)=E_H(\lambda x,x)$ is integral. Moreover
$$
 Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)=2E_H(\lambda x,y)-E_H(x,y).
$$
Consequently $\beta$ takes values $\pm1$ and supplies precisely the sign needed when composing the translations. Unitarity makes its pullback on the fourfold lattice the product of the four block characters. [Quadratic character](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/QuadraticSemicharacters/ProductThetaMem.lean#L13-L26) · [Block normalization](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/KernelLattices/LateralTheory.lean#L20-L35)

On a single elliptic factor the source uses the odd theta section translated by $t_0$ and divided by its value at zero. Its sign on a period $w$ is the odd section's sign $\epsilon(w)$ times $(-1)^{E_H(2t_0,w)}$. Here $\epsilon(w)=1$ on $2\Lambda$ and $-1$ on its other three residue classes. In $\mathcal O_K/2=\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2$, write a class as $(a,b)$. Since $N\mathfrak c$ is odd, reduction identifies $\mathfrak c/2\mathfrak c$ with this ring and $Q(\delta x)=N(x)/N\mathfrak c$ reduces to $ab$. Its polar pairing is $ab'+a'b$. Multiplication by $(1\pm\sqrt{-d})/2$ gives the complementary idempotents. Requiring both to leave $t_0$ outside $\Lambda$ selects $2t_0=(1,1)$ modulo $2\Lambda$. On the four classes the translated sign is $\epsilon(w)(-1)^{a+b}=(-1)^{ab}$. Thus the same $\beta$ works on $P$, including on its intersection with $S$. The other two nonzero choices each fail this four-entry test. [The actual half-period test](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GL3Theta.lean#L198-L204) · [One-dimensional section and normalization](2026-11-23-cm-theta-torsion-trace.md)

## Recover each summand and its norm

Write $D_0=P\cap S$. The indices $[P:D_0]=[S:D_0]=n$ agree because self-dual lattices have equal symplectic covolume. With the release's convention
$$
 U_xF(z)=e^{-\pi H(z,x)-\pi H(x,x)/2}F(z+x),\qquad
 U_pU_x=e^{-2\pi i E_H(p,x)}U_xU_p.
$$
Let $F_0=\prod_{j=1}^{4f}\mathrm{th0}(z_j)$. It satisfies $\beta(p)U_p F_0=F_0$ for $p\in P$ and $F_0(0)=1$. In
$$
 G=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{x\in S/D_0}\beta(x)U_x F_0
$$
the product rule for $\beta$ makes each summand independent of its representative and gives $\beta(s)U_s G=G$ on $S$. Apply $\beta(p)U_p$ to the $x$ summand. Its eigenvalue is $e^{-2\pi iE_H(p,x)}$. Equal eigenvalues for every $p\in P$ force $x-x'\in P^\#=P$, hence the same coset. Averaging over $P/D_0$ with the inverse of any one character recovers exactly its summand. Applying the reverse normalized lattice average recovers $F_0$ itself. The reverse calculation starting at $S$ is identical. [Translations and signs](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/QuadraticSemicharacters/IsSemiChar.lean#L19-L44) · [Normalization and quotient](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/QuadraticSemicharacters/IsSemiChar.lean#L58-L69)

The squared norm is the **mean** over a period domain of $|F(z)|^2e^{-\pi H(z,z)}$. Passing to a finite cover preserves that mean for a periodic density. On the common cover the operations $U_p$ preserve the inner product by translating the Gaussian density. Distinct eigencharacters are orthogonal. Every translated summand has the original norm. This gives $\|G\|^2=n^{-1}n\|F_0\|^2$, with no loss depending on the index. Pullback by $g$ preserves the normalized mean as well. [Norm actually used](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L94-L98)

For completeness, the standard holomorphic theta space on $P$ is one-dimensional. In one variable divide any section by the normalized half-period section with its single simple zero. The ratio is elliptic and has at most one simple pole in a period cell. Its residue must vanish, so it is constant. Restricting one variable at a time gives the product result. The two inverse finite averages give the same dimension on $S$. Fix three blocks at a point where the pulled-back kernel is nonzero. The remaining slice is a nonzero one-block theta section. Its fourth tensor spans the same fourfold line, so a scalar fourth root makes it equal to $\kappa G\circ g$. Every solution differs by a fourth root of unity. This supplies an actual witness for `thMM`. [Choice predicate and tensor](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/KernelLattices/LateralTheory.lean#L20-L35) · [What the fourth root must satisfy](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/QuadraticSemicharacters/ProductThetaMem.lean#L43-L50)

Finally, $\kappa$ is a finite nonzero product of algebraic numbers of complex absolute value one at every embedding. Indeed the relative trace dual of $\mathfrak b^{-1}$ is itself by $\mathfrak b\bar{\mathfrak b}=\mathfrak D$. At a split $p$, the two local components of its Hermitian lattice are mutually dual. A basis in the first and the dual basis in the second identify it integrally with the standard split lattice. One can choose the isometry globally over $K$ with this same local property. The target is an open coset in the split unitary group. Pick $h_p$ there with $1+h_p$ invertible, possible because the excluded determinant cuts out a proper polynomial zero set in $GL_{4f}(\mathbb Q_p)$. Approximate the skew-Hermitian Cayley parameter $(1-h_p)(1+h_p)^{-1}$ by rational parameters in its $\mathbb Q$-linear space. The inverse transform stays in that coset. For almost all $p$, $g_0$ and its inverse are already integral on the two lattices, so the relative determinant is a local unit for **every** integral choice. In the finite remaining product each root raised to $2h(K)$ is $\pi/\bar\pi$ for a nonzero generator of a principal ideal power in $K$. Every conjugate of that ratio has absolute value one.
