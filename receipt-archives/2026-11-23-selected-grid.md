# What the selection of a different value at each prime costs

**23 November 2026.** A proof step bounds a scalar by choosing between two algebraic numbers at each finite place. Both numbers can spend their **entire** height allowances on the selected places. We computed the sharp coefficient on that part of the released grid estimate and tested the more restrictive product formulas as well. The published budget covers both contributions. In the arithmetic construction the choice is made above a **single rational prime**. Enforcing that restriction still lets the two allowances be used arbitrarily nearly in full. ## The inequality actually consumed

Work with zero ancillary errors in the numerical estimate, so the terms involving local denominators, exceptional sets and level exponents vanish. Write $B$ for the bound on each separate weighted sum of absolute valuations. There are $m^2$ cells. In one cell with half-rank $h\le N$, write $X_j$ for the common valuation plus its correction and $T_{b,j}$ for the two alternatives with that same correction. At each place one Boolean label $b(w)$ is used. The source bounds the scalar valuation by the two base valuations in absolute value plus
$$
 4\sum_{j=1}^{h}\big(|X_j(w)|+|T_{b(w),j}(w)|\big).
$$
The two base terms cost at most $2B$. For each $j$ the common term costs $B$ and the place-selected term costs at most $2B$, by summing separately on the two parts of the set of places. Thus the sharp height coefficient in this special case is $2+12h$ per cell. The published height coefficient is $2(2+8N)$ per cell, with an additional nonnegative $2N$ in its budget. [Inputs and coefficient in the aggregation lemma](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ExponentGrids/ExponentGridsProofs/GridSquaresIndexEstimate.lean#L14-L38) · [Final constants](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ExponentGrids/ExplicitSizeInequalityIndexed.lean#L13-L18)

For exact attainment in this aggregation lemma use $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3,i)$ and $a=4+\sqrt3$, $b=4-\sqrt3$, so $ab=13$. Their supports are distinct conjugation-stable pairs of primes in $K$. The finite absolute height of either $a^n$ or $b^n$ equals $B=n\log(13)/2$. In each cell use $a^n$ for both base terms and for the common positive profile, and use $a^n,b^n$ for the alternatives. At the primes of $a$ select the first alternative and at the primes of $b$ select the second. The single scalar $a^{(2+8h)n}b^{4hn}$ attains the displayed local upper bound at each prime and hence the asserted global cost. Conjugation preserves the selection in this example. The more restrictive selector in the arithmetic data is tested below.

## The reflected product

The release also contains a formulation with literal product identities before the pointwise inequality. In its notation put $h={\tt hh}$, $e={\tt ep}$, $u={\tt pp}$ and $v={\tt qp}$. Its integer constraints give $e,h-e\ge2(u+v)+2$. On the zero-error slice, the factors at nonpositive indices have unit correction. The ratio associated to index $s$ uses the positive index $1-s$ there. [Ranks, products and local inequalities](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ExponentGrids/ExplicitSizeInequalityIndexed.lean#L79-L123) · [Reflection formula](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ExponentGrids/ScaledCoordinatesValuationLemma.lean#L22-L26)

Set $H=h-u-v$ and $L=e+v-u$. In the bulk product the indices $1,\ldots,H$ occur once and reflection adds $1,\ldots,L$. The separate numerator appends $L+1,\ldots,e+v$. The separate denominator removes $e+1,\ldots,e+v$. Thus the net multiplicity of the positive ratio $y_j/x_j$ is exactly
$$
 {\bf1}_{1\le j\le H}+{\bf1}_{1\le j\le e}.
$$
In this specialization, $0\le v_w(y_j)+g_j(w)\le T_{b(w),j}(w)$. Each copy of $v_w(y_j/x_j)$ therefore costs at most $3B$ after summing over places. The two base terms cost at most $2B$. For the stated rank shape the sharp coefficient in the literal formula is $2+3(h-u-v+e)$ per cell. The maximum for $h\le N$, $N\ge4$, is $6N-4$, attained at $h=N,e=N-2,u=v=0$.

For attainment at any allowed rank shape, use the same $a,b$ as above, and put $A=a^n$, $C=b^n$. Take both base values equal to $A$, the common $x_j=1$, the gauge $g_j=-v_w(A)$, and the two comparison values $A^2,AC$. Set $y_j=A^2C$. On the selected primes its corrected valuation equals the selected comparison valuation. Use unit factors for the auxiliary products, take the bulk value as its stated product times $A$, set the first bottom factor to that bulk value and the second to $1/A$. All the literal equalities then hold, with scalar
$$
 A^2(A^2C)^{h-u-v+e}.
$$
All nonzero height hypotheses are met. In particular the common corrected profile has height $B$ even though its underlying number is one. This calculation tests the literal identities printed alongside the proof. The compiled dependency from the maximum-slope argument traverses the pointwise height estimate and a separate theorem proving its arithmetic inputs. ## The choice allowed at arithmetic inputs

The actual arithmetic record fixes the common comparison value at one. It sets the Boolean choice to true precisely at primes above the rational prime `pde`. Thus selecting different places of $F$ over 13 as in the first example would overstate the freedom of this selector. [The actual base, arithmetic values, and choice](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/FinitePrimesUnder.lean#L324-L342)

To test the stricter rule take two rational primes $181,61$, both one modulo 60. Let $A=181^{2k}$ and take $C$ the largest even power of 61 with $C\le A$. Choose the second alternative exactly at places over 61. For each place, the corrected valuations of $1,A^2,AC$ with gauge $-v_w(A)$ become $-v_w(A),v_w(A),v_w(C)$. The same product $A^2(A^2C)^{h-u-v+e}$ still works. All these ordinary inputs are positive rational squares. Since
$$
 \log A-2\log61 < \log C \le \log A,
$$
the ratio of the achieved cost to the sharp bound approaches one. The pointwise aggregation example can similarly use the same three corrected profiles and the corresponding scalar. Reinstating a fixed exceptional-prime term at these two primes adds a bounded quantity independent of $k$. These constructions test the base value and rational-prime selector. The actual products of toric periods, their characters and their arithmetic denominator data impose additional requirements.
