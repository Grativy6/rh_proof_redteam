# When the proof writes in the desired eigenvalue

**23 November 2026.** We found a literal match for Christopher D. Pang’s audit question. At the distinguished embedding, a zero spectral block receives the number $\exp(\ell)$, where $\ell$ is the requested logarithmic lower bound. At another place, the same convention applies when no qualifying rational vector exists. In the family used here the error multiplier on this bound is zero. The subsequent proof on that branch is just $\log(\exp\ell)=\ell$. Here is a paper check of whether this can pay for a **nonzero period direction** in the archimedean column estimate. ## A test that can expose a false transfer

Take $K=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2)$ and $G=\{1,s\}$ acting on $K^2$ by $s(x,y)=(x,-y)$. Take the Hecke operator $\mathrm{diag}(1,2)$ and retain both characters. At level 0 demand the trivial character of all of $G$. At level 1 impose the trivial subgroup. Both levels have positive dimension. The Hecke label $2$ occurs at level 1, on the second coordinate, and has no vector at level 0. Any scalar passes a test confined to its zero subspace at level 0. Its value at level 1 could be $1/2$ even if the vacant slot was assigned $1$. This example tests the distinction between the **two levels**, since mere nonemptiness of the spaces at each level misses it.

That distinction matters in the source. The absence test reads coordinates at `false`, the base level. The height calculation uses the column data at `true`. Both records literally take their scalars from the same function. At a nondistinguished place the column metric is constructed using the product of those scalars on each rational part. Its construction alone cannot tell us whether a scalar came from a measured vector. ## Which labels can carry a column?

Write $p$ for a spectral label, $b=0,1$ for the two levels and $Q_{b,p}$ for the rational row-coordinate subspace of label $p$ in the untwisted data. For the labels used by the period estimate, we obtain

$$
 Q_{1,p}\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q_{0,p}\ne0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{each lift index }j\text{ has a rational witness at every place.}
$$

For the first implication, work in the common rational space of forms before choosing a level. Intersect its selected subspace $Z$ with the sum of Hecke eigenspaces of label $p$ and with the allowed character subspace. Call this intersection $U_p$. It is stable under $G$. The selected $Z$ is stable under the group action, the allowed characters give a central projector, and **both** families of Hecke operators commute with $G$. In these coordinates $Q_{b,p}$ identifies with the $\psi_b$-character subspace of $U_p$ for the subgroup $P_b$. The spectral label and $Z$ are the same on both sides of this identification. Over an algebraic closure write $U_p=\bigoplus_s \tau_s^{\oplus m_{s,p}}$ after extending scalars. Only allowed irreducible types occur. Forming the range of a finite character projector preserves its dimension on extending the field, so this computes the rational dimensions as well. Put $n_{b,s}=\dim\{z:\tau_s(h)z=\psi_b(h)z\text{ for all }h\in P_b\}$. Taking the projector's trace gives

$$
 \dim_K Q_{b,p}=\sum_s m_{s,p}n_{b,s},\qquad
 n_{b,s}=\frac1{|P_b|}\sum_{h\in P_b}\chi_s(h)\psi_b(h)^{-1}.
$$

The release supplies $n_{0,s}=1$ for **every** allowed type. If the level-1 sum is positive, some $m_{s,p}>0$, so the level-0 sum is positive as well. In the proposed two-coordinate example the sign character gives $(1-1)/2=0$ at level 0. For a control where the hypothesis holds, take $G=S_3$, $P_0=\langle(12)\rangle$ with its sign character, and $P_1=\{1\}$. Two sign representations and three standard representations in one label give dimensions $5$ and $8$ respectively. In the release the trace identity comes from the finite Whittaker multiplicity of the selected characters. For the second implication, the base-level coordinate changes are bijective. They carry $Q_{0,p}$ onto the base row block and the rational lift space $Z_{j,p}$ onto its lift block. The two block dimensions agree for each $j,p$. Thus $\dim_K Z_{j,p}=\dim_K Q_{0,p}>0$. Take $0\ne x\in Z_{j,p}$. The base-coordinate test puts its image in the declared complex lift block, and the lift coordinate map is injective. The frame at **each** infinite place is invertible. Its Hermitian norm on the embedded $x$ is positive there. Consequently this same rational $x$ supplies the witness in the absence test at every other place, and the distinguished complex block is also nonzero. This use of equal dimensions concerns the actual rational parts. A complex eigenline can otherwise have no nonzero vector over the chosen field. ## Effect on the height estimate

For a nonzero rational column block at the untwisted level used by the height estimate, the fallback cannot supply any of its lift factors under the stated hypotheses. The rational column blocks span the quotient, so passing to complex coordinates adds no missing direction in this estimate. For a label with zero column block any scalar equation holds there. Including such labels in a minimum can only lower the minimum compared with taking it on the occupied labels. [Spanning condition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteForms/IndefSide.lean#L88-L96) · [Norm estimate using the scalar product](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/ConstantGeTwentyEight.lean#L123-L146) · [Minimum taken at each place](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/ConstantGeTwentyEight.lean#L156-L166)

A separate pitfall is to bound the geometric mean of each eigenvalue across all embeddings and then take the smallest one. With $u=3+2\sqrt2$, take two labels with values given by $u^N$ and $u^{-N}$ under the two complex places of $K$. Both labels have geometric mean one. At both places the smaller real value is $(3+2\sqrt2)^{-N}$. This discrepancy grows without bound on the logarithmic scale. The released hypothesis quantifies over the place and the label before the minimum. It still needs an analytic lower bound on every **occupied** block. ## Unresolved

The substantive remaining target on this route is the size of the lift on a nonzero vector, including the real formal-degree estimate and its transfer to other embeddings.
