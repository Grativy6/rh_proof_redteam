# A Selmer class that enters one coefficient level late

**24 November 2026.** On the zeta branch of the Alpöge–Claude release, an order-two global cohomology class can require coefficients of order four before it satisfies both local conditions. We construct the class on the proof’s own cyclotomic and quadratic fields. It supplies a nonzero term for **every positive odd twist** and disproves the pointwise predicate `VAt` on an explicit interval with certified prime inputs. The release’s theorem `VWinAt` chooses its threshold after $\epsilon$ and requires appropriately selected late intervals. The example identifies why that order matters. ## A sign at one dyadic place

Take distinct primes $q\equiv3\bmod8$, $d\equiv7\bmod16$, with $q\mid d+1$. For the trivial character and page number $e\ge2$, put $s=e+2$ and
$$E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^s})^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d}).$$
For the released `IsChiField` condition take cyclotomic modulus $2^s qd$. Fixing the real cyclotomic subfield forces the residue at $2^s$ to be $\pm1$, and fixing the two quadratic subfields gives exactly the two Jacobi conditions. The degree factors follow from the Chinese remainder theorem. [Galois stabilizer defining the field](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L19-L25) · [Field and layer definitions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/StmtFields.lean#L30-L49)

There are two places $v_1,v_2$ above 2. The real $2$-power cyclotomic field has a unique, totally ramified dyadic place. Adjoining $\sqrt{-q}$ there gives the unramified quadratic extension, since $-q\equiv5\bmod8$. Adjoining $\sqrt{-d}$ splits it, since $-d\equiv1\bmod8$. Both completions contain $\sqrt5$. The element $-1$ stays nonsquare in both completions. Adjoining $i$ to the real local cyclotomic field gives the missing, totally ramified quadratic step. The unramified quadratic extension cannot supply that step. Thus the rational dyadic unit squareclasses have just two images in either completion, represented by $1$ and $-1$.

We will exhibit $\alpha\in E^\times$ such that $E(\sqrt\alpha)/E$ is unramified away from 2, splits at $v_1$, and at $v_2$ is precisely the extension obtained by adjoining $i$. Write $\chi_\alpha,\chi_i:G_E\to\mathbb F_2$ for the two quadratic characters, with 1 indicating the nontrivial value. Their restrictions give this table.

| Character | At $v_1$ | At $v_2$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\chi_i$ | Nonzero | Nonzero |
| $\chi_\alpha$ | Zero | $\chi_i$ |
| $\chi_\alpha+\chi_i$ | $\chi_i$ | Zero |

### A fully explicit arithmetic input

Set $\epsilon=1/55$, $Y=2^{40}$, $q=3$ and
$$u=1+4\cdot3^{205},\qquad d=3u^2-4,\qquad \alpha=2+\sqrt{-d}.$$
The 656-bit $d$ is prime. It is $23\bmod48$. With $z=Y^{\epsilon/5}=2^{8/55}$, one has $1.10<z<1.11$, $z/2<\log3<z$, and $z(\log Y)^2/2<\log d<z(\log Y)^2$. For the last pair of inequalities use $2^{655}<d<2^{656}$ and $0.69<\log2<0.70$. There are no odd primes below $z$. This checks every clause of `WinPair`. At $x=Y$, the page inequality is satisfied with $e=86$ because $40(2+8/55)=944/11$. [Prime, page and scale conditions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L27-L42)

The primality certificate uses one short recurrence. Define $V_0=2,V_1=8,V_{j+1}=8V_j-V_{j-1}$. Modular doubling checks $V_{d+1}=2\bmod d$ and $\gcd(V_{(d+1)/r}-2,d)=1$ for $r=2,3$. To verify why this suffices, for any prime divisor $p$ of $d$ choose a root $v$ of $X^2-8X+1$ in $\mathbb F_{p^2}$. Then $V_j=v^j+v^{-j}$. The tests force the order of $v$ to divide $d+1$ and to contain its full 2- and 3-parts. In particular that order is divisible by $F=3(u-1)=4\cdot3^{206}$. Frobenius fixes $v$ or exchanges it with $v^{-1}$, so $F\mid p-1$ or $F\mid p+1$. Since $(F-1)^2>d$, no prime divisor can be at most $\sqrt d$. [Certificate and verification](check_dyadic_window_class.py)

In $D=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ the norm of $\alpha$ is $3u^2$. The odd-prime divisors of $\alpha$ and $\bar\alpha$ are disjoint since their sum is 4. Consequently every odd valuation of $\alpha$ is even except at one place above 3, where it is 1. Indeed any odd prime dividing $\alpha$ must split in $D$, and the rational norm gives the valuation at its unique contributing place. In $E$, the prime above 3 has even ramification index because $E$ contains $\sqrt{-3}$. Hence adjoining $\sqrt\alpha$ is unramified at every odd place of $E$. At odd residue characteristic a quadratic extension obtained from a unit is split or unramified. At the two places above 2 the roots of $-d$ reduce to 3 and 5 mod 8. Thus $\alpha$ reduces to 5 and 7 respectively. After adjoining $\sqrt5$ these are exactly the squareclasses of 1 and $-1$. This proves the table without computing a class group.

### Why this persists for every eligible prime pair

For general $q,d$ as above, $q$ splits in $D$ since $-d\equiv1\bmod q$. Its class number is odd. One proof of parity is to suppose $D$ had an everywhere finite-prime-unramified quadratic extension. Its normal closure over $\mathbb Q$ is a 2-extension, formed from the two conjugate extensions over $D$. Only $d$ ramifies at finite places, with inertia of order 2 because the extension over $D$ is unramified. A proper inertia subgroup lies in a maximal subgroup of this finite 2-group. That subgroup is normal of index two, giving a quadratic field over $\mathbb Q$ unramified at all finite primes. Its discriminant would be 1, which no quadratic field has. The Hilbert class field correspondence gives the asserted parity. [Hilbert class field correspondence](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-783-elliptic-curves-fall-2025/mit18_783_f25_lec21.pdf)

Take a prime ideal $\mathfrak q$ over $q$ in $D$, and let $\alpha$ generate its odd-order principal power $\mathfrak q^h$. Then $N_{D/\mathbb Q}\alpha=q^h$. Its only positive finite valuation in $D$ becomes even in $E$ on adjoining $\sqrt{-q}$. At the two dyadic places its values are rational 2-adic units, whose product has squareclass $q^h=-1$ modulo squares in the common completion. As the only images of rational unit classes there are $1,-1$, exactly one local value is a square. Interchanging the place labels gives the same table. This general argument applies even when the arithmetic selection has imposed additional goodness conditions on $q,d$.

## Why this is an element of the source’s group

For positive odd twist $k$, take $A=(\mathbb Q_2/\mathbb Z_2)(-k)$. The Galois action multiplies by $\chi_{\rm cyc}^{-k}$, as in the source. Write $S(E,A)$ for classes unramified at odd primes and zero in $H^1(E_v,A)$ at both dyadic places. This is the strict condition defining the released `Sha`. The absolute formulation imposes decomposition-group triviality above 2 and inertia triviality away from 2. The quadratic cocycle constructed here itself factors through the quotient unramified away from 2. [Coefficient and Sha definitions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GaloisCohomology/Statement.lean#L54-L80) · [Explicit local coboundary test](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/GaloisLibrary/GaloisCohomologyLength/LevelInertiaLeLevelDec.lean#L41-L65)

Consider the cocycle $z(g)=\chi_\alpha(g)/2$ with values in $A[2]$. This is a cocycle because $G_E$ fixes the order-two subgroup. At $v_1$ the local coboundary witness is 0. At $v_2$ the witness $1/4$ works because every local $g$ satisfies
$$(\chi_{\rm cyc}(g)^{-k}-1)/4=\chi_i(g)/2\pmod{\mathbb Z_2}.$$
This follows by reducing the cyclotomic character modulo 4 and using odd $k$. Thus $z$ gives a strict class with coefficients in $A[4]$ and in $A$.

It is nonzero globally. Since $i\notin E$, some $g$ acts by $3\bmod4$ before twisting, and still by $3\bmod4$ after the odd twist. Hence $A^{G_E}=A[2]$. If $z(g)=gb-b$ for a single global witness $b\in A$, then $2b$ is globally invariant and $4b=0$. The only resulting quadratic characters are zero and $\chi_i$. Neither is $\chi_\alpha$, by the table. The image $y_k$ is therefore of exact order 2.

**There is no strict lift at coefficient level 2.** Any other $A[2]$-valued lift of $y_k$ differs from $z$ by zero or $\chi_i/2$, by the same global coboundary calculation. Both lifts have a nontrivial dyadic restriction in the table. At coefficient level 2 the action is trivial, so neither nontrivial restriction is a coboundary there. At coefficient level 4 the two explicit local witnesses work. These levels are coefficient *orders* $2^1,2^2$.

In fact the obstruction has exactly two values. From $0\to A[2]\to A\xrightarrow{2}A\to0$, any class in $S(E,A)[2]$ has a global $A[2]$-valued lift. Each dyadic restriction of this lift is either zero or the local $\chi_i/2$ since the local invariants are again $A[2]$. A change of global lift toggles both entries at once. Away from 2, inertia acts trivially on $A$ and $2A=A$, so the lift restricts to zero on inertia. Thus the cokernel of $S(E,A[2])\to S(E,A)[2]$ injects into $\mathbb F_2^2/\langle(1,1)\rangle$. The displayed class has unequal entries and realizes its generator. The kernel of this map is zero, since the nonzero global coboundary character $\chi_i/2$ is visible at both places. Adding a single allowable global correction preserves the difference of the two local readings. [APCI criterion](../corpus/Interface.lean)

The [finite check](check_dyadic_window_class.py) exhausts cocycles on the quotient cut out by $i$ and $\sqrt\alpha$, imposing both local subgroups. It gives one strict class including zero at order 2, and two at order 4. Those sizes describe the finite quotient model. The argument with $2b$ above is what establishes survival in the full number-field cohomology group.

## A failed pointwise window and a necessary size bound

Take the singleton set of coefficient primes $\{2\}$ in `VVle`. If any required length is infinite, its explicit finiteness clause fails. Otherwise every positive odd $k<t$ contributes at least one to the length. Each odd twist indexes a distinct term of the required sum. A necessary condition for `VAt` at $t=\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor$ is consequently
$$(\log 2)\lfloor t/2\rfloor\le \exp(x^\epsilon/2).$$
At the certified $x=2^{40}$, the right side is less than $e<3$. Just $k=1,3,5,7,9$ contribute $5\log2>3$. For the entire interval $2^{40}\le x\le2^{80}$ the right side is less than $e^{3/2}<5$, while eight odd twists contribute $8\log2>5$. The proof works for every permitted page at these arguments. [Finiteness, singleton prime and weighted lengths](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L12-L17) · [Exact local predicate and late-window statement](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L39-L49)

At $\epsilon=1/55$ this obstruction actually excludes every $x\in[2^{40},2^{1053}]$ for the same field construction and all eligible prime pairs. For $b=\log_2x$ in this range, $\lfloor\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor/2\rfloor\ge x^{401}/4$ and $\log(\log2/4)>-2$. The convex function $2^{b/55}/2-401b\log2+2$ is negative at $b=40$ and $b=1053$. The integer certificate checks these inequalities by raising rational bounds to the 55th power. At $x=2^{1054}$ the lower bound from these odd twists alone fits inside the budget. No assertion of goodness follows at that next power.

There are even arbitrarily late counterexamples to a **uniform-in-$\epsilon$** reading. Fix any $J\ge2$ and the same $z=2^{8/55}$ and $q=3$. Take successively larger primes $d=23\bmod48$, which exist by Dirichlet’s theorem. Set $\log Y=\sqrt{3\log d/(2z)}$ and $\epsilon=5\log z/\log Y$. Then $0<\epsilon<1/52$ eventually and every `WinPair` clause holds at $Y$, including both size bounds for $d$. For $X=Y^J$ and any $Y\le x\le X$ the budget is at most $\exp(z^{5J}/2)$, fixed while $Y\to\infty$. The lower bound from odd twists diverges already at $Y$. No subwindow can deliver `VAt`. With $1\le J<2$ the required subwindow does not fit geometrically. This rules out choosing one $J,X_0$ to serve all $\epsilon$. It does not put arbitrarily late failures at a **fixed** $\epsilon$.

## The released inference and what remains

The actual `VWinAt` fixes $\epsilon$ and its page selector **before** choosing $J,X_0$. Its producer obtains explicit existential thresholds for absorbing the error terms and for the size of the two primes, then imposes them before applying the local bounds. For fixed $\epsilon>0$, $x^\epsilon$ eventually exceeds $802\log x$ by arbitrarily much. The lower bound above is consistent with that quantifier order. The separate dyadic contribution enters with a positive factor $\log2$ in `vvle_of_parts`. One of the release’s threshold conditions excludes this particular failure for a reason that can be checked directly. It obtains $q>2X^{401}+1$ and uses the same $q$ in `WinPair`. At every selected point $X^{1/J}\le x\le X$, the defining upper bound on $\log q$ implies $x^\epsilon\ge(\log q)^5$. For $X\ge2$ this gives $x^\epsilon/2>401\log X\ge401\log x$. The budget then exceeds $x^{401}$, which pays for the one unit per odd twist found here. For fixed $\epsilon,J>0$ the prime-size guard itself follows eventually from $\log q>X^{\epsilon/(5J)}/2$ and elementary power-versus-logarithm growth. The varying-$\epsilon$ control keeps that last power constant, and the protection fails exactly there. We also tested the exact finite-level comparison used by the cohomology proof. The source’s filtration $P_r$ requires a cocycle killed by $2^r$ **and local coboundary witnesses killed by the same power**. It proves that the $P_r$ exhaust the infinite group. Our $y_k$ lies in $P_2\setminus P_1$ despite having order two. The construction takes a common bound on both the cocycle values and the finitely many local witnesses. The downstream comparison invokes that filtration and an eventual cardinal identity. Equating the first filtration step with *all* two-torsion would give a false result on the released fields. The class also survives restriction to the full cyclotomic tower. After adjoining $i$ it is a nontrivial, everywhere unramified quadratic character split at the dyadic places. Those places are totally ramified in the remaining cyclotomic layers. A [separate calculation](2026-11-24-dyadic-class-in-the-tower.md) proves its survival there and computes which finite-coefficient classes really vanish in a direct limit.

The open question is whether the eventual arithmetic upper bound pays for everything else in those groups, especially the twists set aside by the cyclotomic tower estimate. We have obtained a counterexample to the named pointwise predicate at specified inputs and a nonzero lower bound on **every** successful zeta window. A counterexample to the released implication would still require a failure under the quantifiers it actually uses.
