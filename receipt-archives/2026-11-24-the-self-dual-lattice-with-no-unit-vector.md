# A self-dual lattice with no global vector of norm one

**24 November 2026.** On every sufficiently late trivial-character zeta host, the actual curve-ideal scaling prescription admits a self-dual lattice which represents 1 at every completion and has **zero** global vectors of norm one. A chosen dyadic ideal measures the discrepancy. Its order is at least $\lceil\log_2((d+1)/4)\rceil$ as the host prime $d$ grows. We can read the two opposite valuations in the arithmetic map and identify the separate place where the published construction takes a standard lattice.

## Keep the ideal while changing the height scale

Let $m\ge2$, $q\equiv3\bmod8$, $d\equiv7\bmod16$ be the host primes, and write
$$K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d}),\qquad E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^{m+2}})^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d}),\qquad F=E^+.$$
The degree of $E/K$ is $2^{m+1}$. Take $d>7$. The prime 2 splits in $K$. Write $\mathfrak p$ for one prime above it. Choose the sign of $\sqrt{-d}$ so that $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$ lies in $\mathfrak p$. Its norm is $(d+1)/4$, of exact 2-adic valuation one. Thus
$$\mathfrak j=(\omega)/\mathfrak p,\qquad N\mathfrak j=(d+1)/8$$
is integral and coprime to $2d$. For the reference ideal $\mathfrak c_0$ take $\mathfrak c=\mathfrak j\mathfrak c_0$. It satisfies the source's curve-ideal condition whenever the reference does. Since $\sqrt2$ belongs to the real cyclotomic subfield, the explicit scale $r=\omega/\sqrt2$ satisfies
$$r\bar r=N\mathfrak c/N\mathfrak c_0.$$
At every infinite place its absolute value is $\sqrt{(d+1)/8}$. Multiplying the generators in `bInv` identifies its fractional ideal.
$$J=r\,\overline{\mathfrak c\mathcal O_E}\,(\mathfrak c_0\mathcal O_E)/(N\mathfrak c)
  =r\,(\mathfrak c_0\mathcal O_E)/(\mathfrak c\mathcal O_E)
  =\mathfrak p\mathcal O_E/(\sqrt2).$$
In rank $a$ the source takes $J^{\oplus a}$. In particular $J\bar J=\mathcal O_E$, so its Hermitian dual in the unit Gram space is itself. At the two dyadic places the valuations of $J$ are $+2^{m-1}$ and $-2^{m-1}$. The ramification index over $\mathbb Q_2$ is $2^m$, the residue degree two, and the two places are distinguished by the split quadratic $K$. There are no other nonzero valuations. The normalized logarithm of the denominator norm is just $\tfrac14\log 2$. It stays fixed while the ideal class can have arbitrarily large order. ## Why every late host has this effect

The class number of $K$ is odd. For a direct proof, an order-two class is invariant under conjugation. If $\bar I=tI$, then $t\bar t=1$, and Hilbert 90 changes $I$ to a conjugation-invariant fractional ideal in the same class. An invariant ideal is generated by rational ideals and ramified prime ideals. The only ramified prime is $(\sqrt{-d})$, which is principal. This also proves injectivity of $\mathrm{Cl}(K)\to\mathrm{Cl}(E)$ by extending and taking the norm back, which raises a class to the power $2^{m+1}$.

Let $h$ be the order of $[\mathfrak p]$ in $K$. If $\mathfrak p^n=(\lambda)$ for $n>0$, the generator cannot be rational since the valuations at the two dyadic primes differ. With $\lambda=(x+y\sqrt{-d})/2$ integral and $y\ne0$, we have
$$2^n=N\lambda=(x^2+dy^2)/4\ \ge\ (d+1)/4.$$
So $h>1$ and has the claimed lower bound. The class of $J$ has this same order in $E$. Furthermore $J^h$ has a generator of norm one, namely $\lambda/(\sqrt2)^h$ for a generator $\lambda$ of $\mathfrak p^h$. Thus the central finite-idele translation by $J$ has exact order $h$ modulo rational norm-one scalars and local units.

Locally, a uniformizer to power $2^{m-1}$ on one dyadic side and its inverse on the conjugate side give a norm-one isometry taking the standard lattice to $J^{\oplus a}$. At other finite places the identity works. Globally, suppose a vector $x\in(J^k)^{\oplus a}$ has $\sum_i x_i\bar x_i=1$. Each term belongs to $\mathcal O_F$ and is nonnegative at every real place. If nonzero its conjugates all lie in $(0,1]$, so its positive integer norm forces every conjugate to equal 1. Exactly one coordinate is nonzero. For that coordinate $(x_i)J^{-k}$ is integral and its product with its conjugate is the unit ideal, so $J^k$ is principal. This is impossible for $0<k<h$. Consequently the central translates of the standard lattice give **$h$ distinct global isometry classes in every rank $a\ge1$**. This also works when $h\mid a$, in which case their determinant class alone can be trivial. On the first moved lattice $\sqrt2$ in a single coordinate has norm 2. Thus the least positive *rational integer* norm changes from 1 to 2.

There is a quantitative count even on a different class $\Lambda$ in the standard genus. For normalized vectors $x\in J^i\Lambda$, $y\in J^j\Lambda$, the pairing belongs to $J^{i-j}$. Its absolute value is at most one at each embedding by Cauchy–Schwarz. The same norm argument proves orthogonality if $i\ne j\bmod h$. At a single twist any two nonorthogonal normalized vectors span the same line and differ by a root of unity. There are therefore at most $a$ such lines across the whole $h$-term list, counting a vector at each twist where it appears. In particular the class-mass mean of the number of norm-one vectors is at most $a|\mu_E|/h$ because central translation preserves stabilizers and permutes the genus. An integral isometric frame of length $k$ needs $k$ lines, so at most $\lfloor a/k\rfloor$ of the $h$ twists in this list can contain one. At the standard lattice the vector bound is attained across its orbit.

## Which classes the archimedean average can see

The same argument works across the full ideal class group of $K$. For any integral ideal $I$ of norm prime to $2d$, its positive rational norm is a relative norm from $E$ by the quadratic argument in the [norm receipt](2026-11-24-the-local-norm-that-does-not-compose.md#which-norm-the-source-uses). Pick $r_I\bar r_I=N I$. The norm-one fractional ideal $(r_I)/(I\mathcal O_E)$ defines a class modulo principal norm-one ideals. Its class is independent of the choice and multiplicative, and the map from $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$ is injective by the extension–norm calculation. Every class has a representative away from $2d$. Among these twists of the standard lattice exactly the identity class represents one. The average over these CM classes is precisely $a|\mu_E|/h_K$.

The class orbit is available even when embeddings are required to fix $E$. The Hilbert class field of $K$ has generalized dihedral Galois group over $\mathbb Q$, with conjugation inverting the odd class group. Its intersection with the abelian host $E$ is $K$. The first main theorem of complex multiplication therefore gives all $h_K$ ideal-lattice shapes over a fixed embedding of $E$, with equal multiplicities in any normal coefficient field containing the class values. The [earlier CM height audit](2026-11-23-theta-height-at-the-cusp.md) uses this class averaging. This statement identifies the CM shapes and the result of applying the scale construction to them. ## Follow the same map at the finite places

The scalar identity gives the correct Gaussian length at every embedding. For the integral map one also has the exact fractional-ideal identity
$$r^{-1}(\mathfrak c\mathcal O_E)=(\mathfrak c_0\mathcal O_E)\,J^{-1}.$$
The inverse $J^{-1}$ is precisely the entry ideal of $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_E}(J^{\oplus a},\mathcal O_E^{\oplus b})$. Multiplication by the same different or period factor preserves this equality. In particular the two opposite dyadic valuations cancel against the moved source lattice. Replacing it by the standard lattice would give a nonintegral entry on one side. ## Unresolved

The norm-one coefficient is genuinely erased, and the global class order grows on the true hosts. The arithmetic scale and the finite period inclusion retain the lattice responsible for both facts. A failure at a subsequent rank or height estimate would need that estimate to use a specific missing coefficient or to omit its applicable index.
