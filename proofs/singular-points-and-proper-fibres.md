Could ramified probes miss a singular point? Could taking all the probes still allow a divisor on a blowup to masquerade as a pullback? Here are a singular ring with an exact cutoff, a blowup where a single probe rejects the false pullback, and a paper proof of the local separation used by the release.

## A singular ring with an exact precision cost

For $n\ge2$ let
$$A_n=\mathbb Z[x,y]_{(2,x,y)}/(xy-2^n),\qquad D=(x),\qquad U=D(2).$$
Use the identity proper morphism. This is a normal arithmetic surface. It is a hypersurface domain, hence Cohen–Macaulay, and regular away from the closed point of its local spectrum since one of $x,y$ is then invertible or the point is on the characteristic-zero hyperbola. Thus it satisfies Serre’s two normality conditions. The function field is $\mathbb Q(x)$. The largest norm attained by an element of the maximal ideal at a centred trait with $\delta=v_2(y)$ is $2^{-\min(1,\delta,n-\delta)}$. Here $0<\delta<n$ and $v_2(x)=n-\delta$. Every rational slope in this interval occurs over a finite extension.

Let $I_e$ be the germs passing the source’s approximate-divisibility inequality at every such trait, with base maximal-ideal error of exponent $e\ge1$. Modulo $(x)$ the local ring is $(\mathbb Z/2^n)[y]_{(2,y)}$. After clearing a unit denominator write a numerator as a polynomial in $y$. A term with nonzero coefficient of valuation $i<n$ and power $v$ passes precisely when
$$ i+v\delta\ \ge\ \min(n-\delta,e,e\delta)\quad\text{for every }0<\delta<n. $$
At $e\le n-1$ this says $i+v\ge e$. To see it, the right side increases as $e\delta$ up to $\delta=1$, stays at $e$ up to $n-e$, then decreases. At $e\ge n-1$ the peak is at $\delta=n/(e+1)$, and the condition becomes $(e+1)i+nv\ge ne$. In both cases checking the peak suffices because $i,v\ge0$ and both sides are linear on each segment. These criteria also test whole polynomials term by term. At the critical slope choose $y=2^\delta\xi$ with $\xi$ an unramified unit whose residue avoids the finitely many roots of the lowest-weight polynomial. Then $x=2^n/y$ gives the required centred trait. Adding a multiple of $x$ preserves membership in $I_e$.

In the quotient by $(x)$ ordinary order for a monomial is $i+v$. For $e\le n-1$ the smallest order passing all the probes is $e$. For $e\ge n-1$ it is $n-1+\lceil(e-n+1)/n\rceil$, attained at $i=n-1$. Thus the least $e$ such that $I_e\subset(x)+\mathfrak m^k$ is
$$ e_{\min} =
\begin{cases} k,&k<n,\\ n(k-n+1),&k\ge n.\end{cases} $$
For $n=3,k=5$ the first sufficient exponent is 9. The term $4y^2$ has ordinary order 4 and passes at exponent 8. At exponent 9 the arc $v_2(y)=3/10$ gives $v_2(4y^2)=26/10$, below the required $27/10$. ## A divisor on a proper fibre that cannot descend

Blow up $(2,T)$ on the arithmetic line over $\mathbb Z$, then blow up the point $T/2=0$ on the first exceptional curve. Write $E_1'$ for its strict transform and $E_2$ for the second exceptional curve. The valuations along these curves assign weights $(1,1)$ and $(1,2)$ respectively to $(2,T)$. For $a,b>0$, $aE_1'+bE_2$ is generated by a pulled-back base ideal if and only if $a\le b\le 2a$. Indeed for any base germ with first order $a$, its second order lies between $a$ and $2a$, by inspecting the terms of its polynomial numerator and clearing a unit denominator. Any generating ideal must attain the minimum along each exceptional curve. Conversely, in this interval the three generators
$$T^a,\quad 2^{\,2a-b}T^{\,b-a},\quad 2^b$$
attain the specified orders on the three adjacent charts. On the charts with coordinates $(T,2/T)$, $(u,v)=(T/2,4/T)$ and $(2,T/4)$ respectively, the pulled-back ideal is generated by $T^a$, $u^bv^a$ and $2^b$. In the middle chart $uv=2$ and $u^2v=T$. Hence at a centred arc of slope $s=v_2(T)>0$ the order of a local equation for this divisor is $as$ for $s\le1$, $a(2-s)+b(s-1)$ for $1\le s\le2$, and $b$ for $s\ge2$. The same equation orders apply to effective $a,b$ outside the descent interval by using the local equations of the exceptional curves.

Try $a=1,b=3$. Choose the closed point on $E_1'$ where $T/2=1$ in the residue field. Every reference trait centred there has $v_2(T)=1$ and reads order 1. Any base germ $F$ reproducing this reading belongs to $\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$. At exponent 1, $F=T$ satisfies the approximate condition everywhere using the allowed error $T$. At exponent 2 one arc rejects **every** candidate. Set $T=2^{4/3}$. Then $u=2^{1/3},v=2^{2/3}$ and $u^3v$ has order $5/3$. Every element of $\mathfrak m^2$ has order at least 2 there. A numerator in $\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$ has order either $1$ from its constant term or $4/3$ from its unit linear term. Higher terms cannot cancel the smaller valuation. A unit denominator preserves it. This fails the exact and the allowed approximate inequality. Unramified arcs alone would admit $F=T$ at this exponent since integer slopes at least 2 admit the error $2^2$. The false pullback has therefore been detected using precisely the release’s test. ## Why algebraic probes separate completed germs in the normal case

Let $A$ be the local ring at a closed point of an integral normal arithmetic model of finite type over $\mathbb Z$, with fraction field of characteristic zero, maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and finite residue field of characteristic $p$. Write $R=\widehat A$, a normal local domain by excellence, and fix a nonzero local divisor equation $f$. Choose a nonzero $s\in A$ so the locus where $s$ is nonzero in a local affine neighborhood lies in the required dense open. Every local map $A\to\mathcal O_{K'}$, for a finite extension $K'/\mathbb Q_p$, keeping $s$ nonzero gives an admissible centred trait.

Suppose $G\in\bigcap_e I_e R$. At such a trait each generator of the finitely generated $\mathfrak m$ has norm strictly less than 1. Their maximum $\rho<1$ bounds the norm of every nonunit in $A$ there. The map extends to $R$ by completeness of $\mathcal O_{K'}$. At every $e$, writing $G$ as a finite $R$-linear combination of elements of $I_e$ gives $|G|\le\max(|f|,\rho^e)$. Thus $|G|\le|f|$ at each admissible trait. This takes the limit at one fixed probe at a time.

If $G\notin fR$, put $u=f/G$ and $B=R[u]_{(\mathfrak m_R,u)}$ inside the fraction field. The indicated localization exists with the same residue field as $R$. In fact a polynomial relation on $u$ with unit constant term would, on reversing its coefficients, make $G/f$ integral over $R$. Normality would force $G/f\in R$. Hence reduction of the constant term defines a homomorphism $R[u]\to R/\mathfrak m_R$ sending $u$ to zero, with the stated kernel. In $B$, $f=Gu$ with $u$ in its maximal ideal. [Source construction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsDominatingLocalDomainEqMulMaximal.lean#L55-L98)

Here is why this ring has an algebraic probe keeping $h=Gs$ nonzero. Its completion is flat over $B$, so $ph$ is a nonzerodivisor. Quotient the completion by a minimal prime to obtain a complete local domain $C$ where $ph$ survives and the residue field is finite. Choose a system of parameters $p,t_1,\ldots,t_{d-1}$ in $C$. Then $S=\mathbb Z_p[[T_1,\ldots,T_{d-1}]]\to C$, $T_i\mapsto t_i$, is finite and injective. Indeed the parameter quotient is a finite $\mathbb F_p$-vector space. Lifting a basis and successively expanding modulo powers of the parameter ideal generates $C$ as an $S$-module by completeness. The equal dimensions of the two domains force injectivity. Finite integrality gives a nonzero element $c\in S\cap hC$, for example by the minimal polynomial of $h$ over the fraction field of $S$ and integral closedness of $S$.

Specialize $T_i=p^N a_i$ with ordinary integer $a_i$. Choose them so the lowest-total-degree nonzero homogeneous part of $c$ is nonzero there before scaling. For large $N$ that part has strictly smaller valuation than all higher degrees, so $c$ remains nonzero after specialization. The same claim is immediate if $d=1$. Lift the kernel prime of this specialization to $C$ by lying over. The resulting quotient is finite integral over $\mathbb Z_p$, with fraction field a finite extension $K'/\mathbb Q_p$. Its image lies in $\mathcal O_{K'}$. The composite map is local since the unique maximal ideal of $C$ lies over the maximal ideal of $S$ and the map to the residue field of $\mathcal O_{K'}$ contracts to it. At this probe $h=Gs$ is nonzero and $|u|<1$. It gives $|f|<|G|$, contradicting the previous paragraph. This proves $\bigcap_e I_eR\subset fR$ for the stated models. For finite precision pass to $R/fR$. The descending ideals have zero intersection there because $f$ belongs to each $I_e$. Their images stabilize in every quotient by a power of the maximal ideal, which has finite length. The stabilized images at consecutive precisions map onto one another. Any nonzero stabilized image would lift to a nonzero element of the complete module lying in every one of the closed ideals. Thus for each $k$ some $I_eR$ lies in $fR+\mathfrak m^kR$. Faithful flatness gives $I_e\subset (f)+\mathfrak m^k$ in $A$. The [proper-fibre argument](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-the-ramification-cost-in-divisor-descent.md) uses completed local vanishing at all closed points above the base point and the theorem on formal functions. [Completed finite-precision statement](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsLeSpanSupPow.lean#L79-L94)

Normality has observable content. In $\mathbb Z[t^2,t^3]_{(2,t^2,t^3)}$ take $f=t^2,F=t^3$. For every centred valuation-ring arc one has $F^2=f^3$, hence $|F|\le|f|$. But $F\notin(f)+\mathfrak m^2$. Modulo $(2,t^2)$ its image is the nonzero generator of $\mathbb F_2[t^3]/((t^3)^2)$ and the image of $\mathfrak m^2$ vanishes. Here $F/f=t$ is integral and missing from the ring. The release assumes normality on this passage. ## Unresolved

These tests justify discarding the inference that increasing ramification by itself makes this local divisibility criterion unsound. They still require an approximate **base** germ at every requested precision, with equality at a specified reference trait and the released inequality simultaneously on the other traits. The construction of those germs from same-level theta data on the chosen global model is the remaining point of contact with the genuine coefficient discrepancy.
