## A counterexample after any finite cutoff

Fix $u=(3-\sqrt5)/2$. The embedding $\rho$ that exchanges the two roots of $X^2-3X+1$ sends $u$ to $u^{-1}>1$. It can even fix an imaginary quadratic host such as $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ after adjoining $\sqrt5$. For any prescribed integer $r\ge0$ let

$$
f_r(z)=\sum_{n>r} u^{n^2}z^n .
$$

The root test proves $f_r$ is entire. Its Taylor polynomial through order $r$ is zero, and every coefficient is an algebraic integer in the same quadratic field. After conjugation, the magnitudes of the terms at $z\ne0$ are $(u^{-n}|z|)^n$. They tend to infinity. At zero the sum converges to zero. With the release's summation convention, the value of the conjugated series is consequently **zero everywhere**. No use of the out-of-field fallback is involved. This example works in any nonempty coordinate space by using just one coordinate. [Pinned Mathlib summation convention](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/InfiniteSum/Defs.lean#L130-L147)

There is a second failure on the same series. The conjugate of $1+f_r$ has value 1 at zero and 0 at every other point of the complex line. Thus the unrestricted operation fails additivity and can destroy continuity. Polynomial truncations of $f_r$ converge uniformly to $f_r$ on compact sets in the original embedding. Every truncation has a polynomial conjugate, while the conjugated tail has radius zero. This is a counterexample to using finite jet agreement or membership in one number field to transport convergence.

The release's general Taylor-recovery lemma explicitly requires the conjugated function to be differentiable and nonzero somewhere. The first example fails the nonzero premise; the second fails differentiability. To see why both premises matter, a nonzero continuous totalized sum has a point with nonzero value and all coordinates nonzero. At that point the defining sum must converge. Its absolute series dominates the series on a smaller open polydisc about zero. ## What frames the actual theta tail

The genuine theta sections obey a lattice translation law. On the complex line, for a nonzero period $\omega$ in a positive Hermitian metric $H$, a nonzero section obeys

$$
|G(z+k\omega)|=|G(z)|\exp\!\big(\pi k\,\mathrm{Re}\,H(z,\omega)+\tfrac{\pi}{2}k^2H(\omega,\omega)\big).
$$

Our perturbation $f_r$ cannot satisfy this identity. Writing $c=-\log u>0$ and completing the square in $-cn^2+n\log |z|$ gives $\log^+|f_r(z)|=O(1+\log^2(1+|z|))$. The translation identity forces quadratic growth in $k$ from any point where the section is nonzero. Adding $f_r$ to a theta section with the same multiplier would require the difference $f_r$ to satisfy the same law. This rejects the perturbation at the original embedding, by an argument independent of Lean. [The form, translation and theta-space definitions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L78-L91)

The proof also needs that law on the **receiving** side. Its record `CjF` requires two entire functions and equality of all Taylor coefficients after conjugation. With that witness in hand, the entire Taylor expansion of the receiving function identifies it with `fnConj`. The construction starts with an actual odd theta function on a lattice having the conjugated Weierstrass invariants. The transported discriminant is nonzero. A nondiscrete subgroup has nonzero periods accumulating at zero, so its fourth- and sixth-power reciprocal sums both diverge and totalize to zero. For a cyclic subgroup $\omega\mathbb Z$ the two values are $g_2=4\pi^4/(3\omega^4)$ and $g_3=8\pi^6/(27\omega^6)$, which have zero discriminant as well. The nonzero discriminant excludes both types of spurious subgroup. Two paper checks locate the remaining freedom in that construction.

1. The transported formal equation for the normalized Weierstrass series $P$ is $(X P'-2P)^2=4P^3-g_2X^4P-g_3X^6$, with $P(0)=1$. Suppose two solutions first differ by $eX^n$ for $n\ge1$. Subtracting gives $-4(n-2)e=12e$ in that degree, hence $(n+1)e=0$. Over the coefficient field of characteristic zero every putative first difference is excluded. This checks the whole tail by coefficient order. [First-coefficient argument](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/GaloisTaylorSeriesCanonicalOddThetaAll.lean#L81-L107)
2. An odd theta function normalized to have derivative 1 at zero can still be multiplied by $e^{az^2}$ without changing its zeros or that normalization. A nonreal complex multiplication endomorphism $\alpha$ gives a further identity with $N=|\alpha|^2$. The quotient $G(\alpha z)^2/G(z)^{2N}$ is an elliptic function of $z$. The twist multiplies that quotient by $\exp(2a(\alpha^2-N)z^2)$. If the twisted quotient is also elliptic, translation by a nonzero period forces $a(\alpha^2-N)=0$: divide off the original quotient away from its zeros and differentiate the exponential ratio in $z$. The release takes $\alpha^2=-d$ and $N=d>0$, eliminating precisely the free quadratic factor. Ordinary integer multiplication by $m$ has $\alpha^2=N=m^2$ and would leave this test blind. ## A genuine theta section can still concentrate

Convergence by itself gives no uniform height estimate. Here is a test with the same one-dimensional theta multiplier and an actual period lattice. Let $d=16j+7$, $j\ge0$, $y=\sqrt d/2$, $\tau=1/2+iy$ and $\Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau$. Put

$$
\theta_2(z;\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{\pi i\tau(n+1/2)^2+2\pi i(n+1/2)z},
\quad G(z)=e^{\pi z^2/(2y)}\theta_2(z;\tau).
$$

The series converges normally on compact sets. For $1$ and $\tau$ its two translation signs in the release's Gaussian coordinates are $-1$ and $+1$. Indeed $\theta_2(z+1)=-\theta_2(z)$ and $\theta_2(z+\tau)=e^{-\pi i\tau-2\pi i z}\theta_2(z)$. For a general period $\omega=m+n\tau$, the sign is $(-1)^{m+mn}$, matching $(-1)^{|\omega|^2}$ since $|\omega|^2=m^2+mn+((d+1)/4)n^2$ and $(d+1)/4$ is even. The multiplier therefore has the required arithmetic sign. [Multiplier and Gaussian normalization](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L78-L91) · [Normalization of the integer quadratic form](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L327-L328)

Let $H(z)=|G(z)|^2 e^{-\pi|z|^2/y}$. In the area-normalized coordinates $z=s+t\tau$, $0\le s,t<1$, integration in $s$ kills the unequal frequency pairs. Integration in $t$ joins the remaining unit intervals, giving

$$
\langle H\rangle=\int_0^1\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z} e^{-2\pi y(n+1/2+t)^2}\,dt=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2y}} .
$$

At $z=-\tau/2$, $H(z)=|\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{\pi i\tau n^2}|^2\ge1$: terms at even $n$ have positive real values and terms at odd $n$ are positive imaginary. Thus $\sup H/\langle H\rangle\ge\sqrt{2y}=d^{1/4}$. These tori have unit covolume after rescaling coordinates by $1/\sqrt y$. Their shortest nonzero squared length in those coordinates is $1/y$. The rank, covolume and principal polarization alone permit unbounded pointwise concentration, even for sections with the specified translation law.

The release's supremum estimate explicitly requires that squared length to be at least $1/v$. On this family it requires $v\ge y$, so its right side grows. In the arithmetic application, integrality of the positive form at every nonzero lattice point supplies a minimum, and the field parameter $\mathrm{rd}_E$ bounds the resulting scale. The test exposes a necessary dependence on shape; the release has retained it. The one-dimensional example tests the analytic estimate and its multiplier. ## Unresolved

The unrestricted conjugation operation loses entire tails, and uniform point evaluation fails over thin tori if their shape is suppressed. In the inspected calculation the all-orders complex multiplication identity, the conjugated theta section and the shortest-vector bound address those failures. A refutation of the released argument still requires a mismatch on its selected arithmetic data. For the finite-place analogue, an algebraic factor may have unit modulus at every complex embedding and still have a denominator at a finite place. The release imposes an additional locally normalized form on the standard vacuum. Whether the chosen normalization realizes all its finite-place conditions is a distinct question.
