## The two coefficient spaces

Work over a field $F$ of odd characteristic. In the application $F=k((T))$, with $k$ finite. Write $S=M_2(F)\times F$ for the block-diagonal matrices, and $W=W_+\oplus W_-$ for the upper-right and lower-left rectangles. The coefficient ring has symbols $e_i,f_i$ for $1\le i\le g$ and $h$, with
$$e_i f_j=\delta_{ij}h,\qquad e_i e_j=f_i f_j=0,$$
and $h$ kills all positive-degree symbols. Inside the matrix ring put
$$ A=S\oplus\bigoplus_i e_i W,\qquad J=hS\oplus\bigoplus_i f_i W.$$
Here $J^2=0$. Taking the coefficient of $h$ in the matrix trace gives a perfect pairing $\langle j,a\rangle$ between $J$ and $A$: opposite block matrix units pair to one in the matching coefficient slots. Thus $J$ is $A^*$ as an $A$-bimodule. [Multiplication](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/PatchingModules/PairedDualFirstOrderDualMatrixSpace.lean#L108-L150) · [Block spaces](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/PatchingModules/PairedDualFirstOrderDualMatrixSpace.lean#L192-L226)

Let $r:G\to S^\times$ and let $\kappa_i$ be ordinary adjoint cocycles with values in $W$. Put
$$ R(g)=\left(1+\sum_i e_i\kappa_i(g)\right)r(g). $$
Let $H\trianglelefteq G$ be the testing subgroup. The release uses the kernel of the $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character. Its image under $r$ spans $S$ over the Laurent series field. Its ambient group has an involution $\sigma$, with $r(\sigma g)=\mu(g)r(g)^{-t}$ and $\kappa_i(\sigma g)=-\kappa_i(g)^t$. The $\kappa_i$ are independent modulo odd coboundaries with skew matrix primitive. A character $\chi:G\to F^\times$ is trivial on $H$, nontrivial on $G$, and $G/H$ is abelian. For the release $\chi$ is the reduction of the cyclotomic character. ## Why an induced rank-two block cannot slip into this test

There is a direct check of the full-block spanning condition at the release’s eligible characteristic-$\ell$ two-plus-one curves. In the original ordering the corner occupies positions 0 and 2 and the middle character occupies position 1. Remove the common scalar twist. The residual semisimplification of the corner is $\bar\Psi\oplus\bar\Psi^{-1}$. At the marked element $g_0$, subtract the matrix for $g_0^\star$ from that for $g_0$. The corner of the result is diagonal with entries $v,-v$, where $v$ reduces to $\bar\Psi(g_0)-\bar\Psi(g_0)^{-1}\ne0$. The normalization kills its off-diagonal entries and the polarization reverses its two diagonal entries. An invariant line must therefore be one of the coordinate axes. The two surviving off-diagonal entries required by the even type exclude both axes, possibly with different group elements. This proves absolute irreducibility over the Laurent series field. Suppose the corner admitted a nontrivial quadratic self-twist $\eta$. Reducing its characteristic polynomials forces $\eta$ to swap the two residual characters: distinct characters are linearly independent as functions, and a nontrivial $\eta$ cannot fix a character under multiplication. Hence $\eta=\bar\Psi^2=\bar\Psi^{-2}$. At each tested local decomposition group the frame has an element with $\bar\Psi^2\ne1$. The self-twist must also swap the two characters of the local ordinary corner, so their ratio is $\eta$. On $N_v=I_v\cap\ker\epsilon$, $\bar\Psi$ is trivial. The two local characters would agree on all of $N_v$. The eligible curve requires every pair to be separated there. This rules out the self-twist at the same local group. The local ordinary flag gives the corner's two characters by deleting the middle constituent. A common scalar twist cancels from all ratios. Here is the corresponding image argument. If the testing subgroup $H$ had exactly one invariant line, its normality would make that line invariant under $G$. If it had exactly two, irreducibility forces $G$ to permute them transitively. Acting by $+1,-1$ on the two lines intertwines the corner with the nontrivial quadratic character of the permutation, giving the excluded self-twist. Three invariant lines force $H$ to act by scalars. Then the projective image of $G$ is abelian since $G/H$ is abelian. It preserves the one or two fixed lines of any nonidentity projective element, with the same contradiction. Thus the corner stays absolutely irreducible on $H$. Its matrix span there is $M_2$ by the matrix-algebra density theorem. An algebra projecting onto both $M_2$ and the middle scalar field is their entire product: otherwise projection onto $M_2$ would be injective and there would be a unital homomorphism $M_2\to F$. This independently supplies the block span on precisely the subgroup used in the trace calculation.

## Why the subgroup spans the first-order algebra

Each of the two families of restrictions $\kappa_i^+|_H$ and $\kappa_i^-|_H$ is independent modulo ordinary coboundaries in its rectangle. Indeed the rectangle has no $H$-invariants, since commuting with $r(H)$ entails commuting with both block idempotents. If a global cocycle restricts to a coboundary, subtract that coboundary. Normality then forces every value of the remaining cocycle into the zero invariant space. If a combination of upper parts is a global coboundary with primitive $Y$, the polarization relation gives primitive $-Y^t$ for its lower part. Together these give a skew odd primitive, contradicting the stated independence for any nonzero combination. The lower case follows by transposition.

Now extend $R$ linearly to $F[H]$. Let $K$ be the kernel of the projection $F[H]\to S$ via $r$. Its image under $R$ is an $S$-bimodule in the square-zero odd radical. In one rectangle any sub-bimodule of $g$ copies is the rectangle tensored with a coefficient subspace: multiplication by matrix units separates and transports each entry. If that coefficient subspace were proper, a nonzero linear combination of coordinates would kill $K$. The corresponding map on the group algebra, $x\mapsto(\sum c_i\kappa_i(x))r(x)$ on group elements and extended linearly, would factor to a derivation of $S$ into the rectangle. All such derivations are inner. This would make the corresponding upper or lower cocycle a coboundary on $H$, just excluded. Hence the image contains the whole odd radical, and the surjective diagonal projection gives $R(F[H])=A$.

For the inner-derivation fact, sum $E_{i1}\otimes E_{1i}$ over the two matrix blocks using a separate first index in each. Write the sum as $\sum x_j\otimes y_j$. Then $\sum x_jy_j=1$ and $\sum sx_j\otimes y_j=\sum x_j\otimes y_js$ for $s\in S$. For a derivation $D$, $Y=\sum x_jD(y_j)$ gives $D(s)=sY-Ys$. This works in every characteristic.

## All the invisible directions on $H$

Consider an ordinary adjoint cocycle $z:H\to J$ for $R$. Its observed number at $s\in H$ is $\langle z(s)R(s),1\rangle$. Suppose every such reading vanishes. Extend $\delta(s)=z(s)R(s)$ linearly to $F[H]$. The cocycle law makes $\delta$ a derivation relative to $R$. For $x\in\ker R$, applying the zero-trace condition to $xy$ for arbitrary $y\in F[H]$ gives $\langle\delta(x),R(y)\rangle=0$. The perfect pairing and full image give $\delta(x)=0$. Hence $\delta$ descends to a derivation $A\to J$. Subtract an inner derivation to arrange $\delta|_S=0$, using the matrix-unit argument above.

The remaining map on the radical is an $S$-bimodule map. It preserves each rectangle. On the upper block it takes $e_i X$ to $\sum_j C_{ij} f_j X$ for some $g$-by-$g$ matrix $C$. On the lower block write the corresponding matrix as $D$. The product of an upper and a lower odd element in $A$ is zero. Its differentiated product gives $C_{ij}+D_{ji}=0$, so $D=-C^t$. Conversely these prescriptions give a derivation for **every** $C$, with trace zero. Further inner derivations vanishing on $S$ have primitive in $h$ times the two block scalars, and commute with all of $A$. Thus the kernel modulo inner derivations is exactly $M_g(F)$. This also proves the result for the original group because $F[H]$ surjects onto $A$.

For an explicit invisible change, in the upper entries of $R$ replace $e_i$ by $e_i+\sum_j C_{ij}f_j$, and in the lower entries replace it by $e_i-\sum_j C_{ji}f_j$. Products of opposite new coefficient entries are still zero. The new matrices multiply correctly and their difference from the old matrices has zero trace. Their relative cocycle has as its upper $f_j$ class $\sum_i C_{ij}[\kappa_i^+|_H]$. A nonzero column therefore gives a genuine change modulo coboundaries. When the involution preserves $H$ and the cocycle obeys $z(\sigma g)=s z(g)^t$, $s=\pm1$, the surviving matrices satisfy $C=s C^t$. To verify this, transpose the two off-diagonal coefficient formulas and use the independence just proved. Conversely the substitutions obey the same sign: their mixed $h$ coefficient in the relative cocycle is $-\sum_j\lambda_j\kappa_j$ for the $f_j$ parts $\lambda_j$, and $\sum_j(\kappa_j\lambda_j+\lambda_j\kappa_j)=0$. For the positive sign, the untwisted subgroup kernel includes every symmetric $C$.

## What an element outside $H$ buys

Suppose $z$ is defined on $G$ with the actual action $g\cdot Y=\chi(g)R(g)YR(g)^{-1}$. On $H$ it is an ordinary cocycle. Its upper $f_j$ coefficient, taken in $H^1(H,W_+)$, transforms under ordinary conjugation by $a\in G$ with eigenvalue $\chi(a)^{-1}$. This follows by expanding $z(a^{-1}ha)$ in the cocycle law and removing a coboundary. Each $[\kappa_i^+|_H]$ is fixed by this action since $\kappa_i^+$ is a globally **untwisted** cocycle. Choose $a$ with $\chi(a)\ne1$. The combination from column $j$ must be zero, and independence forces that entire column to vanish. Doing this for each column eliminates all of $C$.

The restriction of $z$ is now inner. Subtract the corresponding twisted coboundary on $G$. The remaining cocycle vanishes on $H$, so it factors through $G/H$ with values in $J^H$. The full algebra span gives $J^H=h(FI_2\oplus F)$: the block idempotents kill the off-diagonal coefficients of an invariant, and commutation with each matrix block makes its diagonal scalar. On this space $G/H$ acts by $\chi$. For a cocycle $b$ on the abelian quotient, commuting $a$ and $x$ gives
$$(\chi(a)-1)b(x)=(\chi(x)-1)b(a).$$
This too is a coboundary. If the required transpose sign is $s$, replace the primitive $Y$ by $(Y+sY^t)/2$. Finally a common power of $T$ clears the finitely many Laurent-series denominators in that primitive. This proves the detection implication at the two-plus-one input with the release’s exact coefficient localization. ## A direct check on the actual two-by-one block sizes

The [field-arithmetic check](../receipt-archives/check_component_and_dual_trace.py) constructs the matrix-unit algebra $A$ and solves directly for all bilinear forms $\beta(x,y)=\langle\delta x,y\rangle$. It imposes $\beta(xy,z)=\beta(y,zx)+\beta(x,yz)$ and $\beta(x,1)=0$. It separately constructs the inner forms and the displayed $C$-modes. Over both $\mathbf F_3$ and $\mathbf F_{11}$ the dimensions are

| $g$ | All invisible derivations | Inner derivations | Extra directions |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 3 | 3 | 0 |
| 1 | 8 | 7 | 1 |
| 2 | 15 | 11 | 4 |
| 3 | 24 | 15 | 9 |

These are dimensions on the subgroup before imposing a transpose sign. The proof over the field $k((T))$ and the character argument account for the global twist.

## Unresolved

Under the stated spanning, independence and cyclotomic hypotheses this trace test is earned. The global numerical upper bound on the Selmer space at the chosen simultaneous tests remains consequential: losing one independent coefficient can split components in the [patched-ring example](two-determinant-tests-at-a-component-intersection.md).
