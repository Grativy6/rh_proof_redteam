At residue size seven, a space of dimension **456** and another of dimension **343** each give a projected space of dimension **36**. Although three divides 36, reduction modulo three preserves all 36 directions in the integral regular-representation calculation. The prime seven sees a different answer.

## The map that can return the identity

Write $v_0$ for the distinguished finite place and $w$ for the chosen prime above it in the CM field. This place splits, has residue characteristic $p\ne\ell$, and is separate from the tame-type places. Write $K=\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_w)$ and $K_1=\ker(K\to\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf F_q))$. The group called `Ub` requires its $w$-component to belong to $K_1$. The function `CaseU.sec` uses an inverse when projection onto all of $\mathrm{GL}_n(F_w)$ is bijective. Otherwise it sends everything to the identity. At this $w$, for $n\ge1$, the matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\varpi,1,\ldots,1)$ lies outside $K$, so surjectivity really fails. [Place hypotheses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/WeilGroupCharacters/ContinuousResP.lean#L82-L100) · [Group and fallback](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/HeckeAlgebras/IsTheta.lean#L75-L92)

The dimension comparison applies `sec0`, whose target is the larger group of adelic components away from $\ell$. Here is its inverse construction on paper. Identify the two completions over the split place using conjugation. The identity Hermitian form requires the components to be $(g,(g^{-1})^{t})$. Given $g$, put that pair at $v_0$, and put the identity everywhere else. It is an adelic unitary matrix, with finite support away from the identity. Conversely, on those two places the second component is forced by the first. The same construction works independently at every member of the finite tame set. Projection from the subgroup supported at these places is therefore bijective. Its inverse followed by projection at $w$ returns $g$. For example, $I+E_{12}$ returns $I+E_{12}$ when $n\ge2$. For $V.S'=s.S$, any extra prime advertised by `QData` would have to lie both in and outside $s.S$, so the variable auxiliary tuple is empty. ## An integer matrix with a sharp denominator

Let $U$ be the upper unitriangular group over $\mathbf F_q$, $n\ge1$, $d=n-1$, $j=n(n-1)/2$, and $N=q^j=|U|$. In $\mathbf Z[U]$ put
$$
 C=\sum_{u\in U}c(u)[u],\qquad c(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\big(q{\bf1}_{u_{i,i+1}=0}-1\big).
$$
Choose a nontrivial additive character $\psi$ of $\mathbf F_q$. The $d$ simple upper entries add under multiplication. For $a\in(\mathbf F_q^\times)^d$, the functions $\chi_a(u)=\psi(\sum_i a_i u_{i,i+1})$ are distinct characters. Summing $\chi_a(u)^{-1}$ over $a$ gives $c(u)$ by the elementary additive character sum in each coordinate. Character orthogonality now gives $C^2=NC$ and makes $C/N$ the sum of the projections onto those character spaces. The equality for $C^2$ holds integrally by comparing group coefficients. Lifts of the finite matrices to $K$ act independently of lift on $K_1$-fixed vectors. [Integer coefficients in the release](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/BuildingsResidueChars/ExistsInjectiveGenericUnipotentCharacters.lean#L160-L173) · [Choice of lifts](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/CuspidalDescent/GLDenseRanges/InertiaPartMaps/MapInj.lean#L86-L106) · [Unipotent cardinality](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/BuildingsResidueChars/CardUnipotentUpperEqPow.lean#L63-L67)

**Theorem.** In the integral regular representation the nonzero Smith factors of $C$ are $q^s$, each with multiplicity $\binom ds(q-2)^s$ for $0\le s\le d$. The remaining $q^j-(q-1)^d$ entries are zero. The least positive integer that makes $C/N$ integral in this representation is $N$.

**Proof.** Map $U$ onto its $d$ simple upper entries. Every fibre has $h=q^{j-d}$ elements. In the regular basis the entry in row $x$, column $y$, is $c(xy^{-1})$. Order the basis by the image in $\mathbf F_q^d$ and then by the element in that fibre. The whole matrix is the tensor product of $d$ copies of $L=qI_q-J_q$ with $J_h$, where $J_r$ has every entry one. This uses the actual nonabelian multiplication through the homomorphism to the simple entries.

The matrix $J_h$ has one unit Smith factor. For $L$, let $A$ be the lattice of coordinate sum zero with basis $e_i-e_q$, $i<q$. The differences $L(e_i)-L(e_q)$ give $qA$, and $L(e_q)=-\sum_{i<q}(e_i-e_q)$ is primitive in $A$. Thus the image of $L$ is $qA$ plus that primitive line. Since $A$ is a direct summand, the Smith entries of $L$ are $1$, then $q$ repeated $q-2$ times, then $0$. Tensor the integral basis changes. Choosing $s$ factors of size $q$ gives exactly the displayed multiplicities and their divisibility ordering. Finally, an element with all simple entries nonzero has coefficient $(-1)^d$, so the denominator $N$ is attained. This also works for $d=0$. The single factor $L$ is the classical complete-graph Laplacian. [Jacobson, Niedermaier and Reiner on complete-graph critical groups](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~reiner/Papers/CritComplete.pdf)

For $n=3,q=7$, the 343 columns give one factor $1$, ten factors $7$, twenty-five factors $49$, and 307 zeros. Modulo three the rank is 36. Modulo seven it is one. In fact $C$ modulo the residue characteristic is a signed all-ones matrix on the regular space. The Hida coefficient ring in the release inverts $q$ because $p\ne\ell$. On any module where $N$ is a unit, $C/N$ is an idempotent and its image is a direct summand. Identifying the dimension on the particular local representation requires one more argument. ## Exactly one line for each character

Let $F$ be the local field with residue field $\mathbf F_q$. Let $V$ be a smooth complex representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ with $V^{K_1}\ne0$ and a Kirillov functional. Explicitly, for the chosen generic character on $U(F)$ there is a linear functional $\lambda$ with $\lambda(uv)=\chi(u)\lambda(v)$, and the functions $W_v(g)=\lambda(gv)$ on the mirabolic $P$ separate vectors. Here $P$ consists of the matrices with last row $e_n^t$. Normalize the additive character to be trivial on $\varpi\mathcal O$ and nontrivial on $\mathcal O$, and lift the entries of $a$ to units. The generic characters are obtained from one another by diagonal conjugation (the last diagonal entry can be one). A change of conductor is absorbed in the same diagonal ratios. [Exact hypotheses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/CuspidalDescent/GLDenseRanges/KirCusp.lean#L33-L68) · [Bernstein and Zelevinsky, §§5.16–5.21](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~bernstei/Publication_list/publication_texts/B-Zel-RepsGL-Usp.pdf)

**Existence.** Pick a nonzero $v\in V^{K_1}$. Some $W_v(p)$ is nonzero for $p\in P$. Eliminate the last column by a left upper unipotent, and use upper triangular Iwasawa decomposition on the leading block. This writes $p=uAk$ with $u\in U(F)$, $A=\operatorname{diag}(A_i)$, $A_n=1$, $k\in P\cap K$. For $v'=kv$, we have $W_{v'}(A)\ne0$ and $v'$ is still $K_1$-fixed. For each $i$ test $I+yE_{i,i+1}$ on the right for $y\in\varpi\mathcal O$. Equivariance on the left forces $\psi(a_i A_i y/A_{i+1})=1$ for all such $y$, hence $A_i/A_{i+1}\in\mathcal O$. This uses the conductor ideal, the largest fractional ideal on which $\psi$ is trivial.

Every $k_1\in K_1$ factors by triangular elimination as $u_+ b u_-$ with upper and lower unipotents and diagonal $b$, all congruent to the identity. The upper factor contributes character one. The inequalities just obtained give $A^{-1}b u_-A\in K_1$. Consequently $\lambda(k_1 A v')=\lambda(A v')\ne0$. Average $A v'$ over $K_1$ with total mass one. Smoothness makes this a finite average on the vector. It yields a fixed vector with the same nonzero reading. Projecting it onto the chosen $\chi_a$ by the finite character average still preserves that reading.

**Uniqueness.** Suppose $v$ is in this character space inside $V^{K_1}$. Put $I_U=U(\mathcal O)K_1$. Then $W_v$ is left equivariant for $U(F)$ and right equivariant for $I_U$. On $P$ its possible nonzero values reduce to representatives $Aw$ where $w$ permutes the first $n-1$ coordinates and fixes the last one. Indeed, the preceding Iwasawa decomposition leaves a compact leading block. Reduce that block modulo $\varpi$, use finite Bruhat decomposition, absorb the upper triangular factors on the left and the upper unipotent on the right, and absorb diagonal units in $A$. Right factors inside $P$ belong to $I_U\cap P$.

If $w(i+1)>w(i)+1$, conjugating the right simple-root subgroup at $i$ by $Aw$ lands in a nonsimple upper root. Its left character is trivial while its right character is nontrivial. Thus $W_v(Aw)=0$. Any permutation ending in $n$ whose ascents all have size one is the identity. Its final consecutive run ending in $n$ cannot have a predecessor: that predecessor would either extend the run or jump over a value. With $w=1$, testing each simple root for every $y\in\mathcal O$ gives $A_i/A_{i+1}\in1+\varpi\mathcal O$. Thus $A\in K_1$. All surviving values on $P$ are determined by $W_v(1)=\lambda(v)$, and the separating property gives dimension at most one. For $n=1$, $P$ and $U$ are trivial, so separation and $V^{K_1}\ne0$ give the result directly.

Summing the lines gives the rank $(q-1)^{n-1}$ on this local representation. The global input used in the release is a local component of a cuspidal $\mathrm{GL}_n$ representation. For a cuspidal automorphic representation of the general linear group, the classical Whittaker expansion gives a nonzero global Whittaker functional. Fixing the other factors of a pure tensor on which it is nonzero gives a nonzero local functional here. Bernstein and Zelevinsky’s mirabolic restriction theorem gives its separating property. The release multiplies the resulting local rank by the rank for the tame type and by a global count. [Shalika on cuspidal forms](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1974/100-2/p01) · [Mirabolic restriction, §5.21](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~bernstei/Publication_list/publication_texts/B-Zel-RepsGL-Usp.pdf) [Local-to-global use](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/TZEqDimE.lean#L69-L103) · [Release's Kirillov argument for cusp forms](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/CuspidalDescent/GLDenseRanges/InertiaPartMaps/KirCusp.lean#L173-L190)

## Two spaces at the same prime

For $n=2$ a generic unramified principal series has $K_1$-fixed space $\mathbf C[\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_q)]$. The Steinberg representation gives its quotient by constants. The unipotent group translates the affine line and fixes infinity. Every nontrivial additive character occurs once, hence the projector has rank $q-1$ in both the $(q+1)$- and the $q$-dimensional spaces.

For $n=3$ take the complete flags in $\mathbf F_q^3$ and the first homology of the incidence graph of its points and lines (the finite Steinberg representation). Its vertices number $2(q^2+q+1)$ and its edges number $(q+1)(q^2+q+1)$. Connectedness gives homology dimension $q^3$. These are the finite models obtained at principal congruence level for the unramified principal series and Steinberg. One can see this by restricting their full- and partial-flag induction models to $K$, reducing modulo $\varpi$, and taking $K_1$-invariants by averaging. The flag quotient by the two partial-flag pullbacks is dual to the homology model.

In the full flag space the open Bruhat cell is a free $U$-orbit. For any other cell its permutation $w$ has some $i$ with $w^{-1}(i)<w^{-1}(i+1)$, so its stabilizer, up to $U$-conjugacy, contains the whole simple root at $i$. A generic character then contributes zero on that orbit. In a point or line orbit the stabilizer also contains a simple root up to $U$-conjugacy. Indeed a two-block flag orders at least one consecutive pair of indices weakly increasingly between its blocks (for three indices and two blocks a strictly decreasing sequence is impossible). Both vertex spaces and the constants have zero generic part. Applying the idempotent to the graph's chain sequence now gives the same rank $(q-1)^2$ on flags and on homology. At seven this is $456\longmapsto36$ and $343\longmapsto36$.

As a field-level check on the local primes used in this example, take $E=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-19},\sqrt{-47})$ and $F^+=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{893})$. The three quadratic discriminants are $-19,-47,893$. Their product is $893^2$, so the biquadratic discriminant formula and the discriminant tower give no finite ramification in $E/F^+$. At seven the radicands reduce to the squares $2,2$ modulo seven. At three they have opposite quadratic symbols, so $F^+$ has residue degree two and splits upstairs at its place over three. Taking $p=7,\ell=3$ and the empty tame set realizes the field and place conditions. This provides local arithmetic inputs. A pair of global RH points realizing these two representations would require further data.
