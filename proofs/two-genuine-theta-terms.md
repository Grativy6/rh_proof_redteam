## A theta family with computable phases

Work at $p=2$. Let $g$ be the class of $X$ in $\mathbb F_{32}=\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^5+X^2+1)$, let $\zeta$ be its Teichmüller lift of order 31, and set $d=\zeta^{20}$. First take $\pi=2$. On the two-dimensional split torus use the period generators $(\pi^2,\zeta)$ and $(\zeta,\pi^2d^2)$. Their pairing is symmetric with positive-definite valuation matrix. These give an abelian surface because raising the generators to the 31st power gives the period lattice of two Tate curves with parameter $\pi^{62}$, and passing to the indicated lattice takes a quotient by a finite torsion subgroup. The identity map from the period lattice to the character lattice gives its principal polarization. [Totally degenerate abelian varieties and their theta series](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08212#page=1)

Take its eighth power with the polarization matrix $B=W_8+W_8^t$ from the release. Write $\langle x,y\rangle=x^tBy$ and $|x|_B^2=\langle x,x\rangle$ on $\Lambda=\mathbb Z^8$. In torus coordinates $z,w$ the theta series is
$$\Theta(z,w)=\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda} A(x,y)\,z^{Bx}w^{By},\qquad
A(x,y)=\pi^{Q(x,y)}d^{|y|_B^2}\zeta^{\langle x,y\rangle},\quad
Q(x,y)=|x|_B^2+|y|_B^2.$$
The powers use two vectors of eight torus coordinates. The form $Q$ is exactly `qW` for $W_8$ and the rank-two form $2I$ in the $\pi$-valuation. Absolute values of terms tend to zero on each bounded valuation region. Translation by the period of $(s,t)$ multiplies a monomial by $A(x+s,y+t)/(A(x,y)A(s,t))$. Reindexing gives the multiplier $A(s,t)^{-1}z^{-Bs}w^{-Bt}$. This verifies the theta equation and symmetry directly. The determinant of $B$ is one, so the Fourier labels $B(x,y)$ and $(x,y)$ are integrally equivalent. Use a base torsion order $b=7^5$, which has the released shape $rN$ with $r=7,N=(7^2)^2$. Take a deeper multiplier $m=bM$, initially $M=49$. Its line-bundle power is $m^2$. In the torus trivialization its distinguished section is $\Theta(z^m,w^m)$, as the same period equation shows. Project it using the character of exponent $mBc$ on the $m^2$-torsion unit coordinates. The average of a monomial is one when $(x,y)\equiv c\bmod m$ and zero otherwise, by the finite geometric series. If the action is indexed by $r'm^2$-torsion through its $r'$th power, the kernel repeats each term equally and the normalized average agrees. All these group orders are 2-adic units. At an integral torsion lift with $m$th power $\kappa$, evaluation is thus
$$F_m(c,\kappa)=\frac{1}{\Theta(1,1)}
       \sum_{(x,y)\equiv c\ (m)} A(x,y)\kappa^{(Bx,By)}.$$
We use the identity torsion value to fix the common scalar. It is $1\bmod\pi^2$, so it is a unit. This explicitly fixes the geometric targets used in the experiment. ## A tie on the actual matrix

Take $r=e_0,\ s=-e_2$ in the release’s numbering. Their squared lengths are 2 and $\langle r,s\rangle=1$. In the residue class of
$$x_+=b\bigl((M-1)r/2+s\bigr),\qquad y=bs,$$
the two shortest pairs are $(x_+,y)$ and $(x_-,y)$ with $x_-=x_+-mr$. Their common valuation is
$$H=b^2(M^2+7)/2.$$
For a proof put $v=2s-r$, so $v\perp r$ and $|v|_B^2=6$. Twice any candidate first coordinate divided by $b$ has the form $M(r+2h)+v$. The only norm-two vectors in $r+2\Lambda$ are $r,-r$. Distinct norm-two vectors differing by a nonzero element of $2\Lambda$ are at least $\sqrt8$ apart, attaining this maximum only for opposites. All other squared lengths in this class are $2\bmod4$ and at least 6. Their squared lengths after scaling exceed the proposed minimum because $6(M-1)^2>2M^2+6$. In the second coordinate $s$ is the unique shortest representative of its class since $M>3$. The positive-definite even lattice has no nonzero vector of squared length below 2. A separate exact enumeration of all 240 roots checks the first minimization at $M=49$.

Every index in this class is divisible by $b$, so the factor $\kappa^{(Bx,By)}$ is 1 for **every** unit torus point of order dividing $b$. The two leading unit exponents are
$$b^2\bigl(40+(3+M)/2\bigr)=7\bmod31,\qquad
  b^2\bigl(40+(3-M)/2\bigr)=22\bmod31.$$
The equalities modulo 31 hold for $M=7^{2+15j}$, $j\ge0$, because 7 has order 15 modulo 31. The two normalized leading residues $g^7,g^{22}$ sum to 1. There is even a two-digit check at $\pi=2$. In the basis $1,X,\ldots,X^4$ of $(\mathbb Z/4)[X]/(X^5+X^2+1)$, take $\zeta=X^{32}$, its order-31 lift. Then $\zeta^7+\zeta^{22}=1+2X+2X^4$. All other terms are at least two integer valuations higher because $Q$ is even. For the first level $m=823543$, $H=340100199796$. The calculation uses only exponents and finite-field operations. PARI/GP independently gave 240 roots and the same polynomial modulo 4.

## What the reconstruction does to those two terms

Feed these $F_m$ into `classSum_of_terms` with $s=\pi$, $n(x,y)=Q(x,y)$ and $q(x,y)=-\log|\pi|_2\,Q(x,y)$. They satisfy every numerical premise. Any class with a unique shortest vector attains its upper bound at every unit torsion evaluation. In the tied class remove one of the two shortest vectors. The trial sum of the remaining $\pi^{Q}$ terms, divided by $\pi^H$, reduces to 1. So does the true target. Their difference is strictly below the leading size. At $\pi=2$ it is exactly one digit below it. This conclusion is independent of the chosen $\kappa$ and of which minimum was removed. [Exact norm and sum premises](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/LatticeClassSumsOfTermsPrescribedNorms.lean#L52-L97) · [Branch that changes the other minimum](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/LatticeClassSumsOfTermsPrescribedNorms.lean#L150-L190)

The reconstruction now installs $\pi^H u$ at the second minimum, where $u^2=u+1$. The two new leading residues are $\bar u$ and $1-\bar u$ in $\mathbb F_4\setminus\mathbb F_2$. Neither is one of the true residues in $\mathbb F_{32}\setminus\mathbb F_2$. Adjoining the full seven-power torsion still does not put these golden residues in the field of the geometric example. The unramified degrees for $\mu_{7^a}$ are $3\cdot7^{a-1}$ and the degree for $\mu_{31}$ is 5. Further seven-power roots of $\pi$ need only odd ramification. The seven-power roots of the period units of order 31 already exist in $\mu_{31}$. All these residue degrees are odd. The numerical lemma explicitly allows $\overline{\mathbb Q}_2$. Its output meets that specification.

Refine to $7m$ on the **same** theta series. The two vectors separate, and each is the unique shortest in its own refined class. The true refined sums reduce separately to $g^7,g^{22}$ after division by $\pi^H$. Summing the old reconstructed terms in either finer class instead gives one of the two golden residues. The difference has absolute value **$|\pi|_2^H$** in either class. The genuine sums themselves are exactly additive on refinement, since the character projectors partition the original series. This is a geometric counterexample to identifying the constructed terms with the original Fourier terms or to reusing a coarse witness for a finer class. The example realizes the analytic projector and the numerical lemma. Whether the specific global compactification needs the discarded readings remains open.
