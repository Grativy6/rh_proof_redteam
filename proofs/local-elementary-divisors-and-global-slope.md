**23 November 2026.** Two sets of arithmetic rows below have the same reference tuple, the same four selected places, the same signed index at each tuple, and the same local elementary divisors of the row lattice at every prime. One glued lattice has maximum slope **zero**, the other **$2n\log13$**, for arbitrary positive $n$. Both satisfy the released structural and numerical row conditions through `IndefSide` and `Untwisted`. This breaks the inference from those data alone to a maximum-slope bound. The release asks separately for a global injective map and pays for it at finite and infinite places. Here that price has a sharp lower bound. ## What drops out of the signed index?

At a finite prime $w$ let $m_w,t_w$ be the valuations of the determinant ideals of two full lattices $M,T$ in fixed coordinates. Write $\bar w$ for the conjugate prime. Let $H$ be a nonsingular Hermitian matrix on $T$'s space and $\theta$ an invertible matrix taking it to the coordinates of $M$. Applying the source definitions gives
$$
 I_w=t_w+v_w(\det\theta)-m_w,\qquad
 j_w(H,T)=-v_w(\det H)-t_w-t_{\bar w}.
$$
The second formula follows by taking the Gram determinant of a basis valid at **both** conjugate primes. The first uses the signed length difference at the single prime. Consequently the sum used as `Aw` at the chosen tuple equals
$$
 I_w+I_{\bar w}+j_w
 =v_w(\det\theta)+v_{\bar w}(\det\theta)-v_w(\det H)-m_w-m_{\bar w}.
$$
The lattice $T$ cancels exactly. This identity is valid even for determinant ideals which are globally nonprincipal, since the calculation takes place at a pair of primes. ## Two rows with the same place selection

Take $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3,i)$, $O=O_K$, $q=13$, $\pi=3+2i$, with $q=\pi\bar\pi$. All four primes over $q$ have residue cardinality 13 and ramification index one. Fix $n>0$. Compare
$$
 A_n=\operatorname{diag}(q^{-n},q^n)O^2,\qquad
 B_n=\operatorname{diag}((\bar\pi/\pi)^n,(\pi/\bar\pi)^n)O^2.
$$
At every prime over 13 the sorted elementary divisors of each inclusion relative to the reference lattice $O^2$ have exponents $(-n,n)$. At other primes both exponents are zero. In $A_n$ the expanded direction is the same rational axis at all four primes. In $B_n$ the expanded axis switches at the conjugate primes. Both determinant ideals are $O$.

Use two tuples $0,1$, with $0$ the reference, one type and one index $j$. All vector spaces have dimension two. All frames, Gram matrices and coordinate maps including $P_q,\iota,\theta$ are identity matrices. Take the full subspace for the single type, $K_0=K$, and either complex place for the distinguished embedding. Set the fixed $M=O^2$ and put
$$
 M_t(0)=O^2,\quad T_l(0)=qO^2,\qquad M_t(1)=T_l(1)=L,
$$
where $L=A_n$ in the first instance and $L=B_n$ in the second. Choose $\kappa=\lambda=1$, with zero errors. At a prime above 13 the reference tuple has $I_w=2$, $j_w=-4$, and $I_w+I_{\bar w}+j_w=0$. Outside 13 all these numbers vanish. The reference-place test reads the individual nonzero indices as well as their conjugates. With $F_0=0$ it therefore selects precisely the four primes above 13, for **both** examples. Choose tuple one there and tuple zero elsewhere. At tuple one each of $I_w,j_w$ is zero for both lattices. The chosen tuple is invariant under conjugation. To check the rest of the rows, take the fields $F_0,J_0,E_1,l_3,l_6,\mathrm{la},\mathrm{de}$ of the numerical record all zero and let $\mathrm{hh}=H\ge0$. The one-type decomposition is direct and spanning. All scalar-on-type metric identities have factor one, and all specified lattices are full. The fixed lattice $O^2$ has normalized log covolume zero. The sum on the left of `ON` at each selected prime is zero. On every unselected prime `Aw` and $F_0$ vanish. This verifies `WF`, `IndefSide` and `Untwisted` for the displayed data. ## The glued lattice retains a common direction

Ordinary prime-local intersection gives `RR` equal to $L$ in both cases. The selected lattice already agrees with $O^2$ everywhere else. $B_n$ is globally isometric to $O^2$ because its two diagonal multipliers have absolute value one at every infinite place. The standard lattice has maximum normalized slope zero. For a rank-one submodule, use a generator over the principal ideal ring $O$. The Euclidean covolume is $3\prod_\tau\sum_i|\tau u_i|^2$, over the two complex places, for a generating vector $u$. The product is at least the absolute norm of any nonzero coordinate, hence at least one. A full-rank integral sublattice has covolume at least $9$. The coordinate line attains zero in the source normalization. The [mass calculation](../receipt-archives/2026-11-23-hermitian-mass.md) gives the field discriminant and the class-number-one proof. For $A_n$, the line $q^{-n}Oe_1$ has normalized slope $2n\log q$. Containment $A_n\subset q^{-n}O^2$ gives the matching upper bound. Both lattices and their duals have the same mean slope, zero. Thus fixed $H$ can pass `FUN` for $B_n$ and fail it for $A_n$ at arbitrarily large margin. Multiplying both lattices by $q^n$ gives an integral version. All finite elementary divisors are then nonnegative; the maximum slopes become zero and $-2n\log q$, respectively. The determinant estimate in the release bounds the mean slope of a dual lattice. A different dependency bounds the maximum. ## Price of any possible detecting map

Let $\Psi:K^2\to K^a$ be injective and give the target its standard lattice and metric. Suppose $\Psi(A_n)_w\subseteq\mathfrak p_w^{-a_w}O_w^a$ at every finite place, for integers $a_w$ of finite support. Choose any nonzero coordinate $\alpha$ of $\Psi e_1$. Since $q^{-n}e_1\in A_n$, we have $v_w(\alpha)\ge n v_w(q)-a_w$. At an infinite place $|\tau\alpha|\le\|\Psi\|_\tau$. The product formula, in the normalization of the release, yields
$$
 2n\log q\ \le\ \operatorname{htMap}(\Psi)+2\sum_w {\tt lw}(w)a_w,\qquad
 {\tt lw}(w)=\log N\mathfrak p_w/[K:\mathbb Q].
$$
This is sharp even with the two contributions prescribed separately at every integer split $0\le t\le n$. Take $\Psi=\operatorname{diag}(q^t,1)$ and $a_w=(n-t)v_w(q)$. For $B_n$ its inverse unitary diagonal map attains total cost zero. The product formula applied to the image of one of its unit-length basis vectors also gives the lower bound zero. The charge can be moved into the proof's tuple-dependent scalar. For the aligned example take $\Psi=I$, use $v_\mu(0)=I$, $v_\mu(1)=q^n I$, and let the product of the square-ratio entries be $1$ at tuple zero and $q^n$ at tuple one. The common-map identity and reference kernel hold. The selected finite images now land in the standard lattice with $d_F=0$, and the reference map has height zero. The missing $2n\log q$ is exactly twice the weighted sum of valuations of the **selected scalar**. The source explicitly adds that valuation to $d_F$ before bounding the height. This realizes the local numerical mechanism; the arithmetic origin of the scalar requires more than these row conditions. ## Unresolved

Matching the determinant data, the conjugate place selection and even every local elementary-divisor list does not bound the globally aligned direction. The proof charges either its map height or its finite denominators, including the selected scalar. A failure on this route must occur in the **actual** arithmetic production or summation of those costs.
