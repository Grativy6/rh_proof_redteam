## The shortest lift in the first coordinate

Write $\Lambda=E_8=D_8+\mathbb Zr$ in its Euclidean realization, where $D_8$ consists of integral vectors with even coordinate sum and $r=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$. The source’s first basis vector corresponds to $r$. In fact
$$r,\ e_1-e_2,\ -e_1-e_2,\ e_2-e_3,\ e_3-e_4,\ e_4-e_5,\ e_5-e_6,\ e_6-e_7$$
have its Gram matrix. The Gram determinant is one and this realization of $E_8$ has covolume one. Let $X$ be a positive-rank free integral lattice with any positive-definite real quadratic metric, write $\lambda_1$ for its minimum nonzero length, and endow $\Lambda\otimes X$ with the tensor product metric. [Pinned Gram matrix](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/CharacterTheory/W8AddTransposeEqE8.lean#L8-L18) · [Released quadratic weight](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/IsUnimodMod.lean#L9-L15)

**Theorem.** For $m\ge1$ and $a\in X$, put $d_0=\min_{u\in a+mX}\|u\|^2$. The minimum on $r\otimes a+m(\Lambda\otimes X)$ is $2d_0$. All its minimizers are $r\otimes u$ for minimizers $u$ in $a+mX$. Any lift outside $r\otimes X$ has squared length at least $2d_0+m^2\lambda_1^2$. The coefficient of the last term is sharp.

**Proof.** Write the eight coordinates of a lift as $v_i=w_i/2$. Expanding in a basis of $X$ gives $w_i=a+mc+2mz_i$, where $c,z_i\in X$ and $\sum_i z_i\in2X$. Thus every $w_i$ and every midpoint $(w_i+w_j)/2$ belongs to $a+mX$. The energy is $\frac14\sum_i\|w_i\|^2$. Taking all $w_i$ equal to a minimizer gives $2d_0$. Two distinct minimizers in the same class modulo $2mX$ would have a strictly shorter midpoint.

For each unequal pair the parallelogram identity gives
$$\|w_i\|^2+\|w_j\|^2\ \ge\ 2d_0+2m^2\lambda_1^2.$$
If at least two disjoint unequal pairs exist, add both bounds. Otherwise seven coordinates agree and one differs. The parity of $\sum_i z_i$ forces that one difference to lie in $4mX$, giving an even stronger bound from one pair. For sharpness take $X=\mathbb Z$, $a=m/2$ for even $m$, and subtract $m(e_1+e_2)$ from $ar$. Exactly two of the $w_i$ become $a-2m$, giving excess $m^2$. For odd $m$ use $a=(m-1)/2$ and the excess is $m^2+m$, so no larger uniform coefficient works on the odd prime powers either.

## A tied pair on the actual type of orbit

Take $b=7^5$, $d=b+1$, $m=b7^k$ for positive odd $k$, and $a=(m-b)/2$. On $X=\mathbb Z^2$ use
$$Q_X(u,v)=d(u^2+v^2)+buv.$$
The integral monodromy matrix has diagonal $2d$ and off-diagonal $b$. The squared norm on $r\otimes(u,v)$ is $2Q_X(u,v)$. The shortest nonzero vectors in $X$ are precisely $\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1)$, of squared length $d$. Indeed $Q_X\ge(d-b/2)(u^2+v^2)$ and $2d-b>d$.

The vector $(a,d)$ lies in the orbit of the second standard basis vector under $\Gamma(b)=\{\gamma\in{\rm SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\gamma\equiv I\bmod b\}$. It is congruent to $(0,1)$ and is primitive. An odd prime dividing $(7^k-1)/2$ and $b+1$ would give 7 both odd and even multiplicative order at that prime. The former integer is odd. Choose $u,w$ with $ud-wa=1$ and adjust $w$ by multiples of $d$ to obtain $w\equiv0\bmod b$. Then $u\equiv1\bmod b$. The two columns $(u,w),(a,d)$ give one simultaneous transformation of the full basis tuple. This realizes the release’s orbit definition for $O=\mathbb Z$, $N_1=b$, $N=m$, $k_y=I$. For example at $m=117649$ the columns can be $(-282492055,-94169621),(50421,16808)$. [Orbit predicate](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/IsUnimodMod.lean#L21-L26)

In the class of $(a,d)$ modulo $m$, exactly $(a,d)$ and $(a-m,d)$ minimize $2Q_X$. Completing the square gives
$$2Q_X(x,y)=2d\left(x+\frac{by}{2d}\right)^2+w_0y^2,\qquad w_0=2d-\frac{b^2}{2d}.$$
At $y=d$ the two displacements from the centre are $\pm m/2$ and the common energy is $H=d(m^2+4d^2-b^2)/2$. Other first coordinates cost at least $4dm^2$ extra. Changing $y$ modulo $m$ costs more than $dm^2/2$ extra, since $d/m<1/6$ and $w_0>3d/2$. The theorem above keeps every off-axis point at least $dm^2$ above the minimum. Thus these are the only two leading terms in the entire 16-dimensional class. At $7m$ they separate and each has a unique minimum.

## Making the whole projected sum zero

Let $\delta$ be the order-31 Teichmüller lift used in the [earlier theta series](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-when-the-golden-unit-changes-a-genuine-theta-term.md). Take $K=\mathbb Q_2(\delta,\xi_{32})$ with $\xi_{32}$ primitive of order 32, and normalize $v_2(2)=1$. For a unit $\eta\in K$ use the two period generators $(P,C),(C,P)$ with $P=2^{2d}\delta^2$ and $C=2^b\eta$. This is an actual principally polarized abelian surface. The map $(s,t)\mapsto(st,s/t)$ sends the period generators of the product of Tate curves with parameters $PC,P/C$ to the product and the quotient of the displayed period generators. The resulting finite isogeny realizes the quotient as an abelian variety. The pairing is symmetric, positive in valuation and takes a basis of periods to a basis of characters, giving the principal polarization. On its eighth power take the release’s $E_8$ matrix $B$.

In the notation $(x,y)\in\Lambda^2$, write $\langle x,y\rangle=x^tBy$ in the released basis, and $|x|^2=\langle x,x\rangle$. The Fourier coefficients are
$$2^{\,d(|x|^2+|y|^2)+b\langle x,y\rangle}
  \delta^{|x|^2+|y|^2}\eta^{\langle x,y\rangle}.$$
Their translation ratio is the period pairing, giving the theta equation by reindexing. The constant $\Theta(1)$ is a unit since its constant coefficient is 1 and every other exponent is positive. The normalized projector is the sum over one residue class divided by $\Theta(1)$, by orthogonality of the odd-order torsion characters. [Geometric theta construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08212) · [Source projector](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Degenerations/ArithBase.lean#L64-L72)

At $\kappa=1$ multiply the projected numerator of the indicated class by $2^{-H}$ and call it $f(\eta)$. The ratio of its two leading monomials, in the order $x=ar,(a-m)r$, is $\delta^{-2mb}\eta^{2md}$. Put $\eta_0=\xi_{32}\delta^6$. Since $6d\equiv b\bmod31$ and $v_2(2md)=4$, this ratio is exactly $-1$. Every other term has valuation greater than 9 by the explicit gaps. The Laurent series in $\eta$ and its derivative converge on units. On the open ball about $\eta_0$, expansion in $\eta/\eta_0-1$ has integral coefficients, including for negative exponents. At $\eta_0$ the derivative of the two leading terms has valuation exactly 4, since their exponents differ by $2md$ and their values are opposite units. The tail derivative still has valuation greater than 9.

Therefore $v_2(f(\eta_0))>2v_2(f'(\eta_0))$. Newton iteration in the complete field $K$ gives a unit $\eta_*$ with $f(\eta_*)=0$ and the same residue as $\eta_0$. Each correction has valuation strictly greater than that of the derivative, preserves the derivative valuation, and at least doubles the excess of the error valuation above 8. This constructs the zero inside a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_2$. Every base torsion character factor is constant on the whole class because $b\mid m$. The coarse projector is consequently zero at **every** such evaluation. Each of the two fine projectors is nonzero with valuation $H$. [Analytic Hensel criterion, Theorem 3](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol364-stacs2026/html/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.8/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.8.html)

For $m=7b$, $H=123445073616832$. At the identity evaluation both fine leading residues, after division by $2^H$, are $g^{21}$ for $g=\bar\delta$. The exponents reduce to $2(a^2+d^2)+12ad=21\bmod31$. With zero coarse target the release’s trial sum after deleting one minimum already has maximal size. It therefore uses its plain powers. Its two predicted fine leading residues both equal 1 in characteristic two. To read the same calculation with a genuine uniformizer take $\omega=\xi_{32}-1$ in $K$ and multiply all integral weights by 16. The ratio $2/\omega^{16}$ is a unit of the totally ramified cyclotomic subfield with residue 1. The explicit correction for seven-power torsion has seven-power order, so multiplying the true fine reading by that phase cannot turn $g^{21}$ into 1. ## Which term the zero can remove

At the reference point the basis tuple $(r\otimes e_1,r\otimes e_2)$ costs $4d$. Every tuple in its orbit reduces to these two vectors modulo $b$ respectively. In each block the root-line theorem bounds the energy below by $2d$. Equality forces a vector on $r\otimes X$ belonging to the four shortest vectors. Since $b>2$, congruence picks exactly $e_1$ in the first block and $e_2$ in the second. This proves uniqueness in the entire orbit. At $\kappa=1$ both of these true small terms are units after normalization by their indicated powers. The projected class just made zero has one block of cost $H>4d$ by itself. Dropping it cannot change the norm of the reference product. This conclusion holds for the tested basis and metric even at the tuned period. [Where strict separation enters](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/CongModOfSpan.lean#L222-L265)

There is also a short check of the general leading-term criterion conditional on the asserted fan and supporting tuple. Suppose an integral automorphism $e$ congruent to the identity modulo $N_1\ge3$ ties the reference energy. The equality criterion in the support hypothesis places $e^*\beta$ in the same cone as $\beta$. It is in the relative interior of the transformed cone, since $\beta$ was in the relative interior of the original. In a fan the intersection is a face. The two cones have the same dimension, so this forces equality of cones. An integral isomorphism then permutes their primitive rays and preserves their sum. That sum is positive definite because a positive linear combination of the positive-semidefinite rays already was. Its integral isometry group is finite. The kernel of reduction modulo $N_1\ge3$ has no nonidentity finite-order element, by the [binomial argument](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-what-compatible-theta-sums-recover.md#all-the-labels-at-one-fixed-trait). Thus $e$ is the identity. This uses the full equality criterion and the fan face property. An arbitrary list of spanning vectors need not have either property.
