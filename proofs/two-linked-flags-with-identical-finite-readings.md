## Use a local character from the moving window

Fix $\ell=11$ and twist $j=1$. For the trivial outer character take the odd inner character $\psi=\chi_{-d}$ of the second quadratic prime. Once the window's small-prime condition includes 11, it forces $d\equiv-1\bmod11$. Thus $\psi$ is unramified and trivial at 11. The auxiliary quadratic field $\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-7})$ splits there since $-7\equiv4\bmod11$. On the resulting local field $\mathbf Q_{11}$ the three target diagonal characters are $\epsilon,1,\epsilon^{-1}$, where $\epsilon$ is the 11-adic cyclotomic character. The Case I condition $10\nmid 2j$ holds. This pins the local test to a character allowed at arbitrarily late scales whenever the window supplies its pair. ## A unit and a uniformizer

For any integer $a\ge1$, put $u_a=12^{11^a}$ and $t_a=11^{11^a}$. For $x$ equal to either, let $c_x(g)\in\mathbf Z_{11}(1)$ be the Kummer cocycle, writing its coordinates using one compatible system of roots of unity. Compatible root choices give $c_{u_a}=11^a c_{12}$ and $c_{t_a}=11^a c_{11}$. On the free rank-two lattice with basis $e_1,e_2$, take $g(e_1)=\epsilon(g)e_1$, $g(e_2)=e_2+c_x(g)e_1$.

Take the symmetric square and twist by $\epsilon^{-1}$. Writing $s=\epsilon(g)$ and $c=c_x(g)$ gives the actual matrix
$$
R(s,c)=
\begin{pmatrix}
s&c&c^2/s\\
0&1&2c/s\\
0&0&s^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
R(s,c)R(t,d)=R(st,c+sd).
$$
This is integral and has determinant one. Both adjacent diagonal ratios are $\epsilon$. For every group element, both representations have the same characteristic polynomial $(X-s)(X-1)(X-s^{-1})$. Modulo $11^a$ even their entries agree with the prescribed diagonal matrix. The Kummer classes over $\mathbf Q_{11}$ remain distinct rationally: in $\mathbf Q_{11}^\times$ one parameter has valuation zero and nonzero logarithm, and the other has valuation $11^a$.

**Their monodromy.** In the standard Kummer calculation for $V_x$ the unit classes are crystalline and a uniformizer class is semistable with nonzero monodromy. One way to see the distinction explicitly is to let $t_{\rm cyc}$ denote Fontaine's period and $L_x$ the logarithm for a compatible root system. The transformation rule is $g(L_x)=L_x+c_x(g)t_{\rm cyc}$. For $x=12$, $[\widetilde x]/x-1$ is in the integral kernel of Fontaine's $\theta$. Its logarithm lies in the crystalline divided-power ring after inverting 11: the $k$th logarithm term is a divided power times $(k-1)!$, which tends to zero 11-adically. Consequently $e_1/t_{\rm cyc}$ and $e_2-L_x e_1/t_{\rm cyc}$ are two independent crystalline invariant periods. For a uniformizer the semistable logarithm has nonzero monodromy. Multiplying its logarithm by $11^a$ preserves that fact. Equivalently $V_{t_a}$ is the Tate module of the split Tate curve of parameter $t_a$, tensored with $\mathbf Q_{11}$. Its multiplicative reduction persists under finite extension. [Bloch–Kato on finite local classes](https://virtualmath1.stanford.edu/~conrad/BSDseminar/refs/BKTamagawa.pdf) · [Explicit Kummer and Tate-curve periods, Brinon–Conrad §9.2](https://virtualmath1.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/notes.pdf)

On the rank-three symmetric square the unit case has $N=0$. In the Tate case one can scale the two period vectors so the rank-two operator sends the second vector to the first. The induced operator then has upper adjacent entries $1,2$ and its square has upper-right entry 2. It is one Jordan block of size three. Both rank-three twists have the same semisimple Frobenius multiset, $\{11^{-1},1,11\}$, in the crystalline Frobenius convention. Inverting all three entries gives the same set. The nonzero monodromy survives any finite extension. These are two actual local Galois representations with the same diagonal, the same characteristic polynomials and arbitrarily long coincident finite reductions.

## What the release can read

The [ordinary calculation](one-ordinary-line-through-all-congruence-levels.md) shows why counting generalized Hecke lines alone cannot distinguish a linked Steinberg input by an excess of lines. The release's `AtLT` interface explicitly asks for a principal series with pairwise distinct restrictions to the units before requiring the local characteristic-polynomial comparison. At a Steinberg representation no such presentation exists. The conditional predicate then holds for any proposed local Galois representation. In particular, citing that predicate at this input supplies no comparison of its monodromy. Its actual consumer requests the missing presentation and distinct characters explicitly. In the rank-three unlinking argument a potential-crystallinity condition also enters for a proposed collision. It eliminates the Tate example on the local field itself. The unit example meets that local condition and still has both linked ratios. There is a further, independent mathematical barrier to realizing it globally. For a cohomological conjugate-self-dual cuspidal $\Pi$ over a CM field, Caraiani's Theorem 1.1, including the prior odd-rank case, identifies the attached local Weil–Deligne representation up to Frobenius semisimplification **with monodromy retained**. The normalized local representation is tempered and the corresponding Weil–Deligne representation is pure. If $N=0$, its Frobenius eigenvalues must all have the same complex absolute value. Our crystalline triple has unequal absolute values. Restricting to a finite local extension replaces the Frobenius eigenvalues by the corresponding powers in this unramified example. A common character twist rescales all three absolute values equally. Hence this particular triple cannot occur at such a cuspidal point under the stated attachment. This uses a theorem independent of the released proof. ## Is that cuspidal point the same marked point?

This is where a formal assertion about attachment needs mathematical content. Write $S.R$ for the release's normalized local deformation ring and $\xi:S.R\to O$ for its marked integral point. The source's `Space.lift` condition includes diagonality of **all** group matrices modulo the maximal ideal. The map $\xi$ is an $O$-algebra map back to $O$, hence surjective. The inverse image of the maximal ideal of $O$ is the unique maximal ideal of $S.R$. Every off-diagonal entry therefore remains strictly small at $\xi$. Restricting $\xi$ to the subalgebra $S.A$ gives exactly the point the source calls `ptA`. With the integrality condition in `XiTI` this verifies the extra smallness required by `VPt` on that very point. No new choice of its matrix entries is needed. The high-value remaining inference is the passage from seed automorphy to the marked component. In the source, `AllGood` asserts that the image of the modular ideal is contained in the contraction of **every** prime of $S.A$ satisfying its irreducibility conditions. Applied to the kernel of the marked point, it makes that point eligible for the classicality assertion at the chosen auxiliary place. A second choice of auxiliary place supplies the complementary local data. The release obtains the universal containment by rank-two and rank-three propagation. The local examples above provide no such global containment. This is the point at which the bare local counterexample stops and a counterexample to the release would have to advance.
