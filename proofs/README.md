# Proofs developed in the PAL audit

**These results grew out of Christopher Daniel Pang’s PAL corpus.** Pang originated the research questions and directed the work. Hearthline developed and wrote the derivations with him. The [contribution record](PROVENANCE.md) gives independent publication dates and explains exactly where the Alpöge–Claude release enters each proof.

## Start with the lattice reconstruction theorem

**In every dimension, a marked table of shortest squared lengths at level $N\ge4$ determines the whole positive-definite lattice metric.** For each residue label $c$, the table gives a number $d(c)$. Look for the labels with $d(2c)=4d(c)$. Each has one shortest representative. Those representatives generate the lattice, and the readings of their sums recover every mutual inner product. The proof includes a small numerical example and the extension to rational radicals. [Read the doubling proof](marked-lattice-reconstruction.md)

The papers state their own conclusions and the scope of their application to the RH argument. The overall audit still seeks a verified failure at a necessary step of that deduction.

## Building upward from PAL

[Two prime families can carry the full local price](two-prime-families-carry-the-full-price.md) gives an all-label reduction to primes and five times primes. The calculation at $100^4$ retains less than 0.588% of the absolute weight, and a separate construction shows why small fractions alone cannot deliver the global estimate.

[Why one large prime leaves nine](why-one-large-prime-leaves-nine.md) explains a positive cofactor surplus, computes the signed cost of both sides at one trillion, and proves that one family of semiprimes can eventually carry all five surviving moments.

[Where an integer label can go quiet](where-an-integer-label-can-go-quiet.md) gives a pencil proof of exactly which prices can vanish. The [2663 example](../pal-rh/what-2663-carries-between-frames.md) shows how an arithmetic sign can reverse while that price stays fixed.

[Which prime labels cancel?](which-prime-labels-cancel.md) starts with six factored integers and measures the actual share of the trillion-sector cancellation they teach us to isolate. The [all-scale extension](../pal-rh/how-much-can-travel-with-a-prime.md) carries a growing cofactor through the same calculation.

[What the central mirror earns](what-the-central-mirror-earns.md) changes the arithmetic at 5 and lets you compute how far the resulting cancellation moves. A second construction preserves every prime value and any chosen finite head.

[What a reflected remainder carries](../pal-rh/what-a-reflected-remainder-carries.md) isolates the odd and even parts of an actual return. [How far a negative run would have to travel](../pal-rh/how-far-a-negative-run-must-travel.md) proves the power-exponent law for signed runs and gives a prime clock with forced late sign reversals.

[What the first omitted label sees](../pal-rh/what-the-first-omitted-label-sees.md) builds a rational positive average directly on the original half-rows, finds its sign reversal after one label changes, and derives a sufficient test using one pass at fourth powers.

[How many square passes the sign needs](../pal-rh/how-many-square-passes-the-sign-needs.md) gives an exact four-row inverse and proves positivity through a growing interval. In the finite experiment all the original negative readings can be recovered from the transformed positive ones.

[What the ordinary primes still owe](../pal-rh/what-the-ordinary-primes-still-owe.md) derives the logarithmic update, exposes its first repeated visit at 25, and prices the discarded part before applying the signed-area criterion.

[What the two signs cost together](../pal-rh/what-the-two-signs-cost-together.md) gives an exact finite comparison on Pang’s two-row reading, proves both accounts grow, and locates the exact cofinal upper test.

[What the last two rows carry](../pal-rh/what-the-last-two-rows-carry.md) proves that the construction’s boundary reading must take both signs infinitely often on fourth-power frames, and gives a sufficient one-sided bound on fourth powers.

[One coefficient list earns the next square](../pal-rh/the-balanced-newton-handoff.md). An [error-exponent calculation](../pal-rh/when-a-drop-would-count.md) proves that infinitely many nonincreasing steps of its finite arithmetic test would imply RH. The first three steps decrease.

[Averaging the square](../pal-rh/averaging-the-square.md) gives a different, still RH-sensitive target that pays the joint crossing, together with the exact cost of undoing the average.

The [signed square calculation](../pal-rh/the-signed-matrix-inside-a-square.md) recovers a prime aggregate using only side coefficients, and the [fifth-boundary test](../pal-rh/what-the-fifth-boundary-needs.md) shows exactly why an old signed window cannot pay for every joint crossing on its own. [Corpus and classical source credit](PROVENANCE.md)

[What the next doubling window costs](../pal-rh/what-the-next-octave-costs.md) proves how an exact head changes the power that a missed wave must charge, and constructs a finite rational fit whose next interval contains only signed Möbius increments.

[What 5 carries through 2 and 3](../pal-rh/what-five-carries.md) follows one prime through every triangular layer and proves a strict separation between two finite widths on both physical targets. The first 256 measured rows agree exactly throughout.

In Pang’s independent constructive branch, [what every copy of one finite reading misses](../pal-rh/what-one-parent-misses.md) rules out an entire class of attempted RH constructions by a paper argument. [Changing the first wrong row](../pal-rh/changing-the-first-wrong-row.md) gives an exact arithmetic optimum and a six-row example. [Source credit and corpus roles](../pal-rh/CORPUS-ROUTES.md)
