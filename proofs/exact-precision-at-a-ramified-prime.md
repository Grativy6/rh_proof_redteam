## Precisely how much an approximate symmetry can lose

Let $L/K$ be a totally ramified cyclic extension of degree $p$ of $p$-adic local fields. Normalize the valuations by $v_L(L^\times)=v_K(K^\times)=\mathbb Z$. Choose a uniformizer $\varpi$ of $L$ and a generator $\sigma$ of the Galois group. Let $r=v_L(\sigma\varpi-\varpi)-1$, its lower ramification break. Here $r\ge1$. Indeed $\overline{\sigma\varpi/\varpi}$ defines a character of this order-$p$ group into the common residue field’s multiplicative group, which has order prime to $p$.

**Proposition.** For every $x\in L\setminus K$,
$$v_L(\sigma x-x)=r+\max_{a\in K}v_L(x-a).$$
For integral $x$ the maximum is attained in $\mathcal O_K$. If an integral $x$ is fixed modulo $\varpi^E$ for $E\ge1$, the largest precision guaranteed for an integral base lift is
$$j(E)=\min\{j\ge\max(1,E-r):p\nmid j\}.$$

**Proof.** The powers $1,\varpi,\ldots,\varpi^{p-1}$ form a $K$-basis because the uniformizer has degree $p$. Write $x=\sum a_i\varpi^i$. The nonzero terms have distinct valuations modulo $p$, so deleting $a_0$ gives the best approximation by $K$. Every $a_i$ is integral when $x$ is. Write $\sigma\varpi/\varpi=1+h$ with $v_L(h)=r$. For $1\le i<p$, $(1+h)^i-1$ has valuation $r$, since $i$ is a unit and the remaining terms have higher order. The valuations of the nonconstant terms of $\sigma x-x$ have therefore all increased by exactly $r$, and remain distinct modulo $p$. This proves the formula. The best approximation order of a nonbase integral element is positive and prime to $p$. For sharpness at the stated precision take $x=\varpi^{j(E)}$. Its own valuation is outside $p\mathbb Z$, and the same binomial argument applies since $p\nmid j(E)$.

For instance in $\mathbb Q_2(i)$ take $\varpi=1+i$, so $r=1$. Conjugating $i$ changes it by $-2i$, of $\varpi$-order 2. The best integral base approximation has order 1, attained by 1. Thus a class can be exactly fixed in the quotient modulo $\varpi^2$ and have no invariant lift to that precision. This example is independent of theta functions.

## The actual theta phases have a different arithmetic constraint

Let $b=S.r\,S.N$ and $B=S'.r\,S'.N$ be the two torsion orders in the geometric comparison, with $S'.N=m^2$ and $b\mid m$. Each real theta setup takes its order from a power of one odd prime. Divisibility forces the same level prime $\ell$ at both depths. The base explicitly makes $b$ invertible. Hence an integral trait over residue characteristic $p$ has $p\ne\ell$. The displayed proof supplies a phase $\xi$ with $\xi^{b^2B^2}=1$ before the theorem statement shortens this to existence of some exponent. This order bound concerns the exhibited choice. The weaker statement also permits multiplying every corrected value at a level by another root of unity in the base. One can see why multiplication of rigidified fibres gives a root. Choose compatible normalized identifications $L_x^{n^2}\simeq L_{nx}$ for the symmetric line bundle, fixed at the identity by a rigidifying generator. Two composites for commuting multiplications have the same value at the identity. Their ratio is a global invertible function on the proper connected abelian variety, so it is that same constant everywhere. A torsion frame is a generator of $L_x$ whose indicated power maps to the generator at zero. Two frames for the same torsion order and rigidification then differ by a root of that squared order. For two rigidifications, divide their ratio by the ratio at the origin and compare after both powers. The error in commuting the two fibre comparisons has become exactly 1 after these powers. In the source $\xi$ is the quotient of such fibre generators, evaluated **in the common field**. It acts by one scalar for each base torsion evaluation and is independent of the Fourier character. There is an elementary consequence. If $\eta$ has $\ell$-power order in a local field of residue characteristic $p\ne\ell$, then any nonidentity $\eta$ satisfies $|\eta-1|=1$. Indeed if it reduced to 1, reducing $1+\eta+\cdots+\eta^{n-1}=0$ for its order $n>1$ would give $n=0$ in the residue field. Consequently two nonzero values differing solely by such a phase either coincide or differ by their **full** norm. The conclusion survives arbitrary ramification. It applies as well to products of the corrections. In particular the Gaussian-integer example’s hidden digit cannot be carried by the geometric phase at the dyadic, seven-power levels of the [zero-class test](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-a-zero-theta-sum-on-the-supported-lattice-orbit.md). An arbitrary unit of a wildly ramified field would have different behavior.

At a fixed level the corrected value is an actual finite character sum of twisted theta values divided by a base theta value. Conjugating permutes the torsion indices and takes their Weil characters along with them. Changing a chosen torsion lift by an orthogonal torsion point contributes its Weil commutator, which is 1. The odd-order symmetric lift is unique: the ratio of two symmetric lifts has square 1 by symmetry and odd power 1 by the order condition. These identities give equality before taking norms. In a product the same tuple of residue labels is permuted as a whole. ## A base germ without dividing by a group order

Here is the independent finite-ring argument used after exact equivariance. Let a finite group $G$ act on a finite algebra $B$ over a noetherian local base $(A,\mathfrak m)$ with $B^G=A$ (identified with its image). Fix a maximal ideal $\mathfrak z$ above $\mathfrak m$, let $D$ be its stabilizer, and let $F\in B$ be fixed **exactly** by $D$. For any $E\ge1$, distinct maximal ideals in the $G$-orbit have comaximal $E$th powers. By the Chinese remainder theorem choose $u_0$ equal to 1 modulo $\mathfrak z^E$ and zero modulo all its other translates. Put $u=\prod_{\sigma\in D}\sigma u_0$. Then $u$ is $D$-fixed, still 1 at the chosen factor and zero at the others. The sum
$$f=\sum_{\tau D\in G/D}\tau(uF)$$
is well defined and $G$-invariant. Only the identity coset contributes modulo $\mathfrak z^E$, hence $f-F\in\mathfrak z^E$. In particular this construction works even when $p$ divides $|D|$. [Released invariant construction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ApproxOrbitSumsOfGaloisClassSums.lean#L17-L88)

The uniformity in the local argument has a concrete bound. Let $t$ be the least positive power killing the maximal ideal of the local Artinian fibre $B_{\mathfrak z}/\mathfrak m B_{\mathfrak z}$. Then $\mathfrak z^{et}B_{\mathfrak z}\subset\mathfrak m^e B_{\mathfrak z}$, and $t$ is at most the length of that fibre. Clear the local denominator for $f-F$. At any integral evaluation centred at $\mathfrak z$ this denominator has absolute value 1. The largest norm of a finite generating set of $\mathfrak m$ bounds the evaluated error by its $e$th power. This permits a single base germ at the specified depth even if the absolute size of that error varies between traits. For $A=\mathbb Z_{(2)}$, $B=A[i]$, $\mathfrak z=(1+i)$, $t=2$ and the ideal containment needs $E=2e$, so the linear precision cost can be sharp. ## Unresolved

The wild example is a counterexample to descending from mere congruence at unchanged precision. The written theta comparison supplies exact equivariance and prime-to-residue-characteristic phases. With its stated finite algebra and common evaluation centre, the invariant-germ argument also works on paper. The unresolved application concerns the global model and its actual support and geometric data. The [direct orbit test](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-a-zero-theta-sum-on-the-supported-lattice-orbit.md#which-term-the-zero-can-remove) shows why exact cancellation of one genuine projected class still leaves a particular reference norm intact.
