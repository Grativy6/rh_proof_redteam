# Which board sizes have to fit?

Christopher Daniel Pang and Hearthline · 26 November 2026

**For a proof of RH, it would suffice to pack the prime board at the fourth powers.** Think of the sizes $1,16,81,256,625,\ldots$. Packing at every sufficiently late size on this list would settle the one-sided question on paper. The Lean version uses the paired tests of sufficient capacity and bounded spare room at those same sizes. Its input consists entirely of whole-number comparisons. [What the board stores](how-much-room-the-prime-board-needs.md) · [Interpolation and proof](../pal-rh/fourth-power-boards-and-their-funded-intervals.md)

The reason that so many sizes can be skipped is visible with a ruler. Each prime $p$ contributes a ramp. It is flat until $p$ appears, then its slope becomes $\log p$. Add the ramps and the slope can only increase. A chord between two known readings lies above the entire prime reading between them. The comparison curve is a parabola. If two readings are $h$ apart, the parabola’s greatest separation from its chord is $h^2/8$. At fourth powers the gaps are of order $n^{3/4}$, so the interpolation costs order $n^{3/2}$. That is exactly the size of error the argument can afford.

**There are also arbitrarily large stretches where every board fits, with both digits in each borrowed pair recoverable.** In fact there are centers $N$ tending to infinity where every size within $\sqrt N\log N$ of $N$ has about **1.924 spare bits per $n^{3/2}$**. The error in that ratio tends to zero uniformly on the whole stretch. One real prime-square contribution fixes the central amount. An actual nonreal zeta zero forces fluctuations to either side. The successive integer readings change slowly enough that they have to pass through the middle. This argument allows other zeros to be off the line. It gives no upper bound on how far one must travel to reach another such stretch. [Where the number comes from](../pal-rh/fourth-power-boards-and-their-funded-intervals.md#where-the-middle-of-the-band-comes-from)

There is a concrete stretch you can locate without searching. **Every positive size up to one billion fits the paired encoding and the stated spare-room allowance.** After the first 100,000, it takes 1,284 intervals to establish that. For the final 2,597,589 sizes the lower estimate for the log of spare capacity exceeds 40 trillion. Two independent prime counts agree at the endpoint. The calculation applies the chord and tangent inequalities to every intervening integer.
