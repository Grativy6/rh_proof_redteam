In $K_d=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ put
$$\alpha=(a+\sqrt{-d})/2,\qquad N\alpha=2^{577}.$$
The trace $a$ is odd, so exactly one dyadic prime $\mathfrak p$ divides $\alpha$ and $(\alpha)=\mathfrak p^{577}$. A smaller positive power cannot be principal because a nonrational integer generator would have norm at least $(d+1)/4>2^{576}$, and a rational generator has equal dyadic valuations. In the host $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{256})^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$ put $J=(\mathfrak p\mathcal O_E)/(\sqrt2)$. The extension degree over $K_d$ is a power of two, hence $J$ still has exact class order 577. We have the equality of fractional ideals
$$J^{577}=(\alpha/(\sqrt2)^{577}).$$
The generator on the right has CM norm one. If it were a global 577th power, torsion-freeness of the fractional-ideal group would make $J$ principal. Up to a 577th power its Kummer radicand is **the same $\alpha$**. One can choose either dyadic orientation in the earlier lattice construction by choosing the sign in $\omega=(1\pm\sqrt{-d})/2$.

## Where the class enters the arithmetic sum

The choice of trace gives
$$a\equiv1+2^{577}\equiv188682\bmod577^2.$$
The roots of $X^2-aX+2^{577}$ modulo $577^2$ are $1,2^{577}$, distinct modulo 577. Hence 577 splits in $K_d$ and $\alpha$ is a local 577th power in both completions. On principal units in $\mathbb Q_p$, $p$th powering maps $1+p\mathbb Z_p$ onto $1+p^2\mathbb Z_p$. Globally, a $p$th power for $\alpha$ would principalize $\mathfrak p$. This remains a non-power over $K_d(\mu_p)$ by norm across its prime-to-$p$ degree.

Let $\psi=\chi_{-d}$ and let $\varepsilon,\omega_p$ denote the arithmetic $p$-adic cyclotomic character and its reduction. Conjugation sends $\alpha$ to $2^p/\alpha$, so translations of a $p$th root have coefficient character $\psi\omega_p$. This equals $\bar\psi^{-1}\omega_p^{-575}$. Inflation and restriction give the class over $\mathbb Q$ because the splitting coefficient extension has prime-to-$p$ degree. All valuations of $\alpha$ are divisible by $p$. Thus the Kummer class is unramified away from $p$ after base change, and this condition descends on inertia, also at $d$ because its extra inertia has order two. At $p$ the class is locally zero. At $d$ inertia acts by $-1$ on the coefficients, giving no further unramified class. At another finite level prime the nonzero Frobenius-minus-one scalar on the divisible coefficient line is surjective. Global invariants of the divisible line vanish by inertia at $d$, so the order-$p$ class survives coefficient inclusion. It contributes at least one unit of length to the strict divisible group. Larger independent classes or higher lifts have not been counted.

## An explicit point field and the cost of its polynomial coordinate

Define $D_0(X)=2$, $D_1(X)=X$, $D_{n+1}(X)=X D_n(X)-2D_{n-1}(X)$. Then $D_n(u+2/u)=u^n+(2/u)^n$. Take $\beta^p=\alpha$ and put $\theta=\beta+2/\beta$. The polynomial **$D_{577}(X)-a$** defines the degree-577 affine point field of this class. Its 577 distinct roots are $Z^i\beta+2/(Z^i\beta)$, where $Z$ is a primitive $p$th root of unity. They are all real because $|\alpha|=2^{p/2}$. The nonzero translation group permutes them transitively. The multiplier on the indices is $\psi\omega_p$ and has full order 576. This also shows irreducibility modulo 20773 from the preceding Frobenius reading. A separate PARI finite-polynomial test gives the same result.

At $p$ the local radical splits, so the point field has one rational factor and one tame, totally ramified factor of degree 576. Its $p$-adic discriminant exponent is 575. At $d$ the sole inertia generator fixes one point and exchanges 288 pairs, giving exponent 288. There is no other ramification. On the other hand, the absolute discriminant of the displayed polynomial is $p^p d^{288}$. Indeed its derivative has leading coefficient $p$ and its $p-1$ critical values for $D_p$ are $\pm 2\cdot 2^{p/2}$ with equal multiplicities, by the substitution $u+2/u$. The resultant now gives the formula. Therefore
$$[\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\theta)}:\mathbb Z[\theta]]=577.$$

A missing integral element is $g=(\theta-3)^{576}/577$. At the rational factor $\theta\equiv3\bmod p$. At the other factor $\theta-3$ has valuation one in units of $Z-1$, since the coefficient of $Z-1$ reduces to $1-2\ne0$ at the branch with $\beta\equiv1$. Thus $g$ is integral everywhere and has respective residues $0,-1$ at the two factors. Its leading power over the monic basis has denominator $p$. Its class generates the quotient of order $p$. The monogenic order modulo $p$ reads $k[t]/t^{577}$ with $t=\theta-3$. The full integers modulo $p$ give $k\times k[\pi]/\pi^{576}$. In particular the 576th power of $t$ is a genuine nonzero in the order and maps to zero in $\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\theta)}/p$. This is a concrete reduction after a nonsaturated inclusion. It is a test of this polynomial coordinate, not a nonzero power of either rank-three trace in the [cube criterion](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-two-raw-traces-on-the-released-subgroup.md#a-numerical-milestone-for-the-next-obstruction).

For this same explicit host, $6911=103^2-2\cdot43^2$ splits completely. Both residues of $103+43\sqrt2$ over $d$ are nonsquares, as the verifier checks by modular exponentiation. Thus the [full-host norm obstruction](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-the-local-norm-that-does-not-compose.md#the-two-nonsquares-at-23) gives an additional explicit locally norm, globally non-norm prime here.

## Test the rank-two corner on this input

The rank-two corner would give a classical ordinary newform of weight $2j+1=1151$. Twisting the two-dimensional representation by $\psi$ removes the scalar quadratic inertia at $d$. Indeed both residual corner characters restrict to the same $\psi$ there and the released killed-inertia condition leaves just a group of order two. A further quadratic choice at 7 arranges residual characters $1,\eta\omega_p^{2j}$, with $\eta=\chi_{-7}$. The corner determinant is $\eta$ before Tate normalization, inertia at 7 has order two, and the two ordinary weights at 577 differ by $2j>1$, excluding special monodromy there. The same [conductor and bad-prime argument as for the previous corner](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-the-rank-three-obligation-at-577.md#an-elementary-obstruction-at-the-tame-modular-level) gives primitive tame level seven and the Eisenstein residues at every positive Fourier coefficient.

Use $1151=575+576$. Direct Bernoulli recursion gives $B_{575,\eta}=502\bmod577$, so the constant of $E_{575}^{1,\eta}$ is **414 modulo 577**. Independently $\sum_{a=1}^{7p}\eta(a)a^{575}=30004\bmod p^2$, giving the same Bernoulli value after division by $7p$. For this identity, group the sum by residue modulo seven and expand Faulhaber's formula over $p$ periods. The possible constant Bernoulli denominator at index $p-1$ cancels in the polynomial difference, and the terms beyond first order are divisible by $p^2$. Multiplication by the Hasse invariant matches the weights. A cusp eigenform carrying the required residues would leave a nonzero constant expansion in weight 1151. Raising to the 576th power and taking the order at a supersingular point would give $576\mid1151$. The rank-two corner therefore fails on this new character too. This excludes that alternative under the stated ordinary modular compatibility. The rank-three alternatives require their own test.
