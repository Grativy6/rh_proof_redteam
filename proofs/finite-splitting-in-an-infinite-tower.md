## Follow the same extension

Put $L=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})$, $F_n=L(\zeta_{2^n})$, $F_\infty=\bigcup_{n\ge2} F_n$. Take the $\alpha$ of the [explicit construction](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-dyadic-class-enters-late.md). The quadratic extension $L(\sqrt\alpha)/L$ is unramified at every finite place and is split above 2. Every nontrivial finite subextension of $F_\infty/L$ is totally ramified above 2 with the full global degree. Consequently $\sqrt\alpha\notin F_\infty$ and $F_\infty(\sqrt\alpha)/F_\infty$ is genuinely quadratic.

Now fix an odd place $v$ of $L$ with residue size $Q$. Since $i\in L$, $Q\equiv1\bmod4$. Put $h=v_2(Q-1)$. The local cyclotomic extension at $v$ is unramified and has residue degree $\operatorname{ord}_{2^n}(Q)=2^{\max(0,n-h)}$. This follows directly from $v_2(Q^{2^j}-1)=h+j$. If $v$ is nonsplit in $L(\sqrt\alpha)$, the residue degree in $F_{h+1}$ is even, and restriction to $F_{h+1}$ makes its quadratic Frobenius trivial. It stays trivial at every higher layer. The argument applies at primes dividing the displayed representative $\alpha$ as well, because the field extension is unramified there and the argument uses Frobenius. Dyadic places are already split, and archimedean places are complex.

Thus $\alpha$ has a square root in the completion at each finite place of $F_\infty$ and has no square root in $F_\infty$. This phenomenon occurs in the theory of locally cyclotomic extensions. The proof here identifies the particular class on the released fields. [Jaulent and Soriano, *Sur les tours localement cyclotomiques*](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~jjaulent/Articles/J32.pdf)

For a finite set $\Sigma$ of rational primes let $D_n(\Sigma)$ consist of the everywhere unramified quadratic characters of $F_n$ that split at all places above $\Sigma$, and let `res` denote restriction to the infinite field. The two orders of operation give different results, as expressed by
$$\bigcup_n\operatorname{res}\!\left(\bigcap_{\Sigma\ {\rm finite}}D_n(\Sigma)\right)=0,\qquad
\chi_\alpha\in\bigcap_{\Sigma\ {\rm finite}}\bigcup_n\operatorname{res}D_n(\Sigma),\quad\chi_\alpha\ne0.$$
The first equality follows by Chebotarev applied to each number field $F_n$. In the second expression take the maximum of the finitely many disappearance levels. At each finite layer there are infinitely many rational primes of residue degree one that still detect the character. For example, apply Chebotarev to a nonidentity automorphism of $F_n(\sqrt\alpha)/F_n$ and lift it in a finite normal closure over $\mathbb Q$. Its restriction fixes the copy of $F_n$, giving a degree-one place there. This also permits avoiding any prescribed finite excluded set.

There is a distinction between two higher-power tests. For the **raw** $\alpha$ at either prime, its order in $\mathbb F_p^\times$ has 2-part $2^h$. At layer $n>h$ the larger residue field has $v_2(Q_n-1)=n$, so raising $\alpha$ to $(Q_n-1)/2^n$, an odd exponent, can still return a root of order $2^h$. For the **twisted norm** in the [class-counting test](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-when-minus-eighty-erases-a-class.md), the exponent on $\alpha$ has valuation $n-1$. Its $2^n$-power residue is exactly the quadratic sign in the table. At the first prime it becomes trivial at layer 89. Also at that layer the first prime’s larger residue field is no longer the image of the integers of the fixed smaller base. The numerical primes verify these raw comparisons. The source may exclude additional primes in constructing a particular cycle. The Chebotarev argument above covers arbitrary finite exclusions.

## What the released descent actually requires

The source’s `xdualExt` fixes the nonzero integer $N$ describing the requested splitting primes before choosing a finite layer. Its compactness argument concerns the decomposition groups at primes dividing $N$ and inertia at 2. It imposes inertia triviality at the other primes separately. Our character has a finite representative for every fixed $N$. Taking $N$ to contain the detector from the table forces a later layer, exactly as the local calculation predicts. In the counting application, the proof first fixes a **finite set of global characters**. It takes a common upper bound for their individual levels, uses the class group at that finite number field, then counts there. The Frobenius witnesses are free to depend on that field. One can see the finite-layer descent directly for unramified finite characters. Pick a finite normal quotient of the original absolute group through which the character on the closed cyclotomic kernel factors. The decreasing images of the layer groups in this finite quotient stabilize to the image of the kernel, by compactness. This extends the character to a sufficiently high finite layer. Only finitely many rational primes ramify in the chosen normal quotient. At each one, the union of conjugate inertia groups is compact. The same holds for conjugate decomposition groups at the finitely many required splitting primes. Intersect these with the decreasing layer groups. Eventual triviality on the compact sets follows from triviality on their intersection and openness of the character kernel. For the remaining primes unramifiedness holds already. This proof buys a layer for a finite set of restrictions. ## Unresolved

The local-to-global square implication fails on the infinite field and succeeds at the finite-field point where it was actually invoked. An inference that discarded a global class merely because every fixed prime eventually agreed with zero would fail on this exact tower. The [twisted-norm receipt](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-when-minus-eighty-erases-a-class.md) exhibits the separate, genuine loss that occurs even on a finite layer and checks the counting term that handles it.
