## A size correction to the preceding example

The previous numerical example has base parameters $r=7,N=7^4$ and $b=rN=7^5$. Its asserted analytic zero, congruence orbit and minimum calculations stand. For the eighth power of an abelian surface the theta-system dimension is $g_S=16$. These small parameters fail **all three** inequalities in the release’s additional condition \(\mathrm{Big}\). In even dimension the inequalities are
$$N>1+2g_S2^{g_S/2},\qquad
r2^{g_S/2}>g_S\,3^{g_S}g_S!\,N^{g_S},\qquad rN>6N^{g_S}g_S!.$$
Here is a scale that meets them. Use $N=7^6,r=7^{149},b=7^{155}$ at the base and $m=7b,N'=m^2=7^{312},r'=7^{5020}$ at depth. The integer inequalities hold at both levels. The earlier proof works with this $b$: $b=5\bmod31$, $b=7\bmod8$, $d=b+1$ has $v_2(d)=3$, and $(7^k-1)/2$ is coprime to $d$ for positive odd $k$. At $m=7b$ the two fine leading residues remain $g^{21}$. This verifies the additional numerical condition, while the global family, centre and generic witness are separate data. ## What the permitted Fourier reading retains

Fix an odd level $N=m^2$, an auxiliary power $r$ of the same odd prime, and residue characteristic prime to $rN$. On a split torus with a unimodular polarization matrix $B$, let $A_x$ be the true coefficients of a convergent theta series. Project its pullback by $[m]$ to the class $c\bmod m$, and use torus frames. Up to a common unit denominator the value at a unit torus point $\tau$ is
$$P_c(\tau)=\sum_{x\equiv c\ (m)} A_x\,\tau^{mBx}.$$
If $\tau$ has order dividing $m^2$, this equals $\tau^{mBc}P_c(1)$. Thus an exact zero at 1 vanishes on **all** these probes. On $T=(\mu_{rm^2})^h$, where $h$ is the number of torus coordinates, character orthogonality gives for every $z\equiv c\bmod m$
$$\frac1{|T|}\sum_{\tau\in T}\tau^{-mBz}P_c(\tau)
 =\sum_{x\equiv z\ (rm)} A_x.$$
The order of $T$ is a unit. A character weight on the left is among the phase choices allowed in $S.\mathrm{FF}$, whose exponent modulus is $(rN)^2$. The section index can be the distinguished pullback section. The full toric torsion subgroup is isotropic. Translation through its $r$th power gives the original $N$-torsion projector. In the unit torus trivialization the frames at these torsion points can be taken to be 1, since the symmetric quadratic factor of automorphy is compatible with multiplication. A change by permitted torsion-frame phases can be absorbed in the phase function. [Basis, frames and distinguished section](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Degenerations/RigGen.lean#L35-L56) · [The two different sums in the source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Degenerations/ArithBase.lean#L63-L72)

Any two distinct shortest lifts of one class modulo $m$ separate modulo $rm$ when $r\ge2$. If they differed by $rmh$, the point one $m h$ step from the first toward the second would be in the same coarse class and strictly shorter by strict convexity. Consequently the displayed filter isolates each optimal leading coefficient in the split case. The old synthetic terms are unnecessary for this calculation.

## A genuine two-parameter wall

Take $m=9$ for the small arithmetic below, or any odd $m\ge9$ of the allowed prime-power shape. Work over $R=\mathcal O_F[[u,v]]$ for a finite dyadic field containing the level roots used in the experiment. For a fixed unit $\eta$ take period generators $(P,C),(C,P)$ with
$$P=u^4v^4,\qquad C=v^2\eta.$$
On any trait with nonzero $u,v$ of positive valuation, $PC$ and $P/C$ are Tate parameters. The monomial map $(s,t)\mapsto(st,s/t)$ gives an isogeny from the product of their Tate curves to the chosen period quotient. The symmetric period pairing gives a principal polarization. On the eighth power take the released $E_8$ form. Write $r_0$ for its first root. The unnormalized coefficient at $(x,y)\in E_8^2$ is
$$u^{U(x,y)}v^{V(x,y)}\eta^{\langle x,y\rangle},\qquad
 U=2(|x|^2+|y|^2),\quad V=2(|x|^2+|y|^2)+2\langle x,y\rangle.$$
Both weights are positive definite, so these are well-defined convergent formal series and $\Theta(1)$ is a unit. [Non-Archimedean theta functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08212)

Put $a=(m-1)/2$. Consider the class of $(ar_0,2r_0)$. Its two competing points are $x_+=ar_0,x_-=(a-m)r_0$ with $y=2r_0$. At $m=9$ their $(U,V)$ exponents are $(80,112)$ and $(116,76)$. At general $m$ the difference between these exponent pairs is $(-4m,4m)$. All other points have $U$ and $V$ **each** strictly above the larger of the two readings in that coordinate.

Here are bounds for that last assertion. On the root line, $U=4(X^2+Y^2)$ and $V=4(X+Y/2)^2+3Y^2$. For $Y=2$, the only two close $X$ values are $a,a-m$. For the other $Y\equiv2\bmod m$, one has $|Y|\ge m-2$. Hence the lower bounds $4(m-2)^2$ and $3(m-2)^2$ exceed the corresponding two root-line readings for $m\ge9$. The [sharp root-line theorem](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-a-zero-theta-sum-on-the-supported-lattice-orbit.md#the-shortest-lift-in-the-first-coordinate) gives excess at least $2m^2$ above the minimum for any off-line point under either metric, exceeding $4m$.

Let $G=u^{U(x_+,y)}v^{V(x_-,y)}$. Each of the two distinguished finer class sums is $Gv^{4m}$ or $Gu^{4m}$ times a unit of $R$, respectively. Every other finer class belongs to the ideal generated by these two. Fourier inversion is invertible over $R$, so the ideal generated by **all the larger toric evaluations of this projected section** is exactly
$$G\,(u^{4m},v^{4m}).$$
It is nonprincipal at $(\mathfrak m_F,u,v)$ because $u,v$ are relatively prime parameters and neither power is a unit. For this maximal toric character projector the weight space has dimension one over the generic coefficient field: the period recurrence connects all exponents in a given class modulo $N$. Other projected basis sections are scalar multiples there. When the scalars lie in the common fraction field, adding all such columns multiplies the evaluation ideal by their fractional ideal. A product of nonzero fractional ideals can be principal in a local domain only if each factor is invertible, hence principal. Merely changing the section basis cannot remove this local obstruction.

The usual blowup of $(u,v)$ makes the two choices explicit. In the chart $v=ut$ the second fine class is $Gu^{4m}$ times a unit and divides all readings. In the chart $u=vt'$ the first fine class has that role. This gives one geometric explanation of what the released \(\mathrm{HasDiv}\) hypothesis buys. The release chooses a proper model principalizing the finite families of nonzero Fourier readings before selecting a generator at the centre. The raw two-parameter chart fails that selection test. ## Exact zero and exact recovery at fixed periods

Specialize $u=v=2$. Divide the coarse numerator by $2^H$ where $H=2m^2+30$; both candidates have this weight. As a function of the remaining unit $\eta$, their leading monomials have exponents $4a$ and $4(a-m)$. At a primitive eighth root $\eta_0$ they sum to zero, since their ratio is $\eta_0^{4m}=-1$. The normalized derivative has valuation $v_2(4m)=2$. The preceding integer gap bounds put all other terms, and their derivatives on units, above valuation 4. Hensel iteration therefore produces an exact zero at a unit $\eta_*$ in $\mathbb Q_2(\mu_8)$, with the same residue as $\eta_0$. Fix this $\eta_*$ in the family. The common normalized leading residues of the two true terms are 1. At a larger toric probe their ratio in residue is $\tau^{m^2Br_0}$ in the first group of coordinates. The character is primitive because $Br_0$ is primitive. On $T=(\mu_{rm^2})^{16}$ the ratio runs uniformly over $\mu_r$. Exactly $(r-1)/r$ of these readings have the full leading size $2^{-H}$. The others have smaller size.

For $m=9$, every probe of order dividing 81 gives exactly zero. Two thirds of the probes in $(\mu_{243})^{16}$ give size $2^{-192}$. The small zero has a more precise check. Off the root line the combined energy is at least $192+4\cdot81=516$. On the line it is $8(X^2+Y^2)+4XY$. The only additional term below 516 is $(X,Y)=(4,-7)$ at 408, as one sees from $8(X^2+Y^2)+4XY\ge6(X^2+Y^2)$ and the two congruences. Put $z=\eta/\eta_0$. The normalized numerator is $z^{16}-z^{-20}+2^{216}z^{-56}$ modulo $2^{324}$ on units. Its derivative at $z=1$ has valuation 2, so $v_2(\eta_*/\eta_0-1)=214$. Substituting $z=1-2^{214}/9$ makes the displayed expression zero modulo $2^{324}$. This also certifies that the chosen eighth root by itself is not the exact zero.

At the fully size-admissible parameters $m=243,N=59049,r=3^{210}$, every probe of order dividing 59,049 still gives zero, whereas two thirds in the subgroup with coordinate orders dividing 177,147 give size $2^{-118128}$. These are two evaluations of the same projector at the same period. The Fourier filter above also recovers the chosen child exactly. ## Unresolved

This settles the split-torus tie and the need for a local modification in this family. A [mixed product with an equianharmonic good factor](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-a-theta-zero-at-every-toric-depth.md) shows what happens when a toric probe at every depth vanishes. The common evaluation in a general degeneration also involves that abelian part and the identification of one group over all traits at a centre. Our calculation does not establish those global inputs. The [marked-metric theorem](../receipt-archives/2026-11-24-reconstructing-a-marked-lattice-by-doubling.md) describes what the complete table of genuine minima can recover once it is available.
