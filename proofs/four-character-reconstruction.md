## What one row can see

Write $\chi_q(n)=(n/q)$ and $\chi_d(n)=(n/d)$ for the primitive quadratic characters, and $\chi_{qd}=\chi_q\chi_d$. The host has $q\equiv3\bmod8$, $d\equiv7\bmod16$, and $q\mid d+1$. At level one the group of characters surviving the subgroup average has size four. The two independent quadratic signs and their product already give four distinct characters including the identity. In the source’s additive coordinates the row is evaluated at $((1,0),t)$. The coordinate change puts $d$ first and $q$ second. The four multipliers in the order $1,q,d,qd$ are therefore $(1,1,-1,-1)$ for every $t$. | Prime and exponent | $w(p^j)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $p\nmid qd$, odd $j$, and $(\chi_q(p),\chi_d(p))=(+1,-1)$ | 4 |

## An actual zeta zero this measurement loses

The Euler product identifies the signed row with the logarithmic derivative of a concrete quotient. Put
$$R(s)=\frac{\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_q)}{L(s,\chi_d)L(s,\chi_{qd})}.$$
For $\Re s>1$, $-R'/R=\sum_{p,j\ge1}w(p^j)\log p\,p^{-js}$, where $p$ ranges over primes. The local factor of $R$ is $((1+p^{-s})/(1-p^{-s}))^2$ in the selected unramified sign class, $(1-d^{-2s})^{-1}$ at $d$, and 1 elsewhere. Equivalently $R=\zeta_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-q})}^2/\zeta_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})}$ by quadratic factorization. [Euler products and logarithmic derivatives](https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.4) · [Quadratic factorization and root numbers](https://people.reed.edu/~jerry/361/lectures/feqn.pdf)

The normalization also matters. Write $\ell_a=L'(1,\chi_a)/L(1,\chi_a)$ and $b=1+\gamma+\ell_q-\ell_d-\ell_{qd}$. Then
$$T(x)=\sum_{p^j\le x}w(p^j)\log p\,(x/p^j-1)-x\log x+bx.$$
In particular its Mellin transform on $\Re s>1$ is
$$\int_1^\infty T(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx=
\frac{-R'(s)/R(s)}{s(s-1)}-\frac1{(s-1)^2}+\frac b{s-1}.$$
Each ramp integral equals $p^{-js}/[s(s-1)]$ by direct integration. The pole terms at 1 cancel using $\zeta'/\zeta=-1/(s-1)+\gamma+O(s-1)$. This identifies what the signed observation measures, including the linear term. [Released normalization](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/Analytic.lean#L9-L24)

In the completed quotient both gamma factors cancel. The characters $q,d$ are odd and $1,qd$ are even. Each primitive quadratic root number is $+1$. With completion $(f/\pi)^{(s+a)/2}\Gamma((s+a)/2)L(s,\chi)$ for conductor $f$ and parity $a$, the remaining multiplier is $\sqrt{q/d}\,d^{-s}$. Hence
$$R(s)=d^{\,2s-1}R(1-s),\qquad R(-2)=d^{-5}R(3)>0.$$
Both $\zeta$ and $L(\,\cdot\,,\chi_{qd})$ have a simple zero at $-2$. The meromorphic quotient has a removable singularity there. More generally $R(-m)=d^{-2m-1}R(1+m)>0$ for every positive integer $m$. This argument uses the classical functional equation and the positive real Euler factors at $1+m$. [Functional equation and trivial zeros](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.15) · [Quadratic root-number argument](https://people.reed.edu/~jerry/361/lectures/feqn.pdf)

For the small congruence control $q=3,d=23$, the three quadratic values at $-2$ are $-2/9,-48,0$, respectively. The Python replay obtains them over the rationals using $B_3(u)=u^3-3u^2/2+u/2$. Separately PARI/GP 2.15.4 gave $R(-2)=0.00000015731074710012988355248353258131593045\ldots$ both from derivatives at $-2$ and from values at 3. These two commands reproduce the numerical check.


## Put the pieces back

At every ordinate, meromorphic orders obey
$$\operatorname{ord}\zeta=\operatorname{ord} R-\operatorname{ord} L_q+
\operatorname{ord} L_d+\operatorname{ord} L_{qd}.$$
At every real scale the exact inverse row is $G_1=T-G_q+G_d+G_{qd}$. If the three auxiliary readings each have absolute value at most $B$, then $G_1\le T+3B$. Even knowing $T$ exactly leaves three real degrees of freedom among four scalar readings. For instance adding the same function to $G_1$ and $G_d$ preserves $T$. That variation tests the measurement alone. The release also fixes the primitive coefficients and requires all three other bounds.

The published inequality allows the sum of the three absolute errors and budgets $4x^{1/2+\epsilon}$ for the discarded characters. Its geometric estimate has its own errors, including the shifts of argument. The identity above exposes why the auxiliary bounds matter. At a fixed argument the scalar allowance $3B$ is sharp from absolute values alone. Different prime pairs can serve on later windows because the four readings are put back together **before** the analytic step receives the fixed $G_1$.
