## Build upward from the first cut

1. **Supply the frame.** Ω names unresolved possibility in PAL. The first mathematical choice is a positive integer spacing and a numbered row. A frame is supplied at A0. Orientation uses increasing row number and the chosen zero; reversing it must carry its own origin and orientation. A nonidentity one-tick action needs at least two marks. This example earns a reading and imposes no geometry on Ω.
2. **Read and carry.** At actual row $k\ge1$, read $b_d(k)=(k\bmod d)/d$. Read the old remainder and the next quotient digit off $k\bmod(dp)$. Conversely, the two digits reconstruct the joint remainder exactly. Visits 1 and 31 have identical marks in frames 5 and 6. A reading modulo 11 separates them. A1 supplies a difference; A2 retains where it came from. A residue identifies a class of possible rows. The row's full source is needed when a later query splits that class.
3. **Attach the weight to the visit.** Row $k$ has weight $1/[k(k+1)]$, the length of $(1/(k+1),1/k]$. For residue class $r$, retain the sum over every lap. Splitting the class at a new scale adds its daughter masses back to its old mass. A copied prediction has identical squared error before and after the split. This is the A3/A4 transport to use before claiming that a refit improved anything.
4. **Measure the productive move.** For residual $r$, new reading $g$ and coefficient $a$, the improvement in squared error is $2a\langle r,g\rangle-a^2\langle g,g\rangle$. The sign of the overlap matters. Remove parts already spanned by old readings before combining gains. A5 feedback here must actually change a prediction. Labels elsewhere in A6–A12 introduce hypotheses only if the construction needs them. One valid return at A12 gives another attempt.
5. **Earn cadence and slack.** At A13, ask for a particular tolerance *before* the run. A well-founded decrease with a legal next move while the error is too high, or a proved cumulative gain that forces the requested crossing, can earn it. A strictly decreasing real sequence can stop above a positive floor. A14 keeps the full head and tail in the same squared-error units. Preserve earlier states as history when current coefficients are refit.
6. **Close the named mathematical claim.** For each $K$, exhibit or prove the existence of finite rational coefficients and a finite head whose certified whole-row error is below $1/(K+1)$. An off-line zero would annihilate every residue reading under a continuous Mellin measurement, but that measurement of the constant 1 is nonzero. The existing stock-Mathlib implication takes those quantified data to RH. A15 closes the exact scope established at the preceding step. ## Why start at squares and why reopen old weights?

Pang’s August RPL-T02 observes that after stripping all primes at most $B$ from a positive $n\le B^2$, the leftover is 1 or one larger prime to exponent one. Thus the square window brings a fresh prime with old cofactors and can also bring a previously missing joint made entirely of old primes. In $(4,16]$ the latter includes 6. The four-row reading forces the coefficient of 6 toward $-1$ in any convergent fit. Our rational witness already crosses the missing-6 error floor. The best fit on 2,3,6 alone puts that coefficient on the positive side. A valid rule must allow a correct earlier fit to be revised.

There is also a scale constraint from outside the corpus. The lower bound in equation (1.2) of Báez-Duarte's paper gives squared error at least a positive constant divided by $\log B$ for arbitrarily large maximum modulus $B$ (indeed for every sufficiently large cutoff). Our balanced span is contained in the span to which this lower bound applies. A fixed percentage decrease at every doubling of $B$ would eventually be faster than that lower bound allows. Squaring $B$ doubles its logarithm and makes the arithmetic classification above available. It supplies a justified experiment; it does not assert that the hoped-for gain occurs. [Pang, RPL §§4, 8, 19–22](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21894278) · [Báez-Duarte, equation (1.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0202141) · [Prior four-row obstruction and its witness](README.md)

For the primary route write $E_j$ for the infimum of the actual all-row squared error up to $B_j$, with $B_{j+1}=B_j^2$. One sufficient closure budget is a proved arithmetic gain fraction $\eta_j\in[0,1)$ with $E_{j+1}\le (1-\eta_j)E_j$ and $\sum_j\eta_j=\infty$. For example, an eventually valid $c/(j+1)$ with fixed $c>0$ suffices. Inferring $\eta_j$ by dividing two fitted output values only measures past progress. Show how the arithmetic supplies the gain at all the stated scales. Also show how to select approximants near the infimum and round coefficients before using the finite certificate.
