# What two nearly coincident clocks can actually read

Christopher Daniel Pang and Hearthline · 25 November 2026

A near-parallel pair defeated the unscaled inverse bound in the [fresh-prime calculation](fresh-prime-transfer.md). Here we carry its **difference**. The signal is made entirely from admissible forward readings. The first experiment spends two successive, opposite-signed corrections on the actual first-tick residual, then tests those corrections again on the constant target.

## Which rows meet?

Let $D_p f(k)=f(\lfloor k/p\rfloor)$, $q=p+h$, with positive integers $p,h$. Put $C f=D_p f-D_q f$. Both transports send rational residue fits to rational residue fits. Write $k=pt+r$ with $t\ge1$, $0\le r<p$ and $ht\le p$. Then
$$
Cf(pt+r)=
\begin{cases}
f(t)-f(t-1),&0\le r<ht,\\
0,&ht\le r<p.
\end{cases}
$$
Indeed $k/q=t+(r-ht)/q$. The clocks either agree or sit on opposite sides of exactly one old tick. The combined original weight of the rows that disagree is
$$
\sum_{k=pt}^{qt-1}\frac1{k(k+1)}
=\frac1{pt}-\frac1{qt}=\frac{h}{pqt}.
$$
This comparison earns a signed derivative on a known piece of the fine frame. One cannot grant a one-step shift on the entire original frame from this formula. [Lean location, reading, mass and whole-error bounds](PALRH/CloseClocks.lean)

For bounded $|f|\le M$, $T\ge0$ with $hT\le p$, let
$$
H_f=\frac h{pq}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{(f(t)-f(t-1))^2}{t}.
$$
All remaining physical rows start at $p(T+1)$. Their total weight is $1/[p(T+1)]$. Consequently
$$
H_f\le\|Cf\|^2\le H_f+\frac{4M^2}{p(T+1)}.
$$
For the actual Möbius signal from the [sieve route](reversing-the-sieve.md), the difference on the responsive band is **exactly** $\rho_B-\sigma_B(t)$. That signed sieve observation is now generated by two legal forward clocks on the stated band. [Lean specialization](PALRH/Sieve.lean)

## The new metric records common factors

Write $\Delta b_a(t)=1/a-[a\mid t]$ for $t\ge1$ and $\ell=\mathrm{lcm}(a,b)$. In a complete common period the mean of $\Delta b_a\Delta b_b$ is $1/\ell-1/(ab)=(\gcd(a,b)-1)/(ab)$. Over the actual weighted bands, the following identity holds for **every** horizon, including horizons shorter than one period. With $H_n=\sum_{i=1}^n1/i$ and $H_0=0$,
$$
\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{\Delta b_a(t)\Delta b_b(t)}t
=\frac{H_T-H_{\lfloor T/a\rfloor}-H_{\lfloor T/b\rfloor}}{ab}
 +\frac{H_{\lfloor T/\ell\rfloor}}{\ell}.
$$
Expand the two divisibility indicators; the intersection is the set of multiples of $\ell$. Summing the multiples of $d$ with weight $1/t$ gives $H_{\lfloor T/d\rfloor}/d$. These finite identities are proved in Lean under the names `edge_gram_period` and `edge_harmonic`.

Take $T=\lfloor p/h\rfloor$ with $p/h\to\infty$. The uninspected cross term after normalization by $pq/h$ has magnitude at most a fixed constant for fixed channels. The same tail weight applies to the bounded product of their two differences. Dividing by $\log(p/h)$ therefore yields the paper limit
$$
\frac{pq}{h\log(p/h)}\langle Cb_a,Cb_b\rangle
 \longrightarrow \frac{\gcd(a,b)-1}{ab}.
$$
Coprime old channels are orthogonal to leading order in this particular contrast metric. A common factor survives. For a finite old fit $\sum_a c_a b_a$, its limiting squared price is
$$
\sum_{d>1}\varphi(d)\left(\sum_{a:\ d\mid a}\frac{c_a}{a}\right)^2 .
$$
This follows from $\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=n$. The inner sum includes only the old moduli actually present. The formula makes the cross term human-readable by grouping the shared divisors. It is a price for the direction, and an improvement also needs a signed overlap with the actual residual.

## Two paid moves on actual witnesses

Start with the rational constant fit $f$ from the branch's original 61 weights. Its backward transport $g=f-D_2 f$ was the [first-tick witness](one-tick.md). It has ordinary periodic mean zero, since every old phase is visited twice in $D_2 f$. Thus the positive-mean leading term from the earlier fixed-parent analysis cannot predict the correction sign on this pulse.

We tested $C_{101,103}b_2$ and $C_{107,109}b_2$, where the subscripts give the two clock rates. Both pairs have spacing two, and their residues modulo six are $(5,1)$. The corrections vanish on the first hundred physical rows.

| Receiver and move | Coefficients in order | Change in full squared error |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-tick fit, original band for each pair | $+1/2,\ -1/2$ | Error decreases at each step, by more than $1/10000$ and $1/90000$ respectively. |
| Original constant fit, use each contrast together with its doubled-clock copy | $+1/2,\ -1/2$ | Both successive moves **increase** error. |
| Same constant fit, same two transported directions, reopen their weights | $+1/5,\ -1/8$ | Both successive moves **decrease** error. Their combined gain exceeds $1/100000$. |

In each trial the second move uses the residual **after the first move**. The original fit's first hundred readings are preserved while its full constant-target error improves. Binary64 exploration selected places and small rational weights; the claims in the table use integer enclosures of $2r c-c^2$ and a signed two-sided bound beyond the cutoff. A first-tick direction has magnitude at most $1/2$. With its doubled copy it is half the difference of two two-bit digit counts and has magnitude at most one. The checker charges the earlier correction in the envelope of the second. [Exact replay](../receipt-archives/check_pal_close_clocks.py)

## Unresolved

For a growing fitted residual $r$, the new overlap depends on its signed mass on $[pt,qt)$, multiplied by the old event increment. The count and the cost of these bands are now explicit. To apply the key’s cadence theorem we still need enough **realized signed overlap** across a sequence of admissible updates, with the outside bands paid for. Neither the coprime leading term nor two successful updates establishes that lower bound.
