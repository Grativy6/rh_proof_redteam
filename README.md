# RH proof red team

Christopher D. Pang and Hearthline are trying to break the Riemann Hypothesis proof released by Levent Alpöge and Claude on 20 November 2026. We use Pang’s PAL Mathematical Realization Atlas to ask which information a step retains, how its measurements are transported, and exactly where a bound becomes uniform. [Original announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [PAL v2.4](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22888036)

A [paper bound for every linear retraction onto the cusp eigenspace](receipt-archives/2026-11-23-integral-retraction-norm.md) puts a lower bound on the sum of its archimedean height and its logarithmic denominator. Strong approximation gives the exact component orbit size appearing in that bound.

**What happens if the level trace is normalized on integral cusp forms?** Cutting an eigenform to one component and averaging back forces an orbit denominator with an odd prime beyond the level. The source uses the full sum at the integral norm estimate. A separate trace on the cover’s coefficient field has denominator $4\cdot15^{s-1}$ or $8\cdot15^{s-1}$, with no change in root discriminant. [Geometric level trace](receipt-archives/2026-11-23-level-trace-on-cusp-components.md) · [Degree remaining over the actual core](receipt-archives/2026-11-23-core-ray-intersection.md)

The [field chosen for the deep cover](receipt-archives/2026-11-23-deep-cover-ray-field.md) has an unramified layer of exact degree $h(K)\varphi(M)/2$ above the cyclotomic field of the level and the quadratic base. Its root discriminant stays fixed across that layer. Averaging back has the whole degree as its denominator.

**What can every complex embedding and every required small prime miss?** On a field forced by the zeta windows, a root of unity averages to an element with exact denominator $(d-1)/2$. The same lattice also gives an exact positive slope gap for an isometric inclusion. The released inequality charges that gap through the discriminant. **How large must the coefficient field be on the actual zeta windows?** Its degree over the host field eventually exceeds every fixed power of the window scale. The splitting conditions force many distinct ideal classes, and the release requires a root of unity of order the host class number. **Can an arithmetic map look ideal at one embedding while its height grows without bound?** Yes. An integral shear has height $\log 15127$ while its squared norm on the tested column is within $1/15126^2$ of one at the chosen place. The release estimates the same pairing at every infinite place. One of its terminal branching maps has exact squared norm $8/T^2$. **Can all character sums be integral while every class has a denominator?** Yes, on actual arithmetic data. All 891 character sums are integral at 3. Each of the 891 individual normalized twelfth powers has valuation exactly $-12$. The total zeta value hides this cost, and the proof’s maximum over partial zeta values charges it exactly. **What happens where the raw sum returns zero?** At the Hecke boundary the direct infinite sum gives zero on an explicit input whose normalized continued value is between 0.00595240410576 and 0.00595240410577. A separate Fourier term also takes the value zero there, with a strictly positive first derivative. [The two zeros and the value used by the release](receipt-archives/2026-11-23-toric-hecke-boundary.md)

**Does dividing by 34 spend a forbidden factor of 17?** The release divides by 34 to recover each complex class function. In a permitted conductor-137 example its arithmetic estimate allows no denominator at 17. All 34 class forms have unit constant coefficients there, and all 34 Fourier constants are divisible by exactly one power of 17. A separate all-cusps calculation proves integrality at the actual CM point. **Which lattice classes actually supply the global count?** In a rank-four example, a lattice with no unit vector contributes 18/23 of the mass. We constructed it and counted its stabilizer. **Can the projection erase a strictly positive pairing?** Yes. On one admitted source an integral test goes from positive to exactly zero. The lost value is divisible by $79^{81024}$. A second test on that same source attains valuation 41524 both before and after projection, inside the required budget. **Can a required weight disappear on an admitted input?** Yes. A divided derivative has weight one where the proof needs a weight-two part. In our example the discrepancy disappears modulo 79 to the power 110600 and reappears at the next power. The allowed error covers it. **Must the detecting test have the exact source type?** We fixed a vector whose exact type is a line and varied the weights in the pairing. Positive factorial weights make every integral test on it miss the bound to arbitrary precision. A permitted cofactor test one weight lower detects the same input. **When does the upward theta integral cancel?** Two degree-two oscillator vectors have the same compact weight. One gives zero. The harmonic vector gives $1/(b+1)$ for $b$ oscillator pairs. The [local integral calculation](receipt-archives/2026-11-23-oscillator-cancellation.md) determines the whole kernel in rank one and tests the higher-rank positivity argument.

**Which directions can the period lose?** An integer vector can have an orthogonal quotient representative with denominator 5. We traced the condition that keeps the period integral, and proved a bound for the proportion of source directions that can disappear after projection. **A residue in the quotient metric.** In a rank-one test, the algebraic and geometric answers differ by $\log(1+\sqrt[3]4)-\tfrac13\log5$. The difference can grow without bound even with fixed lattices. We followed its sign through the upper bound used in the proof. [Hand calculation and source trace](receipt-archives/2026-11-23-quotient-covolume.md)

**Can two lattices agree about every rational vector and still disagree about divisibility?** Yes. One can hide half a valuation, and a rank-two example hides a determinant index of $5^N$ with the fields and rational map fixed. The [valuation calculation](receipt-archives/2026-11-23-valuation-descent.md) traces the extra integral-span condition and the denominator of the actual period.

**Can a nonzero coefficient disappear?** The theta-lift code returns zero for a complex coefficient outside the chosen number field. We found an explicit nonzero input that disappears and an integral line where a mistaken choice would lose half the logarithm of 3. The proof builds its field from the original complex coefficients and checks the degrees the operator reads. We found no loss at that step.
